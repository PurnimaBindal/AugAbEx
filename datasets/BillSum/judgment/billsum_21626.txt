This Act may be cited as the ``Mandates Information Act of 1998''.
The Congress finds the following: (1) Before acting on proposed private sector mandates, the Congress should carefully consider the effects on consumers, workers, and small businesses.
The Congress has often acted without adequate information concerning the costs of private sector mandates, instead focusing only on the benefits.
The costs of private sector mandates are often borne in part by consumers, in the form of higher prices and reduced availability of goods and services.
The costs of private sector mandates are often borne in part by workers, in the form of lower wages, reduced benefits, and fewer job opportunities.
The costs of private sector mandates are often borne in part by small businesses, in the form of hiring disincentives and stunted growth.
The purposes of this Act are the following: (1) To improve the quality of the Congress' deliberation with respect to proposed mandates on the private sector, by-- (A) providing the Congress with more complete information about the effects of such mandates; and (B) ensuring that the Congress acts on such mandates only after focused deliberation on the effects.
To enhance the ability of the Congress to distinguish between private sector mandates that harm consumers, workers, and small businesses, and mandates that help those groups.
FEDERAL PRIVATE SECTOR MANDATES.
In General.-- (1) Estimates.--Section 424(b)(2) of the Congressional Budget Act of 1974 (2 United States Code 658c(b)(2)) is amended-- (A) in subparagraph (A) by striking ``and'' after the semicolon; and (B) by redesignating subparagraph (B) as subparagraph (C), and inserting after subparagraph (A) the following: ``(B) when applicable, the impact (including any disproportionate impact in particular regions or industries) on consumers, workers, and small businesses, of the Federal private sector mandates in the bill or joint resolution, including-- ``(i) an analysis of the effect of the Federal private sector mandates in the bill or joint resolution on consumer prices and on the actual supply of goods and services in consumer markets; ``(ii) an analysis of the effect of the Federal private sector mandates in the bill or joint resolution on worker wages, worker benefits, and employment opportunities; and ``(iii) an analysis of the effect of the Federal private sector mandates in the bill or joint resolution on the hiring practices, expansion, and profitability of businesses with 100 or fewer employees; and''.
Point of order.--Section 424(b)(3) of the Congressional Budget Act of 1974 (2 United States Code 658c(b)(3)) is amended by adding after the period the following: ``If such determination is made by the Director, a point of order under this part shall lie only under section 425(a)(1) and as if the requirement of section 425(a)(1) had not been met.''.
Threshold amounts.--Section 425(a) of the Congressional Budget Act of 1974 (2 United States Code 658d(a)) is amended by-- (A) striking ``and'' after the semicolon at the end of paragraph (1) and redesignating paragraph (2) as paragraph (3); and (B) inserting after paragraph (1) the following new paragraph: ``(2) any bill, joint resolution, amendment, motion, or conference report that would increase the direct costs of Federal private sector mandates (excluding any direct costs that are attributable to revenue resulting from tax or tariff provisions of any such measure if it does not raise net tax and tariff revenues over the 5-fiscal-year period beginning with the first fiscal year such measure affects such revenues) by an amount that causes the thresholds specified in section 424(b)(1) to be exceeded; and''.
