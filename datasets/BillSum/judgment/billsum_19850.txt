This Act may be cited as the ``Cocopah Lands Act''.
Congress finds the following: (1) The reservation of the Cocopah Indian Tribe of Arizona is located in Yuma County, Arizona.
That reservation was created by an Executive order signed by President Woodrow Wilson in 1917.
That reservation is made up of 3 noncontiguous tracts of land.
The Tribe inhabits all 3 parts of the reservation.
The Tribe purchased the additional lands to provide infrastructure to housing areas, water, and economic development to tribal members.
The current trust land base of the reservation is insufficient to provide such needs.
The Tribe acquired 7 parcels of land contiguous to its present reservation lands in 1986, 1993, 1997, and 2005, and these parcels are currently classified as ``Indian Lands'' under Federal law.
The acquired parcels shall not be taken into trust for gaming purposes.
The best means of solving the Tribe's land and economic needs to its tribal members is to require the Secretary to take lands in Yuma County, Arizona, that are acquired by the Tribe into trust for the Tribe subject to the provisions of this Act.
For the purpose of this Act, the following definitions apply: (1) Tribe.--The term ``Tribe'' means the Cocopah Indian Tribe of Arizona.
Secretary.--The term ``Secretary'' means the Secretary of the Interior.
LANDS TO BE TAKEN INTO TRUST.

Lands to Be Made Part of the Reservation.--Land taken into trust pursuant to subsection (a) shall be considered to be part of the Tribe's initial reservation.
Service Area.--For the purposes of the delivery of Federal services to enrolled members of the Tribe, the Tribe's service area shall be Yuma County, Arizona.
Gaming Prohibited.--Land taken into trust for the benefit of the Tribe under this Act shall not be used for gaming under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act.
The Secretary may promulgate such regulations as may be necessary to carry out this Act.
