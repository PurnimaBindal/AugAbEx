Vaccine Injured Children's Compensation Act of 2001 - Amends provisions of the Public Health Service Act relating to the National Vaccine Injury Compensation Program to: designate the Program as a remedial program under which sovereign immunity does not apply; change the burden of proof requirement for the award of compensation from a preponderance of the evidence to evidence sufficient to justify a belief that the petitioner's claims are well grounded (while giving the benefit of doubt to the petitioner); require any defense raised that an illness, injury, or death was due to unrelated factors to be proved by clear and convincing evidence; authorize as Program compensation expenses necessary for the establishment of a trust to receive Program funds, as well as expenses incurred for family counseling or training necessitated by the vaccine-related injury; allow the award of petitioner's attorneys' fees; increase to up to 72 months the statute of limitations under the Program; allow such period to be extended for an additional 36 months after a petitioner first knew or should have known about his or her eligibility for compensation; toll the statute of limitations until a petitioner reaches age 18 and, if a petitioner is incompetent, until 24 months after a guardian is appointed; and authorize the refiling of a previously failed petition if the petitioner would have met the extended statute of limitations provided under this Act.
