Amends the Tariff Act of 1930 to repeal drawback and refund of duty requirements relating to: certain articles made from imported merchandise; substitution for drawback purposes; merchandise not conforming to sample or specifications; imported salt for curing fish; exportation of meats cured with imported salt; materials for construction and equipment of vessels built for foreigners; jet aircraft engines; an unused merchandise drawback; use of domestic merchandise acquired in exchange for imported merchandise of same kind and quality; vessels built for account of resident of North Atlantic Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA) country; substitution of finished petroleum derivatives; packaging material; designation of merchandise by successor; drawback certificates; eligibility of entered or withdrawn merchandise; multiple drawback claims; drawbacks for recovered material; and articles shipped to the US insular possessions.
Revises and/or prescribes procedures regarding: drawback for exported merchandise; drawback claimants; a time limitation for filing drawback claims; the drawback amount; refunds, waivers, or reductions under certain free trade agreements; merchandise for use in certain vessels; agricultural merchandise; destruction of merchandise; a limitation on exportation requirement; claiming exportation or destruction; and drawback regulations.
Defines "drawback" to mean a refund of 99% of applicable duties, taxes, and fees paid pursuant to federal law upon importation of merchandise, and not refunded under any other law, in a case in which: the imported merchandise or its substitute, or drawback merchandise or its substitute, is exported; or the imported merchandise is destroyed.
