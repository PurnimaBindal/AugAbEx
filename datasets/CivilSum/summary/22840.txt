Constitution of India, Articles 310 and 311 - Retiring an employee prematurely due to the leakage of official information is considered a misconduct or an offense.
Giving a workman details of their absence during litigation is not considered either of these things.
Punishing an employee with premature retirement and labeling it as misconduct is arbitrary and an abuse of power by the Disciplinary Authority.
[Paras 11 to 16]

Constitution of India, Articles 226, 310 and 311 - In cases of compulsory retirement, public interest is the primary concern.
If there are doubts about an employee's integrity or usefulness, and public interest demands it, then they may be removed.
However, if there is no evidence or if the order is arbitrary or mala fide, then the decision can be reviewed by a court.
Providing a workman with details of their absence during litigation does not involve public interest or the integrity of the employee.
Therefore, the decision to retire an employee prematurely is not sustainable.
[Paras 17 to 21]
