Section 11-A of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 indicates that the Labour Court may not necessarily direct the reinstatement of an employee who has been acquitted in a criminal case.
This is only one of the factors taken into consideration when determining appropriate punishment under this section.
[Para 26] B.
When a Labour Court reevaluates evidence and concludes that the findings of the Enquiry Officer are based on evidence, it may consider the judgment rendered in the criminal court.
In this case, the Labour Court decided that the petitioner was entitled to reinstatement without backwages or continuity of service.
The award is not legally flawed.
[Para 27] C. There is sufficient evidence that the petitioner drove recklessly and negligently, resulting in a fatal accident involving two women, one of whom died.
The petitioner is therefore not entitled to any backwages or continuity of service.
[Para 27] D. Regulation 28(ix)(a) and (b) of the APSRTC Employees (Conduct) Regulations, 1963 specifies that misconduct in cases of reckless and negligent driving that causes significant harm to the corporation is punishable.
The driving of a bus in a reckless and negligent manner without proper care would certainly qualify as misconduct under these rules.
