The trial of a government officer charged with misappropriation, cheating, etc. was barred under Rule 22 of Punjab Civil Services Rules, Vol. II, which prescribes a limitation of four years from the date of the event.
The officer was allowed to retire in 1990, and the challan was presented in 1991.
The court held that the trial was barred, and the FIR was quashed.
[Paras 6 and 7]
If a government officer is charged with corruption or embezzlement, and he retires at the age of 58, prosecution cannot be launched after four years from the date of the incident.
Rule 22 of Punjab Civil Services Rules, Vol. II prescribes this limitation.
The embargo is not for the limited purpose of pension only; it also enures to the benefit of the accused, who may object to the continuation of the trial.
[Paras 6 and 7]
The rules framed by the Governor under Article 309 of the Constitution of India are legislative in character.
[Para 5]
When the bar of limitation takes away the remedy, the ability of the court to punish the accused is taken away by express provision of law.
The law creating limitation bars the remedy of having the offender punished and thereby does not suffer from the vice of repugnancy.
[Para 8]
No criminal proceedings can be instituted in respect of an event which took place more than four years before the date on which a complaint or report of the Police Officer, on which the court takes cognizance, is made, whether the officer was in service, retired or re-employed at that time.
This applies to an offence of fraud and misappropriation against a government servant.
