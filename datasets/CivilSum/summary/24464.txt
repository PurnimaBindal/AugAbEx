The regularisation of ad hoc, daily wage, and temporary employees appointed without following proper recruitment rules is against the right of equality under Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution and violates the constitutional scheme of appointment to public employment.
Furthermore, the length of ad hoc, temporary, or casual employment is not grounds for regularisation and such appointees do not acquire any enforceable right under the court of law.
Ad hoc, temporary, and casual appointees cannot claim equality with regular recruits in the matter of wages and allowances and treating them equally would violate Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
The doctrine of legitimate expectation cannot be invoked in the absence of any promise made to the ad hoc, temporary, and casual appointees.
Poverty, unemployment, and lack of equality of bargaining power is not grounds for regularisation and deprives a large number of other aspirants of an opportunity.
Finally, ad hoc, temporary, and daily wage employees cannot claim regularisation but should be given preference on the basis of their experience at the time the same posts are filled on a regular basis.
