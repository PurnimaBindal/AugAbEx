Vijay Bank Officer Employees (Discipline and Appeal) Regulations, 1981, Rule 6(7) relating to disciplinary enquiries stipulates that an accused can be represented by a legal practitioner only if the presenting officer is legally trained or a legal practitioner.
In a case where a simple law graduate served as the presenting officer and the accused was a Divisional Manager, the accused was provided a full opportunity to cross-examine and present his defence.
There was no prejudice on account of not allowing legal representation, and the order passed was not illegal.
[Paras 11, 12, and 15]

In disciplinary proceedings that involve punishment authority that disagrees with certain findings of the inquiry officer, the notice to the accused is not necessary when the punishing authority can have its own inquiry and make its own findings.
This follows from the fact that while in disagreement with the inquiry officer, the punishing authority has a complete record of the proceedings.
This stance aligns with the Supreme Court's decision in SK Koshal's case and the principles of natural justice enshrined in Article 14 of the Constitution of India.
[Para 16]

The failure of the appellate authority to provide an opportunity to be heard in disciplinary proceedings does not violate the principles of natural justice spelled out in Article 14 of the Constitution of India.
