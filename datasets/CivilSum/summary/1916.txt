The Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985, Section 24 (Proviso) and Section 19(1) discusses the issue of interim injunction and the notice that must be given to respondents.
It highlights the importance of sufficient evidence, including a prima facie case, irreparable loss, balance of convenience, and public interest before any order is granted.
Additionally, the act mandates that the Tribunal cannot pass an ex parte interim order without recording reasons as to why the loss suffered by an applicant would not be adequately compensated.
The act also stipulates that the ex parte interim order would stand automatically vacated on the expiry of 14 days if the Tribunal does not comply with the conditions.
The Act is essential in ensuring that appointments of selected candidates are not casually stayed, which could result in significant losses.
Lastly, the existence of all necessary ingredients, including the mandate of section 24, must be considered before granting a stay of appointments of selected candidates.
Otherwise, it would be a serious illegality, particularly when the Tribunal had already declined an interim relief in another case against the same selection.
