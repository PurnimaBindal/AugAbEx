Constitution of India, Article 226 - Employees State Insurance Act (34 of 1948), Section 82 - The Petitioner challenged the order that covered them and determined ESI contribution payable without exhausting the remedy available by way of appeal under Section 82 of ESI Act, arguing that a statutory appeal cannot be bypassed.
However, the court found this contention to be not tenable since the main grievance of the Petitioner was the lack of opportunity of hearing and the infirmity and perversity of the impugned order, which directly affected their rights.
Therefore, it is not true that no substantial question of law is involved, and in any case, if an appeal is not maintainable due to the non-involvement of substantial question of law, then a writ would not be maintainable either.
The court cannot depart from legislative wisdom and policy by allowing a party to reduce the writ jurisdiction to an alternative remedy.
[Paras 4 and 5]
Employees State Insurance Act (34 of 1948), Section 45-A - The petitioner's contention that the number of employees was 8 and power was being used for manufacturing was contradicted by their muster showing 9 employees.
The huge payment shown in vouchers cannot be presumed to be paid to only 9 workers, while the Petitioner kept back crucial material from inspectors.
The competent authority, therefore, passed an order assessing the contribution payable based on assessment judgment that was entirely based on evidence oral and documentary, and not perverse and infirm.
The Petitioner's quarrel is restricted to the period up to 1984, and thereafter, the Petitioner has already accepted coverage and made contributions, so the order of coverage does not need any interference.
