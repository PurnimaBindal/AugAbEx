Constitution of India, Articles 30, 226 and 32 - Dentists Act, 1948, Sections 10A and 20 - Enrollment of educational institutions - Approval of Dental Council - Compliance with the eligibility, status and quality standards prescribed for institutions - Courts have limited jurisdiction to interfere with the discretion exercised by expert bodies, despite education being a fundamental right in Part III of the Constitution - However, if such bodies act for a dishonest purpose, the courts can correct the arbitrary exercises of power of these authorities - Although some regard commercialising education as acceptable, private educational `shops' cannot be allowed in the country - Establishing and administering educational institutions without the prior permission or approval by the relevant authority is prohibited by statute - Nevertheless, the Court's involvement can be minimal in this field as it is the role of expert bodies such as the Medical Council or Dental Council.
