Civil Procedure Code, 1908 Order 39 Rule 1 and Section 115 - Transfer - Mala fide - Discretion of the court - The lower courts found that the transfer order was issued as punishment and was mala fide due to prior litigation between the parties.
The courts' conclusions were based on acceptable prima facie evidence presented.
No legal faults or errors were found in the execution of discretionary powers by the lower courts, and their concurrent findings do not require interference.
[Para 5]

Civil Procedure Code, 1908 Order 39, Rule 1 and Section 151 - Transfer - Jurisdiction of the court - The court must respect that transfer is an administrative act given to the employer to determine an employee's place of work.
The court may only interfere if there is a breach of statutory regulations, mala fide, or jurisdictional absence.
The burden of proof lies with the person claiming mala fide.
The Court can consider all circumstances when taking an action.
[Para 5]

Civil Procedure Code, 1908 Section 151 and Order 39 Rule 1 - Interlocutory order - Interim relief - Section 151 is used to further justice and prevent court abuse.
The Court can grant any relief defendant consequential to the main relief sought in the suit.
The employer did not allow their employee to work despite the court's injunction.
As a result, the employee filed an application asking for their unpaid salary.
Section 151 grants the right to the trial court to award consequential relief through interlocutory orders when the primary relief is obtained.
There is no need for interference, particularly when the main relief has been granted and has gotten final.
