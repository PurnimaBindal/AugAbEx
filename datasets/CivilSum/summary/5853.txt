The labour court, when computing claims under Section 33-C(2) of the Industrial Disputes Act, has jurisdiction to interpret statutory provisions and policies or agreements between employers and management, and may grant a claim based on an existing right.
However, it must refrain from intervening where such a right must be conferred within the scope of reference under Section 10 of the Act.
The mere dispute over a right is not sufficient grounds to deny a claim.
Claims of equal pay for equal work can be computed under Section 33-C(2) of the Act, based on Articles 14 and 39(d) of the Constitution.
[Paras 018 and 19]

When it comes to the grant of equal pay for equal work under Section 33-C(2) of the Industrial Disputes Act, the nature of appointment is irrelevant, so long as temporary or daily-wage workers are performing the same duties as their permanent counterparts and possess the same physical strength and capacity to work.
[Para 20]

Daily-wage workers are entitled to the claim of equal pay for equal work under Section 33-C(2) of the Industrial Disputes Act.
This right has been guaranteed to them by the Constitution from the date of their appointment.
Section 33-C(2) comes into effect when any benefit or money claimable from an employer is due, whether in the present or future.
[Para 21]

The labour court can compute these benefits under Section 33-C(2) of the Industrial Disputes Act.
