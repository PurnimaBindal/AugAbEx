Section 10 of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, lays out the process for referring a dispute to the government to be resolved, but the government can deny a reference.
In this case, whether the respondents were considered workmen, supervisors or contract labor was disputed, but there was enough material on record to show the existence of an industrial dispute.
The government is not expected to minutely examine whether there are precise pleadings as in a civil suit when deciding whether to make a reference.
The learned single judge ordered that the respondent seek a reference, and the appellant will not be prejudiced by participating in proceedings before the Tribunal.
