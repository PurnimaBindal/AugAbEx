The Supreme Court held that respondents appointed as Food Inspectors under the Prevention of Food Adulteration Act, 1954, Section 9(1) were not entitled to seek their posting order as they were appointed only for the purposes of discharging their duties under the Act.
The Court also noted that no ad hoc rules were framed at the time of their appointment and they had not acquired any right to seek posting orders.
The Central Administrative Tribunal's direction to issue posting orders was an error and the appeal was allowed.
As a result, all representation petitions were rejected and the orders passed by CAT were set aside.
