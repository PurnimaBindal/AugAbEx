The order of dismissal in this case must be quashed as there was no inquiry conducted by the competent authority, nor was there any material to show that it was not reasonably practicable to hold an inquiry.
The respondents did not provide a plea that holding an inquiry against the petitioners was improbable and they did not present any material to suggest that the persons from whom the petitioners had extorted money would not come forward to depose against them.
Also, there was no evidence to indicate that the petitioners had threatened any person.
As a result, the order of dismissal cannot be sustained.
[Paras 7 and 8]
The plea made by respondents that the impugned order could be sustained with reference to Article 311(2)(c) is baseless as only the Governor of the State can exercise power under Article 311(2)(c).
The Senior Superintendent of Police does not have the authority to do so under this article.
Hence, the impugned order cannot be held to have been issued in accordance with the power granted by Article 311(2)(c).
[Para 6]
The dismissal order has been deemed illegal and quashed, but respondents may take action to suspend the petitioner.
However, since the petitioners have not performed their duties during the intervening period, they will not be entitled to monetary benefits like back wages between the date of their dismissal and the date of this order.
