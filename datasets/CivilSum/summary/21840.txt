Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16, 37, 38 and 39(d) - Regularisation - Regularisation of daily wage workers of a University funded fully by the State - Workers working on the class IV posts for over 10 years must be absorbed and regularised by relaxing qualifications, regardless of financial constraints.
Continuation of work for a long period of time carries a presumption of a regular post.
The institution must only take on work that is within its financial resources and taking work from daily wage or ad hoc employees must only be for a short period or stop gap arrangement.
If the work is continuously taken, the legal obligation is cast on the employer to regularise the employees, filling vacant posts if necessary and relaxing qualifications.
Financial health is a consideration, but this should not provide institutions with the opportunity to engage a large number of daily wage workers for an extended period without absorption or creating posts, which constitutes an unfair labour practice.
Thus, in this case, the prescribed qualifications should not pose a problem for regularisation in light of the employees' long experience, and financial means must be stretched to the maximum.
The State Government should set up an enquiry to assess the number of additional posts required for regularising workers and create such posts within six months.
