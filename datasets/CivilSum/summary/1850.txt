Article 226 of the Constitution of India outlines that an employee can be removed from duty for any proven instances of misconduct or absence from duty.
However, in this case, the punishment of removal from service is disproportionate to the charge and may indicate bias.
Instead, the punishment of withholding two annual grade increments without cumulative effect and reinstating the petitioner in service with continuity and other benefits, except for the withdrawal of 50% of back wages from dismissal date till date can meet the ends of justice, based on the 1999(4) SCT 749 (SC) case.
[Paras 10 & 13] B Article 226 of the Constitution of India allows the High Court to interfere with a punishment awarded by disciplinary authorities if it is found disproportionate to the proven misconduct.
[Para 8] C. Article 14 and 226 of the Constitution of India stipulate that the penalty to be imposed must match the severity of the misconduct, and a disproportionate punishment would be violative of Article 14 of the constitution.
