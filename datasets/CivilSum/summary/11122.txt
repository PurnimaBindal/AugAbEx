Clause 13(2) of Standing Orders under the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 does not align with the spirit of Articles 14 and 21 of the Constitution.
[Para 7]
Clause 13(2) of Standing Orders under the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 permits termination for absence without leave, but the order of termination must conform to principles of natural justice.
An order of termination passed as a measure of punishment can only be sustained in conformity with these principles.
The order of termination in this case, passed without an opportunity for the appellant to defend himself, cannot be upheld.
[Para 8]
The Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, and Section 100 of the Civil Procedure Code, 1908 provide for reinstatement and back wages if termination is found to be unjust.
The appellant was terminated without opportunity to defend himself and was ordered to be reinstated and paid 50% of back wages.
The Board was blamed for not providing an opportunity for the appellant to defend himself, but the appellant also bore some responsibility for the action taken against him by the Board.
The appellant is entitled to full back wages for the period he remained out of service during his termination.
