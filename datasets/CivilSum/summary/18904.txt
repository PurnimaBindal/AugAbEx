The Constitution of India in Articles 14 and 21 provides for Compassionate Appointment, but it is only to be used in the case of extreme hardship.
It should be determined if an earning member is present and is looking after the dependents of the deceased employee.
In the case of this petitioner, the mother is already in a Government service and is getting family pension, which means that the family is not facing any distress.
Therefore, the rejection of the claim for compassionate appointment was appropriate.
[Paras 7, 8 and 10] B. Compassionate Appointment should only be used to alleviate extreme hardship, according to Article 14 and 21 of the Constitution of India.
Compassionate appointments were wrongly given to some persons, and there is no ground for similar relief.
Two wrongs cannot make a right, as outlined in Articles 14, 16 and 226 of the Constitution of India.
