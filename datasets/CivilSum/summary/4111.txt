In order to ensure proper discharge of the duties of the Armed Forces, fundamental rights can be restricted or abrogated in the case of its members, as per the Constitution of India, 1950, Articles 14, 21, 22, and 33, Border Security Force Act, 1968, and Section 64 and 141, Border Security Force Rules, Rules 63(1) and 157.
Rules framed under the Act have the same force of law as if those are contained in the Act, and the rules are not ultra vires the Act.
There is a minor deviation concerning the Summary Security Force Court, where the next friend is not allowed to cross-examine the witness or address the court, but this alone will not lead to the conclusion that the trial was unfair.
In the present case, the accused was given 24 hours to prepare his defense, and the guilt was admittedly admitted by the accused.
The accused did not request any time to prepare his defense or demand copies.
It is not necessary to promulgate the adjourned date of the hearing, and no prejudice was caused to the accused during the trial, as per paragraphs 6, 12, 15, 17, and 19 of the text.
