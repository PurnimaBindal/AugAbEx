The petitioner, who had been acquitted of criminal charges, was superseded twice in promotions without any adverse remarks or charges being issued against him.
The petitioner cannot be superseded merely because the department is considering initiating departmental proceedings.
[Paras 10 and 11]

The post of Financial Advisor was open to all eligible candidates, including the petitioner, and could be filled by direct recruitment as per rule.
The person who was promoted over the petitioner was found to be more suitable, even though he held a higher pay scale than the Financial Advisor post.
The petitioner's argument that the advertisement was a device to ignore him was unfounded.
[Paras 12 and 14]

The petitioner argued that he was denied effective opportunity of defence because he was not allowed legal assistance during the departmental proceedings, but the Presenting Officer was not actually a legal practitioner as defined in the regulation, and nothing showed that legal assistance was necessary for the petitioner.
The petitioner had cross-examined the witnesses at length and availed full opportunity for his defence, so the proceedings were not vitiated merely because legal assistance was denied.
[Para 21]

The enquiry officer had rightly refused to summon witnesses proposed by the delinquent to prove that the chargesheet was issued mala fide, as this was not a subject matter of the enquiry.
[Para 22]

The petitioner had requested for supply of notes recorded on files before the chargesheet was issued, but this was not necessary for proof of the charges, so non-supply of such notes did not result in a denial of reasonable opportunity to the petitioner.
[Para 23]

The petitioner argued that punishing someone for negligence was unfair when the actual embezzler was not punished.
The action should be recommended and appropriate punishment imposed if the petitioner is found guilty.
[Para 24]

The petitioner, who had been charged with misconduct in 1973, had been issued a chargesheet in 1984 and the order passed in 1994.
The fair rule is that the charge would relate back to the year of the alleged misconduct, and since the petitioner's service record was more meritorious than the respondent's, he cannot be ignored based on the stale events in the matter of promotion.
