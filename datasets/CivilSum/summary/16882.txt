The Constitution of India, Article 226, states that employees under contractual appointment have a safeguard of receiving a one-month notice if their service is deemed inefficient.
If this notice is not provided, the condition relating to termination cannot be overlooked under the guise of non-extension of the contractual period.
Any removal contrary to this condition is deemed illegal.
[Paras 7 to 8]

In accordance with the Constitution of India, Article 226, an appointment on a contractual basis for one year, continued for five years, raises serious allegations of illegal appointment and failure to distribute books.
It was determined that the allegations of misconduct against the petitioner were the foundation and not merely a motive; thus, it was necessary to conduct an inquiry.
The order of termination was passed without complying with the condition relating to the one-month notice of termination, and as such, the impugned order of termination must be quashed.
The petitioner must be reinstated, with back wages.
[Para 9]

According to the Constitution of India, Article 226, the principle of "no work, no pay" is not applicable in cases where a petitioner's removal is found to be illegal, contrary to rules, and without following principles of natural justice.
[Para 10]
