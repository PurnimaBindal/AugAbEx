The Workmen's Compensation Act of 1923 was invoked in a case where a truck driver was murdered while on the job.
The claim for compensation was contested by the insurance company on the grounds that the death was not accidental.
However, it was established that the driver would not have been in the situation that led to his death were it not for his role as a truck driver.
As a result, the compensation awarded was upheld.
In the same case, the question of when interest on compensation should be granted was also addressed.
It was held that interest should be awarded from the date of claim rather than the date of death.
