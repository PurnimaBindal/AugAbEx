Article 371-D of the Constitution of India, Section 10-A(2)(b) of the Indian Medical Council Act, 1956, the Dentists Act, 1948, the Establishment of New Medical and Dental Colleges Regulations, 1993, and the Andhra Pradesh Educational Institutions (Regulation of Admission) Order, 1974 (Presidential Order) govern the role of the State Government in the establishment of new medical or dental colleges.
The State Government must record its reasons for refusing to grant an essentiality certificate, though the Medical or Dental Council of India retains the authority to make recommendations to the Central Government.
Applications for new medical or dental colleges must be considered for all locations, and the State cannot withhold such applications through its power under Article 371-D [Paras 18 to 20, 28 and 29]

Section 10-A of the Indian Medical Council Act, 1956, allows for the constitution of a committee to evaluate and make recommendations for new medical or dental colleges.
The absence of the Vice-Chancellor of N.T.R.
University of Health Sciences as a member of the Committee does not invalidate the constitution of the committee, though the inclusion of the Vice-Chancellor could bring valuable academic expertise to the proceedings.
[Para 21]

A sitting judge of the high court may participate in the evaluation of applications for the establishment of new medical or dental institutions, though the issue of the constitutional propriety and validity of such participation remains open for consideration in an appropriate case.
The recommendations of such a committee are not invalidated by the inclusion of a sitting judge.
