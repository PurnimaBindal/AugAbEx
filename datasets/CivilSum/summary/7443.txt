The dismissal of a Border Security Force employee challenged on the grounds of inadequate explanation of charges and rights, as well as an alleged advising to plead guilty.
The court rejected these contentions, as the charge-sheet was supplied before record of evidence and the charges were explained as per record.
Additionally, the petitioner's plea of guilty during the trial was consistent with the evidence presented.
[Paras 13 and 14]
Another challenge to the dismissal of a Border Security Force employee, this time on the grounds of violating rules by not allowing sufficient time to seek legal assistance.
However, the petitioner never requested additional assistance beyond an appointed officer, and had no intention to contest the charges.
Therefore, this plea was rejected as well.
[Para 15]
A challenge to the proportionality of a punishment of dismissal for a Border Security Force employee, who was punished on three earlier occasions.
Despite this, the court found no reason to interfere with the punishment, considering the nature and gravity of the charges and the unsatisfactory past service record.
[Para 19]
The non-compliance of a Border Security Force Rule alleged, specifically Rule 161 and the authority of the countersigning DIG.
However, the court found that the DIG had access to the entire record of evidence and SSFC trial, making the plea baseless.
