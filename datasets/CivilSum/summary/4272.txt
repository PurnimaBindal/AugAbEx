Article 226 of the Constitution of India, along with Sections 11, 11-A, and 25-F of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, govern the guidelines for reinstatement of terminated employees.
If an employee's termination is set aside, they are usually reinstated with full back wages.
However, if there are valid reasons, the labor court can deviate from this rule.
[Para 5] B.
The writ jurisdiction of the High Court is limited, and it can only interfere with the award given if it is arbitrary and unfair.
In a case where a petitioner challenged their termination after a delay of two years, the award of reinstatement with 60% back wages is justified, and the High Court has no jurisdiction unless the award is shown to be against the law.
[Paras 6 and 11] C. The labor court can reduce the sanction awarded to an employee if they find it to be too harsh.
In such cases, the employee may not be entitled to any back wages, and the High Court can only interfere if the award is unfair or arbitrary.
