Under the Employees' Provident Fund and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952, Section 6(c), if a proprietary firm is brought under the provisions of the Act, the proprietor is required to pay contributions.
In the scenario where the proprietor dies and the management is taken over by the sons who fail to pay contributions, they become liable for prosecution.
The firm will continue to be governed by the Act until authorities conclude that it no longer exists, and there is no evidence of the business being wound up.
Therefore, the first and second respondents remain liable.
