Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16 & 226 - Admission/Education - Admission in MDS Course - The petitioner, an in-service candidate, applied for a two-year course in Master of Dental Surgery and met all required qualifications based on an advertisement.
However, due to a Supreme Court judgment to establish uniform criteria for admission in medical colleges throughout India, the course duration was extended to three years and some qualifications were modified.
A new advertisement was issued, and the petitioner was excluded from the entrance test for the three-year MDS course.
The main issue was whether the petitioner, who was previously eligible, could be barred from admission due to the new advertisement.
The Supreme Court noted that its directions were meant to ensure uniformity in medical faculties nationally and not to jeopardize existing candidates' interests.
Individuals who were eligible under the existing rules until 1993 should be considered for admission.
The respondents were ordered to allocate the petitioner to any vacant seat of his choice or to establish a position for him.
