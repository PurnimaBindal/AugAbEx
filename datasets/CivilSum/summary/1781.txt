The Medical Council Act, 1933, and the Constitution of India, Article 14 state that the probation period can be prescribed and extended at the discretion of the competent authority, unless there are specific Rules prohibiting it.
If there are Rules prohibiting it, the authority can still prescribe a probation period longer than what's provided in the Rules.
Additionally, there is deemed confirmation, and the probationer is deemed to be continuing on probation unless the competent authority performs a specific act in the manner prescribed in the Rules.
Moreover, there is no bar on extending the probation period beyond a specific time.
[Paras 24 to 27 and 31]

The Medical Council Act, 1993, and the Constitution of India, Article 14 state that recommendations made by an appraising officer in the notes file for reporting and grading in the ACRs are not binding unless specifically approved by the approving competent authority.
An employee cannot claim any benefits of such notes.
[Paras 21 to 23, 28]

The Medical Council Act, 1933, and the Constitution of India, Article 14 state that a certificate of successful completion of probation issued by a subordinate to the official concerned will not bind the competent authority if it's not issued by the competent authority.
[Paras 4 and 28]

The Constitution of India, Articles 226 and 14, and The Medical Council Act, 1933, state that vague allegations of mala fide cannot be accepted, especially when the person alleged to have acted mala fide has not been impleaded as a party.
Allegations of malice in law and mala fide must show improper use or abuse of power with the motive or use of discretion for purposes not authorized by the law.
