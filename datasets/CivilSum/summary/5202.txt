Section 5(2)(c) of the Sick Textile Undertakings (Nationalisation) Act, 1974 and Section 33-C(2) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 provide for the reinstatement of employees with full back wages after the Central Government takes over an establishment, with all liability for wages, salary, and other dues being transferred to the Central Government from the date of takeover.
Section 5(3)(b) allows such awards to be enforced against a successor company, provided they were passed before the appointed day.
[Para 8]
According to Section 5(2)(c) of the Sick Textile Undertakings (Nationalisation) Act, 1974 and Section 33-C(2) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, successor employers are not required to be heard because they simply step into the shoes of their predecessor-in-interest, who has already been heard by the Labour Court.
[Para 9]
Leasing has no effect on the liability of the successor company for the reinstatement of employees with full back wages, and the unit continues to vest with the successor company regardless of whether it is leased out or the lease ends.
[Para 10]
Under Section 14(1) of the Sick Textile Undertakings (Nationalisation) Act, 1974, in cases where a workman's services were terminated illegally, the workman is entitled to reinstatement and continuing rights to wages from the date of termination to the date of award that cannot be impacted by the provisions of Section 5 of the 1974 Act.
