Food Corporation of India's Scheme for Compassionate Appointment, 1977 (as amended in 1996) permits conditional voluntary retirement notices on medical grounds, which are sometimes used to claim compassionate appointments for a son.
In one instance, a notice was given when the employee was 55 years and 2 months old, with a condition that he would only accept it if his son were given an appointment.
Although the notice was accepted, the compassionate appointment was denied.
Under the scheme, benefits are available only to those who seek voluntary retirement before reaching 55 years of age.
However, since the offer was conditional, the recipient had a choice of accepting or rejecting it.
Once the respondent had been retired, FCI could not refuse to comply with the condition subject to which the offer was made.
Given that the appointment was denied for around seven years, in light of the peculiar facts of the case, it was deemed inappropriate to interfere with the High Court's decision to direct the appointment of the employee's son.
