According to Articles 16 and 226 of the Constitution of India, a transfer can be considered mala fide if it is done with malicious intent.
In one case, a petitioner was transferred from Delhi to Calcutta after conducting departmental enquiries against the General Manager.
However, the court found the plea untenable as the transfer alone cannot be indicative of arbitrariness.
Therefore, there was no ground to quash the transfer order as mala fide.
[Paras 10 and 11]

Even if guidelines are violated during a transfer, it may not be sufficient grounds to consider it mala fide.
According to Articles 16 and 226 of the Constitution of India, a transfer can be considered mala fide if it is done with malicious intent.
However, simply violating guidelines does not automatically qualify for such considerations.
[Para 12]
