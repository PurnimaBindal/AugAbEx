The respondent, who was successful in the written test and interview for the post of Field Supervisor in a bank, approached the court under Article 226 of the Constitution of India.
However, the respondent failed to produce any material evidence that a vacancy existed, which was a requirement to establish his rights, as the court cannot arrive at a conclusion on factual positions based solely on submissions made during the hearing without any supporting evidence.
The High Court was erroneous in concluding that there existed vacancies based on the material produced before it.
[Para 8] B Inclusion of a candidate's name in the list of successful candidates does not guarantee an indefinite right to be appointed.
[Para 9] C The life of the panel of successful candidates, such as in the case of the respondent for the post of Field Supervisor in a bank, is usually valid for one year.
However, it's within the board's power to extend this period, under intimation to the Government in Bank Division, which it did for the respondent's panel.
Still, respondent could not be appointed, as it was not mandatory for all candidates on the panel to be appointed.
The High Court was incorrect in issuing a directive to the bank to appoint people from the expired panel.
