Rule 16.2(1) of Punjab Police Rules, 1934 states that absence from duty due to an overstay of leave may not warrant the extreme penalty of dismissal if there are good reasons for doing so.
However, in the case of a constable who remained absent from duty without informing the authorities while holding a rifle, and did not provide any explanation for eight months, dismissal is not unjustified as it amounts to the gravest of misconduct.
[Paras 8, 9, 11, 13, and 14].
The order of dismissal for a constable who served only six years and was not entitled to any pension is valid under Rule 16.2(1) of Punjab Police Rules, 1934.
It does not matter if the punishing authority did not take into account the total period of service of the petitioner.
[Paras 10 and 12].
Under Rule 16 of Punjab Police Rules, 1934, Enquiry Officers have the right to ask questions to witnesses and the delinquent to clarify facts and investigate the true circumstances.
This does not violate any principle of natural justice.
[Para 8].
In dismissing an appeal against an order of punishment of dismissal from service, the Appellate Authority was alleged to have violated the principles of natural justice for not passing a speaking order.
However, if the court finds no merit in the case after hearing the petitioner, the mere absence of a speaking order by the appellate authority does not cause prejudice and does not vitiate the action.
[Para 18].
