Army Rules, Rule 22(3) - Murder by an Army Soldier - The Convening Order of the Commanding Officer to record a summary of evidence is quasi-judicial.
The Commanding Officer must form an opinion, which the Convening Order did not provide any reasons for.
Therefore, the Convening Order has no meaning and is a violation of Rule 22.
(1989 Criminal Law Journal 1986 relied.
[Para 16])

Army Rules, Rule 22(3) - Murder by an Army Soldier - The Brigade Commander sending the Enquiry Report to the Commanding Officer for initiating disciplinary action against the accused is against Rule 22.
Army Rule 22 does not allow for a higher authority to initiate action and then pass it on to a lower authority.
This mandate goes against the Rule 22.
(Paras 12 and 19)

Army Rules, Rule 22(3) - Murder by an Army Soldier - Rule 22 compliance is mandatory.
The Commanding Officer must form an opinion on whether to dismiss the charge or proceed further.
(Para 18)

Evidence Act, Section 26 - Army Rules, Rule 22 - Murder by an Army Soldier - The confession by the accused cannot be the sole basis for conviction, as there is no independent evidence available.
(Para 13)

Army Rules, Rule 22 - Murder by an Army Soldier - During the disciplinary enquiry against the accused, prosecution counsel seeking an adjournment to brief the witnesses is against the principles of natural justice, as it amounts to witness tutoring.
(Para 10)
