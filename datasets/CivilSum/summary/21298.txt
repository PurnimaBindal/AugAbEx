The respondent was dismissed for misconduct and misappropriation after a proper domestic enquiry, which was challenged before the Appellate Authority.
The Appellate Authority set aside the dismissal and directed reinstatement with full back wages on the basis that a domestic enquiry was not held before passing the dismissal order.
The management filed an appeal, which was allowed on the grounds that the Appellate Authority had committed grave error by not disposing of the application of the appellant for additional evidence, and that the order of dismissal had been passed without a proper enquiry.
[Para 24]

It was held that where the domestic enquiry held by the employer is defective, deficient, incomplete, or not held at all, the Appellate Authority would require parties to produce evidence in order to decide whether the charges were established or not.
In cases where the principles of natural justice were violated, the Appellate Authority would have the jurisdiction to record evidence at the appellate stage to determine the guilt of the delinquent employee.
[Paras 10, 20, and 23]
