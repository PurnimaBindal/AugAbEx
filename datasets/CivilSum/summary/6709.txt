The Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985, Sections 20 & 21 states that before a person can approach the Tribunal for redressal of grievances, they must exhaust the remedies available to them under service rules.
The petitioner, who was working as a Train Superintendent on the train 'Palace on Wheels', claimed overtime allowance.
He made three successive representations but received no orders.
Later, he filed an OA in which the Tribunal ordered respondent Railways to decide on his representation and gave him the right to challenge the passed order.
The said order was not illegal and without jurisdiction, and it was not challenged in any forum and was complied with.
Thus, the second OA filed by the petitioner was not barred by limitation.
[Paras 8, 10 & 11].
As per the Railways Act, 1989, Section 132(4), the petitioner working as a Train Superintendent on the train 'Palace on Wheels' is not entitled to overtime allowance as he is a member of the supervisory staff and the Railway Board has issued a letter stating that the supervisory staff is not entitled to overtime allowance.
The petitioner cannot claim payment for the extra hours of work he puts in because he is given two compensatory rests and one weekly rest.
[Paras 15, 16 & 17].
Article 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India state that discrimination in overtime allowance can be made through valid classification based on rationally.
Train Examiners running all trains except 'Palace on Wheels' are granted OT allowance, but no Train Supervisor employed in the Railway and discharging duties on 'Palace on Wheels' is given any OT allowance.
Moreover, the petitioner, who was working as Train Superintendent on 'Palace on Wheels,' had availed compensatory rests for the extra hours of work he put in, and therefore he cannot claim OT allowance.
[Para 18].
