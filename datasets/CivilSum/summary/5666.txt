Limitation Act, 1963, Section 113 - The statute of limitation for filing suit after an accident is three years from the date of it occurring; however, the right to sue accrues when damages ensue or become evident.
This means that a person cannot be expected to file a suit against a tort feasor without knowing the nature and extent of damages.
Article 113 of the Act should be construed liberally to ensure that the person who suffered the injury is not deprived of their legitimate damages.
A strict interpretation of the article would not be in keeping with the principles of equity and fair play, which could potentially defeat the ends of justice.
The starting point for computing the limitation should be the date on which the extent of actual damages resulting from the accident became evident.

Fatal Accident Act, 1855 - Torts - Damages - The owner of a building is obliged to keep dangerous openings adequately closed.
The plaintiff fell into the basement through an opening and suffered severe injuries that left them bedridden as a result of the owner's lack of care.
This lack of care on behalf of the owner creates danger on their property, which they are then liable to pay damages for if an injury occurs.
The plaintiff incurred medical expenses for their treatment of the injuries sustained from the fall.
Therefore, the court decreed an award of Rs. 12 lacs as damages in favor of the plaintiff.
