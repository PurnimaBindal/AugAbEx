The Maharashtra Civil Services (Pension) Rules, 1982, Rules 10(4) and 65(1)(b) along with Articles 311 and 310 of the Constitution of India state that compulsory retirement can be ordered based on public interest.
If an order of compulsory retirement is passed on the basis of unfounded and contradictory remarks despite good efficiency and performance of the petitioner as reflected in the ACRs, such contradictory remarks are not considered bona fide and the order of compulsory retirement is deemed illegal.
[Paras 4 to 6]
As per Articles 311 and 310 of the Constitution of India, a close scrutiny of the entire record of service, particularly the record of the last period, is necessary for passing an order of compulsory retirement of a government servant.
If adverse remarks are found to be unfounded, proper attention must be given to collect all correct and truthful information.
The employee must also be notified of his deficiency and given sufficient opportunity to improve.
However, if even after such opportunities, the employee does not improve, the adverse remarks would be considered well founded and would form the material basis for the order of compulsory retirement.
[Para 6]
