The termination of the petitioner's services by the respondent and whether it was justifiable or not falls under the purview of the Industrial Tribunal, which is responsible for determining the merits of the dispute.
[Para 7]

The power to determine whether a dispute exists lies with the appropriate government under Sections 10 and 12(5) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.
However, the government cannot encroach upon the adjudicatory power of the Industrial Tribunal by examining the merits of the case.
[Para 8]

Although the power to make or refuse reference under Sections 10 and 12(5) of the Act does not confer the power to adjudicate, in exceptional cases, the government may refuse to make a reference if the demand is patently perverse or frivolous.
[Para 8]

When the demand for reference of the dispute is not frivolous or perverse, the appropriate government cannot consider the merits of the dispute while deciding whether or not to refer it to the Industrial Tribunal.
[Para 11]

If the appropriate government rejects a request for reference of the dispute to the Industrial Tribunal, it must provide reasons.
Merely stating that the termination of the workman during the probationary period was justified is not sufficient and falls short of the requirements of Sections 10 and 12(5) of the Act.
[Para 12]
