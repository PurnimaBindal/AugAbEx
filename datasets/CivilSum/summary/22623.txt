The Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 concerns the award of reinstatement, and in cases involving back wages, the grant of such wages is not automatic.
A driver who was responsible for a fatal accident was acquitted in a criminal case but dismissed after departmental proceedings.
The labour court ordered reinstatement without backwages, while a single judge ordered payment of full backwages with interest.
This was later reduced to 75% by the Division Bench, and the corporation paid Rs. 45000 as compensation to the claimants.
It was held that the acquittal in the criminal case does not nullify the decision taken in the departmental proceedings, and fixing backwages depends on several factors with no automatic entitlement.
The labour court denied backwages and took note of previous acts of misconduct, which the High Court lost sight of, resulting in the direction to pay backwages being set aside.
