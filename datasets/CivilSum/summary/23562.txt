State Bank of Patiala (Officers) Service Regulations, 1979, Regulations 50(4) and 68(iii) - Misappropriation - Contravention of Regulation 50(4) - Disciplinary proceedings - Natural justice - The rule of audi alteram partem states that a violation of a procedural provision should be examined to determine whether it has prejudiced the delinquent officer/employee in a fair hearing and effective defense - The test for prejudice is whether or not the delinquent officer/employee was able to defend himself properly and effectively - This principle is derived from the case law discussed in the text.
[Paras 32 and 36] B State Bank of Patiala (Officers) Service Regulations, 1979, Regulations 50(4) and 68(iii) - Misappropriation - Contravention of Regulation 50(4) - Disciplinary enquiry - Natural justice - Rule of audi alteram partem - Although the copies of statements of two witnesses were not furnished, the respondent was allowed to peruse them and take notes more than three days prior to their examination - One witness was examined - The respondent did not raise any objection during the inquiry - The trial court did not find that any prejudice resulted from said violation - The appellate court did not specify in what manner and in what sense was the respondent prejudiced in his defense - The high court did not refer to the aspect of prejudice at all - The appeal was allowed and the judgment of the high court affirming the judgments of the trial court and appellate court was set aside - No prejudice resulted to the respondent on account of not furnishing him the copies of the statements of witnesses - On account of said violation, it could not be said that respondent did not have a fair hearing or that the disciplinary inquiry against him was not a fair inquiry.
