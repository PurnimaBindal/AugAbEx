Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 10(1) - Reference - Delay - Limitation for making reference - Appropriate Govt. must receive demand for reference of an industrial dispute within a "reasonable time" - Reasonable time is dependent on the individual facts and circumstances of each case - No limitation has been provided for, nor are there guidelines to determine an appropriate time limit - Slight explanations may be sufficient for the Govt. to refer the matter, and the question of delay and laches would be determined by the Tribunal on the evidence provided by both parties - Reasonable time for claims of labourers to Labor Tribunals may be considered up to five years - After this period, the Govt. is within its rights to refuse the reference.

Industrial Disputes Act, Section 10(1) - Limitation - Delay - "Clearly Belated" - An action beyond a prescribed limitation period is termed "clearly belated."
[Para 27] C. Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 33-C(2) - Limitation - All applications under Section 33-C must be made within one year of the workman's due money - The Govt. may consider applications made after the expiry of one year if the applicant has sufficient cause for the delay.
