According to Section 20 of the Minimum Wages Act, 1948, it is required to pay minimum wages to workers.
However, despite directions from the labour inspector, a workman who worked as a machineman was not paid the minimum wages of a machineman.
The management acted in a way that hindered the efforts of the claim authority to decide the claim in a reasonable time.
Additionally, the management proceeded ex parte twice, and no written statement was filed for over a year and a half.
The claim authority should not act like a regular civil court and should decide workmen's claims summarily by giving one or two opportunities.
The management filed a petition challenging the order to pay the due wages to the workman with ten times compensation instead of actually paying them, which was misconceived and dismissed with cost.
