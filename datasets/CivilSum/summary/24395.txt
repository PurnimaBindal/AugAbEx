Indian Oil Corporation Certified Standing Orders under Constitution of India, Article 311, Part XIV, Order Nos. 20(vi) and 32 - Civil Servant - Article 311's protection does not apply to 'Civil Post' employees of corporations - Indian Oil Corporation cannot be considered a Government department and its employees are not employed under the Union.
[Paras 14 and 15]

Indian Oil Corporation Certified Standing Orders under Constitution of India, Articles 12, 14, 136 and 311, Order Nos. 20(vi) and 32 - Termination of service - Standing Orders that allow termination of employment without inquiry in cases of criminal convictions, satisfy Article 14 of the Constitution - Certified Standing Orders' power is limited to the highest administrative head of the Corporation - Sufficient safeguards are provided in the Standing Orders, and appeals can be taken in Higher Courts.
[Paras 34, 35, and 41]

Constitution of India, Articles 12, 14, 16 and 311 - Misconduct - Termination of service - Termination of service of a person charged and acquitted of assault on a Doctor without inquiry is legal and lawful - A Criminal Court's verdict of acquittal does not preclude the employer from taking legal action in accordance with Rules and Regulations in force.
[Para 12, 56]

Constitution of India - Interpretation of statute - A provision that is otherwise valid, legal and intra vires cannot be declared unconstitutional or ultra vires based merely on the possibility of misuse or abuse of the power.
[Para 37]

Service Jurisprudence - Acquittal by a Criminal Court does not preclude an employer from exercising power in accordance with Rules and Regulations in force.
[Para 12]

Departmental Proceedings and Criminal Proceedings - Both are entirely different and operate in different fields and have different objectives.
