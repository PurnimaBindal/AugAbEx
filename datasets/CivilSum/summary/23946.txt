The Text discusses several aspects of the Indian Constitution pertaining to the appointment and recruitment of judicial officers.
The Constitution empowers State legislatures to make laws for regulating the conditions of services of officers belonging to the judicial wing but not regarding the recruitment to the judicial services.
Articles 233 and 234 represent a complete scheme regulating the appointments of District and Subordinate Judges, and the power of State is subject to the other provisions under which the Parliament may make laws for the whole or any part of the territory of India.
The Bihar Reservation of Vacancies in Posts and Services Act, 1991, Section 4, providing reservation in judicial posts cannot operate to that extent and is ultra vires the Constitutional mandate, as per the majority view.
Section 4 of the Bihar Act 1991, providing reservation in appointments to judicial posts is not at a stage anterior to stage of recruitment and appointment.
The consultation with the High Court for appointments to judicial posts cannot be equated with that of consultation with the Service Commission.
The process of creation of cadre and posts comes to an end once the cadres and the posts are constituted and approved along with budgetary provisions made for the same.
Expressions 'appointment' and "other conditions of service" have different connotations in service jurisprudence, and the framers of the Constitution were mindful of the distinction while engrafting Articles 234 and 309.
