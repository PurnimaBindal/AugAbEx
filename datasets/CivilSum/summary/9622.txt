Under a scheme sponsored by the Central Government, the State of Haryana employed people for about four months each year as Health Assistant (Swasthaya Sahayak) Sanitary Supervisors.
However, some of the employees argued that they should be employed throughout the year on a regular basis under the Constitution of India's Articles 14, 16 and 226/227.
Two vacancies for Field Workers (Class III) under the Urban Malaria Scheme went unfilled due to a general cut imposed by the State Government, and the petitioners and similarly situated persons would be considered for absorption when and if the cut was lifted or any additional openings became available.
The petition was dismissed, and a scheme introduced by the State Government was upheld by the High Court.
