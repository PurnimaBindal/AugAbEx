Contempt of Courts Act, 1971, Section 16 - Contempt by judge or presiding officers of Tribunals - The Additional Distt.
Judge granted a stay in appeal despite a transfer application of the appeal pending before Distt.
Judge and stay of proceedings.
There is no substantial evidence on record to prove that the judge had definite knowledge of the stay in the transfer application or any allegation that he was influenced by any extraneous consideration or dishonest motive in making such orders.
No wilful culpability can be inferred, and it is for the person who asserts that a person had knowledge of the order to prove this fact beyond doubt.
Even if it is doubtful that the person had knowledge of the order, he cannot be convicted of committing contempt of court.
Mere presumption is not sufficient because these presumptions and assumptions can be wrong and contrary.

[Para 16] C Contempt of Courts Act, 1971, Sections 10, 11, 16 - Contempt - A fact constituting the core of the charge of contempt of court has to be proved by evidence and not on assumptions and presumptions.
Assumptions or presumptions are deductions of facts and a flight of the imagination of a human mind, and it may be faulty or luring or pervasively away from reality and can be contrary as well.
