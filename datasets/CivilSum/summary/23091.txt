According to Section 2(n) of the Workman Compensation Act, 1923, a casual employee appointed for a specific time to carry out repair work in a building is not considered a 'workman' under the Act.
This is based on the fact that the workman in the present case was employed for a limited time for repairing work in a residential house, which does not fit the definition of a workman under the Act.
[Para 2 and 23]

The Workman Compensation Act, 1923, outlines the requirements for an employee to be considered a 'workman' under the Act.
The employee must not be casually employed, and their work must be related to the employer's trade and business.
