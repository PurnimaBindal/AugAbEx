Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 10 - Reference - Permission by management to present evidence - The labor court is responsible for conducting proceedings as a court of first adjudication, and is supposed to provide both sides with the opportunity to substantiate their case.
As a result, it is the natural right of the management to present evidence in such cases, and it is not necessary for them to seek permission to do so.
The labor court's observation that no evidence could have been allowed because the management did not seek permission to present it is contrary to law.
[Para 8]  B Road Transport Corporation Act, 1950, Section 45 - Misconduct - Proof of - A bus conductor was charged with failing to issue tickets to passengers, not recording tickets in the way voucher, and not stopping the bus for inspection by the flying squad.
Quasi judicial authority and Tribunals are not bound by sophisticated rules of evidence, and are expected to consider any material that is logical, probative for a prudent mind.
The Tribunal did not examine the past record which indicated that the conductor had been punished for the same offense four times.
The award was invalidated for being perverse, lacking evidence or inconsistent with evidence in the record, passed in ignorance of the law.
[Paras 11, 12, and 14]  C. Road Transport Corporation Act, 1950, Section 45 - Misconduct - A charge was brought against a bus conductor for failing to stop the bus for inspection by the flying squad, and the plea was that the driver is responsible for stopping the bus and not the conductor.
In public transport buses, drivers stop and start the bus at the conductor's signal by blowing a whistle, but this cooperation cannot be used for illicit purposes.
It was incumbent on the conductor to have the bus checked when signalled, failing to stop is a grave misconduct.
