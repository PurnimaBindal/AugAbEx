Article 311(2)(b) of the Constitution of India requires a regular departmental inquiry before imposing the penalty of dismissal on a public servant, however, the competent authority disregarded the procedure established by law, and resorted to the shortcut method of dispensing with the inquiry using Article 311(2)(b), without any evidence that the delinquent had been previously indulging in corrupt activities.
The view taken by DGP was reasonably impracticable, erroneous, and unjustified, making the dismissal order incorrect.
It should be set aside.
[Para 8]

Article 311(2)(b) of the Constitution of India mandates a regular inquiry is essential before imposing a penalty of dismissal, removal, or reduction in rank of a public servant, which can be dispensed with under certain eventualities.
[Para 8]

The violation of the principles of natural justice in the case of an ex-head constable is evident, where the dismissal order shows the Appellate Authority and Revisional Authority were of the opinion that the SSP had taken appropriate action.
However, the base of information on which the SSP had concluded that it was impracticable to hold an inquiry is unclear, making the orders illegal, and liable to be set aside.
[Para 15 and 16]

The widow or legal representative of a dismissed ex-head constable who was acquitted in criminal cases is entitled to the consequential monetary benefits as per relevant rules, after the dismissal order is set aside since it was passed without holding a departmental inquiry.
