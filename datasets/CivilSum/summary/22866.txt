The Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16 and 309 - Regularisation Policy - The State cannot make rules or issue any executive instructions to regularize service, as it would violate the rules under Article 309 of the Constitution and the equality clauses under Articles 14 and 16.
[Para 7] B Uttar Pradesh Labour Department (Factories and Boilers Division) Officers Service (Second Amendment) Rules, 1992, Rule 5(iii) - Promotion to the post of Deputy Director - Only substantive appointment as Assistant Director of Factories is required, not just experience, for eligibility for promotion - An ad hoc employee appointed illegally does not have any post, rendering their experience irrelevant - Only experience in a substantive capacity is counted.
[Paras 8 and 9] C. Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16 and 226 - Regularisation - Seniority - Ad hoc services are only taken into account for seniority if the appointment was made according to the prescribed rules - Appointment is void ab initio if prior approval of the Public Service Commission was required and not taken - Regularisation does not apply in these cases.
[Para 8] D. Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16 and 226 - Regularisation - Eligibility - An appointment of an ineligible person to a post requiring eligibility is an illegality and violates Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution, making it void.
[Paras 9 to 12] E. Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16 and 226 - Malice - Malice refers to wrongful acts done intentionally but without just cause or excuse or for a reason that is reasonable or probable - Even without malicious intentions, the principle of malice can be invoked in public services appointments made in deviation from prescribed conditions without sufficient reasons or public interest grounds, as it can be presumed to be vitiated and liable to be quashed.
