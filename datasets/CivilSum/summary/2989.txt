Education - Examination - Question papers - Confusion caused by mistakes in question papers - The combined paper of Physics and Chemistry was not segregated into different sections, creating confusion - Indications to tick marked on right or wrong answers were missing or not found accordingly - Authorities themselves found the question paper confusing and issued clarification, which added to the confusion instead of removing it - The students were certainly prejudiced and suffered - These actions offend the principles of fairness in holding examinations and caused serious prejudice to the interests of the candidates - Students take exams seriously and should not be put under any confusion or stress - Re-examination will not prejudice any candidate, as a person of merit will always maintain academic superiority over others.
[Paras 8, 9, 11, 13, 14, 16, 18, 19 and 25]
Education - Examination - Prospectus - The terms and conditions declared in the brochure by the competent authority are binding and enforceable in law - Such conditions can be waived only by the concerned authority at an appropriate time and with due notice - Merit is intended to pick the best and should not be undermined by the errors of the authorities themselves.
[Para 21]
Constitution of India, Article 226 - Education - Examination - Estoppel - Candidates cannot be prevented from challenging the validity of the examination due to erroneous question papers that created confusion and caused prejudice to their interests, even if they appeared in the examination - Principles of estoppel by acquiescence cannot be pleaded by a person who created a situation inviting the interference of the court.
[Paras 23 and 23]
