The provisions of the Unfair Labour Practices Act can only be invoked if the workman provides specific pleadings to prove unfair labour practices, and the Labour Court's jurisdiction is limited to determining whether the employer has committed such an act.
[Paras 7, 8, 15, 16, and 18]

Dismissal from work due to repeated serious misconduct, such as sleeping during duty hours, is not a minor or technical matter, and cannot be considered disproportionate punishment.
The employer is justified in firing the worker after observing the principles of natural justice, and payment of Rs. 2,50,000 as compensation is not allowed.
[Paras 9, 10, 18, and 22]

Past record of service is relevant to consider whether the punishment is shockingly disproportionate in dismissing an employee due to their misconduct.
[Paras 18 and 22]

A decision is only an authority of what it decides, not anything that can logically be inferred.
[Para 19]

The jurisdiction of the Labour Court is limited to determining if an employer has committed unfair labour practices, and cannot issue an irrational, extraneous, or compassionate order.
[Paras 20 and 21]
