The Court, while exercising its jurisdiction under Article 226 of the Constitution, cannot re-evaluate the evidence like a Court of appeal during a departmental inquiry.
It is not for the Court to assess whether the material relied upon by the Enquiry Officer was sufficient or not, as long as the findings are based on some legal evidence.
The Court can only interfere if the findings are based on no evidence or ground to be perverse on the face of it.
[Para 8] B.
The punishment should not be vindictive or unduly harsh and must be proportionate to the gravity of the misconduct proved.
If the punishment is inadequately disproportionate, the Court has the jurisdiction to correct it.
In this case, the petitioner was charged with absence from duty for a few days, which he explained to be justified on account of his sudden illness.
He had 25 years of meritorious service before the charge-sheet was issued, which the punishing authority ignored.
The order of removal from service at this stage and depriving the petitioner of all his retiral benefits was held to be grossly harsh and uncalled for.
The punishment order was quashed, and the punishing authority was directed to reconsider the punishment so as not to deprive the petitioner of his retiral benefits.
