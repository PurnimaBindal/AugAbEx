Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923, Section 30 - The following tests must be satisfied to determine if a substantial question of law is involved: whether it affects the substantial rights of parties, if it is a question of general public importance, if the issue is not settled by the Supreme Court or other Courts, if the issue is not free from difficulty, or if it requires a discussion of alternative views.
[Para 8]
Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923, Section 30 - There are two situations where interference with findings of fact is permissible; when relevant evidence is not considered, or when a finding is based on inadmissible evidence.
In either situation, a substantial question of law can arise.
[Para 8]
Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923, Section 30 - Appeal - In cases where material evidence is ignored and there is a non-application of mind by the Commissioner, a substantial question of law can be raised and addressed on appeal under Section 30.
[Para 8]
Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923, Section 4 - Compensation - The computation formula to be used when the monthly wages of the deceased worker and age are known was clarified.
An appellant is entitled to a minimum compensation of Rs. 50,000 under Section 4(1)(a).
Interest at a rate of 9% pa and a penalty of Rs. 5,000 were also awarded for delay in payment of compensation.
