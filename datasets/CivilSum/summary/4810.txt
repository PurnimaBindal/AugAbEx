The provisions of the amended Section 23(2) of the Pre-conception and Pre-natal Diagnostic Techniques Act are not retrospective, and offenses committed prior to the amendment must be dealt with according to the pre-amendment provisions.
Moreover, the enhanced penalty of five years prescribed by the amendment cannot be imposed for such offenses because doing so would violate Article 20(1) of the Constitution.
[Paras 9, 13 to 17]

Section 23 of the Pre-conception and Pre-natal Diagnostic Techniques Act is entirely a penal provision, including the removal of a medical practitioner's name under Section 23(2) and rules of the medical register of Medical Practitioners, both of which constitute punishment.
This means that Article 20 of the Constitution applies to this section.
[Para 12]

Retrospective effect is prohibited under the Pre-conception and Pre-natal Diagnostic Techniques Act.
According to customary international law, a citizen who has committed an offense cannot be punished more than what was prescribed on the date of commission of the offense under a statute.
Additionally, as a signatory to the Convention for Protection of Human Rights and Fundamental Rights, India is subject to the rules laid out in that convention.
Finally, Article 20 of the Constitution is one of the most precious fundamental rights, and imposes restrictions on how punishments can be applied.
[Paras 13 and 14]

Section 23(2) of the Pre-conception and Pre-natal Diagnostic Techniques Act requires mandatory removal of the name of a medical practitioner from the register of the Medical Council for five years once the fact of a conviction is reported to the council.
Furthermore, authorities have no discretion when it comes to imposing penalties.
[Para ]

The word "penalty" used in Article 20(1) does not mean that such protections do not apply simply because Section 23(2) of the Pre-conception and Pre-natal Diagnostic Techniques Act uses the terms "sentence" and "imprisonment."
This distinction is irrelevant in determining whether an offense falls under Article 20(1).
