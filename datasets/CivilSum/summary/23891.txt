According to Bombay Industrial Relations Act, 1946, Sections 3(25) and 15, the membership of a union can be cancelled for defaulters.
Such cancellation is deemed to occur under proviso to Section 3(25) of the Act due to arrears of subscription for a period of more than three calendar months.
This phrase implies that a defaulting member's behavior during at least four months within the six-month block preceding the month in question is taken into account.
Additionally, if a member pays subscription far exceeding one month but fails to make up the deficiency of one month during subsequent months, they will not be considered defaulters for more than three months if they're not in arrears of more than three months.

Under Bombay Industrial Relations Act, 1946, Section 3(25), the interpretation of the deeming fiction contained in the proviso for dismembering a person must be strictly constructed.
Any provision catering to the needs of illiterate or disabled workmen masses of workmen must be translated to subserve the goal of industrial democracy and collective bargaining guaranteed by the Act.
An interpretation that promotes such a benevolent scheme and guarantees the continuance of membership of such illiterate masses of workmen must be preferred over one that frustrates the enactment's benevolent scheme.
