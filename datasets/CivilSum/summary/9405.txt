Dismissal from service - Departmental Enquiry - A bank clerk accused of falsifying accounts and withdrawing funds was provided with ample opportunity to participate in the enquiry but informed the Inquiry Officer that he would not participate.
The Inquiry Officer was completely justified in proceeding ex parte against the accused as there was no denial about the withdrawal of funds.
The Tribunal cannot set aside the inquiry report on the basis that a handwriting expert was not examined.
The Tribunal's order directing reinstatement with 50% backwages, without considering the evidence on record, is against the law and principles of natural justice.
The termination of the accused was justified.
[Paras 7 and 9] B. Disciplinary Proceedings - There is a fundamental difference between the approach and objectives in criminal proceedings and disciplinary proceedings.
The standard of proof, rules for holding an enquiry and trial are conceptually different.
In a disciplinary enquiry, technical rules of evidence do not apply.
[Para 7] C. Disciplinary Proceedings - Principles of Natural Justice - The law requires a fair opportunity to be given to the delinquent.
It does not compel forcing the delinquent to appear before the enquiry officer against his will.
