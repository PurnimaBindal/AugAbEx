The Constitution of India, Article 226, outlines the powers of the government in reference to industrial disputes.
The government must evaluate each dispute individually and can refuse to refer a case to the Labour Court if it deems the complaint frivolous, belated or based on extraneous considerations.
However, while examining a case, the appropriate government cannot adjudicate on the merits of the controversy.
[Paragraph 5] B.
In a case where a reference to the Labour Court was declined on the basis of proven serious charges of misbehavior, State Government has in fact adjudicated upon the dispute, instead of deciding about the existence of industrial disputes.
The Labour Court is tasked with determining if there was a fair and proper enquiry, and if the dismissal was justified.
The petition in this matter was allowed, and the matter was sent back to the Government for reconsideration.
