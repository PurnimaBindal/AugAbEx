Sections 14(1)(a) and 34 of the Specific Relief Act deal with the enforcement of a contract of service and challenge to termination of service contract.
A suit claiming that termination is invalid and praying for a declaration that the plaintiff continues in service isn't considered a suit for enforcement of contract of service.
The maintainability of a suit is determined by the ostensible pleadings and stated reliefs claimed in the plaint, and the Specific Relief Act is not exhaustive.
A suit not maintainable under Section 14 of the Act does not persist with the disability of non-admission to civil courts outside the bounds of Chapter 11 of the Act.
Section 34 is sufficient to allow civil courts to admit suits filed for a variety of declarations.
However, the plaintiff in this case was issued an appointment letter as "Probationary Inspector (Grade-I)" and was neither confirmed nor had his probation extended.
Therefore, he cannot be granted a declaration that he continues to be in the service of the Corporation.
