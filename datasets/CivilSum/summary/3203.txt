Constitution of India, Article 226 - The "equal pay for equal work" principle applies to the Petitioners, who work in a private aided recognised school.
They perform the same duties and have the same credentials as their peers in other private aided recognised institutions in Chandigarh.
The pay disparity between these schools is deemed discriminatory.
The Respondents must pay the Petitioners the same salary as their peers in privately managed Govt. aided schools in Chandigarh.
The expenses will be borne by the Chandigarh Administration and the private school management in a 95:5 ratio.

Constitution of India, Article 226 - Hostile discrimination occurs when one receives benefits that others do not.
Grant-in-aid is intended to assist schools that face financial difficulties.
Schools that cannot afford increasing tuition should not be allowed to hike their fees excessively, especially since they are aided recognised schools.

The denial of grant-in-aid cannot impede their rights, and they should be remunerated justly.
The ultimate beneficiary is the teacher, and their rights cannot be ignored even if the school management decides not to file a writ petition.
