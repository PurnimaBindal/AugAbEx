Denial of promotion to an employee on account of penalties imposed in the past is not a further penalty but a necessary consequence of conduct of employee, according to Constitution of India, Article 16.
[Para 29] B.
The rule of "no work, no pay" in Fundamental Rules, Rule 17(1) does not apply to cases where employee is willing to work but is kept away by authorities for no fault of his.
[Para 25] C Sealed cover procedure for promotion, as explained in Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 16, is to be resorted when charge-sheet is issued to an employee and not when preliminary enquiry is pending.
[Paras 8 and 16] D. Promotion cannot be withheld simply because preliminary enquiry against the employee was pending, according to Constitution of India, Articles 16.
Promotion can be withheld when Disciplinary proceedings had been initiated after issuing charge memo or charge-sheet.
Disciplinary proceedings commence when charge memo was issued to an employee.
In case of criminal proceedings, when charge-sheet was issued.
[Paras 16 and 17] E. According to Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 16, if an employee who had been withheld promotion on account of Disciplinary proceedings is completely exonerated of the charge, they must be given salary of higher post and other benefits from the date from which they would have normally been promoted but for Disciplinary/Criminal proceedings.
However, this rule is not absolute - in case the employee was acquitted giving benefit of doubt or on technical grounds or if they delayed the proceedings, the Authority will decide what benefits will be granted.
