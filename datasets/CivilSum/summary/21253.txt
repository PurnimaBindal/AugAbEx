In order to be promoted to a higher cadre, merit-cum-suitability with due regard to seniority or merit and ability has been the settled criteria applied by the government, in the absence of any rule prescribing such criteria.
However, seniority should not have a role to play when meritorious and suitable candidates are selected for the higher post.
Instead, seniority would have its proper place only when merit and ability are approximately equal.
[Paras 6 and 8]

When determining merit and preparing a gradation of officers on the basis of their confidential reports, separate reasons do not need to be recorded.
Considering confidential reports themselves would suffice as a reason, and no further opportunity needs to be provided.
[Paras 9 and 11]

The principle of audi alterim partem is a fundamental concept of natural justice, meaning no one should be condemned without being heard.
In the field of administrative action, this principle has been applied to provide fairness and justice to the affected person, but this doctrine is not a cure to all the ills in the process.
Its application depends on the factual matrix to improve administrative efficiency and expediency and to provide justice.
