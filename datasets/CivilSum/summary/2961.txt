The provision of interview marks in the prospectus for admissions based on written test and interview cannot be challenged by candidates who have unsuccessfully taken the chance; this falls under the Constitution of India's Articles 14 and 226 regarding selection and estoppel.

It cannot be considered excessive that 20% of the marks for admissions is allotted for the interview, as specified in the prospectus and selection criteria.
[Para 12] C. No specific guidelines are necessary to state subject-wise marks in the allocation of interview marks for selection of potentially suitable candidates for the teaching profession; guidelines, though not legally binding, demonstrate what the expert body desires for suitably qualified candidates.
[Para 13] D Wide disparity of marks on written tests and interviews cannot be attributed to the malafide conduct of selection committee members - the totality of the impression created by a candidate on the interviewing body may give a more accurate picture of the candidate's personality; courts cannot sit in judgment of the methods of marking employed by interviewing bodies.
[Paras 47 and 48] E. Although they have no legal force, guidelines for selection provide insight into what the expert body yearns for in appropriate candidates.
