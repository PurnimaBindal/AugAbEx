The writ petition sought to declare Model Standing Order Nos. 25(1)(a) and (b) and 26 as ultra vires Section 2(a) of the Industrial Employment (Standing Order) Act, 1946 along with Item No. 9 of the Schedule.
The court held that the Model Standing Orders fall within the competence of the appropriate Government to frame, define the misconduct, and provide punishments accordingly.
Section 15(2)(b) of the Act and Item No. 9 of the Schedule empower the Government to set MSO for the purpose of the Act in dealing with different types of misconduct.
Thus, Model Standing Order Nos. 25(1)(a)(b) and 26 are applicable as per Section 2(a) of the Industrial Employment (Standing Order) Act, 1946.
[Paras 4 and 5]

The writ petition sought to declare Model Standing Order Nos. 25(1)(a) (b) and (c) and 26 as ultra vires Article 14 of the Constitution of India.
The court held that under SMO 26, a workman can be warned or censured for misconduct, and full observance of the rules of natural justice would be pre-requisite in case of fine or dismissal.
MSO provides for the observance of rules of natural justice, which are not in a straitjacket and need to be molded to the needs of each situation.
Thus, Model Standing Order Nos. 25(1)(a) (b) and (c) and 26 do not infringe Article 14 of the Constitution.
[Para 6]

The writ petition prayed to quash various orders of punishment, but the individuals who received the punishment did not join the petitioner as co-petitioner.
The court dismissed the petition, and no relief can be granted to individuals who are not parties to the writ petition.
