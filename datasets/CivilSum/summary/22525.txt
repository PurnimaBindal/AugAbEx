The government has the right to modify its existing policy of medical reimbursement and frame new policies even if they are not in line with the Articles 21 and 47 of the Constitution of India.
It is the right of the state to change policies as per changing circumstances, keeping the financial aspect as a relevant consideration.
[Paras 18, 20, 29 & 30] B.
While fundamental rights are important, no right can be absolute in a Welfare State.
In such a state, every individual right has to give way to the right of the public at large, with every fundamental right being subject to permissible reasonable restrictions.
[Para 29] C. The State Government has the right to frame a medical policy that gives the employees the choice to receive treatment from any private hospital of their choice at the rates fixed by the Director, Health, and Family Planning.
The employees’ full treatment expenses incurred at private hospitals cannot be reimbursed under this address, but the policy is reasonable and not against the Articles 21 and 47 of the Constitution of India.
[Paras 20, 21, 25 & 29] D. Respondent undergoing heart surgery at Escorts hospital after the Policy decision of the State Government is entitled to medical reimbursement at the fixed rates as per AIIMS, but not at the full expenditure incurred at Escorts.
[Para 31] E. Emergency medical treatment is allowed as an exception to the policy.
Respondent, who suffered a heart attack and received treatment at Escorts due to a strike in AIIMS, is allowed the medical reimbursement at the rates applicable in Escorts.
