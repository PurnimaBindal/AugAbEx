According to Article 16 of the Indian Constitution, the court cannot demand the regularisation of a petitioner's service without a proper scheme or rules as well as available posts - the petitioner's appointments are neither referring to available posts nor ad hoc appointments outside the rules - therefore, there is no question of regularisation.
[Para 7] B Based on Articles 14, 16, 38, 39, 41, and 43 of the Indian Constitution, the service conditions of daily-rated employees must adhere to constitutional provisions and should be modulated by appropriate rules with specific reference to the "social justice" doctrine.
[Para 10]
The writ petitions are disposed of in terms of the following directions: (i) The State Government should prescribe the service conditions of daily-rated employees in its departments by making appropriate rules, which provide for the employment of a given percentage of Class III and Class IV posts from among daily-rated employees; (ii) The rules should be consistent with the laws of reservation and should provide for the mode and conditions of daily-wage appointments in Government departments; (iii) The daily-rated employees' wages must not fall below the minimum scale of pay for the post and should be paid dearness allowances in addition to prescribed wages; and (iv) Until new rules are formulated, any vacancies for Class 3 and Class 4 posts under the State shall remain unoccupied.
