The NCTE Act, 1993, Section 14 enables the State Government and NCTE to exercise jurisdiction over establishing recognised educational institutions.
The State Government can assess and enquire about the need for such institutions, but this does not interfere with the authority of the NCTE.
The NCTE is solely responsible for assessing the financial and infrastructure resources of applicants for granting recognition, as per Section 14 of the Act.
[Para 24] B.
According to Article 226 of the Constitution of India and Section 14 of the NCTE Act, 1993, matters such as the deposit of a mandatory amount by an educational institution and any subsequent delay fall under the purely administrative/procedural aspects of granting a NOC.
The State Government has the discretion to accept late deposits if they are satisfied with the substantial compliance of the applicant.
In such cases, the grant of NOC cannot be interfered with after considering the recommendations of the State Level Selection Committee.
[Paras 15 and 24] C. A petitioner seeking writ jurisdiction of the Court must disclose all materially relevant facts.
Concealment of any material fact disentitles them from being heard on the merits of the case, and the Court will refuse to adjudicate the matter.
