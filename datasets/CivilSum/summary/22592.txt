Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 2(k), 2-A and 10 - Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946 - Standing Orders framed under 1946 Act - Termination of service - A daily basis fitter filed a civil suit against termination of service after prayer to refer the dispute to Industrial Tribunal/Labour Court was refused by the appropriate Government - It was held that a civil suit questioning termination of service and seeking ancillary relief not maintainable - The only remedy open to the aggrieved employee was to approach a forum created under Industrial Disputes Act.
[Paras 4 and 6]  B Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 2(k), 2-A and 10 - Termination of service - A daily basis fitter filed an industrial dispute questioning the termination of service, but it was rejected by the appropriate Government - It was suggested that, when an application for reference under the provisions of Industrial Disputes Act has been rejected by the appropriate authority, the aggrieved party should pursue the same by way of a writ petition and try to get the dispute referred under the Industrial Disputes Act.
