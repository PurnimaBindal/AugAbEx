According to Section 33-C(2) of the Industrial Disputes Act, workman's increments can be withheld and subsistence allowance can be granted in disciplinary proceedings.
Additionally, the pay scale can be revised and pay can be fixed accordingly.
Even if a charge sheet is delayed, the labour court has the jurisdiction to grant claims if the board withholds increments without a specific order or fails to fix pay in the revised scale.
In this case, the workman was suspended without a charge sheet and then reinstated without any order to withhold his salary beyond subsistence allowance.
The labour court's decision to grant all claims was fully justified, especially because the board cannot benefit from its own wrong.
It is not legal to forfeit salary beyond subsistence allowance by issuing a charge sheet as a counter-blast to a claim filed in the Labour Court, especially if the charge sheet relates to absence from duty that occurred 12 years prior.
Ultimately, the charge sheet was not issued in good faith and the Labour Court's order was correct in terms of law and jurisdiction.
