Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 226 - Dismissal from service - Natural justice - Departmental proceedings - The punishing authority must issue proper charge-sheet and give an opportunity to explain, hold proper enquiry in accordance with the procedure prescribed in the Rules associating the delinquent with access to the record to be proved against him, and afford an opportunity to explain against the enquiry report.
Charges must be proved on the basis of cogent material on record.
The Rules of natural justice vary with the varying constitution of statutory bodies and rules prescribed by the Act.
The question of whether or not any rules of natural justice had been contravened should be decided in the light of the statutory rules and provisions.
The High Court fell in an error in returning a finding that rules of natural justice or Regulations have not been followed.
[Para 17]

Constitution of India, Articles 226, 12 and 14 - Writ jurisdiction - Scope of - A writ can be issued against State authorities or co-operative societies to enforce a statutory public duty.
Even if a society could not be characterised as a `State' within the meaning of Article 12, even then a writ would lie against it.
What is material is the nature of the statutory duty placed upon it, and the court is to enforce such statutory public duty.
Persons having statutory protection have a right to invoke writ jurisdiction.
The society being established and governed by the statutory provisions of an Act of the State is amenable to writ jurisdiction.
The provisions of Art. 226 empower the High Court to issue any orders or directions to any authority or person without any difference between public functions and private functions, subject to certain self-imposed limitations like an alternative remedy.
[Paras 23 to 25]
