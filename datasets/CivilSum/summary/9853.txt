According to Article 16 of the Constitution of India, regularisation is allowed.
However, the petitioner was appointed on daily wage on muster rolls between 1980 and 1994 without issuing any official appointment order.
The petitioner made a claim for regularisation, which was subject to a government policy stating that an employee must work for 240 days with no break exceeding 30 days in a year.
The court held that since the petitioner did not meet the primary condition of working for 240 days or more in a year, he cannot take advantage of the governmental instructions.
Thus, his services cannot be regularised, and his petition was dismissed.
