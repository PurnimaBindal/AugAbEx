Under the Rajasthan Shops and Commercial Establishments Act of 1958, if a shop is attached to a sugar factory, it may be exempt from certain rules outlined in the act.
This is because the shop is seen as an extension of the factory, and employees can obtain better benefits as "factory workers."
As a result, a liquor shop attached to a sugar mill is considered part of the sugar mill and is exempt under section 3(1)(a) of the act.

When interpreting statutes, it is important to avoid interpretations that lead to "absurd consequences and incongruous results."
In cases where exemption clauses are present, they should be construed strictly and in line with the overall object and purpose of the act.
This approach helps ensure consistency with the act's intended objectives and mitigates any potential negative effects arising from the act.

In other words, the court cannot re-evaluate the facts of the case and dispute the decision made by the punishing authority.
