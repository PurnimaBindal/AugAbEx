The Constitution of India, specifically Articles 16 and 226, cover appointment and seniority regulations.
When the petitioner was recommended by the board to become a clerk in 1973, she was given a third division in her Higher Secondary Examination and was subsequently posted in a sub-office.
Other individuals recommended for the same position were adjusted in the Head Office, however the petitioner was later transferred to the Head Office at her own request.
Seniority will be determined from the date when the petitioner joined the Head Office.
[Para 5]
Constitution of India, Articles 16 and 226 cover seniority regulations, and claiming seniority can be influenced by delays.
The petitioner cannot claim seniority in the Head Office from the time she was initially recommended for the position, as she was initially posted in a sub-office.
Even with the transfer, the Engineer-in-Chief made it clear that her seniority would be determined from the date she joined the Head Office, meaning that her seniority had been properly assigned.
[Para 5]
Constitution of India, Articles 16 and 226 also cover the determination of seniority.
The respondent had already joined the Head Office earlier than the petitioner, on September 17, 1973.
The date of joining the Head Office is a significant determinant for seniority, meaning that the respondent would be placed senior to the petitioner.
