According to Constitution of India, Articles 226 and 227, and Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 33-C(2), if an official is asked to work on a higher post even in officiating capacity, they must be paid the salary of the higher post.
In this case, the management failed to show that the workman had given any consent to work on a higher post in their own pay scale.
The services of the workman were required on the higher post in the interest of the corporation.
Therefore, the corporation's decision to deny the workman the wages of the higher post was found to be arbitrary and unreasonable by the court, and the corporation was directed to make the payments to the workman.

As per Constitution of India, Articles 226 and 227, the High Court cannot act as a Court of appeal over findings of facts recorded by the Labour Court.
The High Court only exercises supervisory jurisdiction in interfering with an award that suffers from an error apparent on the face of the record.
