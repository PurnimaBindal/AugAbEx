Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 36 - Legal Practitioner - Prohibition of legal practitioners from representing parties before the Labour Tribunals - The prohibition is mandated by specific legislation and takes precedence over general law - The consent of both parties is required, even if the Tribunal grants permission - An exception exists where a lawyer representing a party is a member of the relevant trade union or traders association, in which case no consent or permission is necessary and they may represent that party.
