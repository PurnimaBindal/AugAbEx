The Constitution of India in Article 124(4) outlines that the removal of a Supreme Court judge requires not only a majority of the total membership of the House, but a majority of not less than two-thirds members of that House in support of the motion.
Members of the House are not required to vote and may abstain from voting.
[Paras 5, 6, 8 and 11]
The Oath of a member of Lok Sabha outlined in the Constitution of India's Schedule Third does not require members to vote every time they participate in proceedings.
Thus, members who abstained from voting during the removal of a Supreme Court judge did not act against their oath.
[Paras 10 and 11]
The Constitution of India's Article 226 states that before determining the legality or illegality of a matter, a fact must be established on record through authentication documents.
Press notes cannot be used as proof of fact for the issuance of a writ.
