Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 10(1) and 11-A - Misconduct - Domestic enquiry - Termination - Reference - State Govt. violated its jurisdiction when exercising its powers under Section 10(1) - The State Govt. virtually adjudicated upon the dispute by declining to make a reference and decided the merits of the case by holding that the employee's services were rightly terminated after a domestic enquiry - The provisions of Section 11-A could also apply to this case - The employee could argue before the Labour Court that the enquiry held by management was unfair and the punishment of dismissal was disproportionate to the charges, but the employee could only avail this opportunity if a reference had been made by the State Govt. - The impugned order of the State Govt. declining to make a reference was quashed.
[Para 3] B.
Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 11-A and 10 - Section 11-A of the Act is relevant - The State Govt. is obliged to refer the dispute for adjudication to an appropriate authority under Section 10(1) - The State Govt. cannot take over the functions of the adjudicating authority while exercising its powers under Section 10 of the Act.
