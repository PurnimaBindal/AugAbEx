Army Pension Regulations, Regulations 173(A) and 179 - Army Service Pension Rules, Annexure II, Para 7 - Disability pension - When a person is invalidated on medical grounds, the burden is on the Army authorities to prove that the disease suffered by him was not attributable or aggravated by Army service - The presumption is that the disease was aggravated and attributable to Army service - Diabetes can be a result of dietary conditions available to a soldier while in Army Service and is attributable to Army Service - Hypertension is a consequential disease of diabetes.

Constitution of India, Article 226 - Army Pension Regulations, Regulations 173(A) and 179 - Disability Pension - When a person is invalidated on medical ground, the onus is upon the Army authorities to prove that the disease suffered by him was not attributable or aggravated by Army service - The Medical Board expert opinion is not binding on the court - Authorities initiated pension papers of the petitioner but rejected by the Pension Authorities using its own opinion that diabetes is not attributable to service despite being approved by the Medical Board and Appellate Medical Board - The Petitioner is entitled to Disability pension.
[Para 10] C. Constitution of India, Article 226 - Army Pension Regulations, Regulations 173(A) and 179 - Disability pension - Delay and laches - When a petition is filed after 11 years of discharge on medical ground, the non-grant of pension gives a successive cause of action - The petitioner is allowed pension from the date 38 months prior to filing of the petition.
