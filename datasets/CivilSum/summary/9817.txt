Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, Sections 13(2) and 13(1)(d) - Criminal proceedings - Departmental enquiry - Bank officer facing corruption charges - Criminal trial and departmental enquiry launched against officer - Departmental enquiry can only be stalled if it adversely affects the employee's defence in the criminal trial.

Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, Sections 13(2) and 13(1)(d) - Employee charged with corruption - Criminal trial and departmental enquiry simultaneous proceedings - Supreme Court ruling - (i) Departmental enquiry can only be stayed if it causes serious prejudice to the employee in his defence in the Criminal trial.
(ii) Both proceedings can occur separately and simultaneously, as there is no legal bar against such a scenario.
(iii) In instances where the departmental proceedings and criminal trial are based on the same facts/charges, and the crime is complex with complicated questions of law and facts, it is advised to stay the departmental proceeding until the criminal trial is over.
(iv) The nature of the offence and the legal intricacies of the criminal case will determine how grave the situation is.
(v) The factors stated at (ii) and (iii) should not be treated in isolation to put a stop to department proceedings as undue delays are not acceptable.
(vi) If the criminal trial is delayed and departmental proceedings are stopped, then the latter may be resumed and concluded so that if the employee is found not guilty, their reputation may be salvaged, or if found guilty, the administration can promptly let go of them.
