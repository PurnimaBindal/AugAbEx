The Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 2(k), 10, and 12 - The Act excludes the jurisdiction of Civil Court in respect of remedies available under the Act, for which a complete procedure and machinery have been provided - A plaintiff who was appointed as a Helper/Water Boy on daily wages and worked for almost 275 days claims regularisation and continuance in the post - Whether the plaintiff worked for more than 240 days resulting in conferring of worker rights lies within the ambit of a Labour Court - The jurisdiction of Civil court is specifically barred for disputes covered by the Act - The judgment and decree of the lower Appellate Court is upheld.
[Paras 6 and 7]  B Calculation of the Period of Limitation - The plaintiff files a suit for declaration to acquire regular employee status in Haryana Roadways and be entitled to remain in service benefits - Exclusion of the time spent by plaintiff's prosecuting proceedings before all three courts is essential to determine the period of limitation within which a worker should file a claim before a Labour Court - The time spent, from the date of the suit till passing of the judgment, needs to be excluded, and hence, the appeal is dismissed.
