As per Section 25-F of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, a daily wager cannot be terminated without providing retrenchment compensation.
However, it was held that in this case, the management intended to pay the compensation on time and the delay was due to the workman not being available for service of notice.
Thus, there was sufficient compliance with Section 25-F [Paras 6 and 7]

According to the law laid down in the case of Himanshu Kumar Vidyarthi, a daily wager has no right to a post and hence his termination without compliance of Section 25-F cannot be considered bad.
[Para 8]

Although the termination of a back door entrant was made in violation of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India, the prayer for reinstatement can still be refused if the termination was without compliance of Section 25-F or 25-G of the Act of 1947.
The court relied upon the Division Bench judgment in Brij Bhushan's case.
