Article 226 of the Constitution of India outlines the principle of seniority list.
The Petitioner was satisfied with his position in the tentative seniority list, but the government accepted the objections raised against it by others and downgraded his position.
The government neither gave notice nor an opportunity of hearing to the Petitioner, thereby breaching principles of natural justice.
Consequently, the seniority list was quashed and the government was directed to redetermine the seniority of the Petitioner and other affected persons after providing them with a reasonable opportunity of hearing.
[Paras 4 & 6]
The Punjab Food and Supplies Department (State Service Class II) Rules, 1966, Rule 10 governs seniority lists.
The Commission found the Petitioner suitable for the position of DFSO, but the government ignored the merit assigned by the Commission.
The government did not consult the Commission while issuing seniority lists, thereby violating Rule 10 of the Rules.
Consequently, the seniority list was quashed as the government had decided the issue in disregard of directions given by the Supreme Court.
