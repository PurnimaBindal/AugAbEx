According to Article 311(2) of the Constitution of India, those who are on daily wage basis, have a time bound tenure, fall in the category of paid apprentice, or are allowed to man a post on purely officiating basis or in a stop gap arrangement, are not entitled to the protection of the Principal that protects even temporary servants; this means that no penal order can be passed against them without complying with requirements in Article 311(2).
Additionally, it was held that the order removing the name of the petitioner from the list of Paid apprentices passed without giving him any opportunity of hearing is proper and not violative of Article 311(2) since the petitioner was not found fit for the confirmation even as Paid apprentices.
[Paras 16, 17 and 18]
The provisions of Rule 7 and Rule 54 in UP Civil Services (Classification, Control and Appeal) Rules, 1930 apply only in cases where there is an order of dismissal or any other order inflicting punishment.
The order removing petitioner's name from the list of Paid apprentices or order putting an end to his officiation in higher post is not either order of dismissal or order imposing punishment as contemplated under said Rules.
Therefore, it was held that the order of Tribunal dismissing petition filed against said order as barred by limitation cannot be assailed by invoking remedies provided in Rule 7 or Rule 54 of Rules.
