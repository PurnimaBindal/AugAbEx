The High Court's role under Article 227 with regards to the Labor Court's award is limited to ensuring that the courts and tribunals are within their jurisdiction and boundaries.
The High Court should only interfere in case of erroneous assumption or excess of jurisdiction, error of law that is apparent on the face of the record, violation of natural justice principles, arbitrary or capricious exercise of authority or discretion, or perverse findings based on no material.
[Para 5]

The Labor Court's finding that the workman was a relief worker not entitled to labor law protections was not based on employer pleadings or evidence, and therefore cannot be sustained.
This conclusion was derived from Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 2(s) and 10, and Rajasthan Famine Relief Works Employees (Exemption from Labor Laws) Act, 1964, Section 3.
[Paras 8, 9, 13, 14]

Relief work do not fall under industries.
Daily wagers engaged in relief work for a fixed term are not considered retrenched within the meaning of Section 2(oo) and their termination is automatic once the period of contract is completed.
This is under the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 2(oo)(bb) and 25F, and Rajasthan Famine Relief Works Employees (Exemption from Labor Laws) Act, 1964, Section 3.
[Paras 17, 18]

Workmen who are found guilty of suppressing material facts when filling the application under section 33-C(2) of the ID Act are not entitled to any relief.
This is under Constitution of India, Article 227 and the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 33-C(2).
