The State Government is only required to form an opinion about whether there is an industrial dispute, not to adjudicate upon the dispute, under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India and Section 10 of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.
[Para 4]
Although the appropriate government is not required to refer frivolous disputes to the Labour Court, if the State Government finds that a dispute exists, it cannot determine the dispute's validity or the worker's claims regarding the issue.
Furthermore, the State Government's refusal to refer a dispute because of a worker's failure to report for work is an adjudication and it exceeded its authority.
[Para 4]
The victimization and termination of the petitioner were attributed by the State Government to repeated letters from management about the employee's non-appearance for work.
The Court struck down the government's order, directing the appropriate authority to reconsider the matter.
However, the government reaffirmed its previous decision, resulting in the Court's quashing of the order mandating the government to refer the dispute to the Labour Court under Section 10 of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, in Para 7.
