Article 226 of the Constitution of India and Punjab Police Rules, 1934 (Volume 11), Rule 16.2 allow for dismissal from service on grounds of misconduct, but only after the competent authority has recorded its finding on the guilt of the employee and considered their past service record.
Any punishment awarded without this consideration will be deemed a failure of the authority to apply its mind, as evidenced in a case where a disciplinary authority held the petitioner guilty of misconduct and imposed the penalty of dismissal without considering the question of lesser punishment and did not account for 12 years of clear service record.
The dismissal was subsequently set aside.

While the requirement of discipline in the police force is greater than that of ordinary civil servants, the competent authority cannot ignore the context of the service and the fact that there is a growing tendency for people in general to consume liquor.
Although an act of consuming liquor by a police officer, coupled with misbehavior towards a Sub-Inspector, may be a ground for imposing the extreme penalty of dismissal, it is up to the disciplinary authority to decide whether a more humane and compassionate approach would be appropriate and whether a lesser punishment should be awarded.
