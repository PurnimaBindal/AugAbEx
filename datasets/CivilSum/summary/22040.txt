The U.P.
Co-op.
Societies Act, 1965, specifies the reservations for Schedule Castes and Tribes in Cooperative Societies through Section 130(2)(xii) and (xii-A).
The UP Societies Rules, 1968, also define the guidelines for reservations through Rule 393A, 393B, Rule 440(d)(4), (6), (8), 444-A(3) Rule 453(5)(i).
This case considers whether the High Court is justified in declaring these provisions ultra vires of the Constitution and quashing them, and whether UP Legislature has the power to provide reservations to weaker sections.
The court held that the Act and Rules are consistent with the Constitution of India and guidelines have been indicated by the provisions.
The Court is not competent to interfere with the policy and declare it unconstitutional under Article 19(1)(c).
Additionally, the Constitution of India under Articles 46, 19 ensures that the State promotes the economic interest of weaker sections of society.
While no citizen has the fundamental right to become a member of a co-op society under Article 19(1)(c), the right is a statutory right.
Therefore, no individual member is entitled to assail constitutionality of the provisions of the Act.
The court ultimately found reservations of weaker sections to managing committees of Coop Societies is not ultra vires.
