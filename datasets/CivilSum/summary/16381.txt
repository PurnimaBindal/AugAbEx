According to the Workman's Compensation Act (8 of 1923), Sections 2(n), Schedule 3, only those persons engaged in manual and skilled activities in the enumerated trades and avocations can be considered as `Workmen', and persons employed in schools or those running schools are not covered under this definition.
Therefore, the claimants who contended that a deceased teacher died of renal failure and heart attack due to work-related pressure and tension are not entitled to seek compensation.
The contention that the teacher died due to strain at work cannot be considered an occupational disease, and hence the claim given by the Workmen's Compensation Commissioner was set aside.
