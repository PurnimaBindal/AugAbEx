Subba Rao for B. C. Prabhakar, Advocate argues that the refusal of compassionate appointment to a woman, who is married and has been deserted by her mentally deranged husband and is dependent on her mother for livelihood, is discriminatory and violative of constitutional guarantees such as Articles 14, 15, and 16 of the Constitution of India.
It is suggested that the Karnataka Electricity Board should revise its guidelines and remove such anachronisms as it goes against the trend of times when men and women are competing on equal terms in all areas of society.
The court has directed the Electricity Board to consider the petitioner's case for appointment on compassionate grounds, ignoring the fact that she is married.
The respondent concludes by stating that traditional and social customs hindering Indian women from enjoying equal rights with men must change with the changing family structure, lifestyles, and social norms, to prevent detrimental effects on society caused by intertia.
