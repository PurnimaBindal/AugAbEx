The Respondent, a charitable institution, appointed the Appellant's husband in a Class IV post, but he passed away back on 1691996.
The vacancy was filled in the year 1997, and the Appellant, who is around 38 years old now, is receiving a family pension and has income from immovable properties.
At the relevant time, the Appellant did not fulfill the criteria for compassionate appointment, and therefore, no case is made out for exercising discretionary jurisdiction under Article 136 of the Constitution.
B.
In accordance with the principles summarized, appointment on a compassionate basis can only be granted in situations where the family of the deceased is suddenly faced with a crisis.
Whether or not an individual has the right to receive appointment on a compassionate basis is contingent on the scheme operating in the field.
It is not appropriate to deny compassionate appointment simply because they are receiving a family pension.
Appointments made on a compassionate basis are not a source of common recruitment.
