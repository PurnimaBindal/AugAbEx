According to Section 2(j) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1894, Haryana Urban Development Authority (HUDA) can be considered an 'industry' as its functions are not sovereign or regal.
Although HUDA can impose penalties and recover dues as arrears of land revenue, it still performs industrial functions.
Thus, providing free services on a no profit no loss basis or being a charitable institution does not exclude it from the definition of an industry.
The purpose of the Industrial Disputes Act is to protect the rights of weaker sections of society, avoid industrial tension, settle disputes, and provide social justice for illiterate workers.
The statutory definition of a team must be followed to the logical end of the statute, and interpretation cannot be allowed to make the law vague.
Only sovereign and crown's acts are exempt from the definition of 'Industry,' and the maintenance of street is not a sovereign function.
Moreover, conferring the status of civil servant on the employees of a Corporation will not deprive the Corporation of industry status.
Services or activities carried out by the State that can be carried out by a private person or agency must be considered industrial functions.
Indian Limitation Act or the Civil Procedure Code is not applicable to Industrial Tribunals and Labour Courts, but delay in raising a dispute can be explained, and relief can be adjusted accordingly.
The government has the authority to refer disputes to the Labour Court or the Industrial Tribunal.
The interpretation of whether a dispute falls within the jurisdiction of the Labour Court or the Industrial Tribunal cannot be raised for the first time in writ jurisdiction.
A workman must be deemed a regular worker after 240 days.
Any other interpretation of this provision would undermine the purpose of the Act.
