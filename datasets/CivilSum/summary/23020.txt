The case involves multiple writ petitions and appeals addressing the constitutionality of granting-in-aid to minority educational institutions.
The court finds that minority institutions have the right to establish and administer educational institutions and can prioritize admission to students of their community.
However, if the state chooses to provide grant-in-aid, it cannot discriminate against minority-managed institutions and cannot dictate their admission policies.
The right to establish and manage educational institutions is absolute and not subject to limitations like those under Articles 19, 25, and 26.
The court also notes that minority institutions cannot be considered "other authorities" under Article 12 and that the mandate under Article 28 is only against individual freedom of attending religious instruction.
The interpretation of constitutional provisions cannot refer to speeches made in the Constituent Assembly.
