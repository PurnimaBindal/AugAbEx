Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 226 - Appointment - Selection - Writ of mandamus - Appointment recommendations cannot be interfered by Courts in writ jurisdiction - The condition precedent for the issue of mandamus is the existing legal right of the petitioner himself, who complains the infraction of such a right - Promotions and appointments are discretionary, not purely ministerial - Writ Court has no jurisdiction to decide disputed questions of facts and usurp the power and functions of the recommending body/appointing authority - Courts may quash unlawful decisions and require the public body to reconsider the matter according to law, but cannot substitute their own view - Market Manual of the Corporation is neither statutory nor binding; therefore, no violation of any law is involved.
