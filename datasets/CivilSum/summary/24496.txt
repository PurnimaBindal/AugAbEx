The Constitution of India, specifically Articles 14, 16, and 39(d) promotes equal pay for equal work between daily wage employees and regular employees, such as Ledger Clerks, Ledger Keepers, Pump operators, Mali-cum-chowkidars, fitters, petrol men, surveyors, etc.
However, the claim of equal pay for equal work cannot be granted simply by asking and there must be proper pleading with necessary averments to establish that every aspect of the two posts are equal.
The doctrine of equal pay for equal work must be applied on a case-by-case basis, with a consideration given to the status of the employees.
The High Court had granted the claim without any proper findings on the equality of the nature of work, duties, and responsibilities attached to the pots.
Matters are being remitted back to the High Court to properly adjudicate and decide afresh, and if necessary, relegate the parties to the appropriate forum.
[Paras 17 and 19]  B.
Persons engaged as contract employees are not entitled to claim equal pay for equal work under the Constitution of India.
Their employment is governed by the terms of the contract of their employment.
They cannot claim equality with those appointed after proper selection in accordance with the recruitment rules.
The High Court had allowed the claim of contract workers under the doctrine of equal pay for equal work, but matters are being remitted back to the High Court to properly adjudicate and decide afresh while giving the parties an opportunity to make any necessary amendments to their pleadings.
