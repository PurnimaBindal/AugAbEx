In relation to compulsory premature retirement of a judicial officer, a committee of judges delegated with the powers of the Full Court can validly consider the matter on behalf of the Full Court instead of requiring the whole body of the Judges of the High Court to consider it.
[Para 9]

Despite an increase in the retirement age from 58 and 60 years, the provisions for pre-mature compulsory retirement under Rule 4 of the Uttar Pradesh Judicial Officers (Retirement on Superannuation) Rules, 1992 are not repealed.
Rule 56(c) of the UP Fundamental Rules still empowers the High Court to pass an order of compulsory retirement of an employee attaining the age of 50 years.
The High Court can still consider as to whether an employee is fit, and it is in the public interest to allow him or her to continue in public service after the age of 50 years.
[Paras 10 to 16]

The object and purpose of exercise of power of compulsory retirement is not to allow inefficient or corrupt officers in public service against public interest.
If such an opinion is framed on the basis of adverse service record of an officer, the order is justified.
The rule embodies pleasure doctrine as embodied in Article 310 of the Constitution.
[Paras 17 to 19]

The judicial service is not an ordinary employment and judges are expected to have honesty and integrity that goes beyond doubt.
Therefore, the Committee of the High Court Judges can evaluate a judicial officer's suitability for compulsory premature retirement based on its subjective satisfaction.
This evaluation can be affirmed in the writ petition and should not be interfered with except in very exceptional circumstances.
