The Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 2(oo)(bb) and 25-F pertain to termination by way of discharge from service.
In this particular case, the petitioner was absent from duty after being on leave for an illness and failed to report back despite the management's requests.
He was carrying out his own business on the management's premises without authorization, occupying official accommodation for over nine years without performing any duties.
Consequently, the order of removal from service was in line with Service Rules.
This is not classified as retrenchment, and the Act aims to protect workers in need, not uncooperative employees.
In such situations, management requires protection against workers.
The decision by the Labour Court to deny relief cannot be considered faulty.
