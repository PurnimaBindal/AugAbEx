The State Govt. created the post of Chief Vigilance Officer-cum-Enforcement Officer and required it to be filled by an HCS Officer before considering a departmental candidate for the position.
Since there is no evidence of the State Govt. stating that an HCS Officer is unavailable, a departmental officer cannot be promoted.
Thus, the appointment of Respondent No. 4, who was not an HCS Officer, as CVO-cum-E.O.
with HUDA was illegal and without the authority of the law.
As a result, the order of his appointment was canceled.
[Paras 11 and 13]

The authority's resolution of creating the post of CVO-cum-E.O.
required it to be filled up by an HCS Officer.
There was no resolution regarding the appointment of a departmental officer.
Thus, the approval received by the communication of Chief Administrative dated 30111999 cannot be seen as the approval of the Govt. of the proposed issue.
Once a decision has been taken that the post is for an HCS Officer, relying on the proposal contained in the agenda item is a wholly unfounded and untenable stand.
[Para 10]

It may not be feasible for the State Govt. to include a clause for the appointment of a suitable departmental officer when the proposal is for the appointment of an HCS Officer, and the same has been approved.
However, the court refused to make any statement on the said clause in the agenda item because no question was raised regarding it.
[Para 10]
