FR-13 provides that a deputationist's lien is extendable, with the maximum limit being set by the Government of India.
In this case, the Petitioner's lien was terminated after two years.
The Single Judge had previously determined that the Petitioner had acquiesced to the termination of his lien based on a letter he had written seeking transfer of terminal benefits.
However, the Petitioner's conduct following the termination of the lien showed that he had no intention of giving up his lien, and the principle underlying the doctrine of acquiescence is silence on the part of the petitioner to assert his right.
Therefore, the Petitioner's lien could not be terminated based on the grounds of consent or acquiescence.
[Paras 13 to 15]

The Government has formulated a policy stating that a lien of five years should be provided to a person who comes from a below-board post, in order to harness talent from internal sources.
The Petitioner's lien was terminated after two years, and there is no reason to deviate from this policy in his case.
[Paras 18, 20]

The Petitioner's writ petition should be allowed on the grounds of discrimination.
His lien was terminated after two years, while the liens of several employees in the department were retained for more than two years.
[Para 23]
