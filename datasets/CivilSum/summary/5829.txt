Constitution of India, Articles 14 & 16 - Pay-scale/Different pay scale - Librarians claiming equal pay scale to the lecturers - In order to claim similarity in pay-scales, the two sets of employees must perform similar duties - The burden of proving discrimination falls on the aggrieved employee - No evidence was presented to show that the duties of Librarians and Lecturers are similar - Different pay scales for Lecturers cannot be considered discriminatory.
[Paras 8, 21, and 22]

Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 16 - Revision of pay scales - Parity of pay-scales - The Government cannot prescribe different dates of implementation for different cadres - Such practice is discriminatory and violates Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
[Paras 9, 10, and 21]

Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 16 - Pay-scales - Qualifications - Different pay scales must be provided on the basis of differences in duties, not just qualifications - The employer must justify any classification on the basis of qualifications with a direct nexus to the duties - If there is no direct nexus between the duties and the qualifications, the classification cannot be upheld and the Government must provide material to justify it.
[Paras 12, 13, and 21]

Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 16 - Pay-scales - Qualifications & experience - Classification can be made on the basis of experience, but not just on the basis of qualifications when the duties are the same - Experience can be equated with higher qualifications - Granting a higher pay-scale to a junior employee based solely on higher qualifications within the same group and cadre is not permissible.
[Para 21]
