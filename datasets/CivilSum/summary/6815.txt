Punjab Police Rules, 1934, Rule 16.24(1)(v) Enquiry - Defence - The delinquent must provide a list of witnesses with a summary of the facts they will testify to and the enquiry officer can refuse to hear any irrelevant or unnecessary witnesses.
However, there is no coercive machinery available for the delinquent to produce official witnesses, although the Inquiry Officer can do so.
If no contribution to withholding defence evidence is attributed to the delinquent, and official witnesses are not summoned, then a reasonable opportunity to lead defence was denied to the delinquent.
The order of dismissal, which denies the opportunity to lead the defence, violates Rule 16.24(1)(v) and is unsustainable.
[Para 6]

Punjab Police Rules, 1934, Rule 16.2 - Dismissal/Misconduct/Gravest misconduct/Pension - The authority must take into consideration the length of service of the delinquent and his claim for pension before awarding the severe punishment of dismissal, after recording findings of the gravest act of misconduct or cumulative misconduct proving incorrigibility and complete unfitness for police service.
In this case, the authority did not acknowledge the length of service when awarding the punishment, even though the delinquent did not complete qualifying service for pension.
The order is annulled.
[Paras 8 and 9]

Punjab Police Rules, 1934, Rule 16.2 - Misconduct/Absence from duty - While the absence from duty in a disciplinary force would be a gross misconduct, the mere fact of absence from duty may not amount to gross misconduct in the totality of the circumstances.
If the contents such as habitual absence from duty, duty from which absent, incorrigibility, devotion to duty, and reasons for absence are considered, the gross misconduct envisaged under the Rule will be found.
