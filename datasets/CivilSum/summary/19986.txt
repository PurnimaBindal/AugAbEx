Constitution of India, Article 226 - Industrial Disputes Act 1947, Section 11-A - When jurisdiction is in question, the authority may not lack inherent jurisdiction but still may have committed a jurisdictional error.
[Para 20]  B Industrial Disputes Act 1947, Section 11-A - Labor Court power - Labor Court cannot unfairly resort to Section 11-A to get rid of union leaders or punish workers.
The court must pose a proper question of misconduct and victimization and try to find out the guilt.
[Paras 23, 31 to 34]  C. Constitution of India, Article 226 - Industrial Disputes Act 1947, Section 11-A - Statutory interpretation - Provisions of the statute must be construed with a view to uphold the object and purport of Parliament.
[Para 23]  D Constitution of India, Article 226 - Industrial Disputes Act 1947, Section 11-A - Misconduct - Workman must be punished in accordance with the principles of natural justice.
[Para 25]  E. Industrial Disputes Act 1947, Section 11-A - Fresh evidence can only be permitted if the question of sufficiency of evidence or of fair and proper inquiry is determined in favor of the workman.
[Paras 25 and 26]  F. Constitution of India, Article 226 - Industrial Disputes Act 1947, Section 11-A - The Labor Court should not substitute its own view only because the same was also possible.
[Para 28]  G. Evidence Act, 1872, Section 101 - When both parties adduce evidence, the onus of proof loses its significance for all practical purposes.
[Para 29]  H Industrial Disputes Act 1947, Section 11-A - A clear case of misconduct is made out if workmen resorted to using abuse, threat, quarrel, shouting or had iron rods in hand with a view to assault, even if no injury was caused to anyone.
Assault and intimidation are penal offenses.
