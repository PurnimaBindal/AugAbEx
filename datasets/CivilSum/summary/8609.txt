Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 10 - Reference - Merely referring to a dispute does not establish that the workman's services were terminated illegally, nor is it enough to determine such questions - The Labour Court is responsible for resolving these issues.
[Paras 5 and 6] B.
Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 10 - Reinstatement without back wages - Termination - Abandonment - The employer did not provide a termination order, claiming that the workman abandoned their job - Despite the Labour Court directing the workman to return to work multiple times, they failed to do so - Since they worked for the employer for under six months, the granted relief of reinstatement was excessive - The workman's claim for back wages was considered baseless.
[Para 7] C. Constitution of India, Article 14 - Termination - Enquiry - The employer did not issue a termination order and instead argued that the workman had left and stayed away on their own accord - Without a termination order, a proper enquiry cannot occur - Therefore, it is not valid to argue that the termination was illegal due to improper enquiry.
