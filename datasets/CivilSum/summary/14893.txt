The Oil Selection Board provides initial recommendations for issuance of a letter of intent, and after examining the complaint and evidence and providing the petitioner with an opportunity for a personal hearing, the Board decides to cancel the letter of intent.
Therefore, it was the Oil Selection Board and not the Indian Oil Corporation that had to give the petitioner a hearing, and there is no question of estoppel in this case.
The application form states that if it is found at any time that the letter of intent was obtained through concealing or providing incorrect facts, the letter of intent or the dealership itself can be cancelled.
It does not matter who the complainant is, as the letter of intent/dealership can be cancelled in accordance with the law even if the Oil Selection Board or the Indian Oil Corporation discovered the petitioner filed false declarations or misrepresented facts.
This falls under Constitution of India, Article 226.
