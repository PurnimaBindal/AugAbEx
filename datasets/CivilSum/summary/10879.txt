Constitution of India, Article 226 - Quo Warranto - Promotion - Qualifications - Relaxation of qualifications - It is not enough to claim that a relaxation of prescribed qualifications has occurred in order to automatically apply a writ of quo warranto; rather, it must be demonstrated that the incumbent is significantly lacking in qualifications to hold the public post.
The petitioner's promotion to Vice Principal did not violate any laws, nor did it break any rules, such as the requirement of a Second division in a Master's degree for direct recruits, which allegedly the appellant lacked.
Furthermore, the appellant had the necessary overall experience required for the promotion.
No inherent lack of essential qualifications or experience was evident, and no malafide was proven.
Therefore, issuing a writ of quo warranto based on unnecessary requirements was found to be untenable and unsustainable.
[Paras 10 to 12]
