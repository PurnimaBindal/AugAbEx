The Constitution of India, Articles 16 and 226, discusses the appointment of employees and the interference with the abolition of their post.
The respondents had been employed on a daily wage basis but were issued appointment letters in an attempt to make their appointment regular.
However, they failed to produce the required documents before joining and their appointments were cancelled.
A resolution was later passed to abolish the system of engaging daily wagers, but the High Court quashed this decision, directing the appellants to let the respondents join with backwages and continuity.
This decision was challenged, and it was held that the continuance or abolition of a post is within the power of the employer and any decision in this regard should not be interfered with by the Court unless it is vitiated by mala fide or is arbitrary.
Therefore, the order of the High Court is unsustainable and set aside, with directions given to NAC to consider the respondents for future vacancies as per law.
[Paras 4 and 5]
