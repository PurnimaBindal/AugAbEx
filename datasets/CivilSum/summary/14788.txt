Under the Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952, Section 17-B, when an employer transfers an establishment, they are liable to contribute to the Provident Fund.
An agreement to manufacture sugar on a rental basis does not qualify as a transfer of factory within the Act, and the liability to pay Provident Fund dues prior to the date of the agreement cannot be shifted to the other factory.
Any proceedings taken against the other factory by an authority under the Act without jurisdiction is invalid and must be quashed.
[Paras 10 and 11] B Section 17-B of the Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952, does not require the permission of the Asstt.
Provident Fund Commissioner for the transfer of establishment.
If applications for permission are made, but no orders are passed, the authority cannot claim that permission was not sought.
An order of attachment made against the alleged transferee without proper grounds is illegal.
[Paras 12 and 14] C. The attachment of an establishment made by the Asstt.
Provident Fund Commissioner without notice or opportunity for the establishment to be heard, and resulting in an F.I.R., goes against the principles of natural justice and is therefore illegitimate.
Any proceedings taken as such must be nullified.
