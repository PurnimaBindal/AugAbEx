The Constitution of India, Articles 226 and 311, and Army Rules, 1954, Rule 13-A govern retirement, resignation, and compulsory retirement.
Natural justice principles must be considered in all administrative actions that affect a person unless a statute excludes such a rule by necessary implications or express words.
The respondent in this case was given no opportunity to represent his case.
As a result, the final order passed by the Central Govt. to retire or resign or be compulsorily retired violated Rule 13-A(3) of Army Rules and natural justice principles.
Exceptions should be rare when seeking to displace the natural justice principles.

The failure to comply with clause 15 (b) (i) of Special Army Instruction No. 1 had serious implications for the compulsory retirement of the respondent.
Before taking action under Rule 13-A of the Army Rules, the notice had to be served upon the officer at the end of 18 years of not passing the examination as per clause 15(b)(ii).
The termination of services of the respondent was deemed not valid as there was no show cause notice under clause 15 (b) (i) issued at the end of 18 years as per the provisions of Rule 13-A of Army Rules.
