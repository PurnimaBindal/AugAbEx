Section 31(7) of the Delhi Co-operative Societies Act, 1972 allows orders of the Lt.
Governor to not be challenged in routine.
Judgments based on the Representation of People Act cannot be applied.
The Lt.
Governor passed an order under Section 31(7) after allowing parties to inspect the record and hearing counsel.
Reasons given for conclusions were not illegal or irrational.
[Para 8]
Elections to the committee of a co-operative society under the Delhi Co-operative Societies Act, 1972 are on a different pedestal than elections held under the Representation of People Act.
Provisions of the Representation of People Act, 1958 do not apply to elections to committees of co-operative societies under the Delhi Act, 1972.
[Paras 10 and 12]
Under Article 226 of the Constitution of India, a court can intervene in a matter concerning a co-operative society in which the majority should prevail.
The court has the power to mold relief as appropriate, just, and fair.
A direction was issued for holding fresh elections under the supervision of an officer appointed by the court.
This is the best and fairest solution.
