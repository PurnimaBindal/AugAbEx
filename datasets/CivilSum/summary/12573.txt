Section 33 of Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, along with Rule 9 of Industrial Disputes (Central) Rules, 1957 deals with the conciliation proceedings in case of a strike or lock-out.
Rule 9 mentions that a Conciliation Officer will interview both employer and workmen in the presence of each other, for searching the possibility of a conciliation.
However, in this case, no notice was given and Conciliation Officer initiated the proceedings to conciliate on his own discretion.
Hence, Rule 9 didn't apply and the Conciliation Officer didn't have the right to take the initiative for the conciliation proceedings.
Besides, no permission was needed for terminating appellant.
[Para 6]  B.
According to Rule 15(4) (II)(b) of Central Civil Service (C.C.A.)
rules of 1965, a disciplinary authority may dismiss an employee after establishing the charges against him/her.
In this case, there was sufficient material available from the initiation of domestic enquiry against the appellant.
Hence, the disciplinary authority had delivered the dismissal order on the ground of established charges, and the non-availability of specific reasons can't hold the order invalid.
