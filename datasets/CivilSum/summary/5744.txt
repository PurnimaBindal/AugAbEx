The University Ordinance (Rajasthan), Chapter XXII, Rule 81(b)(ii) along with the Constitution of India's Articles 29(ii) and 14 were applied to a life convict who passed triple MA while serving their sentence.
The petitioner was not permitted to join a B.Ed.
course as a regular student because Rule 81(b)(ii) stated that individuals convicted of an offense involving moral turpitude could not be admitted as a regular student, although they could enroll in a correspondence course.
The Court held that the rule was discriminatory and against Article 29(ii) of the Constitution of India.
[Paras 19 to 21]

Under University Ordinance (Rajasthan), Rule 81(b)(ii), a life convict seeking admission to a B.Ed.
course was not allowed to enroll as a regular student.
However, they could take a correspondence course if available.
The Court ruled that this rule constituted gross discrimination and was ultra vires and unconstitutional, violating the right of education guaranteed under Article 29(ii) of the Constitution of India.
[Para 19]

The definition of moral turpitude was explained.
Not every act of violence constitutes moral turpitude.
Some violent or criminal acts may be committed unintentionally due to a mistake in judgment, which does not qualify as moral turpitude.
[Para 20]
