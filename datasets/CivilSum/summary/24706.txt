It is undisputed that the Chief Justice of India exercises legislative power when making rules under Article 146(2).
These rules are considered subordinate legislation and have the force of law as long as they adhere to the conditions prescribed by the Constitution.
However, they are subordinate to the parent law and require the approval of the President under the proviso to clause (2) of Article 146 to become legitimate.
This approval is essential as it stamps the rules, especially those related to salaries and allowances, with the authority of subordinate legislation.
While the Chief Justice of India's rule-making is a crucial step in the legal process, its significance lies only in the rules' approval by the President.
