The Punjab Civil Services Rules, Vol. II Rule 532A(c) and Vol.
I Rule 3.26(d) as applicable to Haryana, outline the circumstances under which an employee can be subjected to compulsory premature retirement.
Compliance with the principles of natural justice, as stated in Article 311 of the Indian Constitution, will be required for a punitive order of compulsory retirement.
Nonetheless, if the order is administrative and passed in the public interest, then it will not target the conduct, character, or integrity of the employee, and it will not fall under the provisions of Article 311(2) of the Constitution.
The purpose of such compulsory premature retirement is to eliminate deadwood, corrupt, dishonest or inefficient staff from government service.
If a competent authority decides to take a lenient view and retires a person compulsorily at the age of 55 with all due service benefits intact, even if it was possible to find them guilty of harassment that would invite a serious penalty, that decision cannot be considered illegal or unfair.
If the order is punitive, only then will the court consider it as such.
The petitioner in this case, a police officer (DSP), has been involved in various criminal activities and has also been charged with contempt of court and avoiding service of summons for over a year.
The authorities were therefore entitled to retire him compulsorily in the public interest.
