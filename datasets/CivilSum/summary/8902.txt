The Constitution of India's Articles 14, 16, and 226 address the matter of charge-sheet/natural justice and the competent authority responsible for issuing charge-sheets.
In the absence of a specific rule calling for it, charge-sheets can be issued by authorities other than punishing authorities and would not be considered defective.
[Para 8]

The Constitution of India's Articles 14 and 311 pertain to natural justice/documents, specifically the obligation to provide copies of documents accompanying the charge-sheet.
If documents were made available for inspection during the investigation, then the lack of copies of the same does not affect the delinquent's rights, and they may not cry foul for natural justice violations.
[Para 9]

The Constitution of India's Articles 311 and 226 mandate that a copy of the inquiry report be supplied to delinquents as part of natural justice.
However, there is no strict need to provide the same before the punishment order is passed, unless the lack of a previous copy causes prejudice to the delinquent.
The Supreme Court, in Mohamad Ramjan Khan's case, did not order supply of the report as a prerequisite for punishment legality.
[Paras 10 and 11]

The Coal India Executive Conduct, Discipline, and Appeal Rules, 1978, Rule 36, and the Constitution of India's Article 311 cover the subject of speaking orders in appeals.
The Appellate Authority in this case examined charges, records, reports, and grounds of appeal and disposed of the appeal accordingly.
No illegality accompanies the Appellate Authority's order.
[Para 12]

The Constitution of India's Articles 14 and 311 elaborate on the subject of punishment/discrimination in awarding punishment.
While the principle of equality among equals ought to underlie the administration's punishment function, situations where employees have differing duties make identical punishment impossible.
In such cases, employees may receive varied punishment based on relevant factors, and this would not be considered discriminatory.
Different punishment at varying levels should, therefore, be expected.
[Para 14]
