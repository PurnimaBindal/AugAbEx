According to the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 2(j), sovereign functions of the state are those that are inalienable from the functions of the State.
Even though the functions of the State have extended to almost every sphere, the line between sovereign and non-sovereign powers has largely disappeared.
However, there can be inner circles within the wider circle of sovereign functions that could be considered as industry if substantially severable.
In this case, the Forest Department of State for the creation of a park under bio-aesthetic development for the benefit of urban population cannot be regarded as a part of inalienable or inescapable function of the State, and such functions of the state fall within the ambit of definition of 'Industry' under the ID Act.
[Paras 12, 13 and 15]

According to the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 2(ra), Schedule Fifth, Clause 10, keeping the workmen as casuals and not allowing them to complete 240 days continuously for 5-6 years while employed under schemes at hand to advance objects having permanent basis is considered an unfair labor practice.
[Para 22]

Casual workers working in State departments for 5 to 6 years are entitled to be regularised, according to the Constitution of India, Articles 136 and 226, Industrial Disputes Act, Section 2(ra), Schedule Fifth, Clauses 9 and 10, and failure to regularise them for such a long time and to deny them equal pay for equal work at par to their regular counterparts amounts to unfair labour practice.
The financial burden on the State is not a valid reason to deny the relief of regularisation of services.
[Paras 22 and 28]
