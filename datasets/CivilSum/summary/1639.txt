Sections 11-A and 33-A of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 - Domestic inquiry - The absence or inadequacy of a domestic inquiry does not automatically invalidate the order of discharge or dismissal, but the tribunal must assess the order's legality and validity - It must also allow both the employer and employee to present evidence - The tribunal may evaluate the evidence and make its own decisions - The tribunal may also intervene in the nature of the punishment, using powers granted under Section 11-A - [Paras 10, 11, and 12].
B Back wages - When the Labour Court concluded that the dismissal order against a workman was justified based on the evidence presented before it, the order would not affect the day on which the dismissal took place - The Labour Court could not issue any relief once it had established that the dismissal was justified - Compensation of Rs. 50,000/- was awarded as relief to each of the writ petitioners by the Labour Court, and since the award was undisputed, no further action was required - [Para 27].
