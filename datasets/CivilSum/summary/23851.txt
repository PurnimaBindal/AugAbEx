Constitution of India, Articles 16 and 311 - Sexual harassment of female employees - A female employee was subjected to sexual harassment by her superior officer who ignored her reprimand and persisted in trying to touch her - The officer was removed from service by the Departmental enquiry for his unbecoming behavior - Although the High Court set aside the finding, its reasoning was not valid as it substituted its own discretion for that of the Disciplinary authority in determining the facts and punishment.

Constitution of India, Article 21 - Sexual harassment of female employee - Any action or gesture aimed at outraging the modesty of a female employee constitutes sexual harassment - Sexual harassment includes unwelcome advances, requests for sexual favors, and other verbal or physical conduct with sexual overtones - Each act of sexual harassment at the workplace violates the right to gender equality and the right to life and liberty - On the facts of the case, the officer's behaviour towards the female employee amounted to sexual harassment.

Constitution of India, Articles 16, 311 - Departmental enquiry - The Disciplinary Authority has sole jurisdiction over findings of fact - An Appellate Authority may overturn the findings, but the High Court may not do so unless the findings are perverse or contrary to law - The High Court may not act as an Appellate Authority or change the punishment - The adequacy or inadequacy of evidence cannot be questioned before the High Court.

Constitution of India, Article 16 and 226 - Departmental enquiry - The court, in exercising its power of judicial review, is not concerned with the correctness of the factual finding if it is reasonably supported by evidence - Judicial review is limited to examining the decision-making process, not the decision itself.
