The petitioner had completed two years of ad hoc service and, according to executive instructions, should have been considered for regularisation.
The termination of the petitioner's services without such consideration was deemed illegal by the court.
Furthermore, the court deemed the government's assertion that the petitioner's work and conduct were not up to standard to be baseless.
Consequently, the judgment was quashed and the petitioner was ordered to be reinstated with full back wages.
The government was advised to consider the case for regularisation.

When an order of termination is inferred to be penal from the sequence of facts, even if the order is couched in innocuous terms, the court may set aside that order to ensure justice and fair play.
This right is protected by the Constitution of India's Article 14 and 16.
