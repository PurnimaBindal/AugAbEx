The petitioner, who has been appointed as a qualified teacher with sufficient experience, argues that since no one has been appointed to a sanctioned post, his appointment to a full-time and permanent post in the school would not deprive anyone else of their accrued rights.
As such, the petitioner should be spared the formal process of appearing before a selection committee and be appointed to the vacant Assistant Teacher post in order to secure his right to work and right to livelihood, as well as to secure social justice and economic and legal rights.
[Para 11]

Despite the fact that the petitioner is a highly qualified teacher with significant experience, he was paid only Rs. 50 per month for his work, which is a form of "begar" and strictly prohibited under Article 23 of the Constitution.
This sacrifice should not be penalized, and it is essential that social and economic justice is not rendered meaningless.
[Para 10]

The right to life guaranteed under Article 21 of the Constitution includes the right to livelihood.
[Para 8]

The managing committee of the school can appoint temporary teachers and employees against a temporary or permanent vacancy within the sanctioned strength of the teachers, subject to the approval of the Director or authorized officer.
The committee's assurance to the petitioner for absorption or regularisation was merely empty, so the court issued a writ of mandamus to absorb the petitioner in the post of Assistant Teacher of the School in Social Science Group or any other permanent and full-time post - even if necessary, creating new or additional posts to accommodate him.
[Paras 5 and 13]
