The Constitution of India, Articles 32 and 226, along with the All India Council for Technical Education Act, 1987, Section 2(h) and 2(i), allow for judicial review with regards to university and technical institutions.
When the provisions of the Act have specific mention and expression, they cannot be diminished by the supposed purpose of the Act or the purpose for creating a body to supervise its implementation.
[Paras 8, 12 and 14] B.
The All India Council for Technical Education Act, 1987, Sections 2(h), 2(i), 10(1)(k) and 23, states that approval for technical courses is required only for institutions defined under Section 2(h) of the Act, not universities defined under Section 2(i).
The AICTE should not seek to have authority over universities, as it is only an advisory body and cannot superimpose its own regulations under Section 33 of the Act.
To do so would be ultra vires the Act.
