The Central Administrative Tribunal Rules of Practice, 1993, Rule 154, as well as Appendix VII, Entry II and Rule 154(c) of Rules require that matters concerning the regularization of ad hoc appointment are to be decided by a two-member Bench of Tribunal, comprising one Judicial and one Administrative Member.
Thus, as per Sections 21 and 22 of the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985, a single Member and Tribunal could not have dealt with the issue of regularization of casual laborers.
[Para 14]

As per Section 21(1)(a) of the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985, once a final order is passed, an OA is required to be filed within one year.
Here, the respondents had filed an OA in 2003, which was hopelessly time-barred as the cause of action had arisen in 1988, when the terminations occurred.
Furthermore, the mere fact that the applicants' names had been kept in a live casual labor register would not furnish them any cause of action for the relief of regularisation.
[Para 10, 11]

In light of the judgment of the Apex Court in Uma Devi's case, regularisation cannot be considered a mode of appointment, because the engagement of casual employees is not based on proper selection criteria, as recognized by relevant rules or procedures.
[Para 12]
