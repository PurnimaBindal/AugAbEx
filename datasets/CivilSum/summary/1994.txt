Article 226 of the Constitution of India grants the Apparel Export Promotion Council the power to remove employees who engage in misconduct.
A petitioner was removed from service after being accused of trying to molest a female employee, but he claimed that the charge was fabricated out of personal prejudice.
Upon investigation, it was found that he had indeed tried to molest the complainant, but the complainant had not mentioned the incident to anyone for five days and senior officials were present at the time.
The petitioner was reinstated in service without back wages and will be posted outside of Delhi for two years.
[Paras 14 and 15]

The petitioner claimed that he was not provided a copy of the Inquiry Report after he was accused of misconduct by the Apparel Export Promotion Council.
However, principles of natural justice did not require him to receive a copy of the report after the inquiry had taken place.
[Para 11]

The Apparel Export Promotion Council was accused of improperly reviewing its own decision to remove an employee accused of misconduct.
However, the staff committee reviewed the decision on the direction of the Supreme Court given in a prior case where the court made specific provisions for review.
Therefore, there was no infirmity in the council reviewing its own decision.
[Para 12]

A petitioner claimed that he could not be removed from service because his pay scale of Rs. 2200-4000 placed him in Grade A However, the court found that the petitioner remained a class B officer and the director general was able to pass the order of removal.
