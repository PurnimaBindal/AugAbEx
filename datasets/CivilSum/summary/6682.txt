Constitution of India, Article 226 - Selection of respondent No. 2 to the Post of Registrar challenged - The selection committee had expert members who were capable of judging the relative suitability of competing candidates.
Therefore, allegations of malafide or bias could not be inferred from the selection of respondent No. 2.
It was held that respondent No. 2 fulfilled the qualifying eligibility prescribed for the said post, and as such, the petition was dismissed.
[Para 9]

Constitution of India, Article 226 - Selection - Post of Registrar - In order to establish malafides or bias, the allegations must be concrete, clear and sufficiently articulated for the court to appreciate them objectively.
The burden of proving this lies very heavily on the person who alleges it.
The allegations of malafide or bias in this case were couched in vague terms and were not considered sufficient to establish malafide or bias.
Thus, the petition was also dismissed on these grounds.
[Paras 10 to 12]

Burden of proof - The burden of establishing malafides or bias lies very heavily on the person who alleges it.

Constitution of India, Article 226 - Administrative instructions - The instructions contained in the Office Memorandum are mere administrative instructions and are not statutory rules.
Therefore, even if there has been any breach of such executive instructions, that does not confer any right on the petitioners to invoke Article 226 of the Constitution for quashing orders in breach of such instructions.
