Section 10 of the Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act, 1970 and Section 46 of the Factories Act, 1947 outline that if canteens are incidental to the establishment's main activity and a constant necessity, they cannot employ contract labor.
These employees must be regular employees of the establishment and entitled to welfare measures like canteen provisions which are considered essential for the establishment.
[Para 18]

Section 10 of the Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act, 1970 prohibits the employment of contract labor in any process, operation, or other work in any establishment.
Any peripheral activities that are essential to the establishment are included under "other work."
To give any other meaning to the expression "process, operation, or other work" would be to restrict its meaning.
[Paras 21 and 22]

Whilst advisory boards have to be consulted, the government is not compelled to follow their advice.
The government may consider other materials and should form their own opinions regarding notification to enforce the relevant provisions of the Act.
[Para 23]

Individual distinctive features of establishments that run canteens do not exempt them from provisions that require regular employees rather than contract labor.
If the government has concluded that it is suitable to abolish it, the individual establishment's circumstances will not be considered.
[Paras 24 and 25]

If a sick industry is experiencing revival, it cannot be treated differently, unlike other industries, solely because it was sick.
