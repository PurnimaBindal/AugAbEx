The High Court does not have the power to peruse the proceedings of a summary court martial and examine the contentions raised by the parties.
Violations of the provisions of the Army Act or the rules can vitiate a summary court martial if gross prejudice visits an accused.
[Para 10]
The defence version that Rule 34(1) of the rules mandates the service of a charge sheet 96 hours prior to the convening of a summary court martial and, therefore, the proceedings stand vitiated.
However, it caused no prejudice to the accused.
[Paras 5 and 12]
The authority that frames the chargesheet has discretion in prosecuting and for what offence.
Therefore, it is not illegal for the accused to be charged with an offence under Section 69 of the Army Act, but have the charge altered to Section 63 by the prosecution.
