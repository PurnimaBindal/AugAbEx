The writ petition filed by registered or recognized unions of Ex-Govt. servants for their welfare will be maintainable under Public Interest Litigation as per Constitution of India, Articles 226.
[Paras 22 & 23]
Retirement cannot be considered the same as in-service civil service, as mentioned in Constitution of India, Articles 226, 14, 16.
Governed by Article 14, equal treatment cannot be expected for unequal classes.
Retired Govt. servants cannot claim equal privileges to those in service.
[Paras 25 to 29]
As per the Constitution of India, Articles 226, 14, 16 & 21, scheme framed by the Union of India for providing medical facilities to Ex-servicemen of Armed Forces can not be challenged on the doctrine of "legitimate expectation" to claim more and better facilities and incentives.
Doctrine can only be invoked by showing some promise left unfulfilled.
[Paras 30 to 32]
Constitution of India, Articles 226 points to policy decisions of Government.
Court cannot issue directions or guidelines to the Govt. to fill in gaps in exercise of its plenary powers, it only can do so in some appropriate cases.
[Para 33]
Ex-servicemen of armed forces must be respected and provided medical services under Constitution of India, Articles 21 and 14.
They are entitled to privileged treatment as they spent their youth in the service of the nation at the cost of their lives.
[Paras 34, 38 & 50]
There is no fundamental right to claim free medical facilities as per the Regulations for the Medical Services of the Armed Forces, 1983, Regulation 296.
The 2002 Scheme for providing medical services to ex-servicemen of armed forces is constitutional and valid according to Constitution of India, Articles 21 and 14.
