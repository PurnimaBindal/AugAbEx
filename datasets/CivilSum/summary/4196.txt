Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 226 - Education/Admission/Entrance Test - An entrance test can be introduced for B.Com admissions in order to evaluate merit on an equal scale - All candidates would have an equal advantage and disadvantage - The mode of evaluating merit has changed, but the selection criteria and categories have remained the same, thus there is no violation of Article 14 - Academicians should consider all factors when making these decisions, and their authority should not be superseded by court opinions.

Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 226 - Estoppel - Education - Admissions - Entrance test - Change in procedure of admissions - Keeping up with the fast-changing education scenario requires changes and advancements in the procedure of admissions - It is up to the academicians to determine the best method of evaluating merit to select the most suitable candidates for admission - Universities are entitled to prescribe reasonable admission procedures and to change or modify them - Prescribing an entrance test does not change the regulations set forth by the Syndicate or the eligibility criteria - The principle of estoppel does not apply in this case.
