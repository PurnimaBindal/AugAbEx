The validity of an order passed under Article 311(2) Second Proviso clause (c) can be reviewed by a court or tribunal to ensure that the satisfaction of the President or Governor was not based on mala fides or irrelevant grounds.
The government is obliged to provide the material basis for the satisfaction, subject to claims of privilege under Sections 123 and 124 of the Evidence Act.
Even with a claim of privilege, the government must disclose the nature of the activities that the government employee is alleged to have engaged in.
[Paras 31 and 33] B Appellants working in sensitive government organizations responsible for gathering intelligence may have their dismissal from service without an inquiry reviewed by a court or tribunal.
The government has a privilege to withhold production of certain documents, however, it must disclose the nature of the activities in which the appellant is alleged to have engaged.
The tribunal has the authority to look into records to ensure that the satisfaction of the President or Governor for dismissal was not based on irrelevant grounds or mala fides.
[Para 36] C. Security of the state is a legitimate concern in a case where the President relies on activities of the appellant which would be harmful to the state.
The satisfaction of the President or Governor was not based on irrelevant or mala fide grounds, so there was no basis for the tribunal to interfere with the orders of dismissal.
A court or tribunal may look into material that requires continued confidentiality in the interest of public interest.
[Paras 37 and 39] D The court cannot inquire into the advice given by ministers to the President or Governor, but the government must produce the material basis for such advice, subject to claims of privilege.
[Para 32] E. A claim of privilege does not extend to the disclosure of the nature of activities, but the tribunal was justified in upholding the claim of privilege for certain documents.
