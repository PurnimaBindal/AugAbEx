Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 10 and 12 - Industrial Dispute - The Act contains a complete mechanism for identifying and adjudicating on industrial disputes - The appropriate government must consider all factors, including the Conciliation Officer's report, to determine if an industrial dispute exists - If the government decides to reject a demand for a reference, reasons must be recorded.
[Paras 12 and 13]
Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 34, 25T and 2(ra) - Unfair Labour Practices - Alternate remedy - The Act provides the only way to address industrial disputes, even if the dispute is based on unfair labour practices - Civil courts do not have jurisdiction in these matters.
[Para 13]
Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 10 and 12 - Function of Conciliation Officer - Statement of dispute - The Conciliation Officer must make every effort to bring about a fair settlement for all parties during conciliation proceedings and then report the outcome to the government - The government can decline to make a reference, but must give reasons.
[Para 12]
Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 34, 25T and 2(ra) - Unfair Labour Practices - Technicalities - Lack of specificity or a correct legal citation in a workman's submission cannot be grounds for dismissal.
