The appointment of a Principal of a recognised College of Education was subject to certain prescribed qualifications, and the Vice-Chancellor approved an appointment after an amendment was made to these qualifications.
However, the appointee was not eligible for the position based on the original qualifications at the time of appointment by the Managing Committee, even though the Vice-Chancellor had yet to give final approval.
The Supreme Court upheld the High Court's ruling that the appointment was invalid, as the appellant did not possess the required qualifications at the time of his appointment.

The term "Master's Degree" in the context of qualifications for a teaching position does not include a Degree of Master of Education.
Instead, candidates were required to have a Master of Arts in any subject, as well as a Degree in Education, which could be either a B.Ed.
or an M.Ed.
This interpretation is based on Constitution of India, Article 16.
