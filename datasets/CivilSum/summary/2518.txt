Constitution of India, Article 226 - Concealment of facts - Pre-mature retirement - Petitioner concealed his past punishments twice and alleged an arbitrary pre-mature retirement on extraneous reasons - A person must disclose all facts relating to his service when approaching the court for relief, for or against - The petitioner was disentitled from relief due to deliberate concealment of facts - Petition liable to be dismissed with assessed costs of Rs. 5,000/-.
[Paras 8 to 11 and 15]

Punjab Civil Services Rules, Vol.I, Part I, Rule 3.26(d) and Vol.II Rule 532A(c) - Pre-mature retirement - Service record - The Screening Committee can consider the entire service record of the employee, with more weightage placed on the last entries - The petitioner received various punishments and adverse entries, making his service record blemished - The order of pre-mature retirement was in public interest due to the petitioner's blemished record.
[Paras 11 and 12]

Pre-mature Retirement/Selection Grade - Punishment - Charge-sheet - The petitioner was issued charge sheets and punishment, and received adverse remarks after the grant of selection grade - Grant of selection grade did not decrease the severity of the punishment.
[Para 13]

Pre-mature Retirement - Promotion - Punishment - Past punishments - Despite being promoted, the petitioner was found guilty of misconduct - The competent authority considered the petitioner's past punishments when deciding he was unfit for service.
[Para 14]

Constitution of India, Article 226 - Concealment of facts - The petitioner concealed facts about his service record and did not disclose his various punishments and adverse remarks - Officials were held responsible for concealing facts, and the State was directed to take action against such officers in the public interest.
