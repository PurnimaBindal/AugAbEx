The 1971 Maharashtra Act, Section 26, Schedule IV, Item I, Clauses (a), (b), (d), (f), (g) and the 1947 Industrial Disputes Act, Section 2(ra), Schedule fifth, Item 5 - Dismissal or termination of employment for a proved misconduct that is disproportionate cannot be considered as unfair labour practice when it is a major misconduct.
Reading all clauses of Item I of Schedule IV of 1971 Maharashtra Act disjunctively would only cover minor and technical misconducts.
Discharge or dismissal for misconduct of technical or minor nature would amount to disproportionate punishment.
[Para 8]
In case of victimization for a misconduct not amounting to major misconduct, dismissal or discharge from service that is shockingly disproportionate will be considered as victimization.
Dismissal of respondents for alleged misconduct of sleeping on duty was held to be grossly and shockingly disproportionate.
[Paras 9 and 10]
Upon finding that dismissal from service is grossly disproportionate to the proved misconduct, the Labour Court can order reinstatement with some back wages.
However, an award of 100% back wages is not warranted in such cases.
Back wages in a specific case are reduced from 50 & 60% to 40 & 50%, while reinstatement is upheld.
