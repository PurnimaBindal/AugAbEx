Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 311 - Departmental proceedings - Natural justice - In order to deny a document, it must not be legally available to the delinquent - Denying the documents on the ground that the delinquent had earlier wanted to question the illegal appointments, was not a sound legal reason - But since the delinquent himself dropped the plea, the denial did not cause any harm to him.

Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 311 - Departmental proceedings - Natural justice - In departmental enquiries, there is no need to strictly follow the rules of procedure - Substantial compliance is sufficient.
[Para 47] C. Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 311 - Departmental proceedings - Natural justice - Burden of proof - A delinquent who takes a specific defence of having personal knowledge must prove it themselves - They cannot later plead that it should have been proven by the prosecution - This amounts to shifting the burden of proof.
[Para 48] D. Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 16 - Public appointments - Appointments must be made in accordance with Article 14, even in the absence of specific rules or administrative instructions - Judicial courts do not condone nepotism - Appointments made without notice to the public at large violate the mandate of Article 14.
[Para 49] E. Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 311 - Departmental proceedings - Quantum of punishment - Levelling allegations against the judicial system of approving illegal appointments without proof adds gravity to the misconduct and may affect the punishment given.
