The petitioner obtained voluntary retirement from SAIL with the condition that he could not work for any other government organization or undertaking.
However, he joined the National Institute of Secondary Steel Technology without disclosing the condition of his retirement.
Although he received a tenure appointment which was extended beyond the age of 62 years, according to the age limit set by the Board of Governors of the establishment, he could not get an extension beyond the age of 60 years.
The competent authority ordered the petitioner's release from the establishment by curtailing his tenure as he had already crossed the age of 62 years.
The court held that the petitioner had no vested right to continue in the establishment's service and that there was no flaw in the order curtailing his tenure of service.
[Paras 19 and 25]

A tenure appointment is a term during which an office is held, and it is a condition of holding the office.
Once a person is appointed to a tenure post, his or her appointment to the office commences when he or she joins it, and it ends on the completion of the tenure unless curtailed on justifiable grounds.
Such a person does not attain superannuation; he or she only goes out of the office upon completion of his or her tenure.
The question of prematurely retiring him or her does not arise.
[Para 22]
