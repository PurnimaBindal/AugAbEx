Section 22(3)(f) of the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985 and Order 47, Rule 1 of the Civil Procedure Code, 1908 - Review can only be based on grounds enumerated in Order 47, Rule 1 and cannot be used to correct an erroneous decision.
"Error apparent on face of record" refers to errors that are self-evident and can be corrected through a simple process.
A decision cannot be reviewed on the basis of subsequent decisions.
Only material available at the time of initial decision can be considered during a review.
[Paras 11 and 15]
The power of the Tribunal to review its decision is similar to that of a civil court under Section 114 read with Order 47 Rule 1 of CPC.
The Tribunal can only review its decision on grounds specified in Order 47 Rule 1 and not otherwise.
The expression "any other sufficient reason" must be read in light of other specified grounds.
Errors that require a long process of reasoning are not considered errors apparent on the face of record.
Erroneous decisions cannot be corrected through reviews.
Decisions cannot be reviewed on the basis of subsequent decisions.
The Tribunal's adjudication must rely on material available at the time of initial decision.
Mere discovery of new or important matter or evidence is not sufficient grounds for review.
The party seeking review must show that such matter or evidence was not within its knowledge and could not be produced earlier.

Under Section 23(3)(f) of the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985, review of an order can only be done on the grounds specified in Order 47, Rule 1 of CPC.
A bald assertion of stagnation is not sufficient grounds for the review of an earlier decision.
Reliance on subsequent government orders or decisions is not proper grounds for review either.
Only material available at the time of the initial decision can be considered during reviews.
