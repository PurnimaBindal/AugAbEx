Constitution of India, Article 16, 136 - Indian Police Service (Appointment by Promotion) Rules, 1955 - Appellant was appointed as Assistant Commandant in Orissa Military Police after his release from Army as per an advertisement.
However, he was deemed ineligible for promotion to IPS as only Deputy Superintendents of Police in the Orissa Police Force were eligible.
The Tribunal allowed his application on the grounds that the post of Deputy Superintendent of Police and Assistant Commandant constituted a single cadre.
However, on review, the Tribunal deemed that the two cadres of Assistant Commandants of Orissa Military Police and Deputy Superintendent of Police were separate cadres from inception and Assistant Commandants were not eligible for promotion to the IPS.
The power to review cannot be exercised if an appeal is preferred, and since the appellant's SLP was declined, the judgment and order appealed against became final and binding.
Even persons who were not parties in the case cannot get the order reviewed once the appeal has been dismissed.
The only remedy for a person who wants to challenge the judgment is to file a separate application before the Tribunal in their name and persuade the Tribunal either to refer the question to a larger bench or file an appeal from the Tribunal's judgment and have Tribunal's judgment set aside in appeal.
The order of the Tribunal in review applications was overturned.
