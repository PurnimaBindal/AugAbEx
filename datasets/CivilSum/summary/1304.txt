Constitution of India, Article 226 - Army Act, 1950, Section 122 - General Court Martial - Convicted petitioner contests that trial could not have started under Army Act after the expiration of the 3-year time limit under Section 122.
Petitioner's objection was rejected after appreciating the evidence raised before the General Court Martial, and this court cannot re-appreciate the evidence, especially when there is no case of no evidence or wrong appreciation of the law.
[Para 7]

Constitution of India, Article 226 - Army Act, 1950, Section 128 - General Court Martial Proceedings - Petitioner asserts that commanding officer could not be the convening authority due to Para 449(b) of Army Regulations.
The court held that there was no infringement of Rule 22 or 23 of Army Rules as the petitioner was under the control of the commanding officer of the unit he was attached to after attachment of petitioner.
[Para 11]

Constitution of India, Article 226 - Army Act, 1950, Section 130 - General Court Martial Proceedings - Petitioner contends presiding officer was aware of the facts and was biased.
The court held that the presiding officer does not fall under category of Rule 39(1)(c) and there was no objection that was raised under Section 130 of Army Act, thus there is no question of bias.
[Para 15]

Constitution of India, Article 226 - General Court Martial - Judicial Review - Scope of - Limited judicial review allowed to see whether the procedure laid down was followed and General Court Martial was convened.
When findings of General Court Martial are based on the appreciation of evidence, they cannot be questioned.
[Para 17]

Constitution of India, Article 226 - Army Act, 1950, Section 62 - General Court Martial - Brief reasoning - Validity of - Petitioner argues that the finding is void as no reason has been given in support of that conclusion.
Held that the provision in Rule 62 requires brief reasoning, which is sufficient to support findings.
