The court cannot interpret qualifications prescribed by Selection Committee of academicians, and the principle that decisions for academic bodies and educational institutions should be respected by the courts applies to academic matters such as admissions, examinations, courses of studies, syllabus, and discipline.
Matters regarding appointments, promotions, seniority, and other service conditions are not subject to judicial review in the context of the doctrine of equality enshrined in Articles 14 and 16.
[Paras 9 and 10] B.
To be eligible for appointment as a Lecturer in Religion, one must possess a Master's degree in the relevant subject with at least 55% marks.
The term "in the relevant subject" means in the particular subject in which appointment is sought.
A person possessing an MA in History is not necessarily eligible for appointment as a Lecturer in Religion merely because one of the papers in the syllabus of MA in History relates to religion.
