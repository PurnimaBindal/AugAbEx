Civil Procedure Code, 1908, Section 20(c) - Territorial jurisdiction of High Court - Cause of action - The Court where part of the cause of action arises has jurisdiction to entertain the writ petition if the cause of action had arisen in more than one Court.
It was held that the definition of 'cause of action' for the purpose of Article 226(2) of the Constitution of India must be assigned the same meaning as envisaged under Section 20(c) of the Code of Civil Procedure.
[Paras 6 and 7] B.
Constitution of India, Articles 226 and 226(2) - Civil Procedure Code, 1908, Section 20(c) - Cause of action - The respondent, an employee of Appellant No. 1, had filed a writ petition before Calcutta High Court after the service was terminated at Mugma.
The entire cause of action arose in Mugma area within the State of Jharkhand, but because the Head Office of the appellant-company was situated in the State of West Bengal and had nothing to do with the order of punishment passed against the respondent, the Calcutta High Court did not have jurisdiction.
