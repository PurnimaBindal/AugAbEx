Article 226 of the Constitution of India pertains to the post of Assistant Store Keeper and the cancellation of appointments.
In cases where it is not possible for the employer to differentiate between the guilty and the innocent, en masse action is upheld to maintain the integrity of the selection process.
However, in cases where the employer can identify the innocent, the cancellation of the entire process is regarded as arbitrary and unfair.
[Paras 18 and 19]

Following the cancellation of appointments for the post of Assistant Store Keeper, it was held that adherence to the rule of natural justice is essential unless such adherence is deemed inexpedient, especially in cases related to public interest, national security or in cases of a sensitive nature.
In this case, none of the stated exceptions warranted the absence of a hearing, and as such, the impugned orders were considered to be arbitrary.
[Para 20]

Petitioners had been appointed as probationers in the post of Assistant Store Keeper, but their appointments were subsequently cancelled on allegations of deceiving the authorities during the recruitment process.
It was argued that since they were probationers, they had no right to the said post.
However, it was held that this stand was taken only to justify the case of an individual and did not warrant en masse reversal of appointments.
If an order is based on misconduct, even if it is innocuous on the face of it, the court can lift the veil and discern its true nature.
If such an order is found to be punitive and not preceded by an inquiry, it cannot sustain under the rules.
[Para 20]
