The Minimum Wages Act of 1948, specifically Sections 3, 4, and 5, states that the minimum rates of wages must be fixed and revised periodically.
A group of petitioners challenged a notification, claiming that the revised rates did not show the base, month, and were applied retrospectively without considering their objections.
However, because no representation was received from the petitioners within the stipulated period, the advisory board took a decision to revise the rates of wages.
The court held that the Act is intended to protect workers' interests and that the state can provide for it otherwise and make it operational from any other date.
The writ was ultimately dismissed.
