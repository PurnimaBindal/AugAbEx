Sections 4(1)(4) and 35(3) of the Bihar State University Act, 1976, state that the absorption of teaching and non-teaching staff of affiliated colleges taken over as constituent colleges is the exclusive jurisdiction of universities and not the government.
[Para 26]
Regarding the absorption of existing teaching and non-teaching staff of affiliated colleges taken over as constituent colleges, teachers appointed against posts for which there were no recommendations sent by the universities and for which recommendations were sent after the cut-off date have no claim for consideration for absorption.
Additionally, the constraints in Section 35, which require approval for creation and appointment of posts in affiliated colleges, do not apply to staff absorption.
Sections 35(3) and 57A(2)(c) have no retrospective application.
[Paras 28, 29, 47, 49 to 52]
Section 4(1)(4) and 35 of the Bihar State University Act, 1976, deal with different areas of jurisdiction for the universities and states regarding appointments and absorption of existing teaching and non-teaching staff of affiliated colleges taken over as constituent colleges, and there is no conflict between them.

Non-obstante clauses in statutes do not always repeal or supersede other provisions of the law, but rather remove obstructions that might arise.
[Paras 41, 42, 44 and 45]
Orders of the State Government, even if not formally expressed in the name of the Governor, have binding effects on subsequent governments.
A change in an elected government does not excuse dishonouring decisions made by previous elected governments.
