Constitution of India, Articles 12 and 226 - State/Cooperative Societies - A cooperative society's registration under the Cooperative Societies Act doesn't automatically make it an instrumentality or agency of the Government - The key test is whether it functions as an instrumentality or agent of the Government rather than how it's established - Cooperative societies, as registered under the Cooperative Societies Act, cannot be considered authorities within the meaning of Article 12 of the Constitution and therefore not subject to writ jurisdiction.
[Paras 4 to 8] B.
Constitution of India, Article 226 - Suspension - An employee of a cooperative society lacks the direct right to approach the High Court against a suspension order - Writ jurisdiction is not applicable, and the writ petition can't be entertained.
