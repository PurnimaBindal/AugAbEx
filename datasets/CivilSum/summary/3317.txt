Remedy for challenging election results may be available under Section 13-B of the Punjab Gram Panchayat Act, 1952.
However, High Court may interfere based on the specific circumstances of a case.
The availability of an alternative remedy is not the only criterion.
It must also be adequate and efficacious.
This is supported by Article 226 of the Constitution of India.
[Para 8]

Writ petitions can be filed with respect to local bodies like Gram Panchayats, Municipal Committees, and District Boards.
Article 329(b) of the Constitution indicates the manner for challenging elections held for State Assemblies or Parliament.
As there is no constitutional bar for writ petitions for local body elections, Article 226 has widespread scope to provide justice in such cases.
[Para 8]

Any member of the Gram Panchayat can challenge an election to the office of Sarpanch or Panch, even if they were not a candidate in the election.
Therefore, the petitioners who contested for the office of Sarpanch were entitled to challenge the election for both the offices of Sarpanch and Panch.
