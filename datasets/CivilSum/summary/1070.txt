According to Section 2(s) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, a workman who has been appointed as the leader of a workers' team, and who has been given certain responsibilities such as issuing gate passes or recommending leaves, does not have the power to grant or sanction leave, or take disciplinary action against any workman under him.
Such a workman has not been appointed to a supervisory capacity, and is not depriving himself of the protection afforded under the ID Act.
[Paras 11, 13 to 18]

Article 226 of the Constitution of India and Section 10 of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 provide writ jurisdiction for interference in findings of fact recorded by Labour Court.
If the Labour Court's findings are arbitrary and without reasonable basis, resulting in grave injustice, a writ court should not refuse to interfere with the wrong findings of fact.
[Para 19]
