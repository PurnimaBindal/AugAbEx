Article 226 of the Constitution of India deals with departmental enquiries, and a writ against the order of the Disciplinary Authority can only be issued if there is a violation of principles of natural justice or statutory regulations for the enquiry.
Additionally, if the decision of the authority is influenced by extraneous considerations or if the conclusion is arbitrary, it is possible to interfere in the proceedings under Article 226.
If the findings of the inquiry officer are based on legal evidence, then reliability and adequacy of that evidence cannot be questioned in the High Court under writ jurisdiction.
[Para 16] B.
When there is a disciplinary enquiry, it is not mandatory for the disciplinary authority to challenge the decision of the inquiry officer.
The disciplinary authority is not an appellate or revisional authority over the inquiry officer's report.
The findings of the Inquiry Officer are only his opinion based on the materials before him, and not binding on the disciplinary authority as the punishing authority.
The disciplinary authority can come to its own conclusion.
[Paras 19, 20 & 21] C. As per Article 235 of the Constitution of India, every High Court has a constitutional duty to watch over the subordinate judiciary, and protect honest judicial officers from ill-conceived or motivated complaints.
However, the High Court cannot ignore any dishonest deed by a member of subordinate judiciary and must ensure that such instances are dealt with properly.
Any attempt by the High Court to condone or compromise with an officer's dishonest behaviour would contribute to the erosion of the judicial foundation.
Judicial service is not just employment, and judges are not merely employees as they exercise judicial power.
