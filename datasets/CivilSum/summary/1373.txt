Industrial Disputes Act, Sections 10, 11-A, 2-A and 12 - Reference - The introduction of Sections 11-A and 2A in the Industrial Disputes Act does not take away the appropriate government's jurisdiction to consider the prima facie existence of the dispute and effect of the reference on industrial peace and harmony if the dispute is referred to the tribunal.
Section 2-A declares an individual dispute to be an industrial dispute, enlarging the scope of Section 2-K but not amending the definition of 'industrial dispute.'
Section 11-A enlarges the scope of adjudication of disputes on facts and evidence by the tribunal and reduces or modifies the quantum of punishment if found harsh or disproportionate, but it has no effect on the government's power under Section 10 unless it is amended to consider the necessity of making reference.
Partap Singh v. State of Haryana 1995(2) SCT 739 and Ramphal v. State of Haryana 1995(1) RSJ 826 are overruled to that extent.

Industrial Disputes Act, Sections 10 and 12(5) - Reference - The government has to pass a speaking order and communicate the reasons to the parties if it does not make a reference, but it cannot delve into the merits of the dispute on disputed facts.
