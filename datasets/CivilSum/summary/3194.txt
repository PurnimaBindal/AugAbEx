Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 18, 29 and 10 - Settlement - A settlement resulting from a Conciliation proceeding under the Act has a broader scope than one under sub-section (1) of Section 18 - The term 'Report' in sub-section (3) of Section 12 takes its meaning from the context in which it is used - Detailed reports may not be necessary when there is a settlement - Lack of a report U/s 12(3) will not invalidate the settlement - A settlement reached during Conciliation proceedings is binding not only on the parties to the dispute, but also on the employer's heirs, successors, and assigns, as well as all present or future employees in the establishment - These specific provisions deviate from the standard law of contracts and implement the principle of collective bargaining - Individuals who have received benefits under the settlement, even if they were not parties to it, are bound by it - It is irrelevant whether the settlement falls under Section 18(3) or (1) as long as the existence of the settlement is not disputed.
