Workmen's Compensation Act, 1928, Sections 20 and 30 - Disposal of appeal - High Court did not apply its mind - The appeal raised a substantial question of law - The High Court's dismissed the appeal summarily without giving any reasons - This order is non-reasoned - The Order of the High Court has been set aside - The matter has been remitted for fresh consideration.
[Para 11] B Administration of Justice - Reasoning - Disposal of appeal - The High Court was required to give reasons, even brief ones, in its order - The principles explained in this regard include that (i) reasons are fundamental to good administration, (ii) failure to give reasons amounts to denial of justice, (iii) reasons connect the decision-taker's mind to the controversy in question, (iv) the right to reasons is essential to a sound judicial system, (v) affected parties have a right to know why a decision went against them, (vi) spelling out reasons is a salutary requirement of natural justice, (vii) withholding reasons is incongruous with a judicial or quasi-judicial performance, and (viii) reasons introduce clarity in an order.
