According to the MP Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Rules, 1963, Standing Order No. 12(1)(a), and Madhya Pradesh Rajya Sadak Pariwahan Sewa (Bina Ticket Yatra Ki Rok) Adhiniyam, 1974, Section 16, a conviction in a court of law alone is not enough to warrant dismissal.
The deceased petitioner was fired after being caught without a ticket, but there is no indication that he was guilty of moral turpitude.
Consequently, the order of dismissal is invalid, and the petitioner's legal heirs are entitled to back wages and all other associated benefits.

The concept of moral turpitude is a legal term that refers to crimes that significantly violate community standards.
It is not to be taken lightly, as determining whether or not an offense entails moral turpitude is a fact-specific inquiry.
