Correction of Date of Birth - The appellant's claim for the correction of his date of birth was rejected due to his delay in filing the claim after 32 years of service in the Electric Department.
His service book received his date of birth from his Matriculation Certificate, which states that he is 58 years old with a birth date of 931936 instead of the claimed 991941.
No reliable transcription or translation of his horoscope has been provided to support his assertion on his birth date.
The official date of birth recorded in his service book would determine his date of superannuation, regardless of his current age.
Thus, the trial court lawfully upheld the impugned order.
[Paras 13-22]
Correction of Date of Birth - The apex court suggested that Courts and Tribunals must not be quick in granting any interim relief for continuation in service after a claim for the correction of a public servant's date of birth has been made, given that many public servants have taken advantage of the system to extend their loyal stay in the service even beyond the date of their retirement by questioning their service book entries.
Although rejected, they often end up enjoying an undeserved extended service.
Therefore, courts and tribunals must be careful to examine the prima facie evidence produced before granting any interim relief.
[Paras 19-20]
Correction of Date of Birth - Plaintiff/Appellant was tasked with producing irrefutable evidence to establish that his reported date of birth in his service book was incorrect.
He bore the burden of proof to show that record was invalid and that his actual birth date was 991941.
