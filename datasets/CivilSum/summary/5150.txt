In the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 10(1), an order of cancellation during the pending adjudication of a dispute is not maintainable.
The appropriate government doesn't have the power to cancel an earlier order of reference, as there is no provision in the Act giving even an implied power for them to do so.
Therefore, an order that is contrary to provisions of the Act is without jurisdiction.
[Paras 9 and 12]
An order of cancellation of a reference without giving an opportunity of hearing to the parties involved is illegal, bad in law, and not maintainable as it violates the principles of natural justice.
This rule applies in the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 10(1).
The disputes are pending adjudication before the Labor Court.
