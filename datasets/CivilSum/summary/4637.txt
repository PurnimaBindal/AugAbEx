Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 16 - Bias/Selection - The relationship between a candidate and selection committee members is important - Proving bias is difficult since it is a state of mind - It is not necessary to prove whether someone was actually biased for or against - A reasonable ground for believing that someone was likely to be biased is enough - If bias is alleged on the grounds of a close relationship, it should be shown to be very close and near which could create an impression that the authority will favor its favorite candidate and vitiate the selection.
[Paras 9 and 11]

Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 16 - Bias/Selection/Relationship of teacher and student - Challenge to the selection cannot be based solely on the fact that an expert member co-opted happened to be the teacher of a selected candidate - No allegation of close friendship or intimacy between the two, creating an impression of bias in favor of the candidate - No inference that the teacher would be biased in favor of one and against the others can be drawn based solely on their relationship as a teacher and student.
[Paras 12 and 13]

Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 16 - Bias/Selection/Selection Commission - The Selection Commission is entrusted with the task of making selections on the basis of merit - Experts are co-opted to ensure a fair assessment and evaluation of candidate performance, without influence on the selection based on their liking or disliking - Bias cannot be alleged against an expert co-opted by the Commission unless the expert is proved to be in a dominating position in the matter of selection.
[Para 14]

Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 226 - Selection/Selection Commission - The task of making selections is given to expert bodies, which are expected to discharge their duties fairly - Courts have no jurisdiction to encroach upon their field unless their decisions are blatantly perverse and tainted with extraneous and mala fide considerations.
[Para 15]
