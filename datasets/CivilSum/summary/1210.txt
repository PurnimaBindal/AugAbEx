According to the Industrial Dispute Act, 1947 and Section 106 of the Evidence Act 1872, a workman must prove that he was not gainfully employed in order to receive back wages.
The granting of back wages is not automatic.
[Para 16]
The Constitution of India, 1950, Article 226 indicates that a writ court cannot interfere with findings of fact in an industrial dispute.
However, if the Labour Court ignores material evidence, the High Court may interfere.
[Para 15]
Under the Industrial Dispute Act, 1947, Sections 2A and 25F, the determination of whether a workman was forced to resign or voluntarily resigned must be made.
In a specific case, the workman alleged that he was forced to resign and the record showed that he did resign, but then raised a demand under Section 2A.
The sequence of events suggests that he was forced to resign and not that he voluntarily resigned, and thus the provision of Section 25F of the Act was not complied with.
The petitioner was reinstated with 25% of the back wages as a result and the writ was allowed.
