According to the Constitution of India, Articles 226 and 30, teachers of private schools are entitled to equal pay for equal work.
However, the pay scales recommended by State Pay Commissions cannot be imposed upon private school managements in the absence of any legal mandate.
The Bengal Act does not have a Section 10 similar to the Delhi School Education Act.
If private schools are required to pay their teachers wages that are equal to what government school teachers receive, it would put a burden on the children and their parents through higher fees.
A teacher's job is not merely a means of obtaining a livelihood.
Such professionals should not be focused only on obtaining the same pay scales as others.
Instead, they play a critical role in promoting a social cause.

The Constitution of India, Articles 226, 14, and 16 specify that private educational institutions cannot be compelled by a court to adhere to a mandated pay scale, except when a public law element is involved.
If only a private law remedy is accessible, there is no basis for a Writ Petition.
