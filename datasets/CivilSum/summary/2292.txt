The Punjab Civil Services (Punishment and Appeal) Rules, 1970, Rule 5(i) to (iv) and Articles 226 and 227 of the Constitution of India state that reasons must be recorded when rejecting an appeal in order to ensure adherence to the rule of law, prevent external considerations, and reduce arbitrary decisions.
A court is unable to perform its judicial review duty effectively in the absence of a clear and reasoned order.
The order is not in compliance with the principles of natural justice and is therefore unlawful.
[Paras 10, 11, 13]

According to Rule 19(2) of the Punjab Civil Services (Punishment and Appeal) Rules, 1970, when deciding on an appeal against a punishment order, the appellate authority must consider the procedural aspects of the inquiry, the merits of the findings made by the punishing authority, and the severity of the punishment.
If the appellate authority fails to follow these rules, the order is invalid and contravenes Rule 19(2) of the Rules.
[Paras 7, 9]
