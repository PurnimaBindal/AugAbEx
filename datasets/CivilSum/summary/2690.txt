Sections 3 and 10 of the Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923 were applicable to a case where a workman died of a heart attack during work.
The employee, who was working in a Tea Estate and performing tasks such as trimming, plucking tea leaves, and carrying them on his head, was required to climb hilly terrain.
Evidence presented to the Labour Commissioner proved that the workload was the cause of the death.
The Commissioner managed to conclude that there was a direct connection between the nature of work and the death, and awarded compensation within the purview of "arising out of and in the course of employment" as contained in Section 3 of the Act.
The order of the Commissioner was upheld.
