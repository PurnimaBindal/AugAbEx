Punjab Police Rules, 1934, Rule 16.2 - Dismissal from service - Gravity of misconduct - Repeated absence from duty - Police constable - The disciplinary authority determined that the petitioner's habitual absence from duty, despite previous warnings and punishment, constituted the gravest kind of misconduct and that he was incorrigible - It was not a single instance of absence, so Parkash Chand's case did not apply - In his 7-year career, the petitioner did not demonstrate the potential to be a good police officer, and his record was mixed - As a member of a disciplined force, he was expected to be more accountable - The verdict of the competent authority was not unreasonable or arbitrary.

Punjab Police Rules, 1934, Rule 16.2 - Pension - Dismissal from service - Since the petitioner had served just 7 years in the police and had not fulfilled the qualifying service requirement for a pension, the punishing authority was not obliged to consider the petitioner's length of service for pension when he had no right to pension.
