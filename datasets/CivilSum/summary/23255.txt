All the paramilitary forces, including Assam Rifles, must be at par with each other in the principle of "equal pay for equal work" under Constitution of India Articles 14, 16 and 39(4).
The order denying the equal pay claim was found to be arbitrary, irrational, and set aside.
More specifically, the case involved a respondent, Havildar (Radio Mechanic) Rifles in the Assam, who was re-designated as a warrant officer after being recommended by the Ministry of Home Affairs.
The respondent claimed equal pay scale as admissible to their counterparts in the CRPF and BSF.
The administrative authorities were not permitted to perpetuate apparent discriminatory differentiation in the pay scales because of disparity in pre-revised and revised scales of the personnel of Assam Rifles prior to the recommendations of the fourth pay commission, irrespective of the identity of their powers, duties and responsibilities with other paramilitary forces.
The absence of any plea on the part of the Govt. that Radio Mechanics in other paramilitary forces were performing different or more generous duties than those in Assam Rifles rendered the impugned decision of the Govt. clearly irrational and arbitrary and thus, violative of Article 14 of the constitution.
Consequently, the high court's judgment allowing the equal pay scale as claimed by respondents was upheld (Paras 18 to 21).
