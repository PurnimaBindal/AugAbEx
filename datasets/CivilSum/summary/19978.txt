The Lt.
Governor of Delhi has the authority to divide the territory of Delhi under his administration into nine civil districts under the Punjab Courts Act, 1918, as extended to the National Capital Territory of Delhi, Section 19(1), according to government notifications that divide Delhi into nine Civil Districts and is considered valid.
The notification does not fall within Entity 11A of the concerned list of the constitution of India.
It has the dominance over any legislation made by the Delhi Legislative Assembly on the subject.
The Delhi Legislative Assembly cannot invalidate the policy decision made by the government, which is not unreasonable, arbitrary and beyond the legislative power of the state, and which divides Delhi into nine judicial districts after consultation with the Lt.
Governor and the Delhi High Court under the Punjab Courts Act, 1918 (Punjab Act 6 of 1918) as extended to the National Capital Territory of Delhi, Section 19(1).
The Delhi Legislative Assembly has the power to make laws with respect to the administration of justice, implying the power of defining, altering, amending, and diminishing the jurisdiction of the Courts.
