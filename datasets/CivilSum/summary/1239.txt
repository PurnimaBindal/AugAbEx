The Shastri Indo Canadian Institute, despite receiving grants from the Central Government and having some of its nominees on the Board of Directors, does not fall within the definition of State under Article 12 of the Constitution.
This is due to the fact that the Institute was incorporated in Canada, has its Head Quarters in that country, and is subject to Canadian laws.
Additionally, there is no evidence to indicate that the Central Government or any of its agencies have control over the Institute.

Although Article 226 of the Constitution allows for writ jurisdiction over any person or body performing a public duty, including those not classified as statutory authorities or instrumentalities of the State, the post held by the petitioner in the Shastri Indo Canadian Institute was not a public post.
His function was related to the work of the Institute, which was partially funded by the Government of India.
As the Institute is incorporated in Canada and has its Head Quarters in that country, it is not subject to jurisdiction under Article 226 of the Constitution of India.
