When it comes to entrance examinations, it is impermissible to require a minimum of 50% marks in a bachelor degree as a condition to even take the exam.
Requiring 55% marks as an eligibility condition is equally problematic, regardless of whether it is the University or someone else making the requirement.
This condition violates articles 14 and 21.
[Para 4]
All bachelor degree holders must be treated equally for the entrance examination of the LL.B course.
Merit for the 1st year course will be determined based solely on marks obtained in the entrance test.
No condition of pre-requisite percentage of marks can be imposed for the post-graduate LL.B course.
[Para 4]
The purpose of the entrance test is to select candidates purely on the basis of merit.
A candidate's performance in the entrance test will determine their eligibility for a seat in the course.
[Para 5]
In order to determine whether constitutional rights are being violated, the High Court has the jurisdiction to judge the actions of Universities and their committees.
This falls under the purview of Constitution of India articles 226/227.
