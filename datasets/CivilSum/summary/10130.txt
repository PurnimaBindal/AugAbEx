The Chartered Accountants Act (XXXVIII of 1949) and the Indian Constitution's Articles 14, 19(1)(g), 19(6) are violated by notifications that specify how many cases or audits Chartered Accountants can accept and what fees they can charge.
These restrictions are considered unreasonable under Article 19(6) and infringe upon the right to carry on one's professional work under Article 19(1)(g) and Article 14 of the Constitution Accepting a large number of cases and charging fees cannot be regarded as professional misconduct, and Chartered Accountants are not comparable to business enterprises since they have their own historical conventions and traditions developed over centuries.
Additionally, the meaning of professional misconduct needs clarification.
