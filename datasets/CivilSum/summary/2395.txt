Air Force Act, 1950, Section 19 - Air Force Rules, 1969, Rule 16 - Defence Service Regulation for Air force 1964 Regulation 578(g) - Gross Misconduct - Compulsory Retirement - Petitioner, a permanent commissioned officer in Air Force charged with multiple offences, including contracting plural marriage and meting out criminal torture to his legal wife - Petitioner compulsorily retired from service after Court of Enquiry - The petitioner filed a civil suit for annulment of his first marriage - Contention of the petitioner that entire proceeding of Court of Enquiry is a violation of Regulation 578(g) as during the pendency of civil suit filed by him, Court of Enquiry ought to be suspended and commanding officer incompetent to initiate any administrative action against the petitioner till disposal of civil suit - It was held that the suit filed by petitioner for annulment of marriage does not fall within the ambit of regulation 578(g) - Initiation of Court of Enquiry and continuation thereof during the pendency of the suit are not violative of sub-clause (g) of Regulation 578 as the authorities had sufficient material to presume that the petitioner contracted plural marriage on the date of initiation of Court of Enquiry - Authorities justified in initiating department action.

Air Force Act, 1950, Section 19 - Air Force Rules, 1969, Rules 16 - Gross misconduct - Compulsory Retirement - Petitioner, a permanent commissioned officer in Air Force charged with multiple offences, including contracting plural marriage and meting out criminal torture to his legal wife - Petitioner compulsorily retired from service after Court of Enquiry - The petitioner contended that Court of Enquiry initiated on the basis of a complaint made by his wife had been withdrawn- Held, the mere withdrawal of complaint does not legally prohibit the Authorities from initiating administrative action for misconduct - Department under provisions of Air Force Act and Rules fully justified in initiating Court of Enquiry to find out whether acts, behaviour, conduct of petitioner rendered his retention in Air Force undesirable, even though complaint withdrawn by complainant - Complaint withdrawn by complainant only under heavy pressure - Departmental action was legal and justified.
Petition dismissed.

Constitution of India, Article 226 - Gross misconduct - Compulsory retirement - Mala fides- Petitioner challenged the order of compulsory retirement on the ground of mala fides - Petitioner had filed petitions against frequent transfers, and succeeded in getting the orders either cancelled or modified - He further contended that he had been victimized for making a complaint against the Vice Air Chief - Held, there was no reason to hold impugned order mala fide, no material placed on record to show that Vice Air Marshall had any personal grudge against petitioner - Impugned order neither mala fide nor passed in colourable exercise of power by Authority - Petition dismissed with costs.
