Constitution of India, Articles 245, 246 read with Lists to Seventh Schedule - The legislative powers of States and Union of India are not always clear cut and there may be overlapping between legislative subjects in Lists I, II and of the Seventh Schedule - Power to legislate on residuary subjects is conferred upon Union Parliament, which also has the power of taxation under Article 248.

Constitution of India, Articles 245, 246 read with Lists to Seventh Schedule - When examining the constitutionality of an Act concerning competing legislative competence of Parliament and State Legislature, the doctrine of "pith and substance" is used.
[Para 14]

Constitution of India, Articles 245, 246 read with Lists to Seventh Schedule - In case of conflict between the legislative powers of the State and the Parliament, the law made by Parliament on the Central List or Concurrent List prevails to the extent of repugnancy - The law made by the State Legislature on matters in the Concurrent List prevails if reserved for and assented to by the President - Both can prevail in their fields if there is no conflict - State Government has the exclusive power to enact legislation for regulating industrial relations and resolving labour disputes within its territory.
[Paras 15 & 16 to 20]

Constitution of India, Articles 245, 246 read with Entries 22, 23, 24 and 38 List III to Seventh Schedule - The Madhya Pradesh Industrial Relations Act, 1960 and the Madhya Pradesh Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1961 were enacted to provide service conditions for industrial employees in the State - The State Legislature can legislate on its local and incidental matters under Entries 22, 23 and 24 of List III, and the Parliament cannot override such specific State legislation - Provisions of Article 254 apply only when legislations of both the Centre and the State cover the same ground and there is a direct conflict - The 1961 Act is a special law and Board regulations under Section 79(c) of the Electricity Supply Act, 1948 are general provisions - The 1961 Act applies unless excluded by an appropriate notification under Section 2(2) thereof.

Constitution of India, Articles 245, 246 read with Lists to Seventh Schedule - Special laws made by States within their competence and jurisdiction will prevail over general laws made earlier by Parliament on the same subject.

Constitution of India, Articles 309 & 148 - The Madhya Pradesh Industrial Relations Act, 1960, Sections 33 and 99 - Fundamental Rules 1973, Rule 14A - Retirement age - When an agreement does not prescribe retirement age less than 58 years, the terms of the agreement/settlement on the subject will apply until its expiry - Once the agreement expires, the 1960 Act applies automatically from that date - Alteration in retirement age by the employer is a matter of policy - Board regulations under Section 79(c) of the 1948 Act prevail over certified standing orders or rules made under the 1961 Act if there is a conflict.
