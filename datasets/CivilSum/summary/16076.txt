Articles 14, 16, and 226 of the Constitution of India deal with reservation and admissions.
If someone applies for admission in a reserved category, they cannot later challenge the constitutionality of that reservation for that category after already taking advantage of it.
[Paras 6 and 7]

Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India address fraudulent activities related to reservation and admissions.
If a student gets admission in an educational institution through fraudulent means, they have no right to stay in the institution.
Their admission, degree, or diploma can be canceled at any time the fraud comes to the notice of the authorities.
[Para 8]

Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India also cover natural justice, admissions, and cancellation of admission.
If a student has a domicile certificate issued by the competent authority after due verification through the department concerned, the authority cannot revoke it suo motu without notice or without giving the student an opportunity to explain.
The university is also obligated to allow the student to respond before canceling their admission based on revocation of the domicile certificate issued by the same authority.
The student has the right to challenge the authority's decision on the basis that it was created without adequate verification and is false.
[Paras 10 and 11]
