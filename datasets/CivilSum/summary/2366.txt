The Punjab Civil Services (Punishment and Appeal) Rules, 1970, Rule 9(4) before and after the amendment of 911985, Constitution of India Articles 14 and 311(2), and natural justice dictate that reasons must be given for reaching different conclusions and findings as required by Rule 5.
The requirement for recording reasons is essential for both equality before the law and principles of natural justice so that the delinquent can make an effective representation/appeal, and the orders being in the form of quasi-judicial can be subject to judicial review by the courts.
Despite dispensing with the duty and obligation of supplying a copy of the enquiry report by amending Rule 9(4) after the 42nd amendment in the Constitution, there is no dispensing with the requirement of a punishing authority to record good and sufficient reasons for disagreement with the enquiry report and for imposing major penalties.
The Court can effectively adjudicate upon such executive orders only if the reasons are indicated.
