It has been held that an Annual Confidential Report (ACR) of an employee cannot be withheld from scrutiny of court on the ground that it is a privileged document which would relate to affairs of state.
The disclosure of an ACR would not be detrimental to public interest, even though it may affect promotion or may provide for public censure.
Furthermore, an ACR cannot be protected from disclosure merely because it has been supplied in confidence.
Openness in government is now the basis principle and widely accepted.
It cannot be called such a document which would relate to affairs of state but is essentially a private right.
Where an objection is raised under Section 123 of the Evidence Act as disclosure of a document, the Court will have to balance two competing aspects- injury to the interest of the state on its disclosure and injustice to the party requiring it in case of non-disclosure thereof.
Even the class immunity was not held to be sacrosanct or inviolable in all circumstances.
The information on ACRs is not protected from disclosure merely because it has been supplied in confidence.
Therefore, no public interest immunity can be extended to and claimed in respect of annual confidential reports sought by the Govt. servant concerned itself, not by any third person.
