Constitution of India, Articles 226 - Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 10(1) - Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act, 1970, Section 10 - Court Jurisdiction - Disputes regarding contract workers' employment are governed by the 1970 Act and fall under the exclusive domain of the government - The court will not ordinarily intervene in such a matter - The government must take into account relevant material before exercising jurisdiction under Section 10 of the ID Act - The government must first establish the prima facie case of the relationship between workmen and the employer before invoking Section 10 of the ID Act - The court cannot direct the government to make a reference merely because the management has a right to raise the issue before the adjudicator.

Constitution of India, Article 226 - Evidence Act, Section 58 - Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 18(3) and 10(1) - Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act, 1970, Section 10 - Judicial Admissions - The dispute whether contract workers are employees of the principal employer is already determined in a collective bargain and is binding on the parties - Such admissions are also binding in industrial adjudication and cannot be pleading contradictory to it.
