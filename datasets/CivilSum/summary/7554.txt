The "appropriate Government" to make a reference under Section 10(1) of the Industrial Disputes Act will be the one in whose territory the substantial cause of action arose.
Principles under the Civil Procedure Code can be borrowed to determine the substantial cause of action for industrial disputes as well.
[Paras 1, 8, 12, 13, 16 and 17]

The substantial cause of action for an industrial dispute with an establishment spread over various States to trace Appropriate Government will be the bundle of facts that led to the resultant final action, not just the place of work of the workman at the time of termination.
The Appropriate Government with territorial jurisdiction to make a reference under Section 10(1) will be the one where the substantial cause of action arose.
In this particular case, the Government of Chandigarh Administration will be the Appropriate Government.
[Para 13]

The Union Territory Administration is considered as the Central Government for matters under Section 10(1) of the Industrial Disputes Act, and will be the Appropriate State Government within the meaning of Section 2(a)(ii) for any disputes arising within its territorial jurisdiction, regardless of whether the dispute relates to a State Government establishment or its workers.
[Para 14]

The termination or retrenchment order becomes final once it is dispatched, and its service at a different place will not constitute a cause of action at that place.
