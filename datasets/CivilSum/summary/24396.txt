When an enquiry was not conducted and no finding of guilt was reached, and an employer did not wish to retain an employee who was the subject of complaints, the motive may be the only basis for the order, and it would not be bad.
Based on the factual situation and legal principles background, the High Court was not right in interfering with the termination order.
Therefore, a contractual appointee, who was terminated due to misconduct, did not have their order of termination deemed invalid as the employer did not want to continue and there was no proof of guilt or an investigation conducted.
