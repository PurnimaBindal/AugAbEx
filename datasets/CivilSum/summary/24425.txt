Under the Constitution of India, Article 163, the State has the power to issue policy instructions to regulate a particular field in the absence of any opposing statutory provisions or regulations.
Instructions must be issued by the State, and not by an official, to ensure they are in line with the Constitutional Scheme or any other legislature.
If policy or instructions are unconstitutional, the State may modify or re-frame them.

The concept of regularisation presupposes irregular appointment and enables employers to regularize appointments.
Regularisation must occur before a legislative act or statutory regulation, in line with provisos in Article 309.

The doctrine of eminent domain comprises of two parts - the acquisition of property in the public interest and payment of reasonable compensation.
Actions to deprive citizens of property can only take place through legal force, not administrative action alone.
Any action must take place under Article 300-A rather than 162.

Bihar and Chhattisgarh States must provide adequate schools and educational institutions for both genders.
Bihar is failing to promote education in the State, with policies and actions far beyond the ground realities.
The Supreme Court directed the States to improve their efforts, form a committee, provide staff and regular recruitment, and file reports and cases.
All educational institutions must file reports and claims, and a decision must be made within four months.
The State must also take action against those who may be found responsible for commission of irregularities and/or illegalities in the process of implementation of the Government scheme in accordance with the law.
All teaching and non-teaching staff must be a minimum of 18 years old, with educational qualification chosen by the State.
