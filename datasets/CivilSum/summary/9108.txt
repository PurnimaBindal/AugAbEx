Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 33C(2) - Resignation - Since the workman's resignation became effective, the relationship of master and servant ended, and the Labour Court had no jurisdiction to entertain a petition under Section 33C(2) of the Act.
[Para 8]

Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 33C(2) - Monetary benefits - A Labour Court is not permitted to proceed on the basis that a relationship between a master and servant still exists and cannot make a declaration of such in proceedings under Section 33C(2) of the Act.
Such proceedings only execute pre-existing rights of the workman and cannot creat new rights, and any disputes arising should be decided on a reference under Section 10 of the Act.
[Para 8]

Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 33C(2) - Scope - Jurisdiction - In these proceedings, a Labour Court only executes the pre-existing rights of the workman and cannot create new rights in their favor.
The question of whether a specific right exists must be decided by the Labour Court itself in such proceedings.
An industrial dispute should be handled through a reference under Section 10 of the Act, and the Labour Court does not have the jurisdiction to give a declaration in such proceedings that a particular relationship still exists.
