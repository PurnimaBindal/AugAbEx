Constitution of India, Article 226 - Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 10 and 11-A - A workman was caught playing cards during working hours and confessed before the enquiry officer.
The charge was held to be proved on the basis of his confession, and the employer imposed a penalty of dismissal from service, though the punishment was held to be disproportionate.
The employer did not challenge the award on the ground of fairness of enquiry, and therefore, the respondent cannot be allowed to challenge the findings.
[Para 5] B Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 11-A - In cases of proven misconduct, the Labour Court is under a duty to examine the punishment awarded by the employer and can interfere with it if it is unduly harsh or highly disproportionate to the misconduct.

Constitution of India, Articles 226 and 227 - Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 10 and 11-A - The jurisdiction of the High Court to interfere with the award of the Labour Court is very limited and can only be done if the award is without jurisdiction, contrary to the principles of natural justice or suffers from an error of law apparent on the face of it.
