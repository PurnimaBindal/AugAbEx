The Food Corporation of India was found in breach of the Payment of Bonus Act, 1965, for not providing ex-gratia payment in lieu of bonus on attendance allowance to their employees, with the corporation claiming attendance allowance did not form part of salary or wages.
The tribunal found that attendance allowance was part of wages, thus ex-gratia had to be paid, and this decision was upheld by the court.
The attendance allowance was included as compensation for no work being provided for four days a month, and it was found that for all purposes, attendance allowance was accepted as part of wages.
Despite the management's argument, attendance allowance could be considered part of wages to meet ex-gratia payment demands for the appropriate compensation for the breach.
