The petitioner's claim for allotment of Government accommodation cannot be converted into a public interest litigation as no relief has been claimed for other government servants.
The judicial process cannot be used for individual satisfaction.
The Government Residences Allotment Rules, 1972 need to be harmoniously interpreted.
Rule 26 provides for relaxation in specific circumstances, and the power of relaxation is only given to the Chief Commissioner.
Therefore, every order of allotment under Rule 26 cannot be considered unfair and arbitrary.
When few houses are available and many persons want allotment, equal distribution or treatment is not possible.
Hence the principle of equality enunciated in AIR 1980 SC 1992 is not applicable.
An employee of one state cannot question the allotment of Government houses to servants of another state in their quota based on seniority or priority out of turn as all three states have their own quota in Government houses.
Allottees who have allegedly been allotted Government accommodation without following the conditions provided in General Rules cannot be quashed if not made party to the petition, and no observations can be made against them in their absence.
