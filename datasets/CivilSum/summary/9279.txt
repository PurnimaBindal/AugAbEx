According to Constitution of India, Article 19(1)(a), freedom of speech and assembly does not permit exercising these rights wherever and whenever one wants.
Therefore, individuals cannot exercise this right by holding meetings and shouting slogans on premises legally occupied by others.
B Industrial Disputes Act, Section 34 states that the jurisdiction of the civil court is not barred in civil matters.
C. The Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985, Section 19 does not explicitly or implicitly bar the jurisdiction of civil court to deal with matters arising from demonstrations and dharnas by employees.
D. A fresh cause of action can arise if a workmen's union gives a fresh demand notice with the same threats of dharna and demonstrations.
E. Although holding demonstrations is a part of the right to freedom of speech and expression that must be preserved, the court must intervene in situations where the interest of the society at large is violated.
Fundamental freedoms under Article 19 of the Constitution of India are not absolute, and restrictions on them are clearly stated in the Constitution.
No one can exercise their right to the detriment of someone else's right.
F. According to the Constitution of India, Article 19(1)(9), using abusive language during a demonstration against others is outside the reach of Article 19(1)(9) and beyond its protection.
