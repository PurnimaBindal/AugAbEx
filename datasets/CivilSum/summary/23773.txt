Constitution of India, Articles 234, 233, 235 - Industrial Disputes Act, Section 7 - Industrial Disputes (Maharashtra Amendment) Act, 1974, Section 7 - Maharastra Recognition of Trade Unions and Prevention of Unfair Labour Practices Act, 1971, Section 6 - Labour Court Judge - Amendments in the Maharashtra Acts have reduced the eligibility conditions for appointment of Labour Court Judges.
Assistant Labour Commissioners are now also eligible for appointment - The Labour Court is considered a court, as it performs judicial functions similar to those of civil courts, adjudicating upon disputes that would be within the jurisdiction of civil courts, yet cannot grant the relief contemplated under the Labour Acts.
As a court, Labour Courts are subordinate to the High Courts, and their presiding officers must be from the judicial service.
Article 234 of the Constitution governs the recruitment of Labour Court Judges.
