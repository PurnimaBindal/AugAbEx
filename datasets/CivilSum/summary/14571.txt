Punjab Forest Service Class II Rules, 1944, Rule 13 states that seniority is determined from the date of substantive appointment against permanent vacancies, and since the petitioner and respondents No. 4 to 8 were initially appointed against temporary vacancies, promotion is only considered when permanent vacancies are available.
The petitioner was not found suitable for confirmation twice and was appointed against a permanent vacancy eight years after respondents No4 to 8.
Therefore, the petitioner cannot claim discrimination in seniority or promotion for the Punjab Forest Service Class II.
[Paras 4 and 7]
Once it is established that respondents 4 to 8 were rightfully treated as senior to the petitioner, their promotions are not illegal.
[Para 9]
The petitioner's delay of over ten years in filing a writ petition calculated from the date of last order of confirmation issued in favor of one of his alleged juniors and an unexplained delay of four years after rejection of his representation/appeal by Govt. is enough to dismiss his prayer to challenge the prior confirmation of his alleged juniors.
[Para 6]
The unexplained delay of over a decade in filing a petition by the petitioner, and the fact that respondents No. 4 to 8 have already received the benefit of seniority and promotion for over a decade is sufficient to deny relief under Article 226.
