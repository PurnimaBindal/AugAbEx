Under Section 60 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882 and Article 61(a) of the Limitation Act, 1963, a mortgage deed may not include a provision that restricts the possibility of redeeming the property, otherwise known as a clog on equity of redemption.
If a mortgage contains such a clause, a lawsuit may be filed to declare it oppressive.
The limitation period for such a claim does not begin until the court has declared the clause a clog on equity, despite arguments over whether the clause was void or voidable.
In a case filed more than thirty years after the mortgage's execution, the suit was not time-barred.
