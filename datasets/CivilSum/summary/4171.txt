The petitioner was removed from service for alleged assault on his superior, but in actuality, no action was taken by him.
The mere attempt of misconduct, only on one occasion, for unknown reasons, is not a sufficient reason for removal from service.
The period of unemployment faced by him from 1974 till 1996 serves as a deterrent punishment, and at 54 years of age, he is nearing retirement.
The order of removal has been set aside, and the respondents are directed to reinstate the petitioner without back wages but with continuity of service and to provide him with other suitable post if he is not suitable for the duty of driver.
[Paras 4 and 6]

While considering the gravity of misconduct, the Labour Court failed to exercise the power vested in it under section 11-A of the Industrial Disputes Act.
The High Court can exercise its powers under Section 11-A and provide appropriate relief to the delinquent employee if the Labour Court fails to do so.
In this case, the section was not exercised, and the employee was removed from service for attempting to assault his superior with a stone.
[Para 5]
