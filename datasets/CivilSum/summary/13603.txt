The Disability Pension is governed by the Defence Instructions and is provided in the Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 16.
Rule 7(b) states that disability should be attributable to or aggravated by Military Service.
In this case, since there was no recorded note at the time of acceptance for military service nor medical opinion recorded when the disability was detected prior to discharge, the disease cannot be said to not be attributable to military service.
Furthermore, Darshan Singh's case differs in terms of facts.

Disability pension is provided in Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 16.
According to the Defence Instructions Governing the Disability Pension, Rule 7(b), the disability pension is to be paid to an individual for a disease suffered during the period when he stood enrolled in military service.
Whether or not the disease was suffered while the individual was on duty is irrelevant.
