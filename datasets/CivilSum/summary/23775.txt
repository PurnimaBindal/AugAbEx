The Constitution of India's Articles 14 and 16, and Clause 17(g) from the Certified Standing Orders, specifies the automatic termination rule for permanent employees who overstay on their leave without permission for more than seven days.
Even though conferment of permanent status guarantees security of service, any employment by the government or any instrumentality or statutory corporation or authority cannot terminate permanent employees arbitrarily without a hearing.
Even if a stipulation to that effect exists in the service contract or service rules, it would be bad if it doesn't provide an opportunity to the employee whose services are automatically terminated to present his/her explanation.
[Paras 14, 19 and 25]

Regardless of any stipulation in the contract or service rules of automatic termination due to unauthorized absence or overstaying on leave, the employee subject to this action must be given a chance to explain his/her position.
If the employee's service is treated as having come to an end automatically, it would be against the principles of natural justice, Article 14, and Article 16 of the Constitution.
[Paras 20 and 21]

A wrong concession on a question of law made by a counsel is not binding on his/her client.
[Para 23]

Section 2(oo) (bb) of the Industrial Disputes Act of 1947 states that the clause on retrenchment does not apply when a permanent employee's services end, as per stipulation in the appointment letter or laid down in the rules.
This clause applies only when the original term of the contract expires without a renewal or to employees whose services are terminated before the expiry of the term.
