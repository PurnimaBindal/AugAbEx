The Haryana Act 2002 provides for retrospectivity of the statute.
The State has the power to make law retrospective, however, any such statutes should not violate the provisions of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
The Act takes away vested and accrued rights in an arbitrary manner, except for to a limited extent.
The first proviso to Section 4(3) of the Act is harsh, excessive, arbitrary and violative of Article 14 of the Constitution.
[Paras 36, 43, 59, 60, 61, and 43]

The Legislature can make a judgment ineffective by amending the law on which the judgment was based upon, but by giving retrospectivity to such law, it cannot take away the benefits of a writ of mandamus which has become final.
The Haryana Act, 2002 does not amount to usurpation of judicial powers by the Legislature nor it is ultra vires; it has only removed the basis of decisions in Hooda and Sandeep Singh's cases.
[Paras 34, 37 to 40, 44, 50, 56, 58 & 64]

Appointments made to the posts in excess to those advertised and requisitioned to the Selection Commission is not permissible and violates the principle of equality enshrined in Articles 14 and 16.
It deprives those who become eligible to compete for future vacancies.
The State can deviate from the advertisement and make appointments on posts falling vacant after advertisement only in exceptional circumstances or emergent situations and only by taking a policy decision.
[Para 57]
