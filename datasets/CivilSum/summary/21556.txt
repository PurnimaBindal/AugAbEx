Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16 and 141 - Reservation - Seniority - Reservation in promotions - The implementation of the Ajit Singh II judgment does not require any clarification - It cannot be asked for clarification on the ground of difficulty in its implementation - The seniority rules do not permit seniority to be counted from date of initial promotion, and should govern normal promotions - Seniority review of some promotions must be carried out based on The Supreme Court ruling in Ajit Singh II and RK Sabharwal.
- The roster promotions were meant for the representation of backward classes only and, therefore, the rules should not be interpreted to confer undue seniority.
Giving seniority rights to the roster point promotees who have not gone through the normal channels, where basic seniority or selection process is involved, would be ultra vires of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
Any treatment of unequals equally or unequals as equals will be violative of the basic structure of the Constitution of India.
Promotions to the level of Superintending Engineer and Chief Engineer, or equivalent posts/higher, must be reviewed according to the Ajit Singh II ruling, and any promotion contrary to Sabharwal must not be reverted.
Retirees/pensions will not be affected, but the retired general candidates becoming entitled to promotions on that basis would be entitled to notional promotions from the due dates and their retiral benefits shall be refixed in the grades of promotion post - However, they will not be entitled to arrears of salary on that basis.
