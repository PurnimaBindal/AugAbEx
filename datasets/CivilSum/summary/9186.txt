The Constitution of India, Articles 309, 311, and 14 does not mandate the advertisement of temporary appointments and does not give rise to any presumption that the appointment would be treated as permanent.
The Constitution of India, Article 14 deems temporary appointments as stop-gap arrangements that do not vest the employee with any rights against permanent posts.
The Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 309 stipulate that once an appointment letter clearly states that the appointment is temporary, the employee is estopped from challenging the letter using presumptions.
The Constitution of India, Articles 309 and 311 direct that the director is the competent authority to pass a termination order from the post and not the board of Directors.
The Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 309 explain that an appointment made in the lower pay scale does not necessarily lead to the automatic appointment to a higher post.
The Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 16 require that a public authority must take fair decisions without any arbitrariness.
An employee can form an expectation that there will be non-arbitrariness in the decisions taken by public authority.
The Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 16 govern temporary appointments under the Central Civil Services (Temporary Service) Rules, 1965 Rule 11.
An employee cannot claim regularization, especially when no post is available.
The Constitution of India, Article 226 empowers a court to issue directions to appointment authorities to consider appointments when a petitioner has given an undertaking that they will be appointed first as and when the post is made available and filled up.
