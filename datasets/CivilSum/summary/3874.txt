The Labour Court denied the claim of the petitioner under Sec 25F and 10 of the Industrial Dispute Act, stating that he failed to produce the attendance register to prove that he worked for 240 days during any year.
However, the workman can discharge his primary onus by making an oath statement, and it is the management's responsibility to prove the workman's non-compliance with the 240-day requirement.
The Labour Court's adverse inference against the workman for not producing the record was not accurate.
So, the case was remanded for a new decision.

B The High Court has the authority to remand a case under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India if it finds that the Labour Court's adverse inference was incorrect.
In this case, the workman had discharged his primary onus, and Court’s decision was based on wrong premise of the fact.
Therefore, the Court remanded the matter back to the Labour Court for fresh decision.
