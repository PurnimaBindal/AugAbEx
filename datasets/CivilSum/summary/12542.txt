Article 311(2) of the Constitution of India states that punishment of 'Censure' is cumulative with an effective stoppage of one increment and a show cause notice must adhere to natural justice.
A charge was made against the petitioner, alleging negligence in performance of duty leading to unwanted individuals entering Police Complex.
However, the Enquiry Officer exonerated the petitioner, concluding that the charges were not proven.
The Punishing Authority did not provide any reasons for not accepting the findings of the Enquiry Officer and did not inform the petitioner while issuing a show-cause notice.
This violates rules of fair play and principles of natural justice, rendering it unsustainable.
[Para 8]

Article 311(2)(b) of the Constitution of India upholds natural justice and mentions the Appellate Order.
The Appellate Authority reduced the punishment from the stoppage of increment to censure while also ordering that the petitioner would not receive anything more for the period of suspension as they were not fully exonerated.
This implies that the petitioner can only receive the subsistence allowance already paid to them and not full wages.
However, before passing this order, no opportunity was given to the petitioner.
Thus, the Appellate Order cannot be upheld.
[Paras 9 and 10]
