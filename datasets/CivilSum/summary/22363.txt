Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 311 - Criminal and departmental proceedings - Acquittal in Criminal proceedings - Departmental proceedings remain maintainable even after acquittal in criminal proceedings, as proof required in a criminal case differs from that required in a departmental disciplinary proceeding - Criminal charges require proof beyond reasonable doubt whereas departmental proceedings require a mere preponderance of probabilities - In addition, punishment can still be given in departmental proceedings after an acquittal.
[Paras 9 & 10]
Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 311 - Departmental proceedings - Supply of documents relied upon - Failure to supply documents for departmental proceedings violates the principles of natural justice and prejudices the defence - However, this alone is not grounds to quash the proceedings - Instead, the parties should be sent back to the stage where the illegality or irregularity occurred and then proceed in compliance with the law.
[Paras 11 to 14, 17]
Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 311 - Departmental proceedings - Alternate mode of proof for documents - The production of original documents is not always necessary as the disciplinary authority or inquiry officer are not courts and therefore are not strict in following court procedures - Alternate forms of proof may be used.
[Para 15]
Constitution of India, Articles 14, 226 and 311 - Perversity of finding - If a finding of fact was reached without any material or in a way that no reasonable person instructed in the law would have come to that determination, it is considered perverse - In such cases, the Writ Court may take action.
