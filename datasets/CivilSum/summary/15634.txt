Constitution of India, Article 226 - Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 - Transfer - Wages - The contention of workmen that they should receive wages for an intervening period as the respondent-management had not set up any factory at the place of transfer has been refuted by the management's counter-affidavit, which states that wages for the intervening period were not being given to the workmen.
The Industrial Tribunal erroneously made an observation that the workmen were being paid wages.
It was necessary for the tribunal to determine whether the workmen were not reporting for duty or if management was not allowing them to join duty, before deciding whether the workmen were entitled to wages for the intervening period.
The impugned award is set aside, and the matter is remanded to Industrial Tribunal to decide this question.
