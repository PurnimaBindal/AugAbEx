Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923, Section 3-A - Employment - Burden of proof - The authority must draw a legitimate inference based on appreciation of evidence when determining if a workman was in employment - While agricultural workers are not given statutory appointments with appointment letters, oral evidence can be used unless properly rebutted - Deceased workman was earlier employed by the farmer, and there is no record of when he ceased to be employed - Oral statements in favor of claimants must be believed - An incidentally occurring snake bite resulting in death while the deceased was employed entitles his dependents to compensation.
[Paras 7 to 10]

Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923, Section 2(d)(i) - Dependents - Mother - Widowed mother - Compensation to dependents is entitled to the widowed mother of the deceased workman only if she was dependent on the deceased's income - If both parents are alive and working as labourers, it cannot be assumed that the mother was dependent on the deceased's income, and thus she is not entitled to compensation.
