Article 311 of the Constitution of India pertains to the principles of natural justice and departmental enquiry.
In cases where the disciplinary authority does not agree with the favourable findings of the Enquiry Officer in favour of the accused and decides to impose punishment without notice, no rule or regulation requires for a fresh opportunity to be provided.
The disciplinary authority has the power to reach their own conclusions based on the enquiry file and is not required to serve notice on the accused.
[Para 6]
Article 311 of the Constitution of India also provides for the right to appeal in cases where a penalty has been imposed by the disciplinary authority.
In cases where the appellate authority agrees with the penalty of removal from service imposed by the disciplinary authority, they are not required to pass a speaking order.
However, in situations where the appellate authority disagrees with the disciplinary authority, they must provide a detailed justification for their decision.
This shows that the appellate authority has applied their mind to the evidence presented.
[Para 8]
According to an interpretation of Article 311 of the Constitution of India, before the Supreme Court laid down the law in Md Ramzan Khan's case, an order of punishment passed without supplying a copy of the enquiry report is considered valid.
