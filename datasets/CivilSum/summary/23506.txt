The merit promotion scheme of the UGC and the University statutes must be considered alongside Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India, Sections 6, 35(o), 49, and 63 of the Madhya Pradesh Vishwavidyalaya Adhiniyam, and various other legal provisions related to inter se seniority between direct recruits and promotees to determine whether merit promotee Professors and Readers fall into the same class as directly recruited Professors and Readers.
However, these two classes of employees have distinct characteristics and are unequals not only in terms of the source of their appointment but also in the nature and character of their appointment and the posts they hold.
As such, they cannot be treated equally in all respects, particularly in terms of seniority and promotion.
The Madhya Pradesh High Court was correct in concluding that inter se seniority of merit promotee and directly recruited Readers and Professors should not be fixed using a continuous officiation yardstick, as this would violate the mandates of Articles 14 and 16(1).
Similarly, it is not possible to treat a merit promotee Reader or Professor in the same way as a directly recruited Reader or Professor in terms of seniority.
Under these circumstances, a separate seniority list of merit promotee Readers and Professors must be created based on continuous officiation within the same class and acted upon for all purposes except seniority and promotion to the posts available to those in the cadre.
