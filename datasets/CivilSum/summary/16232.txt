The Food Corporations Act (37 of 1964), Section 45 - Food Corporation of India Regulations, 1971, Regulations 70 and 74 require the Board to review an order.
However, a period of limitation is not provided within the Regulations.
It is not acceptable to use the period provided for an appeal as the timeframe for a review.
An order can only be reviewed within a reasonable timeframe, based on the facts and circumstances of each case.
In this case, the petitioner was promoted after being exonerated from the charges, and the CBI's prayer for review had already been declined.
Therefore, the review order passed beyond a reasonable time and cannot be sustained.
[Paras 14 and 16]

The Food Corporations Act (37 of 1964), Section 45 - Food Corporation of India Regulations, 1971, Regulation 14 deal with exoneration, review, and natural justice.
In this case, the employee was exonerated of charges and given further promotions.
However, the Chairman set aside the order of exoneration without giving the delinquent any opportunity of hearing, violating the principles of natural justice.
As a result, the order is void and not sustainable.
[Para 17]

The Food Corporations Act (37 of 1964), Section 45 - Food Corporation of India Regulations, 1971, Regulation 74 requires the competent authority to pass a review of an order.
In this case, the petitioner was working as a Deputy Manager until he was exonerated, after which he was working as a Joint Manager (General) when the order of review was passed.
Despite his promotion, the Chairman still had the authority to pass the review order.
[Para 18]

The Constitution of India, Article 226 - Food Corporations Act (37 of 1964), Section 45 - Food Corporation of India Regulations, 1971, Regulation 74 states that the power of review given under statutory rules is a quasi-judicial function that can only be exercised by the competent authority prescribed.
However, in this case, the order of review was passed at the instance of the Minister, making it illegal.
