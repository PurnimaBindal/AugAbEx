The petitioner, who had initially been granted the family planning incentive increment as he satisfied the criteria of having two children and his wife undergoing a tubectomy operation, was later not entitled to the incentive increment after his wife passed away and he contracted a second marriage and had a child from it.
The discontinuation of the incentive payment was lawful as it was clear that the petitioner could not claim the increment as a matter of right de hors the scheme.
It was held that the payment made to the petitioner was in breach of the scheme and was unauthorised.
The court further affirmed that there was nothing wrong in recovering the wrongful gain made by petitioner and dismissed the petition.
