Constitution of India, Article 226 - Promotion - Recruitment and Promotion Rules - Seniority-cum-Merit -Petitioner, who was initially appointed as a Peon, was later promoted and regularized as a Junior Computer Operator, and then re-designated as a Data Entry Operator.
He contends that he is entitled to promotion to the next level on the principle of seniority-cum-merit.
However, the court holds that an employee cannot claim automatic promotion simply by invoking the seniority-cum-merit principle.
In cases where the employee is found unfit to perform the duties of a higher post, he may be passed over.
The rule cannot be stretched to mean automatic promotion of a senior employee.
[Paras 6, 7, 8 and 10]

Constitution of India, Article 226 - Promotion - Seniority-cum-Merit - The petitioner questions whether the average entry earned by him over four years, following a very good entry for one year, can be viewed as a drastic variation indicating an adverse character.
The court holds that, in the normal course, an authority is required to record the reasons for giving steep downgrading remarks, to enable the employee to improve their performance.
As an average entry is not an adverse entry, it is not a case of drastic variation from top to bottom, therefore it does not require communication.
[Para 9]
