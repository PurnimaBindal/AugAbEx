Indian Evidence Act, 1872, Section 92 - Proviso (4) - Scope - Section 92 proviso 4 applies only to cases where there is a subsequent oral agreement that rescinds or modifies any contract or agreement in writing.
The agreement to pay back wages is incompatible with the express terms of the settlements and subsequent re-employment.
The workman's own submission acknowledges that the alleged oral agreement was not subsequent to the settlements or terms of re-employment but rather prior to the signing of the Agreement.
As a result, the bar contained in Section 92 of the Act is applicable and no evidence may be admitted to establish any such prior oral agreement.

Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 2(p) and 19(1) - Settlements reached by parties are binding in accordance with Sections 2(p) and 19(1) of the Act.
Once parties have reached a settlement, this fact is presented to the Labour Court.
The parties' wishes must be respected, and Labour Courts cannot inquire into any matter covered by the settlement unless there is a valid reason to do so.
To do otherwise would be to undermine the settlement that the parties have already established and prolong the industrial dispute.
Such an approach would be contrary to the Act's very goal of promoting industrial peace.
