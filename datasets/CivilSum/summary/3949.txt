Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923, Section 30 - Scope - A workman died while on duty and the post-mortem report indicated that the cause of death was chemical poisoning.
The sub-inspector of police informed the Revenue Divisional Officer that the deceased was receiving psychiatric treatment, which he was refusing.
The Deputy Commissioner failed to explain how the workman consumed the poisonous substance while performing his duties, and the case was sent back to him for reconsideration of evidence and appropriate decision.
[Paras 10, 13 and 15]

Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923, Section 4 - Evidence Act 1872, Sections 101, 102 and 103 - Burden of proof - A workman died while on duty after consuming poisonous substance.
Once it is established that cause of death was due to consumption of poison and it is shown that such consumption is not part of official duty of deceased, burden is on claimants to establish that death was only due to and in course of official duty.
[Para 9]
