The Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16 and 39 and 226, guarantee equal pay for equal work.
The doctrine of equal pay for equal work is embodied in Article 39(d) and is an integral part of the 'equality before law' contained in Articles 14 and 16.
Executive authorities have a mandatory duty to implement these provisions of the Constitution.
However, after 44 years of adopting the Constitution, the executive has failed to protect the equality rights of Indian citizens.
Despite a common seniority list and total identity between Classical, Vernacular, and Sanskrit Teachers, the Education Department of Haryana pays them different pay scales.
The petitioners pleaded and proved prima facie discrimination, thus discharging the preliminary burden of proof.
It was up to the Department to prove that classification among the groups of employees was reasonable and beyond arbitrariness and hostile discrimination, but they failed to do so.
The petition therefore directed the Department to pay equal pay to Classical, Vernacular, and Sanskrit Teachers.
