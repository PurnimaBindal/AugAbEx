According to Constitution of India, Article 229(2), the Chief Justice of a High Court has the power to determine the pay scales of its employees and consider the merger and equivalent of posts.
The State Government has control over this process but the High Court has absolute control over its staff and the government must accept the suggestions of these higher dignitaries.
If there is a difference of opinion, the government must base their reasons on sufficient and cogent reasons and recommend consultation and discussion.
[Paras 8, 9, 32 to 36, 42, 43, 44, 45 to 49]

Under Constitution of India Articles 226 and 229(2), the High Court has the power to issue a writ of mandamus to prescribe pay scales for its employees.
The writ must be issued with caution and only in cases where legal or fundamental rights and obligations have been breached.
If the competent authority hasn't had the chance to consider and pass an appropriate order, the writ court must not issue a mandamus and should allow the authority to fulfill its obligations first.
[Paras 10, 11, 27, 30, and 45 to 49]
