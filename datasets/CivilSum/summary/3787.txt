Constitution of India, Article 14 - Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 36 - Industrial Dispute - Representation of parties - An application by the President of the respondent union, who is also an advocate, is filed without the respondent-union's consent to the petitioner-employer being represented by a lawyer before the Industrial Tribunal.
As per Section 36 of the I.D.
Act, the petitioner-employer is not entitled to legal representation without consent from the opposite party.
The principles of natural justice and Article 14 of the Constitution of India remain intact, and the contention that both parties should be permitted to have legal representation is invalid.
