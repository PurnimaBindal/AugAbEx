The Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 10 and the rules of natural justice require the enquiry officer to act in a quasi judicial manner and observe natural justice - This includes recording the statement of the workman and allowing for the presentation of evidence - The cryptic and incomplete enquiry report presented to the Labour Court cannot be taken as proof of a fair and proper enquiry - The Court has quashed the interim award as the enquiry was not conducted properly.
[Paras 8, 9 and 10]

The Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16 and 226 provide jurisdiction over the fairness and propriety of enquiries - The award must have proper evidence to support the findings - If the management does not lead any evidence to show a fair and proper enquiry, there is no evidence to deny back wages to the workman - In this case, the enquiry was not conducted properly and there was no opportunity for the workman to present evidence, so the Court quashed the interim award.
[Para 10]
