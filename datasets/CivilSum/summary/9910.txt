Proceedings of enquiry cannot be quashed by the Court under Articles 226 and 227 of the Constitution of India unless it is shown that the violation of rules of procedure or the principles of natural justice has seriously prejudiced the delinquent employee.
[Para 7]
Before the commencement of the enquiry, the petitioner received three notices and did not appear, necessitating ex parte proceedings.
As a result, the petitioner is to blame for his situation.
Therefore, the proceedings of enquiry cannot be held as vitiated due to an ex parte proceeding.
[Para 9]
Even though the SP did not act in strict compliance with the provisions of Rule 1624 of the Punjab Police Rules, 1934, and the principles of natural justice, and considered past punishments without giving proper intimation to the petitioner, no prejudice can be said to have been suffered by the petitioner on that account to vitiate the proceedings of enquiry or the order of punishment.
[Paras 8 and 10]
A person serving in a disciplined force like the police force is expected to be regular in their duties; therefore, dismissal cannot be said to be harsh punishment where there is a case of habitual absenteeism.
