Constitution of India, Article 226 - Delay and laches - Removal from service - If an employee challenges a termination order within 3 years in a civil suit, the writ petition can still be filed after 2 and a half years without being dismissed on the grounds of delay.
The courts do not have a rigid formula for delay and laches, and can exercise discretion if necessary.
[Para 5] B Limitation Act, Articles 113 - Removal from service - Civil suit - An employee has 3 years to file a civil suit challenging their dismissal from service.
[Para 5] C. Constitution of India, Articles 12, 14,16 and 21 - State - University - Contract of service - A university is considered a 'State' and is therefore not permitted to determine employee conditions of service based solely on the terms of a contract.
The university must follow principles outlined in Articles 14, 16(1), 21 and 23 of the Constitution of India.
[Para 5] D. Constitution of India, Articles 14, 21 and 23 - Termination of service - Natural justice - Automatic termination - Rules providing for automatic termination from public service are not in accordance with public law and principles of natural justice.
In cases where a request for leave extension has been denied and the employee has failed to report for duty on time, the provision for automatic termination cannot be applied as such.
The petitioner has failed to complete their studies abroad within the period of sanctioned leave, resulting in automatic termination.
The court held the termination to be arbitrary and directed the university to treat the petitioner as continuing in service with all legal benefits.
