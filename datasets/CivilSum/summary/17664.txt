Article 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India govern seniority and direct recruitment.
In the absence of specific rules, the seniority of direct recruits is determined by merit list prepared by the selecting agency, not from the date of joining.
[Paras 6, 10 and 11].
Article 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India guide instructions on seniority, and uniform instructions applicable to all regarding seniority fixation cannot be considered unfair or arbitrary.
[Para 11].
Article 226 of the Constitution of India pertains to delay and latches in cases related to seniority.
Direct recruits cannot know their place if neither merit list nor seniority list is circulated.
Therefore, writ petition filed when junior was allowed to supersede cannot be dismissed on the ground of delay.
[Para 8].
Articles 14, 116 and 309 of the Constitution of India relate to the amendment of recruitment rules and eligibility.
Vacancies that accrued before the implementation of new rules must be filled according to the earlier prevailing procedure and rules.
[Para 15].
Article 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India pertain to seniority and joining promotions.
A petitioner who joined earlier in time cannot claim seniority and promotion ahead of or over the respondent who remained senior throughout the period.
