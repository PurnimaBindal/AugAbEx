The power of the High Court under Article 226 of the Constitution of India is not limited to enforcing fundamental rights, as it can also enforce legal rights by issuing writs.
In adjudicating matters, the writ court can take into account undisputed documents and the rights of a party.
If an employer has a policy of absorbing badli workers, and the petitioner is eligible under that policy, the writ court can grant relief.

If a case is remanded to a single judge by a Letters Patent Bench, the judge is bound to consider only the issues on which the case is remanded.
In addition, if the plea of an alternate remedy was not raised earlier, it is deemed to be rejected and cannot be raised in an appeal filed subsequently against the order of the single judge after remand.
[Para 11] C. A badli worker under Section 25-C of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, is a substitute or casual employee.
However, if a badli worker is employed against a regular vacancy for more than 240 days, they are considered a workman.
If an employer has a policy of granting status to badli workers for absorption against a vacancy, the court can enforce the policy and grant relief to the worker.
