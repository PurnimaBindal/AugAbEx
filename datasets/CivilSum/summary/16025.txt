The Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923 (as amended in 1984), Section 4(1)(c)(i) - Compensation - The Commissioner has the authority to settle the compensation, including the degree of disability - The amount of compensation payable should be assessed by taking into account the degree of disability sustained by the claimant, but the Commissioner should be satisfied prima facie before proceeding with the application - A Medical Practitioner can certify that the injured has suffered certain disability due to the accident, but cannot precisely assess the effect of it on a worker's earning capacity - The wording of the Section itself saves the power of the Commissioner to assess the loss of earning capacity - To prove the degree of loss of earning capacity, independent evidence must be led - The Commissioner will decide the claim based on the Rules of Reason and Justice - The power will not be exercised in a capricious or impetuous manner, but in a disciplined and reasonable way - To arrive at the quantum of compensation, the Commissioner should disclose the reasoning process adopted by him, and good reasons alone will result in good decisions.
