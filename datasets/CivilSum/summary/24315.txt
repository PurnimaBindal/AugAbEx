The provisions of Section 4(4) of the Apprentices Act, 1961, are directory and not mandatory, and non-registration of the apprenticeship contract will not render it a nullity or ineffective.
The use of the term "shall" in Section 4(4) must be construed in the context and object of the Act, and any interpretation that renders the statutory provision nugatory or ineffective must be rejected.
An apprentice remains an apprentice regardless of registration or non-compliance with the terms of the contract and is not considered a workman under the definition of Section 2(s) of the Industrial Disputes Act and other labor laws.
The Gujarat High Court's decisions that non-registration of the contract renders it ineffective and the trainee becomes a workman do not set a good law.
The provisions of social legislation for poor class must be construed in the light of the philosophy binding the same.
The provisions of Section 37 of the Apprentices Act requiring the laying of rules before Parliament after formulation are directory in nature.
A genuine apprenticeship contract is necessary for the apprentice to not be considered a workman, and non-registration only renders it invalid if it is a mere camouflage or a ruse in effect and substance, or if the appointment is made mala fide for taking work other than those that can be taken from an apprentice.
