Punjab Gram Panchayat Act, 1952, Section 6(5)(1) - Constitution of India, Article 191 - Election - A statutory provision must be strictly interpreted - Being obligated to pay a debt as a result of inheriting property or someone's moral debt does not disqualify a person from running for election - Arrears of rent incurred by a candidate's predecessor or parent does not disqualify the candidate from participating in the election - The petitioner was not proven to be in arrears of rent personally and therefore was not disqualified from contesting in the election - The judgment declaring the petitioner disqualified was overturned.

Punjab Gram Panchayat Act, 1952, Section 6(5) (g) - Punjab Co-operative Societies Act, 1961, Sections 2(c), 4 and 30 - Government - Local Authority - A Co-operative Society is a separate legal entity and is not a government or local authority - The employment of a person in a cooperative society does not disqualify them from contesting in a Gram Panchayat election -  Unless it is established that the cooperative society is part of Sugarfed, an employee of Morinda Cooperative Sugar Mills cannot be considered an employee of Sugarfed.
[Paras 10, 11 and 12]

Constitution of India, Articles 12 and 226 - Punjab Cooperative Societies Act, 1961, Sections 2(c), 4 and 30 - State - Instrumentality of State - Whether a body is considered a "State" depends on the extent of the government's control over the body and the level of financial dependency on the government - Cooperative societies do not qualify as a State under Article 12 of the Constitution - The fact that a body does not discharge any governmental function is also a factor in determining whether it can be considered a State - Punjab Gram Panchayat Act, 1952, Section 6(5) (g) - Constitution of India, Article 191.
