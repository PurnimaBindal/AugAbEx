Section 3 of the Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923, outlines the criteria for injuries to be considered work-related.
This includes the requirement for the injury to have been caused by an accident that arose out of or in the course of employment.
For example, in cases of heart attacks, it must be demonstrated that the injury was caused by a strain related to work.
Proper pleadings and evidence are necessary to prove the causal connection between the injury/death and employment.
Medical opinion is the only possible evidence in cases of sudden heart attack.
The onus is on the applicant to establish the work's contribution to the injury.
As for Section 30, a substantial question of law is necessary for the maintainability of appeal.
A jurisdictional question is also considered a substantial question of law, and a finding of fact without evidence is a perversion and, therefore, a substantial question of law.
