The specific Relief Act, 1963, Section 34 deals with matters like Double Jeopardy, Natural Justice, Break in service, Pension, Warning, and Absent without leave.
In this case, an appellant was given a warning after an enquiry into the charges of his wilful absence without leave.
However, after six years, on receiving pension papers order, a decision was made to treat that period of absence as a break in service.
The superannuation pension is a right and cannot be withheld without providing an opportunity.
Additionally, one cannot be punished twice for a single lapse.
Hence, the order is void, and the petitioner is entitled to the pension.
[Para 4]

The Limitation Act, 1963, Article 58 and the Specific Relief Act, 1963, Section 34 both have provisions regarding limitaions and void orders.
In particular, Article 58 of the Limitation Act does not apply to void orders since they do not have any legal standing.
Furthermore, in this case, the order was passed without affording any opportunity, making it a void order.
As such, there is no need to get the order set aside, and no limitations are involved.
[Para 5]

When pensionary benefits are withheld without affording an opportunity, it is a violation of the rules of natural justice.
As a result, the petitioner is entitled to receive interest at a rate of 12%, starting from the date that the pension was due.
