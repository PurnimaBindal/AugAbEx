Articles 14, 226, and 311 of the Constitution of India - Termination of service/Abolition of post/Judicial Review - The court can set aside the order of abolition of a post under Article 226 of the Indian Constitution if it is apparent that the post was abolished to get rid of an employee.
[Paras 9 and 13]

Articles 14, 226, and 311 of the Constitution of India - Abolition of post/Financial losses - If the Board of Directors of the Corporation wants to abolish a post due to financial losses, the directors should be informed of the agenda.
If a post is abolished due to financial losses, the entire Corporation structure must be considered.
Abolishing a post based solely on an employee's service record indicates an intent to get rid of the employee.
[Paras 14, 16]

Articles 14, 226, and 311 of the Constitution of India - Abolition of Post - When a post is abolished and a post with the same nature, character, duties, and responsibilities but a different designation is created, it implies that no post has been abolished.
[Para 17]

Articles 14 and 311 of the Constitution of India - Abolition of Post/Natural Justice - When an employee’s work and conduct are used as reasons for the abolition of their post, they should be given an opportunity to show that their work and conduct met the required standards.
It will be presumed that a post was abolished to get rid of an employee if they were not informed about the reasons for abolishing their post.
[Paras 18 and 19]

Articles 14 and 311 of the Constitution of India - Abolition of post/First come last go/Termination of services - If posts are interchangeable and only one seniority list exists, all officials on that list are part of the same cadre.
When one post is abolished, the junior-most official should be the one asked to leave, and failure to do so indicates that the functions and duties of the terminated employee are still necessary.
[Para 23]
