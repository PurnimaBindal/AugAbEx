The Constitution of India prohibits discrimination based on various grounds, but it allows for preferential treatment for historically disadvantaged groups to ensure their adequate representation in public services.
Reservation policies must be reasonable and consistent with public interest.
[Paras 11, 12, and 13]
The regulations governing post-graduate medical education require admission on the basis of merit and do not allow for caste/tribe-based reservation.
However, the Constitution empowers the Union and States to make policies that provide adequate representation for disadvantaged groups.
Reservation is meant to ensure that deserving candidates from reserved categories are not denied admission.
But reservation policies cannot be arbitrary or detrimental to public interests and must be consistent with merit and efficiency.
[Para 63]
The judgment's conclusions will not affect admissions already made and will apply prospectively.
[Paras 64 and 116]
