According to the Employees State Insurance Act, 1948, Sections 2(12) and 1(5) and the Factories Act, 1948, Section 2(k), pumping oil at petrol filling stations and washing, cleaning, and repairing vehicles at service centers at petrol pumps are activities that fall under the definition of "manufacturing process."
The words "Pumping oil, water sewage, or any other substance" in Section 2(k) of Factories Act should not be interpreted narrowly.
The judgments of the Single Judge of Pb.
& Hy.
High Court in Bhag Singh v. ESI, 1982 PLR 605, ESI v. M/s Triplex Dry Cleaners, 1982 PLR 600 and in M/s Raison Tailors v. ESI, 1982 PLR 609 are overruled.
The Petrol pump and service station is a place of work where persons are employed and power is used in the process of its activities.
It is not fundamentally different from the other activities of a manufacturing place, and it would be unjustified to give a narrow interpretation to the definition of "manufacturing process."
The object of the Act is to provide certain benefits to employees and ensure conditions of employment that are conducive to proper health, safety, and welfare.
These provisions, therefore, apply to all workplaces.
