Constitution of India, Article 12 - Companies Act, 1956, Sections 617 and 43-A(i) - State - Determining whether a Corporation is an instrumentality of State cannot rely on a set formula, but must consider the totality of circumstances, rather than selectively choosing certain principles.
[Para 13]
Constitution of India, Articles 12 and 14 - Companies Act, 1956, Section 43-A(i) - State/Pervasive control of the Government - Maruti Udyog, which partnered with Suzuki Motor Company to establish itself, is not entirely controlled by the Government of India and has alternative sources of financing besides the government - Maruti Udyog cannot be subjected to Article 14's strictures of the Constitution as an Instrumentality of State.
[Paras 14, 15, 24 and 27]
Constitution of India, Articles 12 and 14 - Companies Act, 1956, Sections 43-A(i) and 617 - Monopoly - Maruti Udyog does not have a monopoly on motor vehicle manufacturing, nor were any Government Departments transferred to the company - Maruti Udyog competes with private sector manufacturing - In no way can it be considered an act of the State.
[Para 16]
Civil Procedure Code, Section 11 - Res judicata - A decision is not legally definitive unless a specific point of contention was raised and litigated over according to law - A decision not made with consideration to statutory provisions is not binding.
[Paras 20 and 29]
Constitution of India, Articles 12 and 226 - State - Maruti Udyog does not qualify as an authority under Article 12 of the Constitution, and therefore cannot be subject to a writ petition against the company.
[Para 21]
Constitution of India, Articles 12, 14 and 226 - Companies Act, 1956, Section 43-A(i) - Streamlined public sector cooperation with foreign concerns under the New Industrial Policy requires certain discretion to maintain discipline - Not every discretionary action is subject to the strictures of Article 14 of the Constitution.
