According to the Constitution of India's Articles 14 and 226, as well as the Contract Act's Sections 8, 9 & 70 and the Evidence Act's Sections 114 and 115, an engagement as an advocate by the competent authority must be settled and accepted through agreement with an acceptance of proposal, and once an advocate's fees have been quoted and accepted, the competent authority's successor cannot refuse to pay their fees.
Moreover, a general ceiling cannot be laid down for the fees of advocates, as it depends on their personal skill, and objections by a person successor in office would be suspect.
