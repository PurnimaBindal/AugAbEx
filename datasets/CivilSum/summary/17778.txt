Service issue - Transfer - Abandonment of employment - Notice period - Salary - Evidence - The plaintiff abandoned his employment with the defendants without giving the contractually agreed upon six months' notice - In fact, the plaintiff acted upon transfer orders by handing over office charge and even applied for leave before joining, without challenging the defendant's right to transfer him - After being transferred, the plaintiff wrote to express his unwillingness to report for duty without challenging the validity of the transfer order - As a result, the plaintiff cannot benefit from his own wrongdoing, and is not entitled to any wages or allowances for six months.
[Para 3] B Limitation - The plaintiff's employment ended on October 19, 1981 - The plaintiff filed a lawsuit against the defendant company for recovery of the amount on April 25, 1985 - Accounts have yet to be supplied by the defendant, while the defendant has asserted that money is owed by the plaintiff - The letter from the defendant dated September 6, 1989, refers to the settlement of the plaintiff's dues, which shows that there is a debtor-creditor relationship between the two parties - If the letter is taken as an acknowledgment of the debt, the plaintiff will have a fresh period of limitation of three years from the date of the letter - Therefore, the plaintiff's lawsuit is within the legally allowable time limit.
