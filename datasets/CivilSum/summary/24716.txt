Counsel for TFAI argued that the strike was invalid, and thus the workers are not entitled to any relief under the Constitution of India, Article 19(1)(c) and the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 10(3), 10A(4A), 22 and 23 - However, this argument lacks merit, as the right to form associations or unions is a fundamental right and the Trade Unions Act outlines the registration requirements - Forming unions is necessary to voice the grievances of labor and trade unionists act as mouthpieces for these issues - The strength of a union depends on its membership and their bargaining power would be reduced if strikes, a form of demonstration, were not permitted - The right to strike has been recognized as an important weapon for workers in almost all democratic countries, and it is a mode of redress for resolving worker grievances - However, this right is not absolute, and restrictions have been placed in India as per sections 10(3), 10A(4A), 22 and 23 of the I.D.
Act.
