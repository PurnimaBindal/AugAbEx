Section 48 of the State Financial Corporations Act, 1951, and Regulation 41(3) of the Karnataka State Financial Corporation (Staff) Regulations, 1965, relate to disciplinary proceedings.
The competency of an Enquiry Officer is essential for the validity of such proceedings, but if an incompetent officer is appointed, the enquiry may not be vitiated.
The principles of estoppel and acquiescence may apply, and the prejudice doctrine may also be considered.
However, superior courts may not allow such a question to be raised for the first time.
[Paras 8, 9, 11, 17, and 18].
When dealing with disciplinary proceedings against a class `A' officer, the Karnataka State Financial Corporation (Staff) Regulations, 1965, and Appendix III must be considered.
According to Section 48 of the State Financial Corporations Act, 1951, the Managing Director is the disciplinary authority only for minor punishment.
In contrast, the Board is the appointing authority for Class `A' officer and possesses the power to impose major penalties.
In the absence of a specific rule, the Managing Director has the incidental power to place the reports of the Enquiry Officer before the Board.
The lack of such a rule won't vitiate the proceedings.
[Paras 19, 20].
