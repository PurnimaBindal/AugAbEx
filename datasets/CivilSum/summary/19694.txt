The Punjab Subordinate Agricultural Service Rules, 1933, prescribe conditions for the regularisation of direct recruits appointed as Surveyors in the Soil Conservation Wing of the Department of Agricultural, Punjab.
However, these rules do not apply to promotees who are subsequently appointed as Sectional Officers on an ad hoc basis.
An impugned order had imposed conditions on the regularisation of services for the promotees, which required them to pass both the Soil Conservation Course and a departmental examination.
The court held that there was no legal sanctity in imposing these conditions on promotees and quashed the impugned order.
[Para 6]

The petitioners challenged an order that required them to fulfill certain conditions to be promoted as Sectional Officers in the Department of Agricultural, Punjab, including passing a departmental examination and a Soil Conservation Course sponsored by the Central Government/Departmental Training Institute.
The court held that there were no rules or instructions that required promotees to comply with these conditions.
The petitioners were not estopped from challenging the imposition of these conditions, even though they had appeared in a departmental test (though failed) and one of the petitioners had completed the training.
The court emphasized that there can never be an estoppel against a statute, and the respondents must show that these conditions have been imposed by any statutory rule or instructions.
As there was no such requirement, the court quashed the conditions.
[Para 7]

Important Point: There can never be an estoppel against a statute.
