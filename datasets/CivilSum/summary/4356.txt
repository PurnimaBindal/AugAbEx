According to Section 11-A of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, before interfering with the quantum of punishment, the Labour Court must come to a conclusion that the punishment is so disproportionate that it amounts to victimisation and shocks the judicial conscience.
Even if there is no element of violence on the part of the workman, it is not sufficient to interfere with the quantum of punishment.
[Para 9]
Section 11-A of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 states that charges of insubordination and use of unparliamentary language are very serious.
Termination of services is not a disproportionate punishment.
[Para 9]
In the case of an award reinstating a workman and the High Court staying the operation of the award, the workman is entitled to his wages under Section 17-B of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, regardless of the fact that he was not taken on duty and ultimately the award was quashed.
