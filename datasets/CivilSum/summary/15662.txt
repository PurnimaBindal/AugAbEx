Article 226 of the Indian Constitution on Education Admission requires proper communication of admission.
A petitioner was offered a place in B.Sc.
(Agri.)
with the understanding that if a vacancy arises in the B.V.Sc.
course, he would be accommodated as per his rank.
He paid tuition fees and didn't attend classes as he wasn't interested in B.Sc.
(Agri.).
Later on, the petitioner was given a seat in the B.V.Sc., but he wasn't aware of this as the communication wasn't served in person or through a registered letter under an acknowledgement.
However, the principal announced the allotment through a microphone and a notice board communication.
The court held that any communication addressed to an individual must be served in person or by registered letter under an acknowledgement.
As neither of these courses was followed, the petition was allowed, and the respondents were directed to adjust the petitioner to the 1st year of the B.V.Sc.
course without any further loss of time, with attendance counted from the date of admission of the petition in the B.V.Sc.
course.
(Paras 4 and 5)
