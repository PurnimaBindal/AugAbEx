The Army Act, 1930, Sections 116 and 130 and the Constitution of India, Article 226 state that Summary Court Martial is presided over by a single member court and the other two attendees are not sworn or affirmed.
Proceedings were conducted by the Colonel, who was the Presiding Officer.
The petitioner did not file any objections to the presiding Commanding Officer nor did they make any statements of bias, making it clear that he did receive a fair trial.
No infirmity was found in the proceedings, and Section 130 did not apply.
[Para 9] B The Army Act, 1930, Section 39(b) and the Constitution of India, Article 226 state that a petitioner who overstays their leave for 236 days admits guilt.
The petitioner did not report to the nearest Military Hospital or inform their unit and did not produce any evidence to support their claim.
As a result, the Summary Court Martial was conducted properly and the conviction was not illegal.
A petition for habeas corpus cannot be maintained.
