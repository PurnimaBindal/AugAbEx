Notaries Act, 1952, Section 3 - Notaries Rules, 1956, Rule 7(3) Notary Public - The appointment of Notaries must be made based on the norms outlined in sub-rule (3) of Rule 7, which requires consideration of qualifications and understanding of the importance of Notary functions - However, appointments were made unrestrained, disregarding established procedures and creating a new, unlawful system without the necessary consultation - Appointment decisions were based solely on the length of time applicants had been residing in a particular area - There was no comparison of individual merit among candidates and no regard for their competence - This arbitrary selection process is subject to serious challenge, and the competent authority must follow proper selection method outlined in clause (c) of Rule 7 (3) of the Rules prior to announcing Notary appointments.
[Paras 6 and 7] B. Notaries Rules, 1956, Rule 7(3) (d) - Appointment of Notaries - Selection and Considerations - No person should be appointed solely on the basis of residency, as there are other qualifications and requirements specified under the Rules - For example, appointing a husband and wife as Notaries in violation of clause (d) of sub-rule (3) of Rule 7 would be improper.
[Para 6] C. Notaries Rules, 1956, Rule 8 - Gazette Notification - Non-compliance - The Gazette Notification is lacking in showing if the Notary appointed is for the entire Delhi region or for some specific police district - In violation of Rule 8.
[Para 7] D. Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 16 - Notaries Act, 1952, Section 3 - Notaries Rule, 1956, Rule 7 - Notary Appointments/ Scheduled Caste category candidates - The appointments of Notaries made without following the prescribed procedure for the appointment of Scheduled Caste candidates is liable to be quashed - Appointments made solely on the candidate's Scheduled Caste status without considering any qualifications or experience are illegitimate.
