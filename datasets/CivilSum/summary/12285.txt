The Constitution of India's Articles 14 and 16, along with the Industrial Disputes Act's Sections 2(oo)(bb) and 25-F, deal with absence from duty and over-stayal of leave.
Automatic termination on over-stayal of leave is mandated by such laws, and any Rules or Standing Orders that provide for the liability of termination on account of over-stayal of leave or absence for a particular length of time do not exclude the principles of natural justice.
These provisions in the Rules only enable the authority to proceed with the action to be taken to inflict such liability.
The employee concerned would have to be given show cause notice of such proposed action and afforded an opportunity to furnish and establish his explanation justifying his absence.
If a justified explanation comes, and the authority is satisfied with it, the action may not be taken, or the extreme penalty may not be inflicted.
However, this is only possible if some enquiry is held in consonance of principles of natural justice.
Automatic deemed termination is liable to be quashed.
