Respondent, Anupam Gupta, Advocate, argued on the Constitution of India, Articles 13, 14 and 16(2) - Education - Admission - Language - Compulsory subject - Punjabi subject - Decision of Syndicate of Panjab University, Chandigarh, dated 161995 and decision of its Academic Council dated 29.5.1995 clause (ii) - Exemption from compulsory subject of Punjabi to those who did not study Punjabi up to Matric - He opined that "upto Class 10th" does not mean any class up to 10th but rather that a student should have studied Punjabi continuously upto 10th so as to be quite familiar with the language - This interpretation would be in consonance with the common understanding and would help achieving the object by the rule - At the graduation level the student has to choose a subject for his career and should not be forced to take subjects compulsorily out of their choice - The validity of clause (i) was doubted but left open - Clause (ii) was interpreted as above - University was directed to grant exemption under clause (ii) to those who had not studied Punjabi continuously up to class 10.
He also argued on the Constitution of India, Articles 5, 13, 14 and 16 - Domicile - Term domicile has a wider meaning and coverage in the present day in our jurisprudence - It denotes concept of permanency of residence and is a legal concept for the purposes of determining the applicability of law to the individual - Even if a person has his permanent home within the territorial limit of particular state or system of law still he is domicile of India not of a particular place.
