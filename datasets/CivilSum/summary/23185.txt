The doctrine of natural justice is intended to ensure justice and prevent miscarriage of justice.
It does not have a definitive meaning, but represents fairness and due procedure.
Its application depends on the circumstances of each case, and if it is found that an executive action has not complied with this doctrine, it can be remedied by courts.
B Bias and ill-will are included in the word 'malice'.
To prove ill-will, there must be evidence on record.
C. The disciplinary authority is the judge in the quantum of punishment, but judicial review is feasible in departmental proceedings in cases where the recorded findings are irrational.
D In a departmental enquiry, facts should be established and the person charged should be allowed to cross-examine witnesses and present his/her own explanation for the evidence.
Holding an enquiry without a charge-sheet, presenting officer, or witnesses shows bias and is a violation of natural justice.
E. The test for bias is whether there is a real danger of bias or just an apprehension of it.
If there is a real danger and the allegations are not baseless, then the administrative action cannot be sustained.
