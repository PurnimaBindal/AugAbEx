The conditions prescribed in the prospectus for admission to postgraduate courses in Medical College, Rohtak were changed relaxing the criteria with the aim of allowing more candidates to have a chance to apply and extend the field of choice to select the best candidates.
The eligibility of the petitioners remained unaltered and since their conditions of qualifying the examination were not changed, they cannot complain.
[Paras 9, 12 and 13]
The idea behind sending probationers for postgraduate courses in Medical College is to improve their efficiency.
A person who has been regularly appointed through P.S.C.
to Civil Medical Service cannot be debarred from admission solely because they have not completed the probation period.
Sufficient safeguards and precautions have been adopted by prescribing that "nothing adverse regarding the efficiency or integrity" of the candidates should be there.
Unless there is any rule to bar the admission of probationers, the action of the Government cannot be termed arbitrary or unfair.
[Para 14]
The government relaxed the criteria of minimum marks for calling candidates for an interview so that all seats would be filled in and the maximum number of candidates would be made available for best medical services.
The petitioner joined and took a full chance but failed to secure even 12% marks, which was the relaxed criteria.
Therefore, they were not eligible even as per the unrevised or relaxed criteria.
As a result, they have no locus to challenge the criteria or the admission.
[Paras 16, 17, 18, 21 to 24]
The method of negative marking or reduction of marks for wrong answers to avoid guesswork answers is not infirm or arbitrary.
