The Constitution of India, along with the Indian Contract Act, Section 23, states in Article 14, 16, 21, 39(a) and 41 - Termination - when stigmatory in nature - requires the opportunity to be heard and principles of natural justice to be upheld.
In this case, termination was ordered due to the petitioner's refusal to take the delivery of a transfer order and taking leave without sanction.
The principles of natural justice require the petitioner to be afforded an opportunity or a domestic enquiry should have been conducted.
Additionally, Para 6 of the appointment order is opposed to public policy and is void under Section 23 of the Indian Contract Act.
Therefore, the order of termination passed without holding any domestic enquiry or affording any opportunity is violative of principles of natural justice.
The petition is thus allowed.
