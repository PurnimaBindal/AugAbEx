Industrial Disputes Act, Section 33(2)(b) - Approval to dismissal from service - Refusal of permission - Jurisdiction of the Tribunal - The tribunal cannot evaluate the sufficiency or insufficiency of evidence in the domestic enquiry held by the employer; however, there are two exceptions in which the tribunal can interfere with the domestic enquiry.
One, if the findings are not based on any legal evidence and two, if no reasonable individual could arrive at such findings based on the available evidence - The query of a defence witness by the inquiry officer and not letting the workman question the said witness afterwards does not constitute bias or unfairness - Even if the approval is granted by the Tribunal, it does not prevent the worker from raising an industrial dispute against the management - The accused workman was accused of failing to provide tickets to 22 passengers while serving as a conductor in State Transport Corpn., the allegation was proved, and there was no denial of opportunity, no violation of principles of natural justice - The order of dismissal cannot be considered harsh, as the accused was charged with a grave offence, and dismissal was deemed to be an appropriate punishment.
