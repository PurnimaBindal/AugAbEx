The order passed by the Labour Court under Section 33-C(2) in favor of Respondent No. 2 awarded arrears of pay, but it was to be determined whether Respondent No. 2 was entitled to a second promotional scale as an officiating lineman before completing 16 years of service.
Before computing any benefits for Respondent No. 2, the finding had to be given, and this could only be done by making a separate reference to the Labour Court under Section 10 of the Act.
The Labour Court could not assume jurisdiction under Section 33-C(2), which is prohibited.
The impugned order was quashed.
[Para 6]

Proceedings under Section 33-C(2) are in the nature of execution proceedings, and only pre-existing rights in favor of the workman can be adjudicated.
Section 33-C(2) would apply only once there is an admission of an existing right of the workman by the employer regarding the benefit that the former is entitled to.
If those rights are in dispute, then there cannot be any proper adjudication under Section 33-C(2).
For such adjudication, the workman has to approach the Act to some other authorities instead of moving an application under Section 33-C(2) of the Act itself.
[Para 6]

The stand of the petitioner before the Labour Court was categorical that Respondent No. 2 was not entitled to the second promotional grade due to reasons that he had already obtained the second promotion as an officiating lineman.
This point could only be adjudicated by a separate reference to the Labour Court if made by the appropriate Government.
There was no pre-existing right admitted by the petitioner in favor of Respondent No. 2 before the Labour Court.
In the absence of any pre-existing right in favor of the workman, the Labour Court could not give any finding regarding entitlement under Section 32-C(2) of the Act and had exceeded its jurisdiction by doing so.
