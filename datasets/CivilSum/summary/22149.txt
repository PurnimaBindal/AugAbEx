Article 136 and 141 of the Constitution of India - SLP - Dismissal of - The dismissal of an SLP against a judgment does not mean that the Supreme Court has laid down any law in the meaning of Article 141 - It simply implies that the Supreme Court decided not to exercise its power based on the facts of that particular case.

Article 136, 141 and 215 of the Constitution of India - Railway Service (Pension) Rules, 1993 - Gratuity Act, 1972, Section 2(e) - Judgments and precedents - A decision only serves as an authority for what it has determined rather than what can be inferred logically - When a decision is made without considering the relevant statutory provisions that assess the issue, it is not considered as binding precedent - The Supreme Court in Pritam Singh's case had not paid attention to the exclusionary clause contained in Section 2(e) of the Gratuity Act, 1972 while deciding that the 1972 Act is applicable to railway employees despite the provisions of 1993 Railway Service (Pension) Rules - The employee of any establishment who is entitled to receive gratuity under any other law stand excluded from the Gratuity Act, 1972.
[Paras 14 to 17]
Railway Service (Pension) Rules, 1993, Rule 15(4)(ii) - Computation of gratuity - Clause (ii) of sub-rule (4) of Rule 15 only specifies the recovery of losses mentioned in sub-clause (a) of clause (i) of sub-rule (4) - It has no relation to the computation of the gratuity payment amount.

Railway Service (Pension) Rules, 1993 - Gratuity Act, 1972 - Computation of gratuity - Dearness pay - While the Fifth Pay Commission proposed linking the dearness pay to the All India Consumer Price Index of 1211966 as on 171993, the entitlement of the employees based on this recommendation was to be prospectively effected from 141995 - Accordingly, 97% of the dearness allowance was to be paid to those who had a salary up to Rs. 3500/- as basic pay - Those who retired before 141995 were not eligible for the benefits of that report.

Articles 14 and 226 of the Constitution of India - Revision of pay scales - Acceptance and enforcement of - Financial implications - According to the well-established principle of law, financial implications are a relevant factor to consider while accepting the revision of pay.
