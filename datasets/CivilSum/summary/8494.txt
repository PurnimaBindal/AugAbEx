Head Constables working for Punjab Police were put under suspension on the basis of a criminal case that was filed against them for forcibly taking possession of land that was the subject matter of a dispute in the civil court, even though findings of the trial court were in their favour.
The suspension order was found to be without application of mind, and hence was quashed.
The petitioners were awarded costs of Rs. 10,000.
[Paras 4, 5, 12, 13 and 14]

A mere order of status-quo by the appellate court does not mean that the findings of the trial court have been reversed.
The person found to be the owner in possession cannot be held guilty of violation of order of status-quo and use of force to enter into possession.
A specific order of stay needs to be passed by the appellate court to restrain the respondents from interfering with the appellant's possession.
[Paras 10 and 13]
