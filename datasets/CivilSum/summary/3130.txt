In accordance with Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertakings) Act, Section 19 and Central Bank of India Officer Employees Conduct Regulations, Regulations 3(1) and 24, misconduct charges can be applied to a delinquent who deviated from banking procedure while sanctioning loan applications.
These charges must clearly convey the offense and cannot be vague.
Delinquent cannot claim prejudice, as the charges do not contain any complicated matter and can be effectively defended by a representative who understands bank procedures and regulations.
As non-provision of legal assistance does not prejudice the delinquent, it is not necessary.
In the case of misconduct charges in accordance with Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertakings) Act, Section 19, Central Bank of India Officers Employees Conduct Regulations, Regulation 3(1), and Article 226 of the Constitution of India, disciplinary authority can accept enquiry officer findings along with reasons, and it is not essential for the disciplinary authority to re-consider the whole evidence.
While reviewing a question of fact, it is firmly established that the Court is not an appellate authority under Article 226.
Moreover, there is no merit in the argument that the disciplinary and appellate authorities did not apply their mind when punishing.
