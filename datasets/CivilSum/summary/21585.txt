According to the Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 16 regarding public appointments and select lists, being on the select list does not provide a legal right to appointment.
Simply having a name on the list is not sufficient for an appointment to be offered.
[Para 11]

When it comes to judicial review of public appointments, each case must be evaluated on its own merit.
The superior court will not typically direct the issuance of a writ unless there is evidence of mala fide or arbitrariness on the part of the employer.
[Para 16]

In the case of the appointment of three security guards where the list contained names of five candidates and the third candidate declined the appointment, the person listed at Sr. No. 4 should have been offered the position.
The decision to abolish the posts was made after the writ petition was filed, and no explanation was given as to why the select panel was ignored.
Therefore, the respondent should have been offered the appointment.
[Para 18]
