The Constitution of India, Articles 1416 and 226 - Promotion/Seniority - cum-merit - Promotion policy.
If a promotion policy is based on seniority-cum-merit basis, then seniority will prevail provided that the senior is fit to hold the higher post.
A senior cannot claim promotion as a right based on their seniority alone.
Comparative merit assessment must be made.
If a more meritorious junior is available, the senior must still be promoted.
A senior will only be superseded if found unfit to hold the higher post.
[Paras 22, 23, 29, 48, 49 and 50].
The Constitution of India, Articles 309, 329, 162, 166(3) - Rules - Executive instructions - Amendment of instructions and policies.
Administrative policy of the Government regarding promotions.
The policy is not issued under the authority of rule making powers.
The policy is issued only by the Finance Department.
The Finance Department can amend the policy itself.
When the policy is amended, it will override the earlier and the doctrine of implied repeal will be applied.
[Paras 37, 40 and 41].
The Constitution of India, Article 226 - Judicial review - Jurisdiction of judicial review of the High Court is very limited.
The High Court is only concerned with the procedure, illegality and irrationality, not the merits of the decision of decision making authority.
[Paras 43, 46 & 47].
The Constitution of India, Article 226 - Parties - Seniority - Petitioner challenging seniority and wants to supersede many seniors shown above him.
Seniors not impleaded.
The petition is liable to be dismissed.
[Para 45].
