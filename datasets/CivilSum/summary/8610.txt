Workmen Compensation Act, 1923, Section 21(1) and (2) - Workman Compensation Rules, 1924, Rule 22 - Territorial Jurisdiction - Although Section 21, as it stood before the amendment, required applications for compensation to be filed before the commissioner of the area where the accident took place resulting in death or injury, this requirement was only directory and not mandatory.
The use of the word "shall" in Section 21(1) does not make this requirement mandatory.
Whether a provision is considered "Mandatory" or "Directory" is determined by the object of the statute and not just by the use of these words.
In this case, the application was filed before the Commissioner of the place where the claimant resides, rather than where the accident occurred, and the employer participated in the proceedings without raising any objections about territorial jurisdiction.
As a result, the awards of compensation passed by the Commissioner cannot be questioned later as being passed without territorial jurisdiction and are, therefore, valid.
Objections about territorial jurisdiction must be raised at the earliest opportunity, and any failure of justice must result from this failure to object.
In this case, objections were not made before the Commissioner about the merits of the awards, and a failure of justice did not occur because the Commissioner lacked territorial jurisdiction.
