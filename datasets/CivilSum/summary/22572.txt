Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 10 and 33 - Employers can seek permission to prove the misconduct of workmen in terminating their services without a fair inquiry - However, it must be done at the earliest stage while filing a claim or written statement - Shambhu Nath Goval's case lays down the rule that employers cannot seek permission to lead evidence at a later stage to protect the defense of the workmen and expedite the dispute resolution process - This decision has been in place for 18 years and must be followed to be the correct law - There is no conflict between this decision and Rajendra Jha's case - Though parties can request to lead additional evidence at any stage if deemed just and necessary, the dissenting view in Shankar Chakravarti's case suggests that the Shambhu Nath Goyal's case does not lay down the correct law and the correct process is stated in Shankar Chakravarti's case.
