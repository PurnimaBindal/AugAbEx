Constitution of India, Article 226 - Administrative Tribunal Act, 1985, Section 28 - Instructions - Withdrawal - Relief granted on instructions issued by the government - The order granting relief to the respondents is administrative in character and cannot be operated retrospectively in a way that affects the rights acquired by the respondents on the basis of instructions - The benefit acquired by the respondents should not be denied in view of the instructions withdrawn without notice to the respondent or court.
[Paras 9 and 10]

Constitution of India, Article 226 - Administrative Tribunal Act, 1985, Section 28 - Jurisdiction - The High Court does not have the jurisdiction to entertain petitions under Article 226 of the Constitution against the order passed by the Tribunal constituted under the Act before 18.3.1997 due to the bar contained in Section 28 of the Act.
[Para 6]

Administrative Tribunal Act, 1985, Section 22(3) - Central Administrative Tribunal (Procedure) Rules, 1987, Rule 17(1) - Limitation - Delay - Review - The review application is liable to be rejected due to the petitioner's inability to explain the delay in filing it and not making any effect to explain the delay.
[Para 8]

Administrative Tribunal Act, 1985, Section 22(3) - Central Administrative Tribunal (Procedure) Rules, 1987, Rule 17(1) - Review against the order of the Tribunal - Dismissed - Challenged in writ - The decision rendered by the Tribunal cannot be faulted through the review application as the review was rejected on the ground that the petitioner failed to take part in the proceedings before the Tribunal, which was a reasoned one.
[Paras 9 and 10]
