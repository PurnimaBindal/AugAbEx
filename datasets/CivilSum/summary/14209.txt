In accordance with the Constitution of India, specifically Articles 226 and 14, a government doctor was granted permission to take on a foreign assignment without pay.
However, this subsequent period of absence was labeled as a dies-non in accordance with a Government order, and was not entered into the petitioner's service record.
The petitioner argued that this period of absence should be counted towards increments and other pensionary benefits as is done in the case of other doctors on foreign assignments without permission.
The court agreed, stating that the petitioner had been treated in a discriminatory and unjust manner.
As such, it was ordered that the period of leave be reflected in the petitioner's service record and be treated at the same level as the doctors referred to.
The period of leave is declared dies-non but is to be counted towards increment and pensionary benefits.
The petition is deemed allowed.
