Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16, 226 and 136 - Admission - Education - The petitioner was unfairly denied admission to the MBBS Course despite his merit-based competitive examination results being in order due to the violation of reservation policy.
The University and College were instructed by the Court to admit the petitioner, and the authorities challenged these orders in an SLP before the Supreme Court, but without success.
The orders passed in the writ petition and SLP are retroactive to the date of filing the writ petition in the High Court, as any time spent in the Court cannot be held against the candidate.
The institution's failure to comply with constitutional provisions and the law caused the candidate to be unable to complete the required number of days of the course, but he should not be prevented from sitting in the examination as this is not his fault.
The candidate would have gained admission and completed the necessary courses had the institution not acted unlawfully.
As a result, the University was ordered to declare the petitioner's results, and he was allowed to sit for the examination on interim orders.
