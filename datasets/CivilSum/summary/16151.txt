The principle of estoppel does not apply against a statute.
The petitioner was requested by the respondents to prepare pension papers, but pension was later denied due to the BSF Rules and the C.C.S.
(Pension) Rules, 1972.
The denial of pension does not result in manifest injustice, and hence, the principle of promissory estoppel cannot be invoked in such cases.
[Para 9]

The writ petition was filed after an inordinate delay of five years since the pension was refused in 1981, and hence, the petition has been dismissed.
[Para 9]

Rule 13(b) of the Central Civil Services (Pension) Rules, 1972, is based on the sound principles of law as a minor cannot enter into a contract of service.
Therefore, the second provision of Rule 13(b) is not arbitrary, discriminatory and violative of the Constitution of India.
[Para 8]

A government servant is entitled to pension after completing a qualifying service of ten years.
In this case, the petitioner's application for discharge was accepted under Rule 19 of the BSF Rules, which resulted in his resignation from service and hence, he became disentitled to any pensionary benefits as per Rule 26 of the C.C.S.
(Pension) Rules, 1972.
Even if his service rendered before attaining the majority is counted, which comes to 10 years 55 days, he is not entitled to any pension.
[Para 8]

Imp.
Point - The application for discharge under Rule 19 of BSF Rules is considered as resignation from service, which leads to the forfeiture of past services and disentitlement to any pensionary benefits as per Rule 26 of C.C.S.
(Pension) Rules, 1972.
