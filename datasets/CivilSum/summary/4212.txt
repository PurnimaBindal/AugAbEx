The plaintiff, who had been suspended from work, was denied his salary for the period of his suspension without notice because of the standing order 14(5), which stipulates that if an inquiry confirms the order of suspension, the employee will be deemed absent from duty and not entitled to any salary during that period.
This was applicable in the case of the plaintiff who was suspended, had charges against him proved and was subsequently dismissed.
Although it is true that the non-supply of the inquiry report to the plaintiff contravened the rules of natural justice, prior to the decision in A.I.R. 1991 SC 471, it was not mandatory to supply the report to the delinquent employee.
In the case of the plaintiff, he was dismissed without the report, and this punishment is not open to challenge on the grounds of non-supply of the report.
The Industrial Disputes Act requires approval from the Tribunal before a dismissal can be made, and the plaintiff had requested it when the dispute was pending.
However, there was no evidence to indicate whether such an approval was granted.
Moreover, since this plea had not been made before in the lower appellate court, it cannot be allowed to be raised in the second appeal.
The inquiry report stated that the plaintiff had been involved in a conspiracy resulting in an assault on a senior administrative manager.
The report was accepted by the General Manager, who dismissed the plaintiff for his involvement in the assault.
The plaintiff did not challenge the report in either of the courts, and the appeal filed by the defendant was allowed while the suit filed by the plaintiff was dismissed.
