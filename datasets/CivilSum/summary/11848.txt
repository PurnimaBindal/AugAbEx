Constitution of India, Article 226 - Civil Procedure Code, 198, Order 1 Rule 10 - Necessary Parties - A Lecturer's services at an aided College were terminated indirectly by the management due to over-staying leave caused by illness with no disciplinary proceedings taken - The Director is not a necessary party in the related writ petition.

Constitution of India, Article 226 - Alternate remedy - Once the writ petition is admitted by the Division Bench and preliminary objections over alternative remedies are resolved, the single Judge's plea for an alternative remedy is negated and the writ petition is to be disposed of on merits.
[Para 7] C. Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16 and 311 - Punjab Affiliated Colleges (Security of Service of Employees) Act, 1974 - Abandonment/Resignation/Termination - A Lecturer at an affiliated College had his services terminated indirectly due to prolonged leave caused by illness without being allowed to return to work by the management - The employee's constant communication with the management regarding his illness fails to presume voluntary abandonment or resignation - Not allowing an employee to join work amounts to indirect termination of services and violates principles of natural justice - Such actions are liable to be annulled.
[Paras 7 to 9] D. Constitution of India, Articles 226 and 311 - No work no pay - Back wages - A Lecturer had over-stayed leave due to illness and did not perform duties for an extended period - In these cases, it is appropriate to apply the principle of no work no pay.
