According to the Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923 sections 2(1) and 4(2), if a workman becomes permanently disabled due to injuries received on the job, compensation must be given.
In one particular case, the workman could no longer work as a Trammer because of a shortened leg and ankle stiffness.
Despite the management offering no alternative work, they terminated the workman which resulted in a 100% loss of earning capacity.
The disability was categorized as partial, but under section 2(1) it could have been classified as permanent.
Compensation must be assessed under section 4(b) of the Act, and simply offering employment to the workman's wife does not disqualify him from receiving compensation.
