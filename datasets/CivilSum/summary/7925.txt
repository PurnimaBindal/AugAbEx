Constitution of India, Article 226 - Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 25-F - The management did not challenge the Labour Court's finding that workmen worked for a long period of time and thus, Section 25-F was applicable, and therefore, the finding cannot be questioned for the first time in appeal by the management.
Only workmen filed a writ petition against the Labour Court's refusal to reinstate them.
[Paras 15 and 16]
Constitution of India, Article 226 - Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 25-F - The Labour Court rightly rejected the plea of the employer regarding abandonment and absence from duty as the evidence showed that the workmen used to make themselves available for work but the employer did not provide work or when the work was not available.
The finding of fact cannot be questioned for the first time in appeal.
[Para 17]
Constitution of India, Article 226 - Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 25-F - The plea that the workers were seasonal workers and Section 25-F was not applicable cannot be heard for the first time in appeal if it was not raised before the Labour Court or the Single Judge of the writ court and only based on bare assertions.
[Para 19]
Constitution of India, Article 226 - Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 25-F, 17-B, and 33-C(2) - The management is not responsible for the delay in the petition filed by the workmen against the award of the Labour Court.
As per Section 17-B, the Writ Court should not award full back wages in the absence of plea and proof of unemployment of workmen during the idleness period.
The management directed to pay 75% of the back wages and the workmen can approach the Labour Court under section 33-C(2) for the remaining 25%, with liberty to the management to prove gainful employment of the workman.
