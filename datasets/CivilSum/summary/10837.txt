Constitution of India, Article 226 - Departmental Enquiry - Trial - The petitioner seeks a stay of enquiry during the subsistence of the trial, stating that disclosing his defence may seriously jeopardize the case.
However, the court held that since the evidence and burden of proof in both proceedings are different and the conviction or acquittal in the criminal case will not affect the outcome of the enquiry, no prejudice will be caused by continuing the enquiry, which has to be completed expeditiously.
[Paras 3 and 5]
Constitution of India, Article 226 - Departmental Enquiry - Representation by Counsel - Denial - The petitioner's prayer for representation by a lawyer in the departmental enquiry was rejected as a representation by a lawyer in a departmental enquiry is not an absolute right.
The court held that the prayer of the petitioner has been rightly rejected.
[Para 7]
