Specific Relief Act, 1963, Section 34 - Relief of declaration - Laches - Plaintiff does not have an automatic right to a declaration - It is up to the court's discretion - If there is a delay on the plaintiff's part, this may disqualify them from receiving relief - The plaintiff's claim for relief involves the correction of his date of birth - He joined the bank's service in 1962 - There were inconsistencies with the date of birth stated in the birth register - The plaintiff filed a suit in 1993 to correct his date of birth, but it was returned for compliance and he did not follow up - He filed a fresh suit in 1996 - The plaintiff's application for correction of date of birth should not only consider the concerned public servant but also the public interest - The court must also consider the repercussions on employment if the date of birth is corrected - The plaintiff is not entitled to discretionary relief, even if he has established the correct date of birth, due to the delay.
[Paras 14, 15 and 20]

Specific Relief Act, 1963, Section 34 - Limitation Act, 1963, Article 113 - Correction of date of birth - The plaintiff joined the bank's service in 1962 - In January 1993, the plaintiff filed a suit to correct his date of birth, which was returned for compliance and not represented - In 1996, the plaintiff filed a second suit - The cause of action arose to the plaintiff in January 1993 - The second suit was filed beyond the period of 3 years from the accrual of the cause of action and is, therefore, barred by limitation.
[Para 20]

Words and Phrases - 'Right to sue' - The right to sue means the right to seek any relief by legal procedure - It arises when a cause of action arises - For there to be a right to sue, there must be a substantive right in asserting the claim - An infringement or the threat of infringement constitutes a cause of action and gives a right to sue.
[Para 20]

Civil Procedure Code, 1908, Section 100 - Second appeal - The High Court is duty-bound to rectify errors committed by the lower appellate court if it did not consider the law declared by the Honorable Supreme Court and the relevant provisions of the law.
