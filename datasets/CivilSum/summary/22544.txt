Section 18 of the Advocates Act of 1961 states that if a counsel provides vague evaluation in the plaint to mislead the plaintiffs, it is a case of misconduct.
Further, if the counsel assesses the valuation of the suit at a lower amount and charges a higher court fee, it amounts to cheating the plaintiffs.

As per Section 18 of the Advocates Act of 1961 and Section 45 of the Evidence Act of 1872, the court can examine signatures without an opinion from a hand-writing expert's report.
If the evidence is circumstantial and indicates that the signatures were not forged, there is no need for an expert's opinion.
