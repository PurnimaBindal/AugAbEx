The power of the Administrative Tribunals to enforce its orders and taking contempt proceedings is inherent and not limited by any rule of procedure except the principles of natural justice.
The jurisdiction contemplated by Articles 129 and 215 of the High Court cannot be whittled down by even legislative enactment.
Provisions of Contempt of Courts Act, 1971 are in addition and not in derogation of the Constitution and cannot be used for limiting or regulating the exercise of jurisdiction contemplated by the said two Acts.
The Tribunal has the powers to issue writs as per Article 226 and 227 of the Constitution equal to the High Court.
The High Court cannot create or assume power to inflict a new type of punishment other than the one recognised and accepted by Section 12 of the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971.
An appeal from the decision of the Tribunal will directly lie before the Supreme Court within 60 days from the date of order in view of the specific provisions of Section 19 of the Contempt Act, 1971 read with Section 17 of the Administrative Tribunals Act.
The power of the High Court is judicial superintendence and not administrative superintendence.
The jurisdiction conferred on the administrative tribunal is not only jurisdiction of the High Court but also of the subordinate Courts as to specified matters.
Section 30 of the Administrative Tribunals Act is merely declaratory of proceedings before the Tribunal.
