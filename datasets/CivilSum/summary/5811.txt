According to the Industrial Disputes Act of 1947, Section 2(00), abandonment means leaving work without notice.
However, this workman, an illiterate person, had sent a registered letter to request that his leave be extended due to illness.
The termination of his services was consistent with the fact that he had gone to the factory and asked the Personnel Officer to allow him to join the service.
The Single Judge was right in holding that the workman did not abandon the service and his services were terminated, amounting to retrenchment.
[Paras 8 and 9] B.
If it is established that there was no abandonment on the part of the workman, then the termination would amount to retrenchment.
[Para 10] C. Under Clause (b) of Section 25-F of the Industrial Disputes Act of 1947, the appellant must work out the total amount payable to the workman.
Since the appellant has not done so, it cannot be said that the provisions of Section 25-F were complied with.
The termination order, which amounts to retrenchment, is illegal and cannot be sustained.
