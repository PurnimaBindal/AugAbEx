According to Sections 10 and 12(5) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, a delay of 16 years in seeking reference for denial of reference cannot be ignored.
The government is only bound to refer a case for adjudication when satisfied with the request.
Although there is no time limit for raising a dispute and making a reference, it cannot be an abstract proposition that a dispute is tenable whenever a worker chooses.
The proposition stands contrary to the law established by the Supreme Court in Dhan Kunwar and Sham Lal's case.
Additionally, the government's refusal to refer the case on the ground that the worker did not raise a dispute with the employer with whom they were working was not illegal or unjustified.
