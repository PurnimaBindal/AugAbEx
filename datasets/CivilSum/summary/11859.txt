According to the Constitution of India, Articles 226/227 and Govt.
Instructions, adverse remarks made in an Annual Confidential Report can result in the withholding of the Efficiency Bar.
However, reporting officers do not have the jurisdiction to make remarks in an A.C.R.
unless they have seen the officer's work for a minimum of three months.
This requirement serves two purposes: to prevent officers from being unjustly praised and to prevent the degradation of an officer's record if they have not yet served for three months under a reporting officer.
In one case, a petitioner served less than three months under a D.I.G.
of Police, who therefore had no authority to make adverse remarks in the petitioner's A.C.R.
The court held that the withholding of the petitioner's Efficiency Bar was unjustified and that their case for crossing it should be reconsidered by the competent authority, as the adverse remarks were made without jurisdiction or justification.
