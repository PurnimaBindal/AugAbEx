The selection committee for the post of Professor in the department of social work at Punjabi University chose not to promote the petitioner, the Reader, and Head of Department.
The petitioner was not satisfied with the decision and challenged the selection of the respondent on the grounds of bias and malpractice.
The court ruled that the Vice-Chancellor had the power to condone the delay in the respondent's application, and the petitioner himself had suggested the experts on the selection panel.
Therefore, the petitioner could not question the integrity of the committee's deliberations.
The Selection Committee approved the selection, and the Vice Chancellor had the right to transfer the position of Professor from one department to another to benefit the department concerned.
The court dismissed the writ petition.
The Vice-Chancellor had the power to send the respondent's application to the Selection Committee, even though he had applied after the last date set out in the advertisement.
The court found no evidence of malpractice in the Vice-Chancellor's actions.
The exercise of this power could not be viewed with suspicion without sufficient grounds.
