According to Army Pension Regulations 1961 and Army Rules 1954, certain diseases are considered attributable or aggravated due to military service for the purposes of disability pension, including hypertension, schizophrenia, neurosis, and epilepsy.
Simply because the cause of a disease cannot be identified does not necessarily disqualify a person from receiving disability pension.
However, experts must come to a reasoned decision in accordance with the rules as to whether the disease in question is constitutional and disqualifying.
[Paras 2, 3 & 4] B.
To qualify for disability pension, the disability or disease must be attributed or aggravated by military service.
If a medical opinion denies such a connection, it must be supported by material and reasons, otherwise it may be considered arbitrary and dismissed.
[Para 4] C. When claiming disability pension, petitioners must avoid delay and laches, as any arrears will not be paid beyond three years and two months prior to the date of filing of the writ petition.
