Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 33(2) (b) - Misconduct/Termination of Services/Wilfull disobedience/Insubordination - The arbitrator ruled that the workmen accused of insubordination were guilty of misconduct, however, the workmen disputed the allegations stating that they weren't proficient in operating newly installed devices - Workmen argued that no training was delivered to them regarding the new devices - The arbitrator's ruling was considered perverse and baseless - Workmen ordered to be reinstated with all consequential benefits.

Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 33(2)(b) - Misconduct/Termination/Insubordination - The workmen refused to operate a newly installed machine, citing a lack of competence and training to operate the device - The punishment decision of discharge for insubordination was deemed disproportionate - Award of punishment quashed.
[Para 22]  D. Constitution of India, Article 226 - Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 10-A - Parties appointed a private person as their arbitrator, which was akin to a quasi-judicial decision - If the arbitrator's award found to be perverse or unreasonable, it is subject to the writ jurisdiction - If the award is contrary to law or evidence, it is liable to be quashed.
