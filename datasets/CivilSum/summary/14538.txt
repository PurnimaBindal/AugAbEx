According to the Constitution of India, specifically Articles 14 and 226, probationary employees may be terminated without notice if it is based purely on unsatisfactory performance and does not amount to stigma.
However, if the termination is stigmatic, even if not initially apparent, the court may investigate to determine the true reason for termination.
If a termination occurs on account of pending enquiry by vigilance authorities, it is not a simpliciter termination and may be stigmatic.
Failure to provide notice and an opportunity to be heard would constitute an infraction of principles of natural justice, rendering the termination illegal.
The employer's appeal in this case was dismissed and the single judge's order was upheld.
