Article 226 of the Constitution of India, along with Section 10 of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, discusses the validity of a reference made by a State Government, where the appropriate Government is the Central Government.
It was held that since the State Farms Corporation of India Pvt Ltd. had taken over administrative control of the establishment, the petitioner-employer was not an agent of the Central Government for internal administration.
Additionally, the powers outlined in Section 39 of the I.D.
Act, 1947 are given to the State Government, while the Central Government has reserved the right to exercise powers only when it deems necessary.
Therefore, the reference made by the State Government was valid.

Sections 10 and 11-B of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, along with Articles 226 and 21 of the Constitution of India, discuss the termination of employment and the payment of backwages.
It was held that on setting aside termination orders, orders for payment of back wages should generally be followed, except in certain exceptional cases.
It was also noted that just because the workmen were able to survive during the termination period, it cannot be presumed that they were gainfully employed.
This follows the precedent set in 1979 AIR 720 FB and RSJ (1950-1988) SC 678.
