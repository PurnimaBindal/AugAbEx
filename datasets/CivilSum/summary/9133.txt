Constitution of India, Article 226 - Speaking order - When a petition is dismissed in limine with no speaking order, the dismissal does not create a bar of res judicata according to Civil Procedure Code, 1908, Section 11.
[Para 13]
Constitution of India, Article 226 - Civil Procedure Code, 1908, Section 11 - The principle of res judicata applies to writ petitions filed under Article 226.
If a petition is dismissed on its merits after contest, the decision binds the parties if the cause of action is the same.
[Para 14]
Constitution of India, Article 226 - Civil Procedure Code, 1908, Section 11 - The correctness of a judicial decision has no impact on whether or not the decision can be considered res judicata.
Instead, it is necessary to determine whether the matter in issue has been heard and finally decided by a competent court.
If so, res judicata applies, and even an erroneous judgment is binding unless reviewed or reversed by a higher court.
[Para 15]
Constitution of India, Article 226 - The issue raised in the present petition is the same as the one raised by the petitioner in their earlier writ petition.
The grounds and prayer are also substantially similar.
The fact that two resolutions upon which the petitioner relies were not mentioned in the previous court order does not mean that they were not taken into account.
The judgment in the previous writ petition is binding in the present writ petition and warrants dismissal based on Civil Procedure Code, 1908, Section 11.
