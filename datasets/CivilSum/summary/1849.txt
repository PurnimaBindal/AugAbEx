Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 311 - Departmental and Criminal proceedings - Acquittal in criminal proceedings doesn't absolutely bar continuation of departmental proceedings - The required degree of proof is not the same for both proceedings, allowing both to occur simultaneously except when the criminal proceedings are based on the same facts and the accused's defence may be prejudiced - In such cases, it is proper to await the result of criminal proceedings, but ordinarily departmental proceedings should not be stayed after acquittal in the criminal case - The disciplinary authority may take into consideration evidence from both proceedings, but cannot rely on the same set of evidence to impose penalties if the accused is acquitted in the criminal case on merit - If the allegations constituting the basis of the departmental enquiry and criminal trial are not identical, the disciplinary authority is not bound by the finding recorded by the court of competent jurisdiction - [Para 19]
