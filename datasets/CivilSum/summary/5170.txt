According to the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Schedule II Item 3 and the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946, Section 3(1), workmen who fail to report at a transferred place despite reminders will have a valid inference drawn against them.
Since the posts held by workmen are transferable and their service is governed by Standing Orders, inference can be drawn under Clause 16 of such Orders.
The probable conclusion is that the workmen had no intention of serving in different places and hence, the inference is rightly drawn.
[Para 24]
Inference under common law cannot be easily drawn unless it can be legitimately drawn from the length of absence or surrounding circumstances.
However, when parties agree upon terms and conditions of service which are included in Certified Standing Orders or Service Rules, the doctrine of common law would not be relevant.
[Para 15]
According to the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 11A, a probationer can be discharged simpliciter without any stigma or attribute of inefficiency.
The order issued by the employer in such cases is proper.
