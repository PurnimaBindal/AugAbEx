Constitution of India, Articles 226/227 - Writ jurisdiction - Confidential report - The assessment of an employee's work and conduct in a confidential report is based on everyday observations, and the Reporting Officer must act bona fide and make objective assessments.
However, documentary evidence cannot support every observation, so long as the report is made in good faith and some evidence supports justifiable allegations - the Writ court cannot normally interfere.
[Para 6]

Constitution of India, Articles 226/227 - Adverse remarks - Representation - Evidence - The petitioner exhibited behaviour and conduct that was not symbolic of discipline, such as adopting a pick-and-choose method of passing bills/cheques and showing extra interest in passing certain bills while delaying the processing of others.
There was sufficient material on the basis of which the Reporting Officer could make observations in the confidential report, and the petitioner's representation was rejected after obtaining the Reporting Officer's comments.
The Court dismisses the writ with costs because the petitioner made reckless allegations without any basis.
[Paras 7, 8, and 9]
