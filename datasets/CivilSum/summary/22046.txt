The Advocates Act of 1961, Sections 35, 36-B, and 38 - Professional misconduct - During a case, a lawyer should be fair to all parties involved, including the court and the opposing party - The administration of justice must remain pure and uncorrupted - The bar is responsible for selecting judges - Lawyers should not act in a way that may damage the reputation of the justice system - While performing their duties, lawyers are officers of the court and must ensure their actions do not interfere with the process of justice - In this case, the appellant's purchase of a disputed property from their client with the intention of depriving the opposing party of the litigation's outcome was seen as misconduct by the Bar Council, leading to a one-year suspension from practicing law.
