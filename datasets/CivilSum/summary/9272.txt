Article 300-A of the Constitution of India and Army Rules 1954, Rule 180 stipulate that a reasonable opportunity must be given to those whose character or military reputation is at stake during an inquiry - failure to comply denies the valued opportunity guaranteed by the rule, vitiating the inquiry.
[Para 9]
Rule 180 of the Army Rules, 1954, defines desertion as a total and utterly unpatriotic attitude that reflects negatively on a soldier's character and military reputation - respondents' argument that the rule does not apply to those accused of desertion is untenable.
[Para 8]
The Constitution of India's Articles 226, 300-A, and 311, in conjunction with Army Rules 1954, Rule 180, mandate natural justice during a pension inquiry and necessitate compliance with Rule 180 - failure to comply results in a vitiating trial, and the period of imprisonment must be counted towards qualifying service for pension purposes - the petitioner is entitled to pension after completing 15 years of qualifying service.
