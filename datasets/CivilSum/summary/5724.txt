Article 311(2) of the Constitution of India and Punjab Civil Services (Punishment and Appeals) Rules, 1970, Rule 9(4)(i) state that the non-supply of an enquiry report before imposing punishment violates principles of natural justice.
The Supreme Court, in Mohmd.
Ramzan Khan's case, applies this to cases of punishment subsequent to the judgement.
If the Rules call for an enquiry report, failure to provide this report causes serious prejudice to the rights of the delinquent, and the punishment order is liable to be quashed.
[Paras 4, 5, and 6]
During disciplinary proceedings, if the delinquent has not been paid subsistence allowance, this violates their statutory right to subsistence allowance.
The inference will then be of a denial of fair trial unless proven otherwise.
This ultimately prejudices the cause of the delinquent, and the punishment order is violative of both the principles of natural justice and the rules.
[Para 6]
