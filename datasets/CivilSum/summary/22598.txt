The Industrial Disputes Act, Section 33(2)(b) - Limited scope of enquiry - For proceedings under Section 33(2)(b) of the Industrial Disputes Act, the scope of adjudication is limited to see only the existence of prima facie case - Even if approval is granted to the proposed action of the management against the worker under Section 33(2)(b), the worker can challenge the order passed by the employer before the Industrial Tribunal - Refusal of approval to termination on the ground that passengers of the ill-fated bus were not examined against the driver despite sufficient evidence would be a wrong principle.
[Paras 8, 11, and 18]

The Industrial Disputes Act, Sections 33(2)(b) and 10 - Evidence in domestic inquiry - Strict principles of Evidence Act are not applicable in a domestic inquiry - The principles of natural justice are applicable - The standard of proof required in a domestic inquiry and a criminal trial is different - Preponderance of probability would suffice in the departmental inquiry - Proof beyond all reasonable doubt is not necessary - Where extensive circumstantial evidence is available against the delinquent, the principle of Res ipsa loquitur applies, and the onus shifts upon the delinquent to rebut the evidence and prove that he was not at fault.
[Paras 10 to 13 and 16]
