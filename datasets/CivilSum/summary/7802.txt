The Rajasthan Agriculture University Resolution dated 22nd January, 1998 states that employees seeking regularisation must satisfy particular conditions, including passing the requisite qualifications, trade test, and interview.
Those who fulfill these conditions are entitled to be regularised from the date of their original appointment.
Those who do not meet these conditions at the time of their initial entry into service will be considered and regularised from the date of fulfilling such conditions, provided that their entry in service was not otherwise tainted as a backdoor entry and that they are not being considered for an existing post.
[Paras 12, 14 to 27]

The distinction between regularisation and regular appointment is addressed in the Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16, and 226.
Whereas regularisation is the appointment against a post that exists in the present, appointments which are inherently bad and void because they go against the rules do not deserve consideration for regularisation.
The necessity for a regularisation scheme for those who have continued on an ad hoc, casual, or temporary basis without a permanent regular status in service is to avoid any arbitrariness in public employment.
[Paras 12, 13 and 30]

Amendment of rules reducing chances of future promotion is not a violation of incumbent rights, as the principle of avoiding stagnation in a particular post does not relate to conditions of service as such.
[Para 29]
