Articles 14 and 39 of the Constitution of India mandate equal pay for equal work, and it is reasonable to classify employees based on experience and length of service.
A daily wage worker who is engaged as a typist for a specific job without being entrusted with the duties and responsibilities of a regular typist is not entitled to the same pay as a regular typist.
However, the daily wage worker is entitled to the regular pay scale of the initial stage with all other allowances.
The court followed AIR 1987 SC 2342.

As per Articles 14 and 311 of the Constitution of India, a daily wage employee has a right to continue and has an interest in work.
A daily wage typist who was allowed to work on short-term/leave vacancies on regular posts and whose work was not shown to be unsatisfactory cannot be terminated on the ground that he was not taking interest in his work.
The employee is entitled to reinstatement on daily wages.
