The Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 10, 2(j) and 12(5) - Reference - Industry - Industrial Dispute - According to section 2(j) of the Act, respondent No. 2 is an industry - If an industrial dispute exists, the State Govt. has to make a reference of dispute for adjudication to Labour Court - There is no provision in the Act except section 25-A making the applicability of definition of factory as contained in Factories Act, 1948 - As there is no provision in Punjab Shops and Commercial Act, 1958, excluding the jurisdiction of Labour Court to adjudicate on a dispute, the order of State Govt. declining to refer dispute to Labour Court on the ground that dispute is covered by Punjab Shops and Commercial Establishment Act, 1958, is incorrect.
[Para 4] B Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 25-A, 25(c), 2(e), 25-F and 10 - Industrial Dispute - Reference - Even though workmen employed in an industrial establishment are less than fifty, the provisions of Section 25-F, which includes recruitment compensation, are applicable - There is no other provision which excludes the jurisdiction of Labour Court to decide a dispute between workmen and the employer, even though the industrial establishment does not come within the definition of a factory as defined in Factories Act, 1948 - Therefore, refusal to refer the dispute to Labour Court by Govt. cannot be sustained - The State Govt. was directed to refer dispute to Labour Court for adjudication.
[Para 4] C. Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 25-F - Recruitment compensation - Industrial dispute - The petitioner's reception of retrenchment compensation and whether there is compliance with provisions of Section 25-F of the Act can be decided only by the Labour Court - It is not for State Govt. to go into the merits of the dispute - State Govt. has to see only whether there exists an industrial dispute within meaning of Section 2K of the Act.
