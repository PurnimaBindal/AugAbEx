According to Section 36 of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, the other party can object to the appointment of a legal practitioner, which cannot be overruled by the Tribunal/Labour Court.
However, the court can still appoint a legal practitioner as an amicus curiae for its assistance.
The holding of a law degree or prior legal practice does not prevent management from being represented by such a person.
The court should not invoke the theory of injustice or other consequences to choose a strained interpretation when the language of S. 36 is clear and unambiguous.
