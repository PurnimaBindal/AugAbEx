According to the Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 39(d), the doctrine of "equal pay for equal work" has been recognized in judicial work.
This is not an abstract principle, but a substantial one.
However, for this principle to apply, there must be common work and employer.
It is clear that the nature of work of District and Sessions Judges is the same, although the pendency of cases may differ.
This cannot be a ground to deny equal pay for equal work.
The burden of proof cannot be placed on the employees to prove equivalence of duties, but it is for the authorities to prove the difference.
In a historical precedent, before the declaration of Delhi as a Metropolitan city, the judicial officers of both Union Territories of Delhi and Goa had the same duties and responsibilities, and their pay scales were identical.
Therefore, judicial officers of Goa were entitled to the same pay scales up until the date Delhi was declared a metropolitan city.
