The Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 311, allows for both departmental and criminal proceedings to take place on the same charges related to tampering with official records.
In this case, an employee was acquitted in a criminal trial, and the charge against the employee required expert testimony, which was not produced in the departmental enquiry.
As a result, the order of punishment was quashed, and the case was not remanded due to the long-standing litigation and financial loss suffered by the employee.
[Paras 8, 9, and 10] B.
When an employee is suspended, they are entitled to suspension allowances, which may include increments for the suspension period.
In this case, the employee's suspension period was treated as a duty period, and they were paid 99% of their wages as a suspension allowance.
As a result, the employee will also be entitled to increments for the suspension period, and their pay should be fixed as it would have been in the normal course.
