Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 10 - Industrial Dispute - Reference - Government's power to refuse reference to adjudication cannot be based on the fairness of the domestic enquiry and appropriateness of the punishment awarded.
[Para 11] B.
If an industrial dispute is not referred based only on the report of a Conciliation Officer, the Government cannot adjudicate rival claims or comment on the penalty imposed.
[Para 12] C. Once a matter is referred to a Labour Court or Industrial Tribunal, there is no obligation on the part of the court/tribunal to consider all objections of the management.
[Para 13] D The Government is not authorized to adjudicate matters of law and facts, as per Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 10.
[Para 14] E. In the absence of prescribed limitations for raising a dispute in the statute, the Government cannot refuse reference on the ground of stale claim.
[Paras 18, 31 and 32] F. Grounds for charges under Section 12(3) cannot be a basis for the Government's refusal to refer a dispute.
[Para 27] G The Govt. cannot decide on whether or not an appraiser is a workman.
