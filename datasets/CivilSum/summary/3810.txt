Rajasthan Technical Education Service Rules, 1973 and 1986 are not violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India just because some of their conditions are different.
The condition of five years of experience as a Head of Department for a promotion to Principal has been fixed in accordance with the relevant considerations and, therefore, cannot be considered arbitrary or discriminatory.
[Paras 2, 8, 12, 13, 14, 17, 18, 20, 22 to 27]
The High Court cannot issue a mandate to the Government to legislate in a particular manner regarding the promotion to a particular post.
It is up to the Rule Making Authority to prescribe the qualification and experience for the post, and this is an exercise of legislative power by the Government under Article 309.
[Paras 9, 10, 11]
The classification of employees for different conditions of service is not a violation of Article 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India.
As long as there is equality and uniformity within each group, and the classification is not palpably arbitrary, the law will not be called discriminatory.
The reasonableness of the classification is to be judged with reference to the object of making the rule, and it cannot be dubbed as arbitrary or discriminatory based on the difference in service conditions.
[Paras 15 to 19]
