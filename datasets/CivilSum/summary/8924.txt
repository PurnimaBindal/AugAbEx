According to the Industrial Disputes Act of 1947, Sections 2(oo) and 25-B(2)(a), a workman who has been terminated is entitled to retrenchment compensation only if he has been in continuous service with the management for at least one year before the termination.
Furthermore, a workman will be considered to be in continuous service for one year if he has worked for at least 240 days during a period of 12 calendar months preceding the termination.
In this case, the workman had not worked for 240 days during any period of 12 calendar months and had been working intermittently as a daily wager.
Because he had not put in one year of continuous service, the provisions of Section 25-F did not apply, and the Labour Court's decision to declare the termination illegal or unjustified was not correct.
The award was set aside.
