Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985, Section 5(6) - Constitution of India, Articles 323-A and 323-B and 227 - Administrative Tribunals have the power to hear matters where the vires of statutory provisions are questioned - They may also test the vires of subordinate Regulations and Rules - Tribunals are not authorized to question the vires of the Statute under which they were established, their role is exclusively supplementary - Their decisions are subject to scrutiny before the Division Bench of High Court - Tribunals are the Courts of first instance, litigants may not directly approach the High Courts even if they question vires of statutory legislation - Supreme Court's directions will come into effect prospectively.

Constitution of India, Articles 226, 227, 323-A and 323-B - Tribunals will act as only the Courts of first instance - All decisions of Tribunals shall be subject to scrutiny before Division Bench of High Courts - Supreme Court's directions making the decisions of Tribunals amenable to scrutiny before Division Bench of High Court will come into effect prospectively.

Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985, Section 5(6) - Constitution of India, Articles 323-A and 323-B - The provision for at least two Members of Tribunal to hear a question that involves the interpretation of a statutory provision or rule in relation to Constitution, one of whom must be a Judicial Member, is valid and constitutional.

Constitution of India, Articles 323-A and 323-B - Administrative Control over Tribunals - It is desirable that all such Tribunals should be under a single nodal Ministry to oversee their working until such a body is set up - A wholly independent agency should be established for administration of Tribunals for their supervision - The Ministry is authorized to appoint an independent supervisory body to oversee the working of Tribunals.

Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985, Sections 5 and 28 - Constitution of India, Article 323-A, 323-B - Appointment of Administrative members of Tribunals cannot be stopped - Selection Committee will ensure that administrative members have the appropriate background to deal with such cases.
[Para 97]
Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985, Section 28 - Constitution of India, Articles 32, 226, 323-A and 323-B - Judicial review of decisions of Administrative Tribunals - High Courts and Supreme Court's power of Judicial review over legislative action is an integral and essential feature of the Constitution and cannot be excluded - "Exclusion of jurisdiction" clauses in Article 323-A and Article 323-B to the extent they exclude the Jurisdiction of High Court and the Supreme Court under Articles 226/227 and 32 are unconstitutional - Section 28 of Administrative Tribunals Act and the "exclusion of jurisdiction" clauses in all other Legislations enacted under Article 323 and 322-B would be unconstitutional.
