The UP Promotion by Selection in Consultation with Public Service Commission (Procedure) Rules (1970), Rules 5, 7 and 13 alongside with UP Public Service Commission (Limitation of Functions) Regulations (1954), Regn.
3 clearly states that ad hoc promotion cannot be counted towards seniority.
If an employee was promoted on an ad hoc basis without following the rules and without consultation with Commission, the period of officiation cannot be considered for purposes of seniority.
[Para 66]

Relief that was not claimed in earlier writ petition cannot be claimed in subsequent writ petition according to the Civil Procedure Code, 1908, Section 11, Order 2, Rule 2, which enshrines the principles of constructive res judicata.
It is further stated in the same provision that such claim is barred by estoppel.
[Paras 69, 75 and 77]

When filing a writ petition, non-joinder of necessary parties may have an effect.
For instance, if a seniority list is being challenged, then persons likely to be affected in case seniority list is disturbed should be impleaded as respondents, or else the petition cannot be entertained.
This is enshrined in Civil Procedure Code, Order 1, Rule 10 alongside Constitution of India, Article 226.
