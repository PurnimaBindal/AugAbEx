Section 25F of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 applies to daily rated workmen, and the mere irregularity or illegality in the appointment process is not sufficient to refuse to follow the provision of Section 25F [Para 8] B. Part-time workers also come under the definition of workmen under Sections 2(s) and 2(oo) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.
The decisive factor in determining the label of a person being a workman is the work for which he was hired and not his designation.
[Para 9] C The order of termination of a part-time worker who has worked for 240 days in a year, and whose work has been satisfactory, cannot be supported if there is a non-compliance of Section 25F before the termination of his service.
[Paras 12 and 13] D The High Court can interfere with erroneous assumptions, excess of jurisdiction, refusal to exercise jurisdiction, violation of principles of natural justice, arbitrary or capricious exercise of authority or discretion, and arriving at a finding which is perverse or based on no material under Article 227 of the Indian Constitution.
However, the findings of the Labour Court based on evidence cannot be interfered within writ jurisdiction as they are not based on no evidence and are not perverse.
