According to Section 25(O) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, a State Government granted permission for a unit to be closed.
However, it was alleged that the permission was granted in a casual and perfunctory manner without taking into account all the facts and circumstances.
It is established law that the viewpoints of the workers' union must be considered, as they are an essential component.
Additionally, allegations that workers adopted the `slow down production' principle to cause losses are not valid.
If a unit becomes unviable, it should not be forced to continue by incurring liabilities through the wages of the workmen.
The petitioner has not demonstrated how losses can be retrieved, and finance is essential.
Once it is lost, machinery cannot function.
Furthermore, there is no evidence to suggest that the decision was illegal, infirm, or against natural justice principles.

As per Para 11, the registration of a trade union is not necessary to have the right to be recognized or heard.
An unregistered union can legally represent the workman and cannot be denied the right to be heard.
