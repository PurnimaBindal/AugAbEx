The Industrial Disputes Act of 1947, Sections 10(5) and 18(3) - Regarding the power to add a party to a reference - Sub-section (3) of Section 18 of the Act makes it clear that the legislature did not intend to give the appropriate government exclusive power under sub-section (5) of Section 10 of the Act to add a party to a reference.
As a result, the tribunal can summon parties other than those who are parties to the industrial dispute.

B.
The Industrial Disputes Act of 1947, Sections 10(5) and 18(3) - Regarding the scope of reference - The tribunal was referred to whether the claim for wages of 37 workers was justified or not, which was at par with direct payment system workers of the Food Corporation of India.
The tribunal held that the deprivation of wages to 37 workers was arbitrary.
The contention that the tribunal could not extend the benefit to 37 workers who were not part of the settlement and that it was contrary to the law was dismissed.
Sub-section (4) of Section 10 of the Act gives the tribunal jurisdiction to adjudicate on points of reference and matters incidental to them.
As a result, the tribunal has the jurisdiction to adjudicate on whether deprivation of revised wages to similarly situated workers was arbitrary or not.
Accordingly, the petition was dismissed.
