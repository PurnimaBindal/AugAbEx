A workman who suffers from a certain disease and dies while being employed, and contributed or accelerated his/her death, the employer is responsible to pay compensation.
The Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923, Sections 22 and 3(1) states that the death of a workman in the course of employment, including the death of a driver who was plying a bus while on duty, and who stopped the bus and found that the nut bolts of the rear tyres were open and suffered a "heart attack" resulting in his death, due to stress and strain in his duties, where the employer is liable for compensation.
Even if the workman was negligent for not having the bus mechanically checked up in the workshop before starting the journey, this does not nullify the employer's liability.
Under the Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923, Sections 22 and 3(1), a workman who suffers a certain disease and dies, the employer is not responsible unless the disease was accelerated by employment, or the death was a direct result of the disease coupled with employment.
