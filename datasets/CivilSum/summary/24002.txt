Looking at Maharashtra Recognition of Trade Unions and Prevention of Unfair Labour Practices Act, 1971, Section 28 and the Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act, 1970, it is mandated that only the competent authority under the Contract Labour Act has jurisdiction to handle adjudication, if employees work under a contract regulated by this Act.
It is also within the appropriate government's purview to abolish the same.
However, if the case presented by the workmen claims that they were directly employed by the employer, then Labour Tribunals have the power to investigate and rule on unfair labour practices, but only when there is an employer-employee relationship between the parties.
These tribunals cannot rule on cases where the workers were employed by the contractor on behalf of the establishment.
Section 32 of 1971 Maharashtra Act does not enhance the court's jurisdiction beyond what is conferred upon it by other statutes.
Only in situations where the contract is a mere camouflage can the court enquire into the nature of the relationship between the parties.
