Articles 310 and 311 of the Constitution of India and Sections 18 and 19 of the Army Act, 1959 - Termination - Judicial review - Service members under the Army Act must be granted procedural safeguards when their services are terminated - Doctrine of pleasure - The concept of camouflage and the principle of lifting the veil would still apply despite the non-applicability of Article 311 of the Constitution - The court must unveil the true intent of an order alleged to have been issued with a covert goal.

Constitution of India, Articles 310 and 311 - Army Act, 1959, Sections 18 and 19 - Punishment - Enquiry - Camouflage is frequently used in military service law - Actions taken under the guise of an innocuous order, to pass muster as a simple termination of service, but is a punishment by way of action taken without an investigation is not conclusive - The court can determine the true nature of the action, even if it appears innocuous on the surface.
[Para 41] C Constitution of India, Articles 310 and 311 - Army Act, 1959, Sections 18 and 19 - The court can determine the true character of an order allegedly made under the guise of an innocuous action - If the court determines that the order, though in the form of a simple termination, is merely a ruse for a punishment, the court can still enforce the rights of the service member.
Anoop Jaiswal v. Government of India and another, 1984(2) SCC 369.
