Article 226 of the Constitution of India concerns misconduct and departmental inquiries, and the penalty of reduction to a lower stage in the time scale by two stages was imposed on a Branch Manager charged with misconduct.
The penalty was imposed by the competent authority under service regulations applicable on the date of the order, and the disciplinary action taken against the delinquent would not be vitiated merely because the petitioner was appointed by a different authority than the authority that passed the order imposing the penalty.
The appeal against the order imposing the penalty was also dismissed by the appellate authority after the due application of mind, who was the appointing authority before the amendment of service regulations, and there was no ground to interfere with the disciplinary proceedings.
[Paras 3 & 4]

Article 226 of the Constitution of India deals with departmental inquiries and the penalty of reduction to a lower stage in the time scale by two stages, which was imposed on a Branch Manager charged with misconduct.
Courts cannot go into the question of adequacy or inadequacy of evidence on which findings are recorded by inquiring authorities and disciplinary authorities when reviewing disciplinary action and findings.
They should only see if the findings recorded by the courts are based on acceptable evidence.
The findings in this case are based on acceptable, substantial, and legal evidence, and no interference is called for.
