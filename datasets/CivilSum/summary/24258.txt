The five-judge bench was constituted to clarify any doubts/ambiguities in the T.M.A. Pai case regarding the rights of minority educational institutions under Articles 141 and 30 of the Indian Constitution.
Under the majority judgement in T.M.A. Pai's case (Para 56), educational institutions are entitled to fix their own fee structure, taking into account the need to generate funds for the institution, provide facilities for students, and generate surplus for the betterment and growth of the institution.
However, non-minority institutions do not have the same advantages as minority institutions.
The judgement also distinguished between private unaided professional colleges and other educational institutions, with different rules applied for minority and non-minority institutions regarding filling seats and admissions.
Private educational institutions are not permitted to charge profiteering or capitation fees, and each state must establish a committee to approve fee structures and penalize institutes found to be engaged in unlawful practices.
Minority institutions are bound to give effect to inter se merit amongst students of their community and merit-based selection.
Finally, while minority educational institutions have the right to admit students from their own community, they are not immune to regulations made by the government or universities that ensure maintenance of standards and control over their functioning.
