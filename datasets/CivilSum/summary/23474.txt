When there is a delay in completing disciplinary proceedings, the court must consider the nature of the offense, the circumstances causing the delay, and any potential harm to the accused due to the delay.
While it is possible for the court to pass an appropriate order to address the accused's grievances, it is not necessary to quash the proceedings.
The court must carefully balance all factors and make a decision based on the totality of the situation.
In this case, the High Court's order to quash disciplinary proceedings was set aside, but the authorities were directed to consider the accused for promotion without reference to the pending allegations.

If there are allegations of mala fide, the parties involved must be made aware.
If a specific authority is not named in the proceedings, no findings of mala fide can be made against them.
