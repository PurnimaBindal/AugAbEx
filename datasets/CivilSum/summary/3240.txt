According to the Punjab Civil Service Rules, Volume I, Rule 326 (a) and (d), officers can be pre-maturely retired, and instructions issued by the government in 1983 stated that officers who have a 70% or above good record in the last 10 years can be retained beyond 55 years without infringing Rule 326.
While interpreting Rule 326(d), public interest must be taken into account, and an officer with a meritorious record can be allowed an extension beyond the age of 55 years in exceptional circumstances.
The state has the absolute right to retire any government employee, and it can issue instructions on this subject which would serve as guidelines for the competent authority to pass orders under Rule 326(d).
If the state government decides, as a policy, that "average" reports that are communicated should be treated as adverse while deciding the question of compulsory retirement of government officers, then such remarks would be treated as adverse, even though they may not be extremely bad.
The concept of 'weeding out dead wood' as embedded in Rule 326 (a) or (d) is inherent, but that is not the only ground available.
There may be varied reasons to be taken into consideration that would constitute public interest, which can lead to an order being passed under Rule 326(d).
Finally, if adverse remarks were "not fit for promotion" but were not communicated, then the same can be taken into consideration, and an order of compulsory retirement cannot be termed as arbitrary.
