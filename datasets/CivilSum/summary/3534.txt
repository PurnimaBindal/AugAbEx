Under the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 11-A, when considering whether to permit additional evidence to justify a punishment, the stage for granting such permission is not limited to the initial stage of filing a written statement.
If the Labour Court finds that an enquiry conducted by the management is flawed, it must permit the additional evidence to be presented at any subsequent stage, so long as the workman has a chance to rebut it.
A flexible approach must be taken by the Court/Tribunal, rather than being limited by a strict interpretation of the law.
AIR 1972 SC 1031 and 2001 (2) SCT 1041 (SC) have been distinguished in 2005 AIR SCW 5525.

Under Section 11-A of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, when the Labour Court replaces a dismissal with a lesser punishment, sufficient evidence is assumed to have been provided.
However, if a dismissal needs to be converted to an acquittal, more consideration is needed.
If the management does not have sufficient evidence on file, they must be given an opportunity to present additional evidence.
Finally, in cases of natural justice violations, the workman must be given an opportunity to respond.
Granting leave to the parties instead of remitting the case for a new inquiry would provide a timely resolution that would benefit everyone.
