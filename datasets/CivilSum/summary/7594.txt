Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 2(j) and 33-C(2) - Industry - A workshop established for handicapped workers cannot be excluded from the definition of 'industry' based solely on its social purpose - For an activity to be considered an 'industry', there must be a systematic collaboration between employer and employee, and the production of goods and services aimed at satisfying human desires and needs must be present - The absence of a profit motive or gainful objective is not relevant.

Minimum Wages Act, 1948, Sections 17 & 26 - Disability- Piece rate workers - The wages of disabled employees are protected regardless of their output, quality, and quantity of work - Therefore, the status of being disabled or a piece rate worker does not apply, particularly if the employer is a government establishment held to the Disabilities Act's non-discrimination provisions.
