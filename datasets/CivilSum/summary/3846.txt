The Railway Protection Force Act, 1957, Section 17 and Railway Protection Force Rules, Rules 153 and 156, outline the process for conducting departmental enquiries.
In this case, a constable took one day of leave and visited the Railway Hospital for a check-up, where it was determined that he had mental imbalance and was de-categorised.
The case was then referred to the Central Institution of Psychiatric, Ranchi, where he was admitted and later certified fit to join light duties.
However, the petitioner did not join back despite several opportunities and failed to seek an extension of leave.
Based on Rule 156, the authorities formed a prima facie opinion of unauthorised absence from duty.
The petitioner did not join the enquiry on the ground that the matter was sub-judice in court, but in the absence of any court direction, the enquiry proceeded.
Thus, the order advising the petitioner to join duty, failing which disciplinary action would be taken against him, had no infirmities.
Additionally, the order for removal passed by the disciplinary authority was also upheld, as the contention that the proposed enquiry violatEd principles of Natural Justice was not tenable, since several opportunities were given to the petitioner to participate in the enquiry.
