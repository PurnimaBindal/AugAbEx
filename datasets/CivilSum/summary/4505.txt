The Constitution of India, specifically Articles 14 and 16(1), states that appointments must be made in an equal opportunity manner by advertising the vacancies to the public.
Appointments made in violation of the Rules or not advertised to the public infringe against the right of equal opportunity for employment guaranteed by the Constitution.
Every Public Authority must adhere to the right of equality as guaranteed by the Constitution.
[Para 7]

In regards to appointment, Article 14 and 226 of the Constitution of India ensure that every citizen is entitled to the principles of natural justice and writ jurisdiction.
An order passed that affects the rights of the petitioner must be legal; therefore, regularisation of services should be effective from the date the appointment became legal.
Although principles of natural justice should be observed, writ jurisdiction power cannot be utilized to restore actions that are patently illegal.
Writ jurisdiction is designated to nullify actions by executive authorities that are arbitrary and contentious regarding constitutional provisions.
[Para 9]

Article 226 of the Constitution of India states that every person has a duty to provide court complete facts.
In this particular case, the petitioner submitted mis-statements regarding the provisions of the 1975 Rules, concealing the fact that he was interviewed by the Selection Board.
By failing to provide complete facts, the petitioner renounced his entitlement to discretionary relief, despite his duty to provide complete facts before the Court.
