The Constitution of India gives reservation for scheduled castes, scheduled tribes, and OBCs in public services and posts, including in educational institutions owned and controlled by the State Govt, or those receiving grant-in-aid from the Govt, except the institutions established under Article 30(1) of the Constitution.
The policy of reservation in public appointments promotes welfare of the people and gives socio-economic justice while preventing social injustice and exploitation.
The policy has to be applied fully while keeping in mind the efficiency of the administration.
A roster should be maintained to ensure the policy is not frustrated.
Even a single post which cannot be reserved has to be clubbed with other posts and calculated in the joint roster.
UP Public Services (Reservation for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and OBCs) Act, 1994, Sections 2(c), 3(5), and 4.
[Paras 5 to 11] B Recruitment procedures for each service or post in each cadre in each field, discipline, specialty, or super-specialty in educational institutions should be subject-wise.
This would make the available posts clear to both general and reserved candidates and if they fulfil qualifications and other requirements.
A joint roster should be applied to any single post of Professor, Reader or Lecturer in each faculty, discipline, specialty so that benefit may be given to the reserved categories.
