The Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923, Sections 2(1)(n), 19 and 22, and the Contract Labour (Regulation & Abolition) Act, 1970, Section 2(1)(b) clarify the definitions of Workman, Labour contractor, and Building contractor.
If a workman is hired through a Labour Contractor to aid in the construction of a house or building, the workman is considered to be employed by the building contractor and the owner of the plot/house, and the Labour Contractor is not a necessary party.
In the process of constructing a building, various operations are involved, but not every sub-contractor is a necessary party in a claim for workmen's compensation under the Act.
