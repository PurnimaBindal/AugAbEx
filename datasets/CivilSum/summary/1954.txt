According to the Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act, 1970 and its corresponding notification, the central government prohibited the employment of contract labour for certain trades, including cleaning, sweeping, dusting, and watching of buildings.
The petitioners claimed that they were engaged in looking after mishandled or detained baggage of passengers and therefore fell under the notification's purview.
However, it was determined that the notification was not applicable to their case because "watch and ward" specifically referred to guarding during the night shift, while the petitioners were managing and supervising a warehouse during the day.
[Para 8]
The petitioners argued that they had been working continually for 10 years and were entitled to be regularised.
However, since the Airports Authority of India (AAI) was only a Principal Employer and the petitioners had no direct relation to it, the appropriate remedy would be to raise an industrial dispute and seek reference to a labor court or Industrial Tribunal under the Industrial Disputes Act, where such disputes are adjudicated with oral and documentary evidence.
