A person seeking employment with a University did not provide information regarding certain lapses committed with the previous employer, including copying research work of other authors and passing it off as his own.
The previous employer had debarring him from research work, but this information was not passed on to the University while seeking a fresh appointment.
The University later found out and terminated the employee, giving one month's notice.
The employee's plea that his services had been terminated by way of punishment was held to be not tenable.
The employee had admitted his lapse with the previous employer and had not given this information to the University, so he was not entitled to contend that there was a breach of principles of natural justice.
Even if there were a breach of principles of natural justice, it does not automatically entitle the employee to relief.
The court can consider whether non-giving of opportunity has led to any injustice, as per the principle of law laid down in Managing Director, ESIL, Hyderabad v. B Karunakar, 1994 Lab IC 792.The principle of natural justice does not apply to a person who gets a benefit by adopting unfair means.
A person seeking employment without giving details of lapses committed with the previous employer cannot contend that he has not been dealt with evenly, as per paras 6 to 11 of the ruling.
