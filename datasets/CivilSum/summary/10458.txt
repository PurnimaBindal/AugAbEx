Sections 61(1)(a) and 62(1)(b) of the 1974 Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, as well as Sections 47(1)(b) and 2(a) of the 1981 Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, give the government the power to supersede the State Board for persistent default or in the public interest.
While notice and opportunity to show cause is required in the former case, it is not required in the latter.
The rule of hearing cannot be read as implicit in the provisions for exercising administrative power by the government under these Acts.
Section 62(1)(b) covers cases of mal-functioning, mal-administration, and misfeasance, and does not require notice or opportunity to the Board, Chairman, or its members.
