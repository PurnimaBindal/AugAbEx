The National Council of Teacher Education (NCTE) Act of 1993 must be followed by States and Universities - it is considered to be within the scope of Entry 66 of List I and Entry 25 of List III of Schedule VII to the Constitution and any State laws on Teacher Education Institutions and new courses will be invalid - States and Universities cannot encroach upon the field covered by Parliament - if the NCTE Act requires it, NOC from the State Government is irrelevant.
[Para 33, 39 to 41, 45, 46 & 48]
The Madras University Act of 1923 may be repealed up to the extent of repugnancy with the 1993 NCTE Central Act - repugnancy will have to be determined based on the usual tests for adjudging repugnancy under Article 254 - there should be direct conflict between the two statutes.
[Paras 33 & 35]
Regulations and Rules made by the NCTE regarding Teacher Education Institutions and courses are considered to have been made by Parliament - any enactment or policy by a State regarding the subject will be void - what cannot be done through statute cannot be done via executive fiat.
[Para 46]
Refusing to give NOC to establish a Teacher Training Institute based on local conditions despite fulfilling all conditions is a violation of rights under Articles 19(1)(g) and 21A.
[Para 47]
Maharashtra Universities must give affiliation to institutions recognised by the NCTE - Sections 82 and 83 of the Maharashtra Universities Act may not be applicable to the extent of Section 14(6) of the NCTE Act.
[Paras 48 & 49]
