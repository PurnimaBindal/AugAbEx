According to the Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923, if a worker suffers a heart attack during employment, they are entitled to compensation, as long as their work accelerated the disease and there is a casual connection.
The employer must prove that the employment had no connection with the heart attack, and the theory of res ipsa loquitur is applicable.
In this case, there is no evidence that the workman had heart disease before.
