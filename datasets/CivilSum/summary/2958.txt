Civil Procedure Code, 1908, Order 6, Rule 4 and Order 39, Rules 1 and 2 - Trade Unions Act, 1926, Section 18 - Injunction suit - Jurisdiction of Civil Court - Banking Regulation Act (10 of 1949), Section 36 AD - The plaintiff seeks an injunction against its employees - The defendants raised an objection, claiming that the plea does not disclose any cause of action and is barred under the Act - It is held that parties are only required to disclose material facts and not their evidence in the pleadings - Section 18(2) of the Act states that the jurisdiction of the Civil Court is not debarred if the act of the Trade Union is tortuous - Therefore, where demonstrations by employees have affected normal transactions of the bank and undermined the confidence of depositors, an injunction can be granted.
[Paras 8, 15, 17, 47, 48] B Banking Regulation Act, 1949, Section 36-AD - Trade Unions Act, 1926, Sections 2(g) and 18 - Injunction suit by bank - Jurisdiction of Civil Court - The bank has filed a suit for an injunction to restrain its employees from committing tortuous acts, interfering and obstructing in normal functioning - The defendants contend that their acts do not fall within the jurisdiction of the civil court being in furtherance of a trade dispute - It is held that the words "without any reasonable excuse" in Section 36 of the Act mean that a person cannot take a plea of trade union activity after committing an offence which ceases to be a trade union activity - Hence, the act of preventing transactions of the bank would fall within the purview of Section 36-AD, giving jurisdiction to the Civil courts, the demonstration being illegal.
