The Bar Council of India Training Rules, 1955 (as amended in July 1998) - Pre-enrollment training as an Advocate - The Bar Council of India does not have the authority to create regulations for pre-enrollment training, and the rules it created exceeded its rule-making authority under the Advocates Act.
Striking off the rules will only apply to future cases.
However, the Supreme Court recommended that the Act be amended to give the Bar Council the power to create regulations for training young entrants.
Until then, the Bar Council can consider the feasibility of creating regulations allowing enrolled advocates to receive in-practice training, which can be made under S. 49(1)(ah).
[Paras 24, 25, 26, 28, 29, 31, 32, and 41]  B. Enrolment as an Advocate - Once a person is admitted as an advocate on the State roll, they are automatically entitled to practice full-fledged in any court, including the Supreme Court.
[Para 13]  C. Framing of rules under the Advocate Act, 1961, Section 49A(1) - The Central Government's power to create rules is congruent to the Bar Council of India or the State Bar Council's rule-making powers.
[Para 13]  D. Rule Making power of Bar Council of India - The Bar Council has the authority to create rules that allow ineligible persons to be enrolled despite what is stated in S. 24(1), but it cannot be used to disqualify people from enrolling despite meeting the eligibility requirements under S. 24(1).
