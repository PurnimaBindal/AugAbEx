Under Constitution of India, Articles 311(2) and 226 - Punjab Police Rules, 1934, Rules 161 and 24(3), a writ court's jurisdiction to interfere with an administrative order that dispensed with a regular enquiry is limited when the authority responsible is competent to do so, has provided sufficient reasoning, and has judged that conducting a regular inquiry would not be reasonably practicable - A case in which a delinquent was found to have been conniving with terrorists and involved in terrorist activities met these conditions - The authority was justified in concluding that terrorists would not testify against the delinquent.
[Paras 7, 8, 9, 10 and 12]
In cases involving Constitution of India, Articles 311(2) and 226 - Punjab Police Rules, 1934, Rules 161 and 24(3), one cannot challenge a final order of dispensing with services on grounds previously raised and dismissed in a writ petition - This is because the dismissal of the earlier writ petition becomes res judicata between the parties - A delinquent who had challenged the initial order of dispensing with an enquiry without success was not entitled to challenge the final order on the same grounds again.
