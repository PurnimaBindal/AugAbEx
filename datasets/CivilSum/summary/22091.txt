Civil Procedure Code, Order 23 Rule 3 - Compromise of decree - To ensure the compromise is genuine and voluntarily entered into, the court may direct the appearance of the concerned party/parties - Counsel's duty is to assist the court in doing justice; they have no right to insist upon the court's acceptance of compromise in the absence of the parties - No appeal or revision is maintainable against the Trial Court's direction in this respect.
[Paras 16, 27 and 28]
Court Proceedings - The record of the proceedings made by the Court is sacrosanct - The correctness thereof cannot be doubted - The party aggrieved of may at most apply for its correction to the same court - Transfer of the presiding officer is not a ground for inaction - If deemed necessary, the proceedings can be sent to the place where the presiding officer stands transferred for correction.
[Paras 17 to 19]
Advocates Act, 1961, Section 37 - Misconduct - Advocates occupy a prime position in the process of administration, and justice delivery system - Any act or omission which interrupts or misdirects the sacred flow of justice or which renders a professional compromised in exercising the privilege of their profession would amount to misconduct - A lawyer has a duty towards his client to help their interest but within the parameters of the law - If the lawyer misleads or pressurizes the court, it amounts to professional misconduct which may result in disciplinary action.
[Paras 20, 21, 24, 25 and 27]
Constitution of India, Article 136 and 226 - Advocates Act, 1961, Section 37 - Unless based on non-reading or misreading of evidence, if the decision of the Bar Council has been based on a proper appreciation of facts and evidence, there would be no scope for interference by the Court - Findings of professional misconduct cannot be held unjustified if it is determined the counsel pursued a proposition of law that resulted in the miscarriage of justice to his own client by pressurizing the court to accept a compromise in his clients' absence with an ulterior motive.
[Para 28]
Advocates Act, 1961, Section 37 - The Bar Council of India has extensive jurisdiction under Section 37(2) of the Act - It can vary or reverse the State Bar Council's order and pass an order of punishment or may remit or remand the matter - The Bar Council may enhance punishment, but the advocate must be given a reasonable opportunity of showing cause against the proposed enhancement of the penalty.
