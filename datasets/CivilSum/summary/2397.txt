Section 11 of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, and Section 45 of the Road Transport Corporation Act, 1950, deal with the dismissal of a conductor charged for non-issuing tickets but charging fares.
The charges against the conductor related to breach of administrative order and negligence, and he was punished with dismissal.
However, the punishment was considered harsh, disproportionate, and unjustified.
Therefore, it was ordered to withhold the backwages of the interim period and reinstate the workman with the continuity of service.
The petition was dismissed.
[Paras 11 and 12]
The workman, a conductor, was charged with non-issuance of tickets after collecting fares from passengers, and the punishment of dismissal was imposed on him.
However, the checking report of the bus was submitted after a delay of 13 days, and the departmental inquiry was completed within one day, without supplying any document to the workman.
Thus, the departmental inquiry violated the principles of natural justice, and the punishment of dismissal was deemed hard and unjustified.
The workman was directed to be reinstated with full backwages.
[Para 11]
A dismissed worker for non-issuing tickets incurred severe punishment, raising questions about the morality of such service penalties.
The disciplinary authority, before awarding the extreme penalty of dismissal, should consider that a person would not deliberately omit to issue tickets to gain a few rupees at the risk of his job.
The real victim of such punishment is the family, whose future is rendered bleak, causing hardship to the society.
Moreover, the management also shares the losses, and someone else has to be placed in the dismissed worker's place.
If the worker is reinstated with backwages, then there will be double payment for a single job, passing on the loss to the common man, the taxpayer.
Non-issuance of tickets may be accidental.
