Two individuals were fired from the Railway Protection Force due to the same charges.
One individual challenged their termination in court and was reinstated.
The petitioner, who waited 10 years, filed a writ petition and explained the delay.
The other individual was also reinstated, and the petitioner promised not to demand back-pay until the writ petition was filed.
The writ was granted.
[Para 3]
The Railway Protection Force Rules, 1959, Rule 60 covers revision.
In such cases, the court should not normally interfere unless the authorities have failed to consider the facts.
The revising authority passed an order without addressing the petitioner's grounds and facts, and the authorities acted on conjecture.
The order was annulled.
[Paras 7,8, and 12]
The finding of stolen property is insufficient evidence to prove theft.
The authority lacks proof of the charges made, and the decision in this case relied solely on conjecture.
Surcharges are insufficient to prove the charges, and the order was quashed.
