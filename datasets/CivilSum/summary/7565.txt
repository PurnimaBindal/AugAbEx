The Workmen's Compensation Act (8 of 1923), Section 3 stipulates that compensation ought to be awarded if an employee dies as a result of an injury incurred during the course of their employment.
In this case, the employee was a watchman who remained on duty throughout the night.
After finishing his shift he developed chest pain and was subsequently declared dead at the hospital.
It is evident that the death was as a result of stress and strain leading to a cardiac arrest.
Applying the theory of notional extension, it can be concluded that the employee died during the course of his employment.
Therefore, the order to award compensation to the claimants was appropriate.

The Workmen's Compensation Act (8 of 1923), Section 3 states that compensation should be granted if death occurs as a result of an injury sustained during the course of employment.
The concept of `during the course of employment' can be extended from the time when the workman leaves home for work and returns home upon completion of their duty.
In this case, the employee died after finishing his shift.
It would be considered that his death occurred during the course of his employment.
