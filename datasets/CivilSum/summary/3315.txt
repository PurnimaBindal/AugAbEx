Constitution of India Article 226 - "Bundh" - High Court is authorized to declare the holding of "bundhs" unconstitutional - Political parties and organizers that call for "bundhs" are liable to compensate the government, public and private citizens for any loss suffered by them - The holding of "bundhs" tends to reduce the progress of the Nation by leading to a national loss of production.
[Para 18]
Constitution of India, Article 19 - Fundamental rights of calling bundh - No party has the right to call for a bundh on the basis of its freedom of speech and expression - Exercise of such a right infringes the fundamental right of another citizen who is equally entitled to exercise his rights.
[Para 14]
Constitution of India, Articles 226, 19 and 21 - The court has a duty to protect citizens' rights if they are curtailed or destroyed by the calling of and holding of a bundh, fundamentally restricting the free movement of citizens, their right to carry on their work, and their freedom - If no legislature law is enacted to prohibit, regulate or curtail bundh, the court will step in to safeguard the rights of citizens.
[Para 12]
"Bundh" Meaning- Bundh means everything is to be blocked or closed except for newspapers, hospitals, and milk supply - The intention is to otherwise affect those services - If the purpose includes, preventing milk supply, distribution of newspapers, travel restriction from attending work, it indicates a denial of citizens' natural rights, fundamental freedom and their exercise of fundamental rights.
[Para 7]
Constitution of India, Articles 19, 21 and 226 - "Bundh" - A call for a "bundh" implies a threat to the citizen, violates their fundamental rights.
