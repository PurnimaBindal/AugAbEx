Article 226 of the Constitution of India gives equitable jurisdiction for the appointment of officials.
In this case, a writ petition was filed challenging the selection of respondent No. 4 as Principal of Delhi Public School (DPS) RK Puram, but it was filed 15 months after the appointment.
The petitioner himself was a candidate for the selection and never questioned the validity of the selection committee.
When the petitioner learned that he was not appointed as Principal of Vasant Kunj School, he challenged the selection of Principal of RK Puram School.
The delay in filing the writ petition was not satisfactorily explained, and the petitioner acquiesced to the development of subsequent events.
As a result, the petitioner cannot now attack the validity of the selection made to the post of Principal of RK Puram School and is not entitled to any relief under equitable jurisdiction.

Rule 96(3)(a) of the Delhi School Education Rules, 1973 states that an educationist could be a person closely connected with the affairs of educational institutions.
The term 'educationist' is not well-defined and is a matter of opinion.
It was held that if an executive officer belonging to the Education Department of the Government could be considered as an educationist, any other person closely connected with the affairs of the institution could also be an educationist, including a Judge of the High Court.
