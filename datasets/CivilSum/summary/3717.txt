Rule 142 of the Border Security Force Rules, 1969 and Section 18(1) of the Border Security Force Act, 1968 prescribe a mandatory procedure for recording admissions of guilt of accused persons - The accused must be informed of the consequences of their admission, and of the prima facie evidence establishing the charges against them - Admission must be withdrawn where there is no prima facie evidence of the charges - Compliance with the Rule is necessary for admissions of guilt to be considered valid.
[Paras 9 to 11] B Absence without leave and desertion are distinct concepts - A mere absence cannot be considered desertion without evidence of animus deserendi - Procedure for declaring a deserter must be followed - Merely being arrested on civil charges such as rape cannot be considered desertion.
[Paras 13 to 16] C. In cases where both the Security Force Court and the ordinary criminal court have jurisdiction to try a defendant who is a Security Force member, if the option is exercised to hold a trial in the Security Force Court, retrial in the ordinary criminal court is not permitted.
