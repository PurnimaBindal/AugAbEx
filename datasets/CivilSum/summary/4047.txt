According to Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 311, non-payment of subsistence allowance during enquiry constitutes using economic pressure to break the defence of the delinquent.
If payment is not made despite demand of the official showing his inability to participate in the proceedings, the conduct of the employer will be completely unfair and illegal and will invalidate the entire proceedings taken against the delinquent.
[Paras 10 to 14]

Under Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 311, when the enquiry officer proceeded ex parte and declined the request of the delinquent for adjournment on the ground of non-payment of subsistence allowance, it violates the principles of natural justice.
Therefore, the punishing authority as well as the appellate authority who ignored this ground taken by the delinquent and passed and upheld the punishment order of dismissal from service, was found to be completely unfair, illegal, arbitrary and violative of principles of natural justice.
[Paras 11 to 14]

When charges of financial irregularities and fraud were claimed against the Principal of a College, it was discovered that no fraud or embezzlement of any funds had been proven.
Plus, proceeding ex parte against the Principal on the absence from duty charge did not allow for an explanation to be provided, hence no authority considered it.
Moreover, the punishment of dismissal was seen as totally disproportionate, unjustified, and uncalled for.
Therefore, the punishment order was quashed.
[Para 17]
