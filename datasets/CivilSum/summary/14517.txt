According to the Employees' Provident Fund and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952, Section 14-B, there is no stipulated time limit for the levy of damages.
Delayed levy, while not invalid, can be considered a mitigating factor in determining the appropriate quantum of damages.
For excessively long delays, a higher percentage may be appropriate, but in cases of marginal delay, token damages may be levied.
[Paras 3 and 6] B.
The relevant date for the levy of damages under the Employees' Provident Fund and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952, Section 14-B should be the date of payment by draft or cheque, and not the date of encashment.
In certain instances, payment may be made according to the prescribed method under the Employees Provident Fund Scheme within the allotted time, but the amount is credited after the due date due to postal or encashment delays.
In such situations, it would be unlawful and inappropriate to levy damages based on the date of credit.
