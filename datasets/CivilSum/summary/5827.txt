Article 144 of the Constitution of India and Section 11 of the Indian Medical Council Act, 1956 pertain to the reservation of seats in privately-run recognized medical colleges affiliated with Punjab University for various categories.
The Supreme Court's decision in Unnikrishnan J.P. etc. v. State of Andhra Pradesh set guidelines for regulating admission to professional colleges, including privately-established medical colleges, and directed the recognizing and affiliating authorities to enforce them in the conditions for granting and continuing recognition and affiliation.
Therefore, the state government and affiliating universities cannot reconsider the scheme made for reservation in paid and open seats for NRI and other candidates after Unnikrishanan's case.
The Supreme Court's directions will apply to all cases under Articles 141 and 144 of the Constitution.
Once the percentage of NRI seats is fixed out of the payment seats after the Supreme Court's decision, it is no longer open to any further discussion or modification.

Section 11 of the Indian Medical Council Act, 1956 enables the Medical Council of India to object to the filling of seats in excess of the limit specified by the Supreme Court in Unnikrishnan's case.
[Para 17] C. After the Supreme Court's directions in Unnikrishanan's case, any action or notification contrary to the scheme framed by the Supreme Court will not bind the authorities under the principle of Estoppel.
Therefore, if the posts are filled in excess of the percentage prescribed by the Supreme Court for a particular category, it must be cured either by removing the students in excess or by adjusting all seats in the following years.
