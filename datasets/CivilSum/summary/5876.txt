The Industrial Disputes Act, Sections 10 and 11, as well as the Constitution of India's Article 226, deal with fact-finding and writ jurisdiction.
A worker who accepts his dues without objection but fails to appear before the Conciliation Officer despite receiving notice to support his demand will not be considered compelling evidence in the Labour Court.
Additionally, if the demand notice and statement of claim are signed and verified only by the worker's representative, the worker's claim may not be persuasive.
The High Court should not act as an appellate body since the findings of the Labour Court that the worker's claims are unsatisfactory were sound, and the award was upheld, resulting in the dismissal of the petition.
