Industrial Dispute Act, 1947, Sections 10(2) and 10(1)(d) - Dearness allowance - It is impermissible to place a ceiling on dearness allowance for basic pay exceeding Rs. 500/- - Company's claim of neutralisation exceeding 100% appears incorrect - Tribunal concluded that the neutralisation ranged from 974% to 86%.
[Para 22]

Industrial Dispute Act, 1947 - Stagnation increment - The Court lifted the interim stay on the modified Award while granting leave to appeal - The Company cannot refuse to implement the Award for granting stagnation increment - The Tribunal needs to reconsider the same while analyzing the wages revision - It is not a valid reason to revise the emoluments upward just because the company is financially sound and in a position to absorb the additional burden - Junior executives receive certain privileges which the workers do not - The better approach is to make the junior executive grade more attractive rather than depriving the workers of what they have received for a long time.
[Paras 23 and 34]

Practice and Procedure - Jurisdiction - The Company invoked the jurisdiction on SB and did not challenge it - It is difficult to grasp how the Company can challenge the appellate Court jurisdiction - If SB had jurisdiction to hear writ petitions against the Industrial Tribunal's decision and his jurisdiction was not questioned by the Company - The Company cannot challenge the appellate Jurisdiction of DB because the Letters Patent confer that jurisdiction - A belated plea cannot and should not be entertained.
