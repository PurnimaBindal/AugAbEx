The settlement regarding age of superannuation entered into by parties rather than in conciliation proceedings is binding only upon the parties to the agreement.
If no settlement is found between the bank and the petitioner, the benefit of the age of superannuation of 60 years provided will not be available to him.
Additionally, the subsequent award passed by the Tribunal stipulates the age of superannuation as 58 years but still protects the existing benefits of employees.
Regulation 24 of the Employees' Service Regulation also provides for the age of superannuation to be 58 years, unless provided otherwise in a contract with the employer.
Since the petitioner failed to prove such a contract, he has been rightly superannuated at the age of 58 years.
[Paras 5, 6, 11, 13 and 14]

According to Regulation 24 of the Co-operative Society Employees Service Regulations, 1975, the age of superannuation for employees is 58 years, unless otherwise specified in a contract with the employer.
The petitioner failed to provide evidence of such a contract.
The age of superannuation of employees under contract of service entered at the time of their appointment would create a separate class where such employees' age of superannuation could be protected without infringing on the rule of equality.
There has been no challenge to the validity of Regulation 24.
[Para 14]

A writ petition is maintainable against co-operative society.
