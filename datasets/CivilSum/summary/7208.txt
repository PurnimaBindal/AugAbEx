Article 226 of the Constitution of India concerns promotion and backwages on promotion.
A petitioner treated as promoted on the basis of the next below rule on the date his immediate junior was granted regular promotion is not entitled to be considered promoted from the date his junior was asked to officiate as a civilian staff officer.
Mere officiation does not give him any right.
[Paras 17] B.
Rule 22 of the Fundamental Rules deals with pay fixation and stepping up of pay.
A claim for stepping up of pay on the ground that a junior of the petitioner was drawing higher pay is not applicable when the junior was getting a higher pay scale because he had the benefit of working on an ad hoc officiating post before regular promotion.
The junior to the petitioner was drawing higher pay than the petitioner on account of earned increment while officiating as a Civilian Staff Officer, so the petitioner is not entitled to stepping up of pay.
[Paras 22 & 23] C. Section 22(3)(f) of the Administrative Tribunal Act, 1985, and Rule 1 of Order 47 of the Civil Procedure Code, 1908, deal with the power of recall of an earlier order.
The power of recall is distinct and separate from the power to review and can be exercised under certain limited circumstances.
[Para 24] D. Whether discretionary power and relief should be exercised/granted in a particular case under Article 226 of the Constitution of India depends on the facts and circumstances of the case.
