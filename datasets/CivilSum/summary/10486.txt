Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16 and 226 - Promotion - Seniority - The petitioner was second on the seniority list and the respondent was third - A person ranked sixth was promoted and this promotion was challenged by the respondent in a civil suit - The respondent's suit was decreed - The petitioner filed objections in execution proceeding, asserting his superior claim based on seniority - However, his objections were dismissed - The petitioner then filed a writ petition to be considered along with the respondent - The petitioner's writ petition cannot be dismissed solely because his objections were rejected - Since the petitioner was not a party to the suit, the decree was not binding on him and his objections were of no consequence - Even the non-disclosure of the dismissal of his objections cannot be used as a ground to dismiss his petition - As the petitioner is senior to the respondent, the promotion of the respondent without considering the petitioner's right first resulted in a violation of Articles 14 and 16 - Therefore, the authorities have been directed to consider the petitioner's claim.
