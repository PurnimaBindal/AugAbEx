Army Act, 1950, Sections 153, 154, 160 - Army Rules, 1954, Rule 68 - An Army Officer is charged with theft and tried by a General Court Martial and acquitted.
The Confirming Authority analyses the findings and remands the case.
The Confirming Authority records that the findings were perverse.
The accused is held guilty by the General Court Martial.
It was contended that the Confirming Authority could not analyze the evidence and influence the Court Martial.
This contention was rejected, and it was held that the Confirming Authority is competent under S.154 to re-analyze the evidence and direct the General Court Martial to reconsider the charge.
The case relied on 1973(2) SCR 1025 and was approved in 1983 Cr.LJ 1059 (Delhi).

Section 160 - A Court Martial may award a sentence to an Army Officer on a charge of theft.
When the Confirming Authority remands the case, it must record reasons for the same.
[Para 15]  C Army Act, 1950, Section 160 - Army Rules, 1954, Rule 68 - An Army Officer is charged with theft, tried by a Court Martial, and acquitted.
The finding or sentence of the Court Martial can be revised by the Confirming Authority.
If the Court Martial returns the same finding after remand, the Confirming Authority is bound by it.
[Para 9]  D. Army Rules, 1956, Rule 68 - Army Act, 1950, Sections 153, 154, 160 - An Army Officer is charged with theft and tried by a Court Martial.
If the accused is acquitted, the finding of Court Martial would be valid only after it is confirmed by the Confirming Authority.
The Confirming Authority has to examine the whole record of the proceedings of the Court Martial before confirming the finding or sentence.
If the Confirming Authority finds that the finding of the Court Martial requires revision, the Confirming Authority would remand the case to Court Martial.
After remand, the Court Martial may take additional evidence if required by the Confirming Authority.
