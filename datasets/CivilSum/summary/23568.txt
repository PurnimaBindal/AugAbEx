The appellant, a medical practitioner who wanted to simultaneously practice law without giving up his medical practice, was refused enrolment as an Advocate by the State Bar Council of Maharashtra & Goa.
The Bar Council was justified in doing so, as the legal profession requires full-time attention and any flinching from such unstinted attention would adversely affect the professional ability and expertise of the appellant.
The impugned rule, which disallows other professionals to enter the Bar unless they give up their other profession and join whole-heartedly the legal profession, is not violative of Articles 19(1)(g), 14, and 21 of the Constitution, and is saved by Article 19(6).
As per the Constitution of India, the right to practise any profession or to carry on any occupation, trade or business is not an absolute right, and if any restriction on the entry to the legal profession is found to be reasonable, it wouldn't stultify Article 19(1)(g).
Hence, the impugned rule, Maharashtra and Goa Bar Council Rules Rule 1, is valid and operative at law.
