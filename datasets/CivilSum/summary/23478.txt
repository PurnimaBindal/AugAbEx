Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 136 - Promotion/Conviction/Criminal Trial - A police officer was charged with murder and convicted, but his appeal was later accepted by the High Court and he was acquitted.
Nevertheless, the State Government reinstated and promoted him during the pendency of an appeal filed by the State in the Supreme Court.
The Supreme Court deemed this move to be inappropriate, stating that the conduct of both the officer and the Government were under the public's eyes.
The Court believed that it is the conduct and behaviour of the officer, as well as the approach of the Government towards them, that are judged on a societal scale.
Such an unwarranted action by the Government can undermine the public's confidence in the system, as they lose faith when an individual being tried for a criminal offence is promoted.
As a result, the Court suggested that governments should consider temporarily granting sealed packet promotions during pending appeals to ensure that the accused officers do not suffer undue hardship.
In this case, since the accused officer underwent a prolonged trial while under threat of an uncertain outcome, the Court did not pass any negative ruling against him.
