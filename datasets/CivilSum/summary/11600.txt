The AP State Electricity Board Discipline and Appeal Regulations, Regulations 10(4) and 10(7)(3) were allegedly violated by the petitioner.
According to records, they defalcated an amount of about Rs. 4 lacs and to suppress evidence put records on fire, dragging in many employees.
The departmental enquiry and criminal case of fire got into motion, followed by dismissal of petitioner in departmental proceedings.
The court held it a clear case of mischief, but acquitted them and others for lack of evidence in the criminal case.
Appellant here, however, contended that no opportunity was given to defend himself, nor were any records or documents made available.
The petitioner's complaint was rejected because as per their letter dated 1481987, they admitted to the verification of records, and their lack of cooperation/participation in the enquiry.
Another contention was that the domestic proceedings could not be launched when the criminal case was pending, which was misconceived.
The charges were different, and thus the petition was dismissed.
[Paras 5 and 6]
In regards to the AP State Electricity Board Discipline and Appeal Regulations, Regulation 10(7)(a), the submission is that Chairman of Board imposed the punishment, and the appellate authority acted as a punishing authority.
On the contrary, Regulation 10(7)(a) specifies that when two or more persons are involved in a case, an authority competent to impose punishment of dismissal on all such members may act as a punishing authority.
Including in this specific case there were multiple members involved, and no such objection was taken or raised earlier in proceedings.
Therefore, the appeal was dismissed.
