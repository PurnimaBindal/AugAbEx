In disciplinary proceedings resulting in an order of removal, the order must be sustainable.
In this case, the Enquiry Officer proceeded as if the appellant was charged with theft or misappropriation, while in reality, the charge was non-maintenance of the ACE-8 Register.
The successor of the appellant stated that the maintenance of muster rolls and ACE-8 Register was proper and that ACE-8 sheets were attached to the estimate file.
The Enquiry Officer made adverse remarks on the correctness of the statements without assigning reasons.
The Enquiry Officer performs a quasi-judicial function, and upon analysing the documents must arrive at a conclusion that there has been a preponderance of probability to prove charges on the basis of materials on record.
However, the Enquiry Officer cannot consider an irrelevant fact, shift the burden of proof or inquire into allegations with which the delinquent is charged.
The report of the Enquiry Officer contains the aforementioned vices, and as such, the orders of disciplinary and appellate authorities based on such a report cannot be sustained.
