Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 2(oo) (bb) - Retrenchment - The amendment Act No. 49 of 1948 which added clause (bb) to Section 2(oo) does not have a retrospective application - Any retrenchments that were illegally made prior to the amendment remained illegal and void, even if they would have been legal following the amendment.
[Paras 4, 5 and 9] B.
Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 2(k) and 25-F - Service rules created under Article 309 of the Constitution do not supersede the provisions of the Industrial Disputes Act established by Parliament via its power under Entries 22, 23, and 24 of List-III Concurrent List of Seventh Schedule of the Constitution - Any individual employed by a public or private employer is eligible for the provisions of ID Act, 1947 as a workman if they fall within the definition of 'workman' and establishment as 'industry' under Section 2(j) - An employee governed by both is entitled to the more beneficial provisions of the Rules or the ID Act as preserved under Section 25-J - If the provisions of Chapter V-A of ID Act are more favorable to the workman compared to the provisions of any other law, the workman is entitled to Chapter V-A's benefits, however, if the benefit is reachable to the workman under any Act, Rules, order, notification, Standing Order, Award, Contract, or service, such benefit will be protected.
[Paras 10 and 13] C. Industrial Disputes Act, Section 2(oo) (bb) - Retrenchment - Arbitrary termination of an employee during probation due to misconduct without holding an inquiry and without providing an opportunity is unacceptable- The matter's background can be examined by the court - If there is no information to establish that the employee was unfit for continued service, termination becomes arbitrary and punitive - The employer cannot use Section 2(oo)(bb) of the ID Act as a defense - The termination order is invalid.
