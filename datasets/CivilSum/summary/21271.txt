According to the Advocates Act, 1961, Section 35, members of the legal profession are officers of the court and must display honest conduct towards the public and court to uphold the credibility of the legal system.
The Bar Council has a responsibility to supervise the conduct of its members to maintain the profession's credibility.
Legal profession is a noble profession, and lawyers must strive for justice and avoid dishonesty and corruption.
The disciplinary bodies have the power and duty to punish lawyers found guilty of misconduct to protect the due administration of justice.
The punishment must be proportionate to the gravity of the offense.
[Paras 1 and 8]

The Advocates Act, 1961, Sections 34 and 44 deal with the scope of review.
The exercise of power of review does not allow a Disciplinary Committee to modify the earlier order passed by another Disciplinary Committee based on different views of the same facts.
All the facts taken into consideration in the impugned order, such as the respondent's serious misconduct, must be reviewed in such cases.
Therefore, the order of review has been quashed.
[Paras 7 and 9]
