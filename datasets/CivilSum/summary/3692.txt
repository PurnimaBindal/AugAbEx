According to the Industrial Disputes Act, certain factors such as direction, control, and supervision of work, mode of payment, and the nature of the business can determine whether an individual is considered a workman or not.
However, even piece-rated workers such as tailors and weavers can be classified as workmen if certain tests are satisfied.
In this case, the corporation had control over the quality of work, working hours, and disciplinary action, and therefore, the workers were considered workmen under Section 2(s) of the ID Act.
The corporation's agreement to adhere to the minimum rate of wages and not to victimize any worker also indicated that they were the employer.
