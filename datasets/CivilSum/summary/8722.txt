According to Section 25 of the Guardians and Wards Act, 1890, custody of a 10-year-old daughter requires the constant presence of a grown-up female interested in her welfare.
Since there is no closely related women available in the petitioner-appellant's house to provide such guidance, the mother is the best option for the child's welfare.
The lower court considered the child's preference but she refused to see her father and has been living with her mother since birth.
Therefore, the trial court has made the right decision in rejecting the petitioner-appellant's petition under Section 25 of the Act.
