Punjab Police Rules, 1934, Rule 162 - Explanation - "Gravest misconduct" - The Explanation given in a Statute must be considered as a part of the main Rule and not as an independent element - The purpose of the Explanation may vary as it can explain any term or phrase set forth in the principal provision, and can even modify or limit its interpretation - When the Explanation is added at the end of a Section/Rule comprising different clauses, its application should be assessed based on the relevant part or clause of the Section - Also, if the Explanation mentions "for the purpose of this Section", it should be interpreted as extending to the entire Section - Therefore, "gravest misconduct" referred to in sub Rule (i) of Rule 162 must be interpreted only as per the definition and acts given in Clauses (i) to (vii) of the Explanation - No other type of conduct can be considered as "gravest misconduct".

Punjab Police Rules, 1934, Rule 162(i) - Explanation - "Gravest misconduct" - The Explanation only defines "gravest misconduct" and not "misconduct" - A penalty of dismissal can be imposed if the delinquent officer is found to have committed the most severe act of misconduct or a series of continued misconduct - In both cases, the delinquent officer's incorrigibility and unsuitability for police service have to be proved.

Punjab Police Rules, 1934, Rule 162 - Explanation - "Gravest misconduct" - Absence from duty - The only charge against the delinquent officer was unauthorized absence for 22 and 100 days - The disciplinary authority may take into account the Enquiry Report and prior punishments along with the delinquent officer's absenteeism from the enquiry proceeding in order to impose the punishment of dismissal - Even if the delinquent officer's conduct does not fall under the definition of "gravest misconduct", it still constitutes misconduct - In such cases, the authority must also consider the length of the delinquent officer's service and their right to pension - If the officer has completed the required length of service to be eligible for pension, disregarding it would make the dismissal order invalid and unjustifiable - An arbitrary use of power when there is a relevant provision available cannot be upheld, hence the authority must issue a fresh order.
