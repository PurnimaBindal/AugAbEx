Constitution of India, Article 14 - Selection - The most appropriate method of selection involves advertising positions and allowing all eligible candidates to apply - Eligible candidates must be impartially and objectively evaluated based on a fair, just, and reasonable criteria, and only those who are selected based on merit should be appointed.
[Para 7]

Constitution of India, Article 14 - Selection - Regularisation - The practice of regularising adhoc employees undermines the principle of equality before the law - Those who are able to apply pressure can secure adhoc jobs and then become permanent members of state services through backdoor entry, while others are unable to compete for a significant number of positions in the state - To promote the interests of the public, only the most skilled candidates should be selected because they would perform their duties better.
[Paras 8 and 9]

Constitution of India, Article 14 - Selection - Regularisation - While the process of regularising adhoc employees undermines equality before the law, recent trends have seen an increase in the number of adhoc appointments, and courts in the country have approved instructions for regularisation - Although regularising an irregular appointment typically should not receive judicial approval, the regularisation of adhoc appointments that have been validated by a high-level administrative committee cannot be considered arbitrary - Ousting such appointees at a later stage, after they have already served the state for approximately 14 years, would be unfair and unjust.
[Para 10]
