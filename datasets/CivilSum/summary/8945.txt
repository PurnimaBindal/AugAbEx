Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 311- Termination - criminal proceedings - If a public servant is acquitted on the benefit of doubt or not on any finding on the charge, it does not bar departmental proceedings.
The punishing authority can still conduct an enquiry and come to its own conclusions to award punishment.
[Para 3] B.
Constitution of India, Articles 226 - Industrial Disputes Act, Section 10 - Termination - Charge - Labour Tribunal upheld the punishment of termination, even though the findings were different from those arrived at by the punishing authority.
The difference in findings is not a ground for interference if they are supported by sufficient material on record.
[Para 3] C. Constitution of India, Article 226 - Award of the labour Court - The Court should not interfere if the findings recorded by the Labour Court are based on proper evaluation of evidence and are not perverse.
[Para 4] D. Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 11-A - Misconduct - Breach of trust by public servant - Once a public servant is held guilty of committing a breach of trust or faith, he loses the right to continue in service.
The court of law protects only against arbitrary action of the employer.
If the employee is found guilty of misappropriation, fraud, embezzlement, cheating, etc., he has no right to serve as a public employee.
The Labour Court was justified in not exercising its jurisdiction under Section 11-A of the ID Act.
[Para 5] E Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 11- Back wages - If the employer was allowed to lead evidence in support of the termination order in the Labour court, and the order was upheld, the termination order will operate only from the date of award.
The principle of relation back will not apply.
The employee is entitled to full back wages till the date of award.
