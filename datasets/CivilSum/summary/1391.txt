Article 309 of the Constitution of India, along with the Punjab Public Works (Irrigation Branch) Patwaris State Service, Class III, Rules of 1955, dictate appointment and rule of appointment for patwaris - The doctrine of desuetude is not applicable unless there has been long disuse of the statute and contrary practice in use - The 1955 Rules should be followed - Rules have not become ineffective due to the doctrine of desuetude.
[Paras 21-28, 32]

When the conditions for appointment have been prescribed in statutory rules framed under Article 309 of the Constitution of India, the government has no power to prescribe additional eligibility or suitability requirements - Executive instructions may only fill in Rule lacunae - The Punjab Public Works (Irrigation Branch) Patwaris State Service, Class III, Rules of 1955 do not provide for the constitution of a Selection Committee, so one that has been constituted is illegal - Appointments resulting from illegal conduct are quashed.
[Paras 30, 32-35, 44-45]

The Selection Committee cannot impose norms for selection that conflict with rules or instructions - it cannot develop its own criteria.
[Paras 36, 37, 39]

Article 226 of the Constitution of India, along with the Evidence Act's Sections 115 and 114, show that waiver is not applicable unless the person waiving is well-informed and willingly surrenders their right - The Selection Committee constituted a selection process that did not follow legal protocols or give candidates prior notice of criteria - Candidates may still contest an appointment even after participating in an interview - Waiver does not apply here.
[Paras 40-43]
