Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 10.

Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 10(8) - Except for those that are personal to the workman, all final rights of every kind vested in a deceased person and all causes of action survive to his heirs - The right of a workman to be reinstated in service after the grant of a declaration that his termination was unlawful may be personal to him and may become impossible to uphold after his death - But the consequences flowing from this declaration are not personal to him - The heirs of the deceased can bring an action in a Court of law to claim the rights that survive after the death of the workman.

Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 - The legislature purposefully kept the applicability of the CPC and the technical rules of evidence out of the industrial adjudication arena - Section 11(3) of the Act made the provisions of the CPC applicable only to enforce the attendance of any person, examining him on oath, producing documents, issuing commission for the examination of witnesses, and so on - Industrial adjudication was thus kept separate from the legalities commonly applied to the resolution of civil disputes.
