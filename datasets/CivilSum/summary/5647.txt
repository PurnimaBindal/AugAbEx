As per Section 2(j) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, an establishment may be considered an "industry" if it engages in systematic activity and has a special emphasis on employer-employee relations.
Profit motive is not relevant to this determination, as per the Bangalore Water Supply and Sewerage Board case.
In applying these tests, the Tata Club is considered an industry given its rules and regulations, property holdings, and investments, since it operates through the cooperation of employer and employees.
[Paras 8 to 11]
The Labour Court rejected a workman's claim against the Club on the technical ground that it was not an "industry," without assessing whether the enquiry was fair and followed principles of natural justice.
However, the High Court determined that the Club is indeed an "industry," and the claim is thus maintainable before the Labour Court.
The case has been remanded for a decision on the merits.
