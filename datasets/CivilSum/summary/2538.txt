The petitioner, a professor of University, was accused of misbehaviour with a girl student.
However, he was denied an opportunity to present his defense and was not provided with a copy or gist of the complaint made against him.
This failure to comply with the principles of natural justice constitutes a violation of fair play, as the person who is to be affected should know the charges against him before any penalty is imposed.
Any proceedings that impose punishment without providing an opportunity to the affected party cannot be sustained.
Therefore, the impugned proceedings were quashed.
The order of punishment was also quashed under Constitution of India, Articles 311 and 226/227, since the petitioner is entitled to know the contents of the complaint before responding to allegations.
The petitioner has the right to approach this Court under Article 226 of the Constitution, seeking redressal of his grievance since an appeal to the appropriate authority is not sufficient to absolve the respondents from complying with the principles of natural justice.
Providing an appeal would not prevent the respondents from complying with the principles of natural justice.
Hence, the petitioner has rightly approached this Court, as seeking an appeal would not absolve the respondents from complying with the requirement of principles of natural justice under Article 226.
