Constitution of India, Articles 235 - Judicial Officers - Misconduct of Judicial Officer - Control of High Court over subordinate judiciary - The possibility of a second view cannot be a subject matter of disciplinary proceedings against a judicial officer.
Such cases may be treated as a bona fide and erroneous exercise of judicial powers and cannot be deemed as misconduct.
The basis for initiating disciplinary action must be if the judicial officer has conducted in a manner that reflects negatively on their reputation, integrity, good faith, or devotion to duty, or if there is prima facie material showing recklessness or misconduct in their discharge of duties, negligent behavior, omission of essential prescribed conditions for the exercise of statutory powers, showing undue favor to a party, or acting out of corrupt motives.
If disciplinary action were initiated every time a subordinate court's order was found to be faulty, it could damage the confidence of subordinate judiciary, and officers would be in constant fear of facing disciplinary investigations.
As a result, they would not be able to act independently or fearlessly.
[Paras 5, 6, and 7]
