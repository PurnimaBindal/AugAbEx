In the case of a delinquent being proceeded against for theft and pilferage, as well as being proceeded against for absence, if an order of removal is passed and the appellate authority finds that the delinquent is not guilty of theft and pilferage but upholds the finding regarding absence, the delinquent can explain the cause of absence by stating that they were attending judicial proceedings.
The disciplinary authority cannot deny leave if it pertains to attending judicial proceedings, as that may amount to interference with the administration of justice, and the appellate authority must take note of this and pass an appropriate order.
B.
Under the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971 Section 2, if a delinquent servant is charged with absence on days when they were supposed to attend judicial proceedings initiated by them, then denying leave would amount to contempt and interfere with the administration of justice.
The disciplinary authority must reconsider the matter and pass fresh orders in accordance with the law.
