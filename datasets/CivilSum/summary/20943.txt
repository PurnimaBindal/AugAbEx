Article 162 of the Indian Constitution, Schedule 7, List 1, Entry 92 states that the Working Journalists (Fixation of Rates of Wages) Act of 1958, Section 2(g) provides retiral benefits to working journalists, such as retirement and pension payments.
These payments are governed by the Working Journalists Act and the Bachawat Award, and the levy of tax on government advertisements in newspapers falls within the realm of Parliament rather than the state.
The imposition of a 5% deduction for a pension fund for working journalists from the amount payable for the publication of government advertisements in newspapers with circulation over 25,000 copies by the government is illegal.
This was allowed in the petition.
[Paragraphs 17, 18, 19, and 20]

Article 366(28) along with Article 162 of the Indian Constitution and Schedule 7, List 3, Entry 23 states that taxation on government advertisements in newspapers, as well as a deduction from the payment of a bill of government advertisements, should not fall upon the newspaper establishments.
This is because the burden of such an impost cannot be placed by the state unless there exists a specific provision of law operating in the field.
Even though the imposition of a duty is within the definition of "tax" under Article 366(28), it is not permissible for the state to make any compulsory exaction from any citizen.
[Paragraphs 27, 29, and 30]

Advertisements in newspapers have a direct nexus with circulation and revenue, and as stipulated in Articles 19(1)(a) and 14 of the Indian Constitution and Section 23 of the Contract Act of 1872, the condition of deduction of 5% from the advertisement bills of newspapers for a pension fund for working journalists is arbitrary and violative of Article 14.
