Articles 14 and 311 of the Constitution of India - Departmental proceedings - Burden of proof - A driver of State Roadways is accused of driving the bus negligently, causing an accident that damaged the bus.
The inquiry officer determines that the driver did not prove his defense, which claimed that the accident occurred due to brake failure.
This decision is ultimately upheld by the punishing authority.
It is ruled that the burden of proof is on the prosecution to bring the charge home through the presentation of positive evidence, and that hearsay evidence is insufficient.
With no mechanical report or other evidence to support the notion of driver negligence, the findings of guilt based on no evidence cannot be sustained.

Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 311 - Limited scope of judicial review - Departmental proceedings - Although the scope of judicial review is narrow in departmental proceedings, if the authority's findings and conclusions are based on some evidence, they cannot be interfered with by the court.
However, the court can step in if the findings of guilt are unsupported by evidence or the proceedings violate principles of natural justice, statutory rules, or are taken without authority or jurisdiction.
