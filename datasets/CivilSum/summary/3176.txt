In cases where the Government seeks to make a reference of an industrial dispute it had previously declined to do, the principles of natural justice must be followed.
The Government is required to give notice to the employer before making a reference under Sections 10 and 12 of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.
This is necessary as the order of reference has civil consequences.
The Govt must also record and communicate its reasons for denying the reference.
As per Section 11-A of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, it is within the jurisdiction of the Labour Court to go into the legality of the dismissal of the workman, even if it has found the reference made by the Government to be bad in law.
The Industrial Tribunal cannot award compensation to the workman when it has held that the employer's decision to dismiss the workman is proper.
In this particular case, the Tribunal has failed to examine the various aspects of the case in the light of Section 11-A, resulting in a miscarriage of justice.
The impugned award passed by the Tribunal has been set aside with a direction to decide the matter afresh after hearing both parties.
