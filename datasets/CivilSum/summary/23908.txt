Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 2(p), 2(k), 17, 18 and 10 - Industrial Dispute - Settlement after award of the Court - No need to publish award after settlement - According to the Act, if parties reach a settlement even after a Labour Court award, the Government doesn't need to notify the award - An agreement would lead to lasting peace and better serve the object of the Act than an award - The settlement can be the subject of another industrial dispute if it's alleged to be the result of fraud or misrepresentation.
[Para 23]  B Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 18 - Settlement - Representative union settlement - A recognized union is expected to protect the interests of labor and negotiate a settlement in the interest of labor - Such an agreement will bind all existing workers, regardless of union membership, under Section 12(3) of the Act - The Act is based on the principle of collective bargaining for resolving industrial disputes and maintaining industrial peace.
[Paras 23 and 25]  C. Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 12(3) and 18 - Settlement - Holiday - A settlement reached on a holiday is not void merely because it was reached on a holiday.
