Article 311(2)(b) of the Indian Constitution allows for judicial review of orders of dismissal or removal.
Courts can invalidate such orders if they find that the competent authority did not apply its mind to relevant considerations, based its satisfaction on irrelevant considerations, applied the wrong test, failed to ask the right questions, did not grant satisfaction based on rationally probative material, or did not act in good faith.
[Para 16] B.
In the case of a dismissal due to absence from duty, the competent authority must ensure that the order is based on verified information.
In a specific case where the SSP dismissed an employee solely based on two notes prepared by lower-ranking officers, the court found that the competent authority had not verified the correctness of the notes and had not attempted to obtain any other materials.
The court held this to be an illegal dismissal and quashed the order.
