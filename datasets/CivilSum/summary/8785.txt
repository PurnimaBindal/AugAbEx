Punjab Police Rules, 1934, Rule 16.2 - Absence of six days - The act of absence for six days would not be considered a grave act of misconduct and the punishment of dismissal from service would hardly be justified.
[Para 7] B. Punjab Police Rules, 1934, Rule 16.2 - Unauthorised absence of six days - Mere absence from duty would not be considered a grave act of misconduct unless corroborated with other facts that indicate continued misconduct and unfitness for police service.
The cumulative effect of such conduct should also be proven properly and prior misconduct should be made known to the delinquent.
[Para 7] C. Civil Procedure Code, 1908, Order 1 Rules 1 and 3 - Parties to the suit - A 'person' who may sue or be sued is not defined in the CPC, but clause (42) of Section 3 of General Clauses Act, 1897, provides a definition that includes any company, association, or body of individuals, whether incorporated or not.
The State Government is a legal entity and its officers have no separate legal entity in their official capacity apart from the Government, except when sued in their personal capacity, thus making the State Government a proper party to sue.
[Para 8] D. Appeal - Plaintiff can challenge the initial dismissal order and subsequent confirmation at the appellate stage during the departmental enquiry.
The ultimate consequence of departmental enquiry is thus challenged, which invokes the jurisdiction of the court to examine the legality of the dismissal order since an appeal in departmental enquiries is a continuation of the same departmental enquiry, just being further examined by higher officers.
