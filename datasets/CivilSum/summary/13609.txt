Article 16 of the Indian Constitution - Advertisement - Qualification - Interview - Selection of Dental Officers - The advertisement noted that candidates with higher dental qualifications would be preferred, and selection by interview was not mentioned - Petitioner who possessed an M.D.S.
degree (higher qualifications) had a right to be selected - If the number of candidates fulfilling qualifications is less than the number of advertised posts and there is no prescribed mode of recruitment for selection purposes, these candidates will be selected based on their educational qualifications unless the Commission finds something against them to make them unsuitable for the job.
[Para 4]
Article 16 of the Indian Constitution- Post of Dental Officer - Qualifications - For general category, 12 posts of Dental Officers were advertised; two candidates (including petitioner) with higher qualification, i.e., MDS, were to be preferred, and in their cases, they had a right to be selected if not found unsuitable by the Commission.
However, in the written statement filed by the Commission, nothing indicated that the petitioner was found unsuitable for the job of Dental Officer.
The Commission's action is not only arbitrary, but it also goes against the spirit and intent of the appointing authority, who had outlined minimum qualifications for the job and preferred higher qualifications.
[Para 5]
Article 16 of the Indian Constitution - Selection - Qualification - Post of Dental Officer - Respondent No. 4, who had a lower qualification than the petitioner, was selected and placed last in the merit order for the general category - Merely selecting him does not grant him any right to the post of Dental Officer - He cannot be preferred over the petitioner, who was to be selected on a preferential basis - The P.S.C.
was directed to forward the petitioner's name as a selected candidate on a preferential basis against one of the posts reserved in the general category.
