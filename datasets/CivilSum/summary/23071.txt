The Corporation revalued the examinations for the post of Assistant Accounts Officer after finding wide variation in marks during test checks conducted by the Chief Examiner.
Respondents who received less than required qualifying marks were deleted from the results without notice.
The Court held that the principles of mass malpractice applied to this case, as even test checks resulted in significant changes in marks.
The High Court was wrong to say that respondents were entitled to notice before revaluation and their direction for revaluation was unauthorized.
[Paras 6 and 7] B.
An individual candidate doesn't need to be given a chance for personal hearing before a revaluation decision is made.
The decisions in 2003(4) SCT 318 and AIR 1970 SC 1289 were followed.
