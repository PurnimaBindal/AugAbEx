According to Section 25 of the Evidence Act and Section 302 of the Indian Penal Code, a confession made by the accused in Australia before an Australian Police Officer is not admissible in evidence for a case registered in India, despite being admissible according to Australian Law.
The case of 1912 Cr.
LJ.
528 provides support for this ruling.
[Paras 22, 23, and 35] B.
In a murder trial under Section 302 of the Indian Penal Code, the prosecution's claim that the accused disclosed his plans to a stranger cannot be believed as it makes no sense for the accused to create evidence against himself by confiding in a stranger.
[Para 25] C. In a murder committed at High Sea under Section 302 of the Indian Penal Code and investigated under the Evidence Act, Section 25, a confession made by the accused after four days of being kept in confinement in chains by the ship's captain is not admissible in court since it was made under an ordeal that could have caused physical or mental distress.
It was necessary for a Doctor or Advocate to be present to examine the accused before the confession.
