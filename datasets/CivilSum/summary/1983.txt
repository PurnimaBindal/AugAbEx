Under the Industrial Disputes Act, Section 11-A, the Labour Court has been given the power and duty to examine the fairness of dismissal, discharge, and termination inquiries, and to evaluate whether the punishment given was justifiable.
If the investigation is found to have been unfair, the employer will be granted another opportunity to present evidence against the punishment.
Additionally, if the penalty is determined to be disproportionate, the Labour Court can substitute it with a lesser one.
The court must not simply dismiss procedural lapses in the inquiry by asserting that no harm was caused to the workman, as demonstrated in the remanded case.
[Paras 7, 8, 12, and 13]

The Industrial Disputes Act's Section 10 mandates an inquiry before disciplinary action is taken against workers.
In this case, in which a worker was simply accused of off-duty absence without prior notice, the lack of a request for assistance from a co-worker does not justify concluding that the inquiry was not up to par.
The workman could defend themselves competently and adequately, according to the court.
[Para 6]
