Under the Workmen's Compensation Act, 1925, a workman who claims 100% loss of earning capacity due to being injured and wants to receive compensation must have the question of their loss of earning capacity decided.
In assessing the percentage of disablement, the Commissioner must determine whether the disablement is permanent and total, which is judged from the point of view of the work the workman was doing and whether the injury incapacitated them from all work they were capable of performing at the time of the accident.
Despite producing a certificate of permanent total disablement of 100% by a medical practitioner, the claimant's injury is not one of the six injuries mentioned in Part-I of Schedule I The Commissioner should have taken into account the question of loss of earning capacity and the case will be remanded back for a fresh decision.
