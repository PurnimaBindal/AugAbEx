The Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 was invoked when a dispute over the validity of a termination order was referred to the Labour Court.
After parties had completed their pleadings and the case had been fixed, the petitioner's counsel failed to appear due to a resolution by members of the Bar to abstain from work.
As a result, an ex parte award was issued.
However, the party that has engaged a counsel cannot be penalized for their absence due to a Bar resolution.
Since the petitioner was unable to provide evidence and prove necessary facts without the presence of their lawyer, it is unfair to hold them liable without a reasonable opportunity.
Therefore, the impugned award was set aside.
[Paras 5 and 6]
