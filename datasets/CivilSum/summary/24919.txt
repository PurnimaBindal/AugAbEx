Constitution of India, Articles 32 and 226 - Education - Admission to Post Graduate Course in Medicine - The University decided to adopt a three-year course for the post-graduate degree and a two-year course for the diploma, starting from the 1988 session.
However, to mitigate the hardship for candidates who had already completed their house job and were entitled to undergo a post-graduate course in two years, the University decided to continue the previous years' practice for a year through a scheme.
As part of the scheme, students doing housemanship were not eligible for admission to the Post-Graduate Degree and Post-Graduate Diploma Courses, but a common selection test was prescribed for both categories.
The Supreme Court, by informed pragmatic adhocism, directed the University to create one seat in every speciality over and above the seats fixed by the University and take away an equal number of seats from every specialty from the reserved seats made for freshers and make all seats so created available to seniors as a transitory measure only due to the initial mistake of having a common selection test for both categories of candidates.
