According to Section 2(S) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, a catering supervisor may not be considered a workman if their primary duties involve managerial and supervisory tasks; however, if they perform clerical or manual work as well, this may blur the lines and make it harder to determine their classification.
The respondent in this case argued that they also performed the roles of store keeper and cook, but it was deemed unlikely that a catering supervisor would be asked to work as a cook when there were already several cooks on staff.
The presumption is that an employee does the job they were hired for, unless proven otherwise, and it is the responsibility of the employee to provide evidence to the contrary.
Thus, the Labour Court made an error by wrongly determining that the respondent was a workman, disregarding the legal and factual complexities involved.
