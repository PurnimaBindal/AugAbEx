Constitution of India, Articles 14, 162, 226 and 309 - According to this ruling, it is within the government's prerogative to make rules and laws for appointments and prescribe the cadre strength of employees.
Courts must not encroach upon the executive's jurisdiction, and appointments must follow certain statutory service rules and instructions regarding service conditions.
Specifically, courts cannot order the regularisation of ad hoc or temporary employees who do not fulfill prescribed conditions.
While courts must ensure the rule of law and remove discrimination, directions to regularise such employees will encourage back door entry.
Issuing directions to regularise all those who have completed one year's service is a hasty decision that is not viable.
Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 309 - Regularisation of Services - Instructions - The government issues instructions from time to time that prescribe valid conditions for the regularisation of ad hoc employees, including the requirement that their names must be sponsored by an Employment Exchange and that they must fulfill the requisite qualifications at the time of entry.
The court has ruled that the instructions are valid and not arbitrary or unconstitutional.
However, the court maintains that all class II and class IV posts, which fall within the purview of the SSSB, should also fall within the purview of regularisation.
Constitution of India, Article 226 - Relief - The court must grant relief on a case-by-case basis, as warranted by the facts and circumstances of each particular case.
Constitution of India Articles 14 and 226 - Work - The court has held that the blanket directions given by the High Court regarding regularisation of work-charged, temporary and casual labour are unsustainable.
While these employees have a right to claim regularisation, the authorities must consider their cases for regularisation in a fair manner consistent with the principles enunciated by the Supreme Court.
Constitution of India, Articles 14, 226 and 136 - Daily Wagers and Casual Labour - The court has directed the Punjab government to frame a scheme within six months that provides for the regularisation of daily wagers and casual labour in a fair and just manner, taking into account the length of their service and other relevant conditions.
Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16/Equal pay for Equal Work - The court has set aside the direction of equal pay for equal work, ruling that it is too vague and lacks discussion.
Constitution of India, Article 14 and 136 - Ad hoc and Temporary Appointments - The court has issued guidelines for regularisation, stating that temporary employees should be replaced by regularly selected employees as early as possible and that unqualified persons ought to be appointed only when qualified persons are not available.
If a temporary employee is continued for a fairly long period, the authorities must consider his case for regularisation provided he is eligible and qualified according to rules and his service record is satisfactory and his appointment does not run counter to the reservation policy of the State.
