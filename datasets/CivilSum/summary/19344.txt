As per the Employees Provident Funds and Other Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952, Sections 1(3) and 19-A, contribution to the provident fund should only be paid by the management if the scheme is applicable and if any deduction is made from the salary or wages of employees.
The Act is not applicable in this case as the establishment had less than twenty workmen.
The carpentry shop was considered an incidental activity for the purpose of the main activity of the Board (Irrigation Wing), and no deduction was made from the wages of employees from 1969 to 1974.
None of the employees of the carpentry shop wished to be covered by the scheme under the Act, hence they cannot insist that the management pay their contributions.
It was held that the carpentry shop was outside the purview of Section 1(3) of the Act and there was no illegality or infirmity in the order passed by the Legal Adviser to the Govt. of India.
