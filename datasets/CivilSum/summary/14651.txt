Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 16 - Classification - Equality - Persons similarly situated have the right to equal treatment - However, the state is allowed to classify reasonably and rationally to achieve an objective - Such action does not violate Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
[Paras 9 and 10] B. Punjab Civil Services (Revised Scale of Pay) Rules, 1979 & 1988 - Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16 - Pay Scales - Classification option for pay scale/Revision of pay scale - If an employee has the option to choose between old and new rules, there is a reasonable classification - Those choosing the old rules make up one class while those opting for new rules make up another class.
[Paras 10, 11 and 12] C. Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16 and 226 - Pay Scale - Biennial increment - Increment/Maximum of scale of pay - An employee is able to receive their salary on a scale according to the rules - Biennial or annual increments are a concession to alleviate hardship after reaching the maximum scale provided by rules - There is no absolute right to claim an increment after reaching the maximum scale - The policy is reasonable and rational because it is related to the objective, and is formulated by experts, and courts cannot substitute their opinion if it is not unreasonable or irrational.
