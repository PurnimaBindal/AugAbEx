Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 226 - Departmental enquiry/Delay in departmental enquiry - The integrity of an officer must be promptly examined when allegations are made, and a prompt regular enquiry must be held if allegations are found to be prima facie correct.
Even if the delinquent does not co-operate, the preliminary enquiry can be proceeded with ex-parte in accordance with the rules.
While there is no inflexible rule to determine the period for the purpose of delay, each case depends on its own facts.
Preponderance of judicial opinion is that whenever delay is likely to prejudice the interest of an officer, enquiry proceedings shall be vitiated.
It is true that the guilty person should not be allowed to go unpunished and that employees must have a fair chance to establish their innocence.
Therefore, where the department takes inordinate long time without satisfactory explanation to initiate proceedings and the official is likely to be prejudiced, the court should step in to protect the rights of the employee.
[Paras 9, 10, 11, and 15]
