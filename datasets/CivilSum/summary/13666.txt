Punjab Emergency Concession Rules, 1965 - Military Service Benefit/Ad hoc Appointment/Regular Appointment - The petitioner was granted military service benefits but it is unclear if he was entitled to them per RS Dhull's case - Since no challenge was made to the order granting benefits, the order stands - However, the petitioner cannot claim military service benefits from the date of his ad hoc appointment; he is only entitled to the benefit from the date of regular appointment - Benefit cannot be granted for ad hoc appointments without rules or instructions - Regularization of ad hoc appointments can grant benefits from the initial appointment.

Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 16 - Equal Pay for Equal Work/Qualification - The petitioner, appointed as a Photographer on an ad hoc basis, served beyond the term of his appointment and was accommodated on compassionate grounds against the post of Assistant Photo and Exhibition Officer for which he was not qualified - He cannot claim the salary of that post - The petitioner is only entitled to the salary of the Photographer position.
[Para 9] C Constitution of India, Article 226 - Delay and Laches - The petitioner's cause of action accrued in 1977 but the petition was filed in 1988 - Therefore the petition is highly belated and cannot be entertained at this stage.
[Paras 9 and 10] D. Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 16 - Regularisation of Service - The petitioner was appointed as a Photographer on an ad hoc basis and later accommodated on compassionate grounds against the post of Assistant Photo and Exhibition Officer for which he was not qualified - Regularization policy requires candidates to be appointed through the Employment Exchange, which was not the case with the petitioner - Therefore, regularisation of the petitioner on the said post cannot be done.
