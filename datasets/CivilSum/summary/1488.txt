Article 226 of the Constitution of India, which pertains to Compassionate Appointment, has been invoked through the appointment of a second wife of a deceased employee as a helper on compassionate grounds.
The petitioner claimed that she should have been appointed as the legally wedded wife of the deceased employee.
Even though the second wife has held her post for over 14 years with the company, it has been held that the High Court cannot interfere with the appointment through its writ jurisdiction, even if it is found to be invalid after such a long time [Para 9].
The objective of Compassionate Appointment under Article 226 of the Constitution of India is to help the family get over the immediate financial crisis that arises after the death of the sole breadwinner.
Giving an appointment after a long interval of more than 15 years is not in accordance with the principle of granting Compassionate Appointment [Para 11].
In the context of Compassionate Appointment under Article 226 of the Constitution of India, the right to appointment must exist on the date on which the cause of action arose.
Accordingly, minor children of the deceased, like a 10-year-old son, cannot be given an appointment after they become major [Paras 16 and 17].
In a case related to Compassionate Appointment under Article 226 of the Constitution of India, the petitioner sought an appointment only for herself but did not pray for giving an appointment to her son.
Therefore, the learned judge was not justified in directing the appointment of the petitioner's son [Para 15].
