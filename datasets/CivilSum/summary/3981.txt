Constitution of India, Article 226 - Findings of fact - Misconduct - Unless the findings of fact recorded by the Enquiry Officer are perverse or based on no evidence, the Court under Article 226 of the Constitution cannot assume the role of an appellate authority.
[Para 10]  B Industrial Disputes Act, Section 2(ra) read with clause 5(a) of Fifth Schedule to the Act - Union leaders - Discharge or dismissal from service as a measure of victimisation - Being union leaders/office-bearers does not give workmen license to commit misconduct or engage in violent activities, and mere findings of serious misconduct cannot be considered as victimisation.
[Para 11]  C Constitution of India, Article 226 - Industrial Disputes Act, Section 11-A - Departmental proceedings - Quantum of punishment - Interference in writ jurisdiction - The writ Court has the power to interfere when it is found that the discretion is not exercised on sound judicial principles or the relevant material is ignored.
However, it would not be appropriate to interfere with the exercise of discretion by the Labour Court when the charges levelled against the workman were serious and despite interference by the Labour Court the workman is not granted reinstatement.
