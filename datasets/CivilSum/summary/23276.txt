The Examination Rules of the National Council for Hotel Management and Catering Technology, Rule 81, 92, as well as the Constitution of India, 1950, Article 14, Education, pertain to the matter of examination malpractice.
In one case, a candidate was caught with a slip during the examination and confessed to having cheated.
The institute disqualified the respondent for one academic session, and it was determined that the punishment was not disproportionate to the offence.
The court emphasized the importance of maintaining strict standards of purity in educational examinations and being harsh with those who engage in unfair practices within the system.
[Paras 4, 7, 18, 27, and 32]

The Constitution of India, 1950, Article 226, Education, discusses judicial review related to examination malpractice.
A judge must refrain from letting personal views influence their judgment.
[Para 20]
