Constitution of India, Article 311 - Punishment - Departmental enquiry - A delinquent official who assaulted his officer was subjected to an enquiry that resulted in dismissal - The employee argued that the punishment was disproportionate, but his argument was rejected - A court is not authorized to examine the punishment unless it is unreasonable given the circumstances of the case.
[Para 5]  B Constitution of India, Article 311 - Natural justice - Departmental enquiry - A departmental enquiry was conducted and a notice was issued to a delinquent official to defend against dismissal - It was claimed that the authority had already decided on the matter - The validity of the departmental enquiry was questioned with respect to this claim, but ultimately the dismissal was upheld - The rule established in Mohd.
Ramzan Khan's case, AIR 1991 SC 471, which states that a departmental enquiry would be tainted if an authority already had their mind made up while serving a show cause notice, applies only to future cases.
