The Industrial Disputes Act, Sections 10 and 11-A - Disciplinary Action - The Appellate Authority and the Labour Court - To assess the appeal, the Appellate Authority must consider all the evidence as well as the pleadings.
The Tribunal's authority does not include the appellate authority's jurisdiction.
It should base its decision solely on the arguments and evidence provided, or on additional evidence if necessary.
The powers of the appellate authority and the Labour Tribunal are different.
The Tribunal's goal is to determine whether the authorities' ruling can be upheld based on the materials or the enquiry filed, and whether no procedures have been violated unfairly to the accused.
If the Tribunal concludes that the punishment is more severe than the gravity of the misconduct or the charges and is not justified according to the well-defined legal principles, it will issue its findings on that basis in light of the available evidence.
The Tribunal cannot alter the punishment unless it unequivocally determines that it is wrong, unsupportable, or unjust, and was passed due to reckless or malicious considerations.

It is not necessary to refer the case back to the Tribunal for that information.
