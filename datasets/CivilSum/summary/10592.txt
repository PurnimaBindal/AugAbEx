In the writ jurisdiction, while disputed questions of facts cannot be decided, if the evidence of the facts is visible from the documents, the Court can consider such facts and make a decision.
The matriculation certificate presented on the record can be considered in the writ jurisdiction.
[Para 6]
When it comes to the question of the correctness of the date of birth in the service record of a Government servant, the matriculation certificate is the authentic record to be accepted as proof of age.
Unless there is a suspicion about the genuineness of the matriculation certificate, it should normally be accepted as reflecting the right date of birth.
[Paras 30 and 32]
If the date of birth as per the matriculation certificate of the appellant is taken into account, he becomes under-aged and ineligible at the date of his appointment.
However, the bank has appointed many other employees who were under-aged when they were appointed and have even completed up to 18 years of service, and some of them have even risen to the higher ranks up to the General Manager.
In such circumstances, the question of the appellant's being under-aged at the time of his appointment becomes irrelevant.
The appellant is not prevented from claiming his date of birth according to his matriculation certificate.
