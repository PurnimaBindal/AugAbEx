Army Act, 1950, Sections 70, 125 and 126 - Criminal Procedure Code, 1973, Section 475 - Criminal Courts and Court-martial (Adjustment of Jurisdiction) Rules, 1978, Rules 3 to 9 - Court Martial and Criminal Court - The Military Authority has the right to try an accused who is a military personnel while putting up a challan before CJM especially when there are multiple accused persons, one of whom is a civilian and the other is in the military - Ordinary criminal courts can only try the military personnel involved when the Military Authority abdicates its right to do so.
[Paras 7, 8 and 9] B.
Criminal Procedure Code, 1973, Section 475 - Army Act, 1950, Sections 125, 70 - Criminal Courts and Court-martial (Adjustment of Jurisdiction) Rules, 1978, Rules 3 to 9 - Offence committed by a Military personnel - Magistrates have the responsibility to inform the Military Authorities when a military personnel accused has been charged - The Military Authority has the first right to try the accused in Court Martial proceedings.
If they surrender their right in favor of ordinary criminal courts, the ordinary criminal courts then have the right to try the military personnel.
[Paras 6 to 9] C. Criminal Courts and Court-martial (Adjustment of Jurisdiction) Rules, 1978, Rules 3 to 9 - Criminal Procedure Code, 1973, Section 475 - Offence committed by a Military personnel - According to a law by the Supreme Court, the Military Authority has the first right to try an accused who is a military personnel - despite the concurrent jurisdiction of both ordinary criminal court and Court Martial to try the offence, the Military Authority reserves the right if they exercise it first - If the Military Authority had surrendered the accused to the Ordinary Criminal Courts, the Military Authority is deemed to have abdicated their right to try the accused.
