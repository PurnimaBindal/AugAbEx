The article discusses various laws and regulations related to admission in medical education, including the Constitution of India's Articles 14 and 16, the Medical Council of India Regulations, and the Madhya Pradesh Medical and Dental Post Graduate Admission (In-service Rules) of 2002.
The rules establish a reservation for in-service and female candidates, as well as the classification and sub-classification of female candidates who have rendered rural service.
However, the paper argues that holding different tests and fixing different criteria for in-service candidates is unconstitutional and that all candidates must achieve a minimum standard.
Additionally, the paper discusses the allocation of 20% of seats to in-service candidates and the validity of two separate channels for entry or sources of admission, as well as the assignment of reasonable weightage for service rendered in rural/tribal areas.
Lastly, the paper supports the provision of relaxed or reduced eligibility criteria for female doctors in rural service and argues that it does not suffer from discrimination.
