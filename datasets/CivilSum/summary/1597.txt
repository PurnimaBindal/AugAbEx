Constitution of India, Article 226 - Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 10 and 11-A - Writ Jurisdiction - Inferior Courts/Tribunals can have their errors of jurisdiction corrected by the issuance of a writ of certiorari if they have committed an error of law or neglected to give an opportunity of hearing to the affected party.
The court cannot act like an appellate Court and hold a new trial in a writ jurisdiction matter.
If a finding has been made mistakenly by refusing to admit evidence or basing a finding of fact on no evidence at all, that is known as an error of law apparent in the record.
However, a finding of fact recorded by the Court/Tribunal cannot be challenged on the ground of insufficiency or inadequacy of evidence.

Constitution of India, Article 311(2)(b) - Punjab State Electricity Board Main Services Regulations, 1972, Regulation 14(ii) - Departmental proceedings - When it is not reasonably practicable to hold an inquiry, the competent authority must record suitable reasons for coming to that conclusion.
It is not compliance with the rule to dispense with the holding of a regular inquiry simply because a worker has been absent from duty for a long period without leave.
[Para 13]

Constitution of India, Article 14 - Back wages - Termination of employment due to long absence from duty without any inquiry is considered illegal.
However, if the delay in the resolution of the matter cannot be wholly attributed to the employer, the employee may not be entitled to receive full back wages.
In one such case, an award of 25% of back wages was given from the date of termination until the employee was allowed to rejoin.
