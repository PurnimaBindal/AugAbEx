The High Court Judges exhibit a complete transformation from the administrative to judicial branch and are able to uphold the independence and majesty of the judiciary through their power of judicial review, despite their previous involvement in the decision-making process.
[Para 2]
The term "control" in Article 235 of the Constitution encompasses disciplinary control, promotions, leaves, postings, and more over the subordinate judiciary, providing the High Court with comprehensive authority.
[Paras 21-23]
Though essentially government servants, the officers of the subordinate judiciary are placed under the control of the High Court, with the Governor having to consult the High Court for any appointments, disciplinary actions, or removals/retirements.
The consultation represents a matter of trust and confidence, and if arbitrary, may not have any binding value.
[Paras 24-27]
Entries recorded at one go for three years, without affording any opportunity for representation, cannot be taken into account for forming an opinion for compulsory retirement.
[Paras 29-30]
If a judicial order is passed in good faith, albeit wrong, and not based on extraneous considerations, then it cannot form a basis for disciplinary action against a judicial officer or compulsory retirement.
[Para 29]
Uncommunicated adverse entries cannot be relied upon for the purpose of compulsory retirement, yet any accompanying representation will be considered but not necessarily bar an order of compulsory retirement.
