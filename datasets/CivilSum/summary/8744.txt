According to Constitution of India, Articles 226 and 227, houses allotted by the Government cannot be sublet to anyone who is not a member of the family, which is defined as the spouse, children, stepchildren, legally adopted children, parents, and siblings who normally live with the employee and are dependent on him/her.
In this particular case, the petitioner had sublet the house to his brother-in-law, and as a result his house was cancelled by the Secretary House Allotment Committee.
If the allottee lets a relation use the premises with the intention that they may settle down in that house or accommodation, this act on the part of allottee will be considered unauthorised.
The eviction order was upheld, and the petitioner failed to prove that the impugned orders were illegal.
[Paras 5 and 6]

According to Constitution of India, Articles 226/227, the writ jurisdiction cannot disturb a question of fact because sub-tenancy/unauthorized occupation is a matter of evidence that is proven only by providing evidence before the authorities.
The court cannot disturb the findings if proper procedures have been followed and appropriate evidence has been presented.
Therefore, this petition was dismissed.
[Para 6]
