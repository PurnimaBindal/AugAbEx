Article 378 of the Constitution of India outlines the purpose of Public Service Commissions, which is to select individuals for the Public Services without any political influence or interference from the executive authorities.
The Commission must act with impartiality and maintain the expected norms of behavior and procedure to uphold the dignity of the Constitution and maintain the confidence of the public in its actions.
Members of the Commission must not engage in mud-slinging in public, which would harm the reputation and effectiveness of the Commission.
Procedures for removing members of the Commission are similar to those for removing High Court judges.
[Paras 17 to 20, 26, 28, and 29]

Articles 14, 16, 226, and 378 of the Constitution of India address the selection process for the Public Service Commission, including grounds for scrapping an examination.
If members of the Commission engage in irregularities or violate secrecy, the exam cannot be canceled entirely.
Scrapping the exam and its results violates the valuable right of candidates who worked hard to prepare and would burden the public exchequer with unnecessary expenses.
Therefore, the Commission must pass a speaking order with valid reasons before taking such a drastic step, and the order to scrap the exam has been quashed.
[Paras 29, 30, and 33]
