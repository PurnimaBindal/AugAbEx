Civil Procedure Code, Order 17, Rules 2 and 3 - Non-appearance of parties - Judges should be trained to decide cases even in the absence of lawyers - Court can proceed with case even if parties are not present - It is the duty of the court to administer justice - Judges cannot avoid their duty by simply showing helplessness.

Constitution of India, Article 226(2) - Jurisdiction/Territorial Jurisdiction - High Court can entertain writ petition if cause of action arises within the territorial jurisdiction - Petitioner's residency in a state does not automatically give jurisdiction to the court - Writ petition cannot be entertained if convict was not tried within the territorial jurisdiction of that court.
[Para 6] C. Advocates Act, 1961, Sections 29 and 30 - Strike by lawyers - Advocates have a right of audience in court - Lawyers cannot legally stop another lawyer from performing his duty.
