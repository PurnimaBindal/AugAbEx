The Constitution of India's Articles 14, 16, and 226, along with Baba Farid University Ordinance on reappearing in the subject not cleared in the first chance in Prof BPT course, displayed inconsistencies between the clauses.
The effect of these inconsistencies was that various clauses were incompatible with one another.
For example, Clause (v) had a direct conflict with clause (vi), which meant that a student who failed to clear the subjects in which he had landed in re-appearing would be reverting back.
However, this interpretation could not have been the University's intention.
Instead of striking down the ordinance, the principle of harmonious construction had to be applied to read down the provisions.
The court held that a candidate who fails in one or more subjects in their first attempt must be admitted to attend class in the 2nd Prof BPT course.
A candidate who passes in one or more subjects would be exempted from appearing in these subjects the next time but must pass the exam within six attempts.
If the student fails to pass all subjects in six attempts, then he would be reverted to his 1st Professional Course and have to appear in all the subjects all over again.
This interpretation considers the concessions offered to the unsuccessful candidate and abides by the University's intention for such an unsuccessful candidate.
