New Bank of India Officers Employees' (Discipline & Appeal) Regulations, 1982, Regulation 7 - Punishing authority's disagreement with enquiry report - Natural justice - Show-cause notice - If the punishing authority disagrees with all or part of the enquiry report and is convinced that the charges have been proved, despite the enquiring authority exonerating the delinquent, a show cause notice with a tentative opinion and reasons must be given to the delinquent, along with a copy of the enquiry report, to allow him the opportunity to persuade the authority for the correctness of the findings that differ from his own - The provision of natural justice must be read into every rule of this nature, irrespective of whether it is explicitly stated or not - Failure to comply with this basic principle will render the punishment order void for flouting the rule.

New Bank of India Officers Employees' (Discipline & Appeal) Regulations, 1982, Regulation 7 - Punishing authority's disagreement with part of enquiry report - Natural justice - Show-cause notice - If the punishing authority disagrees with any part of the enquiry report and wants to base the punishment on the findings of all the charges, a show cause notice, along with a tentative opinion and justification, must be sent to the delinquent, along with a copy of the enquiry report, to allow him to persuade the authority for the correctness of his findings - If the punishment order does not make it clear that it was not based on the charges on which the enquiring authority had exonerated the delinquent, the order would not be valid, even if the guilty is found on all other charges - The punishment imposed may have been different or less severe if the other charges were excluded.
[Paras 13 and 16]  C. Retrospective and prospective effect of judgments - Constitution of India, Articles 141 and 226 - Natural justice - Second show cause notice - A judgment will be either prospective or retrospective, depending on the court's ruling - If it wants a particular judgment to have prospective effect only, it will explicitly state so - If it does not, the law laid down by the court will have both retrospective and prospective effect - A rule of natural justice mandates a second show cause notice in certain instances; this obligation must be satisfied irrespective of any prospective or retrospective implications of the ruling.
