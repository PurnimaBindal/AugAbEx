Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16 and 226 - Appointment - By a contract for specified term which could be extended from time to time - Term extended - No order to extended sanction - Effect.
The scheme under which the respondent had been appointed provided for a contract appointment for a specific term that could be extended from time to time.
After first appointment, the term of the respondent was extended on different occasions, but he was not entitled to service beyond August 30, 1985, unless there was a further extension.
The appellant-government never accepted the position that the respondent was entitled to be treated as a regular employee who had a vested right to continue holding the post until he attained 58 years of age.
The respondent was employed only under a contract with a specified term of appointment, which only extended up to August 30, 1985.
Therefore, since no extension had been sanctioned beyond August 30, 1985, the respondent was only entitled to the salary and allowances due to him until August 30, 1985.
The High Court set aside the order of termination and directed that the respondent was entitled to salary up to the period he was entitled to remain in service.
