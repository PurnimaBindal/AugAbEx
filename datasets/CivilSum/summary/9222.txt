Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 2(s)(iii) and (iv) - Workman - The designation of an employee's post is not the sole factor when determining whether or not someone is a workman - The primary nature of their duties is what needs to be considered - Management or administrative duties differ from supervisory duties - If someone supervises the work and has the power to make temporary appointments, grant leave, or initiate departmental proceedings, they cannot be considered a workman according to the Industrial Disputes Act.
[Paras 5, 13 & 15]

Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 11(a) and 2(s)(iii) and (iv) - Workman/ Pleadings - When the management argues that an employee is not a workman, they can provide evidence to exclude the employee's workman status as part of their defense.
[Para 16]

Constitution of India, Article 14 - Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 10 - Punjab Civil Services (Punishment & Appeal) Rules, 1970, Rule 876 - Natural justice - The Enquiry Officer being a law graduate is not grounds for a workman to have the right to a lawyer's assistance - A workman can only have another employee assist them - Denying a workman a lawyer's assistance does not invalidate the inquiry.
[Para 18]

Constitution of India, Article 14 - Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 10 - Enquiry Officer - An Enquiry Officer being an employee of the same Department does not automatically make the departmental inquiry flawed.
[Para 17]

Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 11(a) - Enquiry - If the Labour Court finds that the inquiry was flawed, the management can request permission to present the prima facie case against the employee before the Labour Court at a suitable time.
[Para 19]
