The Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946, Sections 3 and 2A(1) - Model Standing Orders, Rule 4C - Maharashtra Recognition of Trade Unions and Prevention of Unfair Labour Practices Act, 1971, Schedule TV, Items 5, 6, 9 and 10 (as amended) - Bombay Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Rules, 1959 - Unfair labor practices - The employer cannot eliminate the bar to worker rotation under the Standing Orders Act, 1946 by amending the rules or standing orders, since this is already specifically disallowed under the Act itself - The date that the Model Standing Orders come into effect is that which is designated by the State Govt. - The procedure for defining working conditions for employees in an industrial establishment must adhere to the 1946 Act - Amendment to the model standing orders instituted by the Maharashtra Govt. that disregards rules in the model standing orders related to matters outlined in the Schedule is invalid and without legal effect.

Constitution of India, Articles 136 and 226 - Maharashtra Recognition of Trade Unions and Prevention of Unfair Labour Practices Act, 1971, Schedule IV, Item 6 - Unfair labor practices - The Court shall not interfere in the evidence-based findings of unfair labor practices by the appropriate authority in the event of an appeal.

Maharashtra Recognition of Trade Unions and Prevention of Unfair Labour Practices Act, 1971, Section 30 and Schedule IV, Item 6 - Unfair labor practices - Award - If it is established by the Industrial Tribunal or Labour Court that an industrial establishment is engaging in unfair labor practices, it may issue any order against the employer barring such practices from continuing and may order reinstatement of the employee with or without back wages or the payment of compensation - However, a temporary order of the High Court made during proceedings challenging the validity of decisions of a Competent Authority will not satisfy the requirements u/s 30(1)(b) of an award or settlement.
