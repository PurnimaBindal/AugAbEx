The Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 226 - Admission to MBBS course - Cancellation of admission - Ground for cancellation was that the petitioner did not pass the class XI and XII exams from a recognized school in Delhi - Petitioner passed the XIIth class in the year 2000 when the clause only required passing the XIIth class from a school in Delhi - Petitioner qualified for admission in 2001 when the clause was amended - The action of the respondents is held to be against the principles of equity and unjust as the clause cannot withstand the test of reasonableness enshrined in Article 14 of the Constitution.
[Paras 13 and 19]

The Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 226 - Admission to MBBS course - Cancellation after 2 years - Ground for cancellation was making false declaration - It was held that the primary consideration for admission is merit and the false declaration did not affect merit in the selection process, hence no useful purpose can be served by cancelling admission at this stage.
[Paras 14 and 15]

The Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 226 - Delhi Right to Information Act, 2001, Sections 2(g) and 4(d) - Admission to MBBS course - Cancellation of admission - The Academic Council issued a non-speaking order - The Medical Courses Admission Committee decided to allow the petitioner to continue - It was held that since the order by the Academic Council does not meet the requirement of a speaking order as enshrined in the Act of 2001 applicable to Delhi University, the resolution is not sustainable.
