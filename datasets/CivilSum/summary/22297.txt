Constitution of India, Article 19(1)(g) - The fundamental rights guaranteed by Article 19 are not absolute and are subject to control and regulation by the State as outlined in Clauses (2) to (6) - Liberty must be limited to be effectively possessed - Right under Article 19(1)(g) is distinct from Article 301 dealing with trade, commerce or intercourse both with and within the State.
[Paras 3 and 4]
Industrial Establishments National and Festival Holidays Act, (Kerala) 1958 (as amended by Amending Act, 1990) - The Kerala State amendment increasing national and festival holidays from 7 to 13 does not infringe the fundamental rights guaranteed under Articles 19(1)(g) and 14 of the Constitution of India - It is a social legislation - The contention that it infringes on the rights of industrial employers to carry on trade or business on six additional days is not valid.
[Paras 19, 20 and 21]
Constitution of India, Article 19(1)(g) - The fundamental right guaranteed under Article 19(1) is subject to reasonable restrictions enumerated in Clause (6) - The court must consider the Directive Principles of State Policy while determining reasonableness - Restrictions must not be arbitrary or excessive and must be directly connected to the object of the Act - A just balance must be struck between the restrictions and social control - Prevailing social values and needs must also be considered - The constitutionality of the Act is presumed if there is a direct nexus between restrictions and the object sought to be achieved.
