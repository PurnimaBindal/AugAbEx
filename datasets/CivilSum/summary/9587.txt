On matters such as discipline among students and the use of unfair means during examinations, academic bodies' decisions are rarely interfered with by courts.
While there is no total prohibition on court intervention, judges are hesitant to substitute their judgment for that of academic groups.
Nonetheless, when fundamental principles of natural justice are grossly violated, courts must interfere and protect citizen rights, and academic institutions are not immune to legal consequences.
[Para 8]
Under Regulation 13 of the Haryana Board of School Education's rules prohibiting copying/unfair means, a notice was given to the petitioner during a hearing before the Unfair Means Committee.
However, the committee had already finished the proceedings that same day.
As such, the petitioner was not given real notice before the Board took action, and hence had no chance to defend themselves contrary to principles of natural justice.
[Para 9]
Before punishing a candidate for copying answers, the Respondent-Board has a duty to allow the petitioner to cross-examine the sub-examiner who submitted a report against the candidate.
Without such a chance, the report was no more than a piece of paper and could not suffice as evidence to convict the petitioner.
[Paras 10 and 11]
The availability of an alternative remedy cannot prohibit the consideration of a writ petition, despite the Board's action against the petitioner violating the principles of natural justice.
[Para 13]
The Board's punishment of the petitioner without giving them a reasonable opportunity to defend themselves was contrary to the principles of natural justice, rendering the punishment illegal and void.
