Constitution of India, Article 14 - Dismissal - Violation of principles of natural justice - There was no ground of prejudice given during appeal or personal hearing, and there is no evidence of prejudice on record - Inquiry Officer and Disciplinary Authority findings were challenged in appeal, so the employee had the opportunity to meet the employer's stance - Unless there was failure of justice or it was not in the public interest, relief may be denied - Grant of personal hearing by appellate authority negates the procedural deficiency of pre-decisional hearing - Thus, there was no violation of principles of natural justice.
[Paras 7, 22, 24]  B.
Principles of natural justice - Natural justice is the administration of justice in a common sense liberal way - It is the minimum protection of the rights of an individual against arbitrary procedures that may be adopted by judicial, quasi-judicial, and administrative authorities when making an order affecting those rights - These rules prevent such authorities from acting unjustly.
[Paras 9,10, 13]  C Constitution of India, Article 14 - Disciplinary proceeding - Punishment of dismissal - The employee committed several acts of misconduct unbecoming of a bank official - The Inquiry Officer held the charges were amply proved - Unless there was a finding that the order was contrary to law, relevant factors were not considered or irrelevant factors were considered, or no reasonable person could give such decision, interference in the order of punishment is not allowed - When the order is questioned as arbitrary under Article 14, the Court is confined to the Wednesbury principles as a secondary reviewing authority - In exceptional cases where the punishment shocks the conscience of the Court, it may direct the disciplinary authority or mold the penalty itself.
