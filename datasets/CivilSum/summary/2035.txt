The law of amalgamation of two companies made by Parliament is immune from challenge in view of Article 31-A of the Constitution, but the placement scheme and policy dealing with fitment and criteria for determining seniority of employees after amalgamation of two Banks and merger in the next Higher Scale/Cadre is not a law covered under Article-31-A.
The provisions of Section 9 of 1980 Act are similar to the provisions of Section 45 of Banking Regulations Act, 1949 and is not a law within the meaning of Articles 31-A and 31-C.
Therefore, the placement Scheme and policy of fitment and criteria for determining seniority is not immune from challenge on violation of principles enshrined under Article 14 of the Constitution of India.
The scheme of placement and fitment of employees of New Bank of India with Punjab National Bank is not a State Policy framed under Article 39(b) and (c) of the Constitution of India and, according to the Constitution of India, the terms and conditions of the employees of the transferor bank will be governed by the Rules and Regulations of the transferee bank.
The conditions of service of employees are not altered to their prejudice under the scheme framed under Section 9 of Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertakings) Act, 1980 for placement and fitment of two amalgamated banks, thus the provisions of Sections 9-A and 25FF of Industrial Disputes Act are not applicable.
The operations of both the Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertakings) Act, 1980, Section 9, and the Banking Regulations Act, 1949, Section 45 are different, and any anomalies after amalgamation must be addressed by the government.
If the scheme is rational based on the object it wants to achieve, the scheme cannot be held to be discriminatory and violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
