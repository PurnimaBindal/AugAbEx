Rule 20 of the Border Security Force Rules, 1969, states that the Central Government is not the only competent authority for removing individuals from service, as the Director-General is also authorized to take action in specific circumstances, as described by the term "as the case may be."
Additionally, if the DGP finds the explanation of the employee unsatisfactory, he can recommend action by the Central Govt.
However, even if the explanation is satisfactory, the Govt. is within its rights to direct the case to be submitted to it.
[Paras 13 to 16]
The burden of proving mala fide, bad faith, abuse or misuse of authority lies with the person alleging it, as it is difficult to establish the state of a person's mind.
Rather, the employee must demonstrate that the authority apparently acting in the legitimate exercise of power has in fact, been acting mala fide in the sense of pursuing an illegitimate aim.
[Para 18]
According to Article 226 of the Constitution of India and Rule 20 of the Border Security Force Rules, 1969, the term "as the case may be" means 'whichever the case may be' or 'as the situation may be,' indicating that one out of the various alternatives would apply to one out of the various situations and not otherwise.
