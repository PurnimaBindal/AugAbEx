The Supreme Court has the function of determining the conformity of laws made by the legislature, including rules under Article 309, with the Constitution.
Legislative authority can remove the basis of the Court's judgment with an updated law or rule.
The repealing of the amendment in the AP State and Subordinate Service Rules, 1962 cannot be taken as an effort to nullify or violate the Supreme Court's decision in KV Subba Rao v. Govt. of AP The judgment is subject to the right to legislate and bring forth the rule within the domain of the Government under the proviso to Article 309 of the constitution.

Seniority within a cadre cannot be considered an accrued or vested right of a government servant.
The loss of some places in the seniority list within the cadre does not amount to a reduction in rank, even if the chances of promotion are affected in the future.
In the matter of seniority, the government has the authority to establish any criteria.
The Court cannot strike down such a rule unless it is arbitrary, perverse, or male fide or extraneous to administrative exigencies.
The rule made by the Government to base seniority on the date of appointment and length of total service instead of the date of confirmation is in accordance with established law and cannot be found faulted.
The Government is directed to re-fix the seniority in line with the amendment dated 24.9.1992, and the amendment is held intra vires.
