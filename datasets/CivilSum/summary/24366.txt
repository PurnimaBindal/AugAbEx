Article 217 of the Constitution of India, along with Sections 16(1)(a) and (1-A) of the Consumer Protection Act, 1986, dictate the procedure for appointing the President of the State Commission.
It is made clear that consultation with the High Court does not equate to consultation with the collegium of seniormost judges.
Further, consultation with the Chief Justice in accordance with the guidelines outlined in Ashish Handa's case by the Supreme Court is sufficient.
[Paras 9 & 10].
Under Section 16(1-A) of the Consumer Protection Act, 1986, the President of the State Commission must be selected by a Selection Committee.
It is emphasized that any Selection Committee comprising of Secretaries of the State cannot select a President of the State Commission who is or has been a High Court Judge; such a process would be detrimental to judicial independence.
The proviso to this provision is intended to address a scenario where the President of the State Commission is absent for the appointment of members falling within the purview of Section 16(1)(b).
[Paras 13 & 14].
