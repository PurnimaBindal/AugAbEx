Constitution of India, 1950, Article 226 - Reasoning - Disposal of writ petition by High Court without indicating reasons - The judgment of High Court is set aside and the matter is remitted for fresh consideration.
It is held that reasons introduce clarity in an order, and the High Court should have set forth its reasons.
The giving of reasons is essential for good administration and to substitute subjectivity by objectivity.
The right to reason is an indispensable part of a sound judicial system.
The affected party can know why the decision has gone against him.
One of the salutary requirements of natural justice is spelling out reasons for the order made, in other words, a speaking-out.
The "inscrutable face of the sphinx" is ordinarily incongruous with a judicial or quasi-judicial performance.
