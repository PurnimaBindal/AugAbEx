Constitution of India, Articles 226 and 311 - Writ jurisdiction/Punishment - Departmental enquiry - The Court will not replace its own opinion if two views are possible on the evidence provided.
However, if a punishment order is unreasonable, perverse, and based on no evidence, it must be annulled.
While it is known that the writ court cannot re-examine and judge evidence, it can come forward if evidence is either absent or consists solely of suspicion.
In such cases, the law requires evidence in the form of proof, and even a surmise or conjecture cannot replace the absence of evidence.
When a finding is made on pure surmise and conjecture, in the absence of any evidence whatsoever, such a finding is considered incorrect, and the writ Court will have proper grounds to interfere.
