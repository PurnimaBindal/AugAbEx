The Labour Court is not allowed to direct reinstatement with back wages for workmen found guilty of misconduct to show sympathy, as this would go against public interest.
In cases where a workman commits misconduct in his fiduciary capacity, the loss of confidence from his employer justifies the punishment of dismissal, which cannot be considered excessively harsh.
[Paras 10 to 16]

The examination of passengers of a vehicle is not necessary to prove the misconduct of a bus conductor accused of misappropriation of funds.
The fact that the conductor was in possession of excess money is sufficient evidence, regardless of the amount.
[Para 16]

In situations where punishment has already been established based on evidence, the Labour Court will not typically substitute its own opinion.
[Para 18]

Once a workman has been found guilty of misappropriating employer's money, the punishment of dismissal from service cannot be deemed shockingly disproportionate.
Thus, directing reinstatement with back wages would not be justified in such cases.
[Paras 19, 20, 22 and 23]

Employees of a State Road Transport Corporation are not government servants and are not entitled to the protection of Article 311 of the Constitution.
Their service conditions are governed by the terms and conditions of their appointment, and their employment can be terminated in those terms.
[Para 21]
