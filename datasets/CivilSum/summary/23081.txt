When interpreting statutes, courts should avoid adopting constructions that are absurd or unjust.
The chosen construction should be consistent with the system that the statute aims to regulate, while rejecting alternatives that could introduce uncertainty, friction, or confusion.
[Para 24] B.
The Bihar Development of Ayurvedic and Unani Systems of Medicine Act, 1951, Section 17 - Indian Medicine Central Council Act, 1970 - Indian Medicine Central Council (Amendment) Act, 2003, Sections 13A, 13B and 13C - Education - Graduate of Ayurvedic Medicine and Surgery (GAMS).
The CCIM prescribes regulations for the BAMS (Bachelor of Ayurvedic Medicine and Surgery) program at the graduate level, as well as the MD (Ay.)
course at the postgraduate level.
GAMS degrees will be considered recognized degrees for the purposes of taking admission to higher courses of study and employment.
If a degree was legally earned before the 2003 Amending Act, it should be recognized, even if the medical college did not seek permission from the Central Government within three years of the Amending Act's commencement.
