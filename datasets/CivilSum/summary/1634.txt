Punjab Police Rules, 1934, Rule 8.
121 - Extraordinary leave - Misconduct - Absence from duty - Constable of Police - In the case of a constable who has been absent from work for 40 days and has also been absent during the inquiry until the date of dismissal, a punishment of dismissal from service will be given.
Later on, a separate order will treat the period of absence as extraordinary leave.
This order will have no connection with the penalty order and will not represent forgiveness for misconduct.
The act of granting some type of leave and treating the period of absence as some kind of leave are two different things.
Under no circumstances should one assume that the misconduct has been condoned when the accused did not even attend the inquiry.
Treating the period of absence as extraordinary leave is only for record keeping and does not represent condonation of misconduct.
[Paras 8 to 10]
Punjab Police Rules, 1934, Rule 16.2 - Pension - Length of service - Dismissal from service on the basis of absence from duty for 40 days - Unexcused absence from work does not always constitute an egregious act of misconduct and incurability and absolute unfitness.
The length of service and the demand for a pension must be taken into account.
The dismissal order should be deemed a termination order, with the period of duty during the accused's stay and suspension being used for pension purposes as per rules.
