Under the Payment of Wages Act, 1936, the Court had to decide whether or not the respondent was due any wages after he received a punishment for committing fraud and applied for a claim of wages.
However, both the lower Courts exceeded their jurisdiction by assuming the role of a Civil Court and stating that the punishment was unlawfully imposed.
It was the responsibility of the respondent to approach a Civil Court for the reversal of the penalty and the Payment of Wages Act authority had no power to overturn the department's decision.
As a result, the revision was granted.
