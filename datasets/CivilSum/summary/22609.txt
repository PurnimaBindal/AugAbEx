Constitution of India, Articles 14, 226 - Disciplinary proceedings - Malice - If the delinquent accepts the charges held to be proved even when exonerated of others and given full opportunity to defend themselves, the proceedings cannot be said to be actuated by malice of the authority.
[Para 10]
HMT Limited Conduct, Discipline & Appeal Rules, 1988, Rules 8 and 233 - Suspension - Rule 233 provides that an employee under suspension shall be entitled to subsistence allowance - The order of suspension may be passed as a measure of penalty under Rule 8, and once the order is passed under Rule 233 and subsistence allowance is paid, it cannot be assumed that the order was passed under Rule 8 as punishment.
[Paras 8 & 15 to 17]
Constitution of India, Articles 14, 226 - Alternate remedy - The availability of an alternative forum for redressal of grievance does not necessarily mean that the power of judicial review is not to be exercised.
A writ court may still exercise its discretion in cases where the court or tribunal lacks inherent jurisdiction or for enforcement of fundamental rights or in case principles of natural justice have been violated or vires of the Act or Rules have been questioned.
[Paras 11 and 18]
Constitution of India, Articles 14, 226 - Disciplinary proceedings - Admission - Evidence - It is well settled that things admitted need not be proved and findings on the basis of admission cannot be questioned on the grounds of the availability of no evidence.
[Paras 13 & 14]
Constitution of India, Articles 14, 226 - Use of abuses - Dismissal from service - Quantum of punishment - The quantum of punishment is to be considered on the facts of each case.
Verbal abuse is sufficient for inflicting a punishment of dismissal.
A court or tribunal will not interfere with the quantum of punishment where the employee has been proved guilty of serious misconduct.
Deterrent punishment is a must to maintain discipline in the workplace.
