The question before the Labour Court was whether striking technologists were entitled to wages, with the Court being able to answer the question in favor of either the Institute or the Association after examining the evidence.
The Court should not have passed an interim award, as it may or may not grant relief at the end of the proceeding.
[Para 9]
Breaks in service result in the discontinuity of service and affect seniority and benefits.
The withdrawal of an order regarding breaks in service does not automatically entitle employees to back wages.
The entitlement to wages depends on different factors, and the rule of "no work no pay" applies.
Employees cannot disturb work and demand payment.
[Para 13] Editorial Note: The Labour Court was mistaken in its view that revocation of an order regarding breaks in service makes employees eligible for wages.
The Central Services (Classification, Control, and Appeal) Rules and the Punjab Essential Services Maintenance Act, 1947 declare the services of PGI as essential.
Employees of essential service institutions are not entitled to any wages during the strike period, as the right to wages is closely linked with the performance of duties.
Those who do not work have no right to the wages.
