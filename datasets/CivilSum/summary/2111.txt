Article 16 of the Constitution of India deals with Compassionate appointment.
A petitioner sought appointment in a bank on compassionate grounds.
The request was denied on the basis that under the new "died-in-harness" scheme, the word of the deceased employee was not entitled.
The petitioner claimed to be governed under the earlier scheme as the death of her father took place before Nov 2000.
It was held that when the employer consciously takes a decision not to make any recruitment based on existing rules, on the ground that such rules are undergoing a process of change, like amendment or repeal and re-enactment, no right accrues to any one for seeking consideration for appointment in terms of old rules.
The old rules, when amended, repealed, re-enacted, superseded, or reframed, are replaced by new rules.
[Paras 14 and 15].
Article 226 of the Constitution of India deals with Writ proceedings.
In such proceedings, it is mandatory to implead necessary party.
However, the only necessary party was not impleaded as a party respondent in writ petition even after getting sufficient opportunity.
As a result, the writ petition is liable to be dismissed.
[Para 2].
