The Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 16 state that there should be equal pay for equal work, but the court cannot apply this principle if there is no material for comparison between the two jobs.
The Radio Operators of CRPF claimed that they should have the same pay as Radio Operators in civil and Directorate of Police Wireless, but there was no material to show comparative qualifications, method of recruitment, degree of skill, experience involved in performance of job, training required, responsibilities undertaken, and other facilities in addition to pay scales.
Additionally, recommendations made by the department or Pay Commission are not binding on the government, which is the ultimate authority.
Therefore, the High Court cannot restructure posts in the cadre by directing the authorities to grant pay scale equal to the promotional post to the feeder post.
[Paras 8, 13 and 14]

Under the Constitution of India, Article 226, the court should typically leave matters of pay scale to the administration, except in the proven case of hostile discrimination.
It is not appropriate for the court to grant a particular pay scale to the petitioners without examining the implications and impact of such directions.
[Para 14]
