As per the Constitution of India, Articles 226/227, in case of absence from duty leading to termination of employment, the respondent must provide the petitioner with a reasonable opportunity to explain the situation.
The petitioner, in this case, requested for a one-year leave without pay after the death of his father, thus demonstrating his intent to continue working.
Additionally, there was no proper enquiry conducted to justify the termination of the petitioner's services, and hence, the award has been declared illegal and quashed.
The petitioner has been awarded back wages to the extent of 50 percent.
[Paras 4 and 5]
The Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 2(oo) mandates a proper enquiry against the workman who has abandoned his job, which was not done in the case of the petitioner.
Despite the workman not submitting a reply to the alleged charge-sheet, it was still the responsibility of the management to prove that the workman abandoned his job without any valid reason, thereby falling outside the purview of retrenchment as defined under Section 2(oo) of the Act.
As no such proof was presented before the Enquiry Officer, the impugned award has been quashed, and the petitioner has been reinstated with a directive for Respondent No. 2 to pay back wages to the petitioner at the rate of 50 percent from the date of issuance of the demand notice.
