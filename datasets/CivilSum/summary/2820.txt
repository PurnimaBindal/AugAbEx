Under Section 617 of the Companies Act, 1956, a Government Company means a company in which the Government holds not less than 51% paid-up share capital.
[Para 60]
A company that receives full assistance from the State and is fully controlled by it is considered a State within the meaning of Article 12 of the Constitution and is subject to writ jurisdiction of the High Court, under Sections 192 and 617 of the Companies Act, 1956 and Articles 12 and 226 of the Constitution.
[Para 61]
A writ cannot be issued to enforce a contract of personal service that is absolutely contractual in nature and whose termination was also strictly as per the contract, according to Articles 14 and 226 of the Constitution and Section 192 of the Companies Act, 1956.
[Paras 66 and 72]
An order of termination of contract without any stigma and without being punitive in nature cannot be challenged in a writ petition under Article 226 of the Constitution, and Section 192 of the Companies Act, 1956, even if it is in terms of the contract, under Articles 14 and 226 of the Constitution.
[Paras 66 and 72]
Though Section 318 of the Companies Act, 1956, provides for compensation to the Managing Director, the use of "may" implies that the provision cannot be considered mandatory.
A writ petition is not a remedy to claim damages for wrongful termination under Articles 14 and 226 of the Constitution and Section 318(4) of the Companies Act, 1956.
[Paras 75 and 76]
A petitioner who conceals material facts from the Court is not entitled to any relief under Article 226 of the Constitution.
