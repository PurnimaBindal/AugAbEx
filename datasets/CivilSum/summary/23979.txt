Constitution of India, Articles 341, 342 - Constitution (Scheduled Castes) Order, 1950 - Constitution (Scheduled Tribes) Order, 1950 - Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes Orders (Amendment) Act, 1976, Part IX-Maharashtra, Entry 19 - Parliament is only competent to include/exclude caste/tribe from Scheduled Castes/Tribes list by law - President may specify S/Ts for a state/union territory - Once an order is issued under Article 341(1) and 342(1), no modification or enquiry is permissible - Such orders cannot be altered by State Govt/Court/Tribunal.
[Paras 10, 11, and 13] B.
Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes Orders (Amendment) Act, 1976, Part IX-Maharashtra, Entry 19 - Halba-Koshti is not included in Entry 19 of the list despite amendment of the list in 1976 - The addition of names to the list is done specifically, and representations to include Halba-Koshti were rejected - DB decisions in Bhaiya Ram Munda and Dina v. Narayan Singh is not correct in allowing an enquiry.
[Paras 12, 25, 26, and 29] C. Constitution of India, Articles 226, 162, 341, 342 - State Govt/Court/Tribunal have no power to determine the inclusion of a particular caste/tribe in the Schedule of the Presidential Order, which is not expressly included - Allowing such persons to claim the benefit of presidential orders would defeat the purpose of reservations for socially and educationally backward people - DB decision in Bhaiya Ram Munda and Dina v. Narayan Singh is not correct in allowing an enquiry.
[Paras 13, 20, 21, 24, 26, 28, and 29] The following positions emerge: 1.
No enquiry/evidence is permissible to declare that any tribe/tibal community/group within any tribe or tribal community is included in the general name, even if not specifically mentioned in the concerned entry - 2.
The Scheduled Tribes Order must be read as it is - 3.
Parliament alone can amend a notification issued under Clause (1) of Article 342 - 4.
The list of Scheduled Tribes specified in the notification issued under Clause (1) of Article 342 cannot be modified/alter by State Governments/Courts/Tribunals - 5.
The DB decisions in Bhaiya Ram Munda v. Anirudh Patar & others, 1971(1) SCR 804 and Dina v. Narayan Singh, 38 ELR 212, allowing an enquiry and evidence are not correct.
[Para 29] D. Admission/Reservation - Admission obtained on the basis of a wrong certificate for Scheduled Tribe is invalid - already passed medical course and practicing on that basis - Admission/degree granted is not disturbed, but no further benefits can be given based on the certificate - Final admission/appointments cannot be disturbed.
