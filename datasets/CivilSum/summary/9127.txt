Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 33-C(2) - Working Journalist (Conditions of Service & Miscellaneous Provisions) Rules 1957, Rules 36 - The Working Journalists and other Newspaper Employees (Conditions of Service) and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1955, Sections 13 and 17 - Jurisdiction/Wages due - When a newspaper employee's employer owes them an admitted amount under the Act, the employee can apply to the State Government, which can issue a certificate for the amount due and refer the matter to the Labour Court for a speedy remedy - The jurisdiction of the Labour Court is not barred, even if the workman has not sought settlement or determination of their wages - If the claim is disputed, the Labour Court will make a decision - If the application reveals that the claim can be granted, the Labour Court has the jurisdiction to proceed with the case.

Civil Procedure Code, Section 9 - Jurisdiction - Contents of the plaint - The court has the jurisdiction to proceed with a case if it can grant the relief from a bare reading of the petition - However, if the claim is denied, the court can make the decision, but denying the claim doesn't take away the court's jurisdiction.
