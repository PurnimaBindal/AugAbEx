Constitution of India, Article 191(1)(a) - Representation of the People Act, 1951, Sections 1 - Government servant - Disqualification for member of Legislative Assembly - The Coal India Ltd clerk is not considered to hold an office of profit under the Govt. of India within the meaning of Article 191(1)(a) of the Constitution, nor be a managing agent, manager or secretary of any company or corporation in the capital of which Govt. has not less than 25% shares to be disqualified from being a member of Legislative Assembly.
The Board of Directors of Coal India Ltd has the power to conduct business and manage day-to-day affairs, and the President of India does not have control over appointment, removal, or disciplinary measures below Board level.
The salaries, emoluments, and perks of employees are payable from the company's funds and not augmented by the Central Govt.
The posts of clerk do not satisfy the tests of Article 191(1)(a) of the Constitution, and the rejection of nomination papers of the respondent has rightly been held invalid by the High Court.
Factors such as appointing authority, power to terminate, determination of remuneration, source of payment of remuneration, and authority vested with the power to control and issue directions have to be considered for holding an office of profit under the Govt. No relationship of master and servant between the holder and Govt. is necessary.
The question needs to be considered if holding such office would the Govt. be in a position to so influence the member as to interfere with his independence in functioning as a member of Legislative Assembly and whether his holding of the two offices involves a conflict of interests inter se.
