Violating mandatory provisions of the Constitution of India, specifically Articles 14 and 16, would render an appointment wholly illegal.
In this case, the selection process was fraudulent and deceitful with no room for individual innocence.
Thus, the entire selection must be set aside.
[Para]

According to the Constitution of India, breaching Articles 14 and 16 through appointments made without advertising or inviting applications from the open market is impermissible.
Additionally, regularisation is not allowed if the appointments violated statutory rules, as those who entered through the backdoor should exit the same way.
[Paras 3, 4, 5, and 17]

Under the Constitution of India, an appointment in co-operative societies constitutes a public appointment.
