Constitution of India, Article 311 - Criminal proceedings - Departmental proceedings - There is no constitutional or legal restriction to carrying out both proceedings simultaneously - Employers have the authority to deal with their employees' conduct separately, even when criminal prosecution is being carried out for the same facts, in order to avoid keeping an inefficient employee in service - If an employee is prejudiced, it is up to the court to determine whether both proceedings should be allowed to proceed simultaneously based on the facts and circumstances of the case - In this case, the employee did not object to the proceedings and the proceedings were completed - The appeal against the dismissal from service was also dismissed - The employee was given full opportunity at every stage - Although the employee was later convicted in the criminal case, the acquittal in appeal does not entitle the employee to claim reinstatement or reconsideration of the departmental proceedings, as the punishment was based on the material contained in the enquiry file, without taking into account the prosecution and conviction - The petition was dismissed.

Constitution of India, Articles 311 and 226 - Speaking order - Although a speaking order is desirable in a judicial dismissal case, where the final decision is based on a proper assessment of broader factors, it would not be appropriate to set aside the order solely on the grounds that all relevant reasons have not been stated in the order.
