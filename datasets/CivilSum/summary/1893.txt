Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 10 - Reference - Terms of reference - Issues on reference - The Tribunal or the parties to a reference cannot exceed the limits set out in the terms of reference.
Before framing a reference, the authority should consider all the available materials.
However, this should not impede the adoption of a positive attitude to save the reference and avoid technical objections to its scope and delay in disposal thereof.
The Tribunal should frame issues after hearing the parties so as to include and highlight the entire subject matter of the dispute covered by the terms of reference, including incidental matters.
It is important that the terms of reference are spelled out with care, exactitude, and detail to avoid controversy over their scope.
It is also crucial that all disputes between the parties be referred for adjudication at one instance to enable a single adjudication of all disputes.
The Industrial Tribunal may frame additional issues incidental to and flowing out of the Terms of Reference.
A semantic and hypertechnical interpretation of the Terms of Reference must be avoided to ensure a positive and holistic approach.
