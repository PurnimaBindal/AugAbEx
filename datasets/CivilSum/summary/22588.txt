The BSF Act and Rules provide protection from bias or likelihood of bias against an officer.
An accused must be tried by his Commandant, but if the Commandant himself is a witness or interested in the matter, the accused must be attached to a different unit.
[Paras 17, 18 & 19]
Rule 46 is an exception to Rule 45B and aims to protect accused from Commandant bias.
The Commandant must not exercise power under Rule 46 and show any interest.
[Para 20]
Rule 16(7) can be exercised in a general manner, not in cases where Rule 46 applies.
[Para 21]
The disciplinary authority alone must apply his independent mind to the materials on record and decide if disciplinary action is necessary.
The impact of bias on natural justice is discussed with case law.
[Paras 22 to 26]
A legal or pecuniary interest or personal bias of the authority counts as bias and affects natural justice.
[Paras 23, 24]
