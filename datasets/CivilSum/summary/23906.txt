Constitution of India, Article 235 - Judicial officer/Adverse remarks - It is important to not record adverse remarks against a judicial officer casually and based on doubts or surprise visits alone.
Guidelines laid down by the Supreme Court in IC Jain's case must be followed.
If adverse remarks are based on non-existent facts, they are unjustified and liable to be quashed.
These remarks cannot be the basis for pre-mature retirement.
[Paras 30 to 32]

U.P.
Judicial Officers (Retirement on Superannuation) Rules, 1992 - Fundamental Rule 56, contained in Financial Hand Book, Vol. II, Part 2 to 4 - Retirement of judicial officer - The retirement age is fixed at 60 years in the Rules framed on the directions of the Supreme Court in All India Judges Association case.
Once an incumbent has been retained to be retired at the age of 60 years, there is no occasion to reassess their service record to retire them prematurely.
An order for pre-mature retirement passed on the recommendation of the High Court is wholly erroneous.
[Para 16]
