The Beedi and Cigar Workers (Conditions of Employment) Act, 1966, Sections 31(2) and 38(2) and the Kerala Shops and Commercial Establishment Act, 1960, Section 31(2) regarding appeals are in conflict but the provisions of the former will take precedence.
Even though the Kerala Act has provisions for appeal against orders of dismissal before a designated authority, an appeal made under Section 31(2) of the Beedi and Cigar Workers Act will still be valid.

If an employee wilfully absents themselves from work, it must be reflected in both the Muster Roll/ Register of Hours of Work and Wage Register, and they must be given an opportunity in line with natural justice.
Refusal of wages must also be recorded.
Failure to provide such an opportunity would be a denial of employment, not abandonment by the employee as per the Beedi and Cigar Workers (Conditions of Employment) Act, 1966, Section 31(2), which is upheld by the Constitution of India, Article 14.
[Para 8] C The suspension and dismissal of an employee with ten years of service without reason and without the opportunity to face a domestic enquiry is a violation of natural justice and thus bad in law under the Constitution of India, Article 14.
