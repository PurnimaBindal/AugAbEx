In accordance with the Pension Regulations for Army, Part I, 1961, Paragraph 173, Casual Pensionary Awards Entitlement Rules 1982, Rules 14(b) and (c), the responsible authority bears the onus of proof for disability pension claims for diseases not attributable to military service.
If a person was discharged due to a disease linked to their military service, it is assumed to be attributable to or exacerbated by their military service.
In the case when the Medical Board has labeled the disease "constitutional," it cannot absolve the authority of proving otherwise, especially if the person discharged served largely in the field and ought to be evaluated with leniency under the Rules.
[Para 10]

The Appendix II and III of the Pension Regulations for Army, Part I, 1961, Regulation 173, features an extensive list of diseases associated with the stress and strain of military service.
Although "Schizophrenia" is not specifically included in these records, since the list contains diseases such as "psychosis" and "psychoneurosis" affected by military service stress, it is reasonable to assume that "schizophrenia" is also present.
As a result, a petitioner who developed schizophrenia while serving in the military is eligible for disability benefits equal to the extent of their disability.
[Paras 11 and 12]
