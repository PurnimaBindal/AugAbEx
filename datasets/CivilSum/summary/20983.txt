Constitution of India, Articles 245 & 246 - Education - Legislative powers of Central and State Governments in the field of education - Parliament has plenary power to make laws in relation to any of the matters enumerated in List I, although some of these matters may be also within the purview of State legislatures - The non-obstante clause contained in Article 246(1) establishes the preeminence of the law made by Parliament in the event of a conflict between the law made by Parliament and that made by the State legislature with respect to a matter falling under List I, notwithstanding the latter's exclusive power in that regard - While Parliament and the State legislature are both supreme within their allotted fields, the Parliamentary law would prevail in the event of a conflicting exercise of power by the State legislature - The same applies to matters enumerated under List III - Article 245 provides the means to resolve conflicts.
[Paras 9 to 12]  B Constitution of India, Articles 245 & 246 - Andhra Pradesh Education Act, 1982, Section 20(3)(a)(i) - All India Council of Technical Education Act, 1987, Section 10 - Vires of Andhra Act - Education - Legislative powers of Central and State Governments in the field of education - Repugnancy between the Central and State Acts - Andhra Pradesh Act and AICTE Act operate in different fields - If there is a conflict between a law made by Parliament in exercise of its power under Article 246 with respect to a matter enumerated in List I or List III and a law made by the State legislature which deals with the same matter but falls under List II, the former shall prevail - Section 20 of the Andhra Pradesh Act and Section 10(1)(a) of the AICTE Act do not overlap in any manner - The Andhra Pradesh Act promotes the objectives of the AICTE Act with respect to coordinating higher education while maintaining adequate standards, and is strictly limited to the educational needs and facilities of the State - General surveys under Section 10(1)(a) of the AICTE Act are not directed towards the same end.
