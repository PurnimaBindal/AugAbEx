Constitution of India, Article 226 - Adverse remarks - Relevance of representation - The Director General of Police was justified in refusing to consider the petitioner's representations made after a number of years, as it would be unfair to unsettle the rights of officials who may have been promoted during that time.
The petitioner had prior success in manipulating the administrative system to get adverse remarks expunged, and failed to make timely representations in other cases.
The petition was dismissed.
[Paras 6 and 7]

Constitution of India, Article 226 - Jurisdiction - Adverse remarks - Reversion - Representation - The High Court declined to exercise jurisdiction under Article 226 to set aside the impugned order for adverse remarks, as granting a discretionary favour to the petitioner would protect their undue gain received from the DIG of Police.
However, the dismissal of the writ petition did not preclude the petitioner from making a representation to the competent authority against their reversion on the grounds that juniors have been promoted as SIs.
[Paras 8 and 9]
