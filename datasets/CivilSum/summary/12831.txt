The definition of workman in the Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923 includes a person employed in construction work, such as repair work being carried out in a building.
The lack of pleadings regarding the number of stories or the height of the building in question is not enough to deny the claim of a workman.
Additionally, repair or replacement work on a school building owned by a trust would fall under the employer's trade or business and the person engaged in that activity would be considered a workman.

The term "casual" in the Act is not strictly defined and depends on the facts of each case.
Employers must prove that the work was of a casual nature, and if they fail to do so, the workman will be covered under the Act.
