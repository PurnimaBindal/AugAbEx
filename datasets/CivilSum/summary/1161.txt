The Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Sections 2(g) and 36(2) - Employer - Association of employers - Representative of employer - Section 2(g) of the Act defines the term 'employer' with relation to industries, and this is sufficient for the purpose of the Act.
The intention of legislature is to use the word only in the context of industrial employers.
The concept of employer in the Act is that of employer in relation of an industry alone.
The word 'employer' is used in Section 36(2) of the Act only in relation to employers of workmen as defined in the Act.
Hence, the association of employers referred to in Section 36(2)(a) and (b) is an association of employers alone and not of others.
The Federation of State Chamber of Commerce and Industry consists of not only employers but also of individuals of other status and non-employers and is not an association of employers.
Any officer of such association is not eligible/entitled to represent the management before the Labour Tribunal in the capacity of an officer U/s 36(2)(a) and (b).
Industrial Disputes Act, Section 36(2)(a) and (b) and sub-section (4) - Representative of employer - Officer of association of employers or the Federation of associations of employers - Members of Federation of State Chamber of Commerce and Industry - Advocates designated as Honorary Jt.
Secretaries of federation - Such an advocates are not eligible and entitled to represent the employers without the consent of the workman and permission of the Court/Tribunal.
Such advocates are designated merely to overcome the provisions of Section 36(4) of the Act.
Constitution of India, Article 226 - Industrial Disputes Act, Section 36(2) - Question of fact - Claim of a person being an officer of an association of employers is a question of fact to be determined by the tribunal/Court on evidence.
High Court should not reverse such findings of fact - It must be left to the Tribunals.
