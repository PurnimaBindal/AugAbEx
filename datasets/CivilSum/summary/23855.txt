The Constitution of India, Articles 141 and 162 state that the legislative power of the State extends to rendering a judicial decision ineffective, but only by removing or altering the legal basis of the judgment.
The Division Bench of Bombay High Court affirmed that the community "Bawa" is included in the list of Nomadic Tribes, a finding that was also affirmed by the Supreme Court.
The State cannot invalidate the Court's judgment without altering or neutralizing the legal basis of the decision based on additional relevant material.
[Paras 9 and 10]
The Constitution of India, Articles 16 and 162 allow the State to recognize a particular community in a particular manner with good reasons after considering all relevant factors.
There cannot be a baseless classification between members of the same community unless it is established that the excluded class has lost its disadvantages in society.
The State of Maharashtra's resolution that reservation benefits are only available to members of the "Gosavi" or "Bawa" community originally from Maharashtra and not migrants from Sindh is unconstitutional and an attempt to overrule the Court's judgment, which was quashed.
[Para 10]
