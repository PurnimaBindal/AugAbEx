According to Section 2(15-A) and 2(15-B) of the Employees' State Insurance Act, 1948, total permanent disablement can be considered as any disabling injury that results in the complete and permanent inability to perform work duties.
In the case of the appellant, who sustained an injury resulting in the amputation of his right arm up to the shoulder joint, he not only lost his job but would also be unable to work as a cotton feeder elsewhere.
Despite the fact that the injury would normally be classified as 90% permanent partial disablement, it qualifies as 100% total permanent disablement under the law.
The impugned order was overturned to reflect this determination.
