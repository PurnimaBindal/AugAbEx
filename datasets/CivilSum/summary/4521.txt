According to the Constitution of India, specifically Articles 226/227, whether domestic supply charges or non-domestic supply charges are levied depends on the primary purpose of the building's use.
The schedule of tariff illustrates that if premises are used for residential purposes, they are subject to domestic supply charges.
If they are used for non-residential purposes, they are subject to non-domestic supply charges.
Dharamsala, despite charging nominal fees, is meant for residential purposes and therefore qualifies for domestic supply charges.
However, schools, hospitals, and temples without residential activities are subject to non-domestic supply charges.
