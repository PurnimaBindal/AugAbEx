Petitioners who were conductors of Punjab Roadways had been fined on the spot by Inspectors for allowing certain passengers to travel without tickets, and then later suspended and served with impugned-sheets.
The action of respondents in inflicting fines upon petitioners apparently appears to be the extension of their executive powers to take disciplinary action for alleged violation of service rules or infringement of their duties.
However, since the petitioners were subjected to punishment for their misconduct, subsequently they could not be subjected to the rigours of disciplinary action on the basis of impugned orders, which is a case of double jeopardy under the general law.
The action of respondents is also in violation of provisions of Section 26 of General Clauses Act and the guarantee of Article 20 of the Constitution, and the impugned orders were set aside.
Double jeopardy is a maxim that no one should be punished twice for one fault, and is acknowledged as a principle of civilized society.
The General Clauses Act, Section 26 prohibits double punishment for same offence or cause, and the protection against double jeopardy was given a constitutional status in the country under Article 20(2) of the Constitution.
