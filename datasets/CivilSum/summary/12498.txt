Adverse remarks cannot be passed against an employee without any basis according to the Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 226 as well as the Punjab Civil Services Rules (as applicable to Haryana) Vol. 1, Rule 4.4.
The Reporting Officer is under an obligation to have some material in his possession for making adverse remarks, and no material was shown in support of adverse remarks made against a Traffic Head Constable who was posted in police lines during the mentioned period.
Reporting Officers must be more careful while making such remarks.
Remarks of doubtful integrity made in a very casual manner in violation of instructions are baseless and unjustified.
[Paras 15 and 16]

The Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 226 also require that review of adverse remarks be conducted properly.
In this case, the reviewing authority agreed with unsigned adverse remarks without asking for comments from the Reporting Officer, which is baseless and arbitrary.
No material was available before the reviewing authority to approve the adverse remarks.
[Para 17]

 According to the Constitution of India, Articles 162 and 226, the State Government issues instructions to its functionaries, who are obligated to follow them.
Adverse remarks recorded in breach of the government's instruction in a casual way, affecting the service career of an employee, is arbitrary and illegal.
Consequently, the adverse remarks were quashed.
[Paras 14,19]
