Central Civil Service (Pension) Rules, 1972, Rule 48-B - Pension - The appellant had received reports of the shelter home for women being used by respondents (Ex Minister for Social Welfare and others) for illegal and immoral purposes - Due to an estranged relationship and a series of events, the appellant was eventually removed from service - At the time of removal from service, she had 23 years of qualifying service - The appellant is entitled to pension, with a Weightage of a period not exceeding 5 years being added to the actual service rendered by a civil servant as period of grace, if the total qualifying service does not exceed 33 years and if it does not take the civil servant beyond the date of superannuation - Appellant was highly competent, hard working, sincere and devoted official - She has been referred to as an asset to the department - Decreed that appellant is entitled to pension and other retirement benefits, and awarded a lumpsum compensation for loss of reputation and honour and agony she had to suffer in the long battle, fixed at Rs.3 lakhs, payable jointly by respondents.

Administrative Law - Administrative functions - Public duty - Attempts were made to molest the appellant - The fact that a prima facie case is disclosed and ordered an independent enquiry concerning allegations regarding endeavour to molest the appellant in the interest of justice by a High Constitutional dignitary, Governor of Tamil Nadu, Administrator of Pondicherry, should not have been ignored - A responsible statutory authority or administration owes a duty to the public, to discharge its functions reasonably, honestly and bona fide, without driving the aggrieved persons from pillar to post, and should there be any non-excusable lapse on this score - The concerned authority or administration should be held responsible for the loss or damage accruing thereby to the aggrieved persons.

Evidence - Evidence of a victim of a sex offence is entitled to great weight - If a prosecutrix is an adult and of full understanding, the Court is entitled to base a conviction on her evidence unless the same is shown to be infirm and not trustworthy.
