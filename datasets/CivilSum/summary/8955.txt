As per the law laid down in Ramesh Pal v. State of Haryana, 1992(2) SCT 636 (DB) and the principles laid down by the Supreme Court in AIR 1953 SC 58 DN Banerji v. PR Mukherjee, the functions of the Public Health Department in supplying water to rural and urban areas fall within the definition of 'industry' as per the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 2(j).
Absence of profit motive does not remove said activity from the ambit of term 'Industry' if it is otherwise covered by the said definition.
[Paras 9 & 10]

Daily wage workers are considered workmen within the definition of Section 2(s) and for the purposes of sections 25-F, 25-G and 25-H, even if they are not given any appointment letter nor is their seniority maintained under Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 2(s).
The legislature does not make any distinction between the different types of employees.
[Paras 12 and 13]

Completion of 240 days service is not a necessary condition to attract the provisions of Sections 25-G and 25-H for the rule of 'last come first go.'
The provisions contain a clause of equality on the basis of 'equality clause' enshrined in the Constitution.
Breach of the rule of 'last come first go' violates the 'equality clause' and thus violates the provisions of Section 25-G The employee is entitled to the protection of Sections 25-G and H without any reference to the completion of 240 days.
[Para 14]

The termination of an employee in violation of the Rule of 'last come first go' can be challenged in writ jurisdiction under Constitution of India, Article 226.
However, if the workman seeks back wages, he/she has to go to labour court to claim it by way of application under Section 33-C(2) of the Industrial Disputes Act, along with enabling the management to prove the gainful employment during the termination period.
