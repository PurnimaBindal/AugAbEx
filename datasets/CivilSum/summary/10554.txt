Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India require equal pay for equal work.
This principle does not, however, guarantee that a school teacher should be treated the same as a University Professor.
Teachers may receive equal pay only if they perform the same duties and can be classified according to their qualifications, responsibilities, and job requirements.

To work as a lecturer, a Post Graduate degree of MA or M.Sc is required.
Since the appellant only holds a degree in weaving and hosiery, as well as a graduate degree, he cannot be considered qualified for the position.
Even many individuals with MPhil or PhD degrees are unable to secure positions as lecturers across the country.
[Para 9]

Different pay scales for instructors and lecturers may be justified based on academic qualifications.
If the university chooses to revise the pay scales of instructors to a lower grade, it may classify those instructors according to their academic qualifications.
In this case, the classification was based on these justified criteria, and is not discriminatory or arbitrary.
[Para 10]

Instructors and lecturers have different levels of education and responsibilities.
If a university differentiates between the two according to their academic qualifications and assigns different duties to them, such categorization and differentiation is not unconstitutional.
