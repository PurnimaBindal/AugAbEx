According to the Delhi University Act, Section 31, the Vice Chancellor must first determine if an emergency exists before making any changes to the admission policy, using his emergency powers under Statutes II-G(4).
If there is no such determination, or justification for the use of emergency powers, without consulting the Academic Council, such an order will be void ab initio.
Furthermore, an order cannot be passed mere on advice of the Admission Committee; ratification by the Academic Council later on is immaterial.
Clause VIII(a) of the Bulletin of Information in the 1992 admission policy is not in line with regulations, hence, it has to be struck down.

Article 226 of the Constitution of India allows minimal interference by the courts in University matters, but the court must strike down any arbitrary exercises of power.
In this case, higher-merit students being denied admission to a college while lower-merit students receive admission cannot be allowed.
According to policy, students have a right to shift, which cannot be subverted by changing the policy immediately.
The amendment has to be struck down.
