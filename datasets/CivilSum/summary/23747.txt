As per Army Rules, 1954, Rules 96 and 95, a delinquent officer can be given an advance of Rs. 10,000 to engage counsel for their trial by Court Martial.
However, a second advance of Rs. 15,000 is not permitted by the rules.
The accused cannot use the contention of not being able to engage a second counsel as a defense.
[Para 9]

When a General Court Martial is assembled and the accused is arraigned, Rule 81 of Army Rules, 1954 provides that the court shall continue the trial from day-to-day in accordance with Rule 81 unless an adjournment was necessary for the ends of justice or that such continuance is impracticable.
[Para 9]

Though there is no bar in the Army Rules, 1954 to provide an officer of JAG Branch as a defending officer in a trial by General Court Martial, it is not done as a general policy due to the nature of functions and duties of a JAG Branch officer when appointed to a Court Martial.
[Para 10]

Rule 84 and 85 of Army Rules, 1954 provide that the accused may name three defending officers from JAG Branch, but as per the general policy, officers of JAG Branch are not provided as defending officers.
In case the accused declines to provide any other officer's name, the Convening Officer may depute three officers as defending officers one after the other.
However, if the accused refuses to avail their services, they cannot claim the denial of an opportunity.
[Para 19]

As per Army Rules, 1954, Rules 22, 23, 24, and 25, trial by Court Martial is not affected by procedural defects unless they are vital and substantial.
[Para 17]

As per Army Rules, 1954, Rules 180, 184, and 177, the accused is not entitled to a copy of the report of the court of inquiry as it is not a part of pre-trial investigation.
The court of inquiry is in the nature of fact-finding enquiry committee, and Rule 177 does not mandate that a court of inquiry must invariably be set up in each and every case prior to recording of the summary of evidence or convening of a Court-Martial.
[Para 18]

As per Army Rules, 1954, Rules 22, 23, 24, 25, and 149, and Constitution of India, Articles 226 and 227, the proceedings conducted by Court Martial are subject to judicial review by High Court under Article 226 of Constitution.
However, the Court Martial is not subject to the superintendence of the High Court under Article 227 of the Constitution.
If the Court Martial has been properly convened and there is no challenge to its composition and the proceedings are in accordance with the prescribed procedure, the High Court must not intervene.
Proceedings of a Court Martial cannot be compared with proceedings in a Criminal Court under Cr.
PC [Para 22]

As per the Evidence Act, Section 3, and Army Rules, 1954, Rules 149, 22, 23, 24, and 25, the Court Martial discharges a judicial function and is a court where the provisions of the Evidence Act apply.
A Court Martial has the same responsibility as any court to protect the rights of the accused charged before it and follow the procedural safeguards.
The procedure prescribed for the Court Martial is equally fair, if not more, than the one provided in a criminal court to the accused.
[Para 22]
