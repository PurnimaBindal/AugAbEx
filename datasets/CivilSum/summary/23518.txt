The Karnataka State Civil Services (Regulation of Promotion, Pay and Pension) Act, 1973, Sections 4 and 11(2) have been challenged for their constitutional validity.
Deemed dates of promotions were granted to the petitioners by the Respondent-State pursuant to the direction issued by High Court, but consequential monetary benefits were not made available to them.
Petitioners filed a petition seeking a declaration that impugned provisions are null and void as they try to confiscate financial benefits made available to them by writs of mandamus issued by High Court and seek to deprive them of their fundamental rights.
Section 11(2) has been struck down as ultra vires the powers of the State Legislature, while Sub-Sections (2), (3) and (8) of Section 4 of the Act will have to be read down.
[Paras 11, 12, 17 to 20] B.
A binding judicial pronouncement between parties cannot be made ineffective with the aid of any legislative power by enacting a provision which in substance overrules such judgment.
A legislative enactment cannot displace the foundation of the judgment and uniformly applies to a class of persons concerned with the entire subject sought to be covered by such an enactment having retrospective effect.
[Para 12] C. The final and binding judgment between parties cannot be unsettled by a legislative enactment.
