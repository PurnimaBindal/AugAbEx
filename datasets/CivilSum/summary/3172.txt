Proceedings under Section 33-C(i) of Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 are a mode of speedy recovery of money due to workman from an employer under a settlement or an award, and the appropriate government has no power of adjudication, except to determine if objections to jurisdiction were raised to deprive the workman of money due.
[Para 37]

The appropriate government, in proceedings under Section 33-C(i), does not possess powers of a Labour Court or Industrial Tribunal, except to examine management's objections to a workman's money claim and form a prima facie opinion on their validity.
The appropriate government must also comply with natural justice and issue a speaking order with reasons to allow for judicial review.
[Para 37]

The appropriate government issued recovery certificates for a workman's money claim without considering the validity of management's objections, and thus, the decision was quashed due to a lack of application of mind.
[Para 38]
