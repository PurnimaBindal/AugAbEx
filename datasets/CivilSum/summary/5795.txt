Rajasthan Civil Services (Classification, Control and Appeal) Rules, 1958 - Enquiry - Evidence - The petitioner had access to all the material on the basis of which the enquiry was conducted, and expert evidence was allowed as appropriate and practical - Although the petitioner himself was a judicial officer, he was not permitted to engage a lawyer, and the enquiry was not vitiated by such a refusal.
[Para 5]
Constitution of India, Articles 226/227 and 311 - Rajasthan Civil Service (Classification, Control and Appeal) Rules, 1958, Rules 14, 16(8) and 16(10) - Reversion - Natural justice - The petitioner, a judicial officer, challenged his reversion from the position of CJM to that of Munsiff and Judicial Magistrate - After an enquiry for the interpolation and tampering of records, the Single Judge who acted as Inquiring Authority was also the Disciplinary Authority and recommended the petitioner's removal from service - However, the Full Court ultimately imposed a lesser punishment, which did not require any further hearing.
[Paras 19 and 20]
Practice and Procedure - Penalty - Full Court - When a Single Judge acts as the Disciplinary Authority under a Resolution passed by the Full Court, their decision is subject to review by three other Judges - The Full Court cannot direct the Single Judge to impose a specific punishment, but must either accept the Inquiring Authority's recommendation or impose a lesser punishment itself - If a penalty is imposed by the Full Court, there is no further opportunity for appeal.
