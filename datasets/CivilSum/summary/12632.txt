The notifications issued under Ss.
4 and 6 of the Land Acquisition Act, 1894 are challenged on their legality and validity in a Writ Petition filed under Article 226 of the Constitution of India.
The petitioner claimed that the house was constructed before the notification under S. 4 of the Act was issued, and a petition under S. 5-A of the Act was submitted, stating that he had spent his retirement savings on building a temple and Samadhi of his mother, as well as constructing the house.
During the survey, it was discovered that the land of the petitioner was completely vacant, except for some rooms that were constructed about 7/8 years ago, and 2/3 rooms that were constructed about 5/6 years ago.
The petitioner's hearing under section 5-A of the Act was not held with an eye of law, and there were some defects in the survey where it was found that the land of the Petitioner was vacant.
As such, the Court ordered the authorities to take a final decision after hearing the petitioner's objections under S. 5-A, and if the petitioner is entitled to relief by way of the release of his constructed portion on merits, it should be granted.
