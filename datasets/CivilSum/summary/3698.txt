Constitution of India, Article 311 - Border Security Rules, 1969, Rules 20,21,22 - Army Rules, 1954, Rule 13(3)(iii) - Dismissal from service - In order to be appointed, material facts of previous employment and removal must be disclosed, as the authority may or may not choose to appoint him upon discovery.
This is especially important for members of the disciplinary force, who are expected to be persons of high integrity and honesty.
A candidate's previous removal from service for misconduct cannot be ignored, and the candidate is not absolved of required disclosure simply because the discharge was by a simple, unstigmatized order.
Dismissal on the grounds of material fact concealment is justified.

Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 226 - Dismissal from service - Disciplinary proceedings - If the delinquent deliberately abstains from the disciplinary proceedings despite repeated clear notices for dismissal, he cannot challenge the final order on the ground of violation of principles of natural justice.
[Para 8] C Punjab Recruitment of Ex-Servicemen Rules, 1982, Rules 3 and 4 - Discharge from Army - All persons discharged from military service not covered by the excluded categories of grounds will be considered ex-servicemen.
However, a candidate who deliberately concealed their service and discharge because of their background of misconduct cannot claim any benefit of it later on.
[Para 9] D. Constitution of India, Article 226 - Concealment of facts - A person who conceals material facts from authorities in order to secure employment is not entitled to equitable discretionary relief.
