Under Section 17(1)(a) of the Employees Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952, JC Mills Limited was granted exemption, and this was found to be no less favourable than the statutory scheme.
The appellant had its own Rules and Regulations and was empowered to declare the rate of interest every year.
The government revised the terms and conditions, but it was to be made applicable to fresh cases of exemption and could not become automatically applicable.
The Regional P.F.
Commissioner rightly pointed out that unless the appropriate government issued a notification amending the exempted scheme and published it in the official Gazette, Condition No. 4 did not apply to them.
Because of this, the appellant and respondent No. 10 were not legally bound to credit the account of each of the respondent employees with a higher interest rate for the years between 1984-85 to 1988-89 because for those years, the central government had declared interest at higher rates.
