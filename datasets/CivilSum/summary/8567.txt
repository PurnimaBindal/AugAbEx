The Industrial Disputes Act, Section 33-C(2) outlines that those asked to work on a holiday are entitled to overtime wages, and a Labour Court has ruled that they are entitled to overtime when working on Saturdays which were otherwise holidays.
The Deputy Commissioner has subsequently declared Saturdays as working days and stopped paying wages for them, but the Labour Court's award still stands and has become final between the parties.
Therefore, a claim for overtime wages can be computed under Section 33-C(2), and any decision made by the Deputy Commissioner or other courts cannot nullify the effect of the award.
[Paras 6, 8 and 10]

Section 33-C(2) of the Industrial Disputes Act has no time limitation for filing an application, as opposed to the Payment of Wages Act, which has a specific limitation.
This was intentionally done by the legislature as the Industrial Disputes Act was passed after the Payment of Wages Act, and no provision of limitation was made for claims under Section 33-C(2).
The Limitation Act's Article 137 is not applicable to applications under Section 33-C(2) as labor courts are not considered within the act.
Therefore, the management holds the worker's wages as a trust that can be claimed at any time, and granting delayed claims will not violate any third-party interests.
[Paras 15 to 20]

The Industrial Disputes Act's Sections 10, 19(6), and 33-C(2) state that executive orders cannot annul an award that was already binding and final between parties.
Workers who were not given notice under Section 19(6) of the Industrial Disputes Act regarding an executive order that withdraws overtime payment for Saturdays are still entitled to these wages under the award.
Although Saturdays were declared as a working day by the Deputy Commissioner, the award still stands.
