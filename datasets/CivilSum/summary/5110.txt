The Administrative Tribunals Act of 1985 cites that service matters of someone performing duties connected to the Indian Government on a civil post fall under the Tribunal's domain.
Seeking remedy under Section 19 of the Act is the usual route such an employee would have to take, rendering a writ by them incompetent under Article 226 of the Constitution.
[Paras 13 and 14]
Service matters of a Central Government civil employee under Sections 3(q), 14, and 19 of the Administratives Tribunals Act of 1985 mean relegating the petitioner to an alternative remedy is anomalous.
It would be improper to do so under Section 19 of the Act since the objection with respect to alternative remedy was considerably diluted after the petition was admitted.
[Paras 15 to 16]
Under the Central Civil Services (Pension) Rules of 1972 and related Government orders for resignations by a Government servant to take up another appointment with proper permission, it would be unfair to apply the principle of forfeiture to forfeit service and deny pro-rata pension if the resignation was submitted with the correct permission.
[Paras 18 and 19]
The principle of the Rule of Law and separation of powers means that once a matter has already been considered by the Division Bench and the order passed, the administration authority cannot discuss the same issues in further detail.
