Under Section 2, Item 3 of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, disciplinary proceedings require findings of the enquiry officer to be either accepted or rejected.
In a case where the enquiry officer left the service of the respondent and could not be produced, a possible and plausible explanation for non-production must be considered.
In this case, there is no reason to disbelieve the enquiry report or reject findings based on material, and strict and sophisticated rules of evidence are not applicable in domestic enquiry.
[Para 13]

In a case related to a conductor charged with not collecting fare, misbehaviour, and abusing checking staff, an application for approval of dismissal was rejected by the Tribunal based on the ground of not producing a lady passenger before the enquiry officer.
However, summoning of the witness is not a condition precedent if there is sufficient evidence in proof of findings by domestic Tribunal, and the record of the enquiry clearly establishes that principles of natural justice were not violated.
Therefore, the employer's application under Section 33(2)(b) is allowed and the award is set aside.
