Article 311 of the Constitution of India, Rule 14 of the UP Police Officers of Subordinate Banks (Punishment and Appeal) Rules, 1991 - Constables of PAC Battalion who fired on civilians while on return to headquarters, were charged with indiscipline in the subsequent departmental enquiry.
The enquiry found them guilty and they were dismissed from service.
This decision was challenged on the grounds of a defective enquiry and that the punishing authority acted as the enquiry officer.
These claims were found to be unfounded, and the punishment was upheld.
[Paras 5, 18, 20 and 21] B.
Rule 14 of the UP Police Officers of Subordinate Banks (Punishment and Appeal) Rules, 1991 - A constable of PAC Battalion who was acquitted of a criminal charge of rowdyism in a separate case, challenged the departmental proceedings initiated against him.
The claim that the departmental proceedings should have been stayed during the criminal proceedings was found to be unsubstantiated, as the charges in the two proceedings were different and not interlinked.
The acquittal in the criminal case was not grounds to influence departmental findings on indiscipline.
[Paras 10 and 14] C. In accordance with the decision made in AIR 1999 SC 1416, departmental proceedings and criminal proceedings can proceed simultaneously, though separately, as long as they are not based on identical facts/allegations.
Additionally, if the disposal of a criminal case is being unduly delayed, departmental proceedings that were previously stayed can be resumed so as to conclude on an earlier date.
The purpose of departmental proceedings, as per 2001 All LJ, is not to punish the guilty, but to keep the administrative machinery un-sullied by getting rid of the bad elements.
