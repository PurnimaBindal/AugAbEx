In accordance with Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 25-F, after parties have presented evidence, a proper finding should be made regarding whether the workman had completed 240 days of work or not.
In this case, the management failed to produce records before the labor court.
Copies of muster rolls were provided by the management along with a writ petition, which showed that the workman had not completed 240 days of work in any calendar year.
The finding in favor of the workman, based only on the adverse inference drawn against the management was set aside, and the case was remanded to the labor court, with a direction for the management to produce the record which was summoned by the workman during the proceedings.

Under Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Section 25-H, the allegations made by the workman were that a person junior to him was retained in service, and some other person was employed later.
However, no specific details were mentioned about the persons who were retained or employed.
The finding of the labor court, that the provisions of Section 25-H had been violated, without any material on record, was set aside, and the case was remanded.
