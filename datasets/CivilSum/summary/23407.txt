Constitution of India, Article 14 - General Insurance (Emergency Provision) Act, 1972, Sections 16 - New India Assurance Co Ltd Merger Scheme, 1973, Rule 5 - Interpretation of statutes - When enacting laws, the legislature is expected to adhere to the provisions of the Constitution - Thus, the rules are always assumed to be constitutional - The courts should not interpret the statutes unless the language of the statute is unconstitutional - Although Rule 5 may appear arbitrary in contravention of Article 14 of the Constitution, it is still valid because it empowers the Board of Management to form objective judgments based on evidence that the post is no longer required and to eliminate the post resulting in consequential termination of services of the permanent or temporary employees holding the position - The rule does not violate Article 14 of the Constitution.

Merger Scheme, 1973, Rules 5 and 8 - Natural justice/Misconduct - The revocation of the suspension of employees and the termination of their employment by invoking Rule 5, without eliminating the post, is not in the best interests of the company or the public, as stipulated in the rule – Thus, the termination is deemed illegal.
