Army Act, 1950, Sections 164(1) - Army Rules, 1954, Chapters V and VI - Command influence - Command influence exists in the armed forces to a greater extent than in civil services - It may be applicable in disciplinary proceedings, but only in administrative matters, not disciplinary powers under the Act and Rules.
[Paras 15 and 20] B.
Army Act, 1950 - Army Rule, 1954 - Court of Inquiry - A court of inquiry is not mandatory for taking disciplinary action - It is optional and formed when the facts are unclear or evidence needs to be collected - Statements made are not binding and the controlling officer can dismiss the charge.
[Para 17] C. Army Act and Rules - Disciplinary action is processed from lower to higher level, with authorities at every level having the power to release the accused to ensure a fair and independent application of mind.
[Para 20] D. Army Act and Army Rules - Bias - The opinion of a higher authority before formal proceedings can lead to bias and taint the proceedings, even without specific proof, and despite pleadings.
[Paras 21 and 22] E. Army Act, 1950, Sections 130 - Army Rules, 1954, Rules 40(1) and 44 - Option - Failure to respond to option given at the initial stage precludes a challenge to the GCM's constitution.
[Para 25] F. Evidence Act, 1972, Sections 154 - Section 154 of the Evidence Act gives courts discretion to permit questions that can be asked in cross-examination - Courts should not act as appellate judges in matters of discretion.
[Para 26] G. Army Rules Act, 1954, Rules 23(3), 25 and 56 - Appropriate opportunity - During the trial, the accused was afforded the desired opportunity - Errors or irregularities during investigation would not vitiate the trial.
