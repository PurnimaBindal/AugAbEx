The petitioner, a society, was paying its staff wages and allowances similar to those in hospitals and establishments run by the government.
Without giving any notice required under Section 9-A of the Industrial Disputes Act, the society started paying minimum wages and allowances payable under the Minimum Wages Act, resulting in a demand from employees.
The Tribunal made an award based on Section 9-A and allowed the demands, and no interference was necessary.
The society is required to pay its employees a rate comparable to those in government-run institutions.
[Paras 3 and 4] B.
Although strict rules of pleadings like those in a Civil Court do not apply to disputes before Labour Courts and Industrial Courts, Industrial Tribunals must consider the effect of non-compliance with Section 9-A, even if not specifically mentioned.
