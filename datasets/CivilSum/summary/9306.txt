Punjab State Electricity Employees Punishment & Appeal Regulations, 1971, Regulation 14 - Natural Justice/Dismissal from service - Regulation 14 allows for the competent authority to issue a dismissal order if an employee has been convicted based on his misconduct.
No personal hearing is necessary under the regulation or even under the rules of natural justice.
Notice or an enquiry is not required under the regulation.
[Para 7]  B Res judicata - The principle of res judicata applies when a writ petition is filed against dismissal, but is dismissed due to the Supreme Court upholding a conviction.
Even if it is unclear whether the dismissal was good or bad, the petitioner chose not to invoke the court's decision on the merits of the case, being satisfied that the dismissal was appropriate due to his conviction.
[Para 8]  C Industrial Disputes Act, Section 10 - Reference - If a workman's claim is stale, the State Government must evaluate whether there is a reasonable explanation for the delay.
When a claim is submitted more than 10 years after the dismissal, no reference should be made.
[Para 9]  D. Dismissal with retrospective effect/Suspension allowance - Dismissal orders cannot be made retroactively.
During the time period between conviction and punishment, the petitioner can be treated as suspended and is entitled to subsistence allowance according to the rules.
[Para 10]  E Industrial Disputes Act, 1974, Section 17-B - When a writ petition is accepted, the operation of the award is suspended, and the award is ultimately dismissed, the workman cannot claim benefits under section 17-B [Para 11]
