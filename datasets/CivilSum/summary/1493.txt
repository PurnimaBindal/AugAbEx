Constitution of India, Article 226 - Promotion - Deprivation - Government has given up using the seniority-cum-fitness policy, allocating 20% marks for viva and 80% for an average of the last five years' ARs - Petitioner was an excellent officer, earning 395 AR marks, but was given just 37 marks by only six of the 20 board members in a weightage of 20% - Government has decreased the board marks from 20% to 5% - Less worthy candidates were promoted because of high board marks - No record of the marks assigned by board members was maintained - The selection process raises suspicion, as the exercise of discretion for granting 20% board marks was unreasonable, unjust, and lacked rationality, implying sycophancy - The sweeping power and freedom of the board to use discretion are in violation of Article 14 of the constitution - A directive was given to establish a Special Promotion Board to reassess merit - Ruling in the Air Vice Marshal Harish Masand case was applied.
[Para 2] B Constitution of India, Article 226 - Extension of service - Approval/Non-approval - Extending the service is not a right - The judicial review should protect citizens' fundamental and legal rights and prevent the incidence of injustice - Non-granting extension based on irrationality, arbitrariness, and disregard for the principles of natural justice is not consistent with the established principle of judicial review.
