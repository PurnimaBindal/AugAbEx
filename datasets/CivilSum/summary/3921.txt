The Indian Medical Council Act and Post Graduate Medical Education Regulations mandate that Post Graduate Medical Course selection be strictly based on academic merit and that entrance tests cannot be cleared with less than 50% marks.
State governments cannot reduce these standards, even if the seats remain vacant.
[Paras 8-12]
Reservation in Admission to Post Graduate Medical Courses is not allowed as it dilutes merit and lowers standards, which only the Medical Counsel can institute, which intentionally excluded any form of reservation.
[Paras 13-17, 19]
The Indian Medical Council must be approved by the Central Government to have legal authority.
[Para 19]
The Medical Council did not prescribe reservation in Admission to Post Graduate Medical Courses, only doing so where considered appropriate.
[Para 19]
Medical Council is authorized to prescribe any standards and reservation in Admission to Post Graduate Medical Courses.
[Paras 19, 22]
Institutional preference or any form of college-based reservation via additional percentage of marks is impermissible and offends the Constitution.
[Para 23]
No state-based reservation is allowed as standards cannot be lowered at such a high level of education, as Article 335 does not deal with admissions to educational institutions.
[Paras 25, 26, 28]
Fresh counselling is to be conducted in accordance with Regulation 9(2) without any form of reservation, preference, or concession, as admissions made contrary to public interests have been quashed.
[Paras 29, 30]
