The Constitution of India allows for the Governor and President to promulgate ordinances during recess of Parliament, but the effect and acts done, along with rights conferred, must be of a permanent nature in order for the Ordinance to have a permanent effect, which can withstand the expiry of its statutory period.
If the rights conferred are not permanent, they can be taken away by an act of legislature.
There is disagreement among members of the Bench as to what constitutes enduring, permanent rights that cannot be taken away.
In the case of Ordinance 32/89 of the State of Bihar, Justice Wadhwa believes that the act of taking over non-government schools and vested assets confers upon staff an enduring right that cannot be revoked, while Justice Sujata V Manohar believes that such rights are not necessarily permanent and that an Ordinance can be reversed at a later stage.
In any case, Ordinances issued in violation of the Constitution and not placed before the Legislature are considered a fraud on the Constitution and cannot confer any enduring and irreversible rights, and are not a substitute for regular legislation.
