The Labour Court awarded that despite the non-registration of the contract, the Apprentice cannot be regarded as a worker under the 1947 Act because registration of the apprenticeship contract is not a necessary ingredient for the expression "Apprentice" occurring in section 2(aa) of the Act.
[Para 5]
An Apprentice is not an employee but rather a trainee as they cannot claim to be in the relationship of master and servant, even if they remain engaged after the expiry of their term.
[Para 4]
Once the order was passed, complied with and party derived benefit out of it, the party cannot challenge it on any ground thus, disentitling it for any relief.
In this case, the respondent raised a dispute after three years of engagement as an apprentice when their services were dispensed with, making the dispute unsustainable.
