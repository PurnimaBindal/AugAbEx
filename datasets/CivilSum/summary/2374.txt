As per the Working Journalists and ONP Employees (Conditions of Service) and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1955, Sections 2(c), 2(f) and 17, Respondent No. 1, who sends legal news on a daily basis, falls under the definition of a newspaper employee.
Additionally, as per the Industrial Disputes Rules, Rules 10 and 13, the respondent spending more time on journalism than on the practice of law establishes their claim of being a journalist.
The Labour Court was correct in their decision.

The term "wages" as defined in the Working Journalists and ONP Employees (Conditions of Service) and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1955, Section 2(eee) includes all remuneration capable of being expressed in terms of money.
This even includes the value of house accommodation and other fringe benefits.
Hence, the term "retainer" is considered wages under Section 2(eee).

According to the Constitution of India, Articles 226 and 227, supervisory jurisdiction can only be used to correct an error that is apparent on the face of the record.
It cannot be used for recording different findings even if they are possible on the evidence adduced before the Labour Court.
