According to Article 226 of the Constitution of India and Section 2(a)(i) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, an appropriate government needs to be identified for resolving disputes in employer companies that are engaged in the production or manufacture of various products, including motor and aviation spirits, diesel oil and kerosene.
In one particular labor court case, a petitioner had his dispute referred through the joint secretary of his state government, but the labor court judge rejected his claim on the grounds that the dispute had not been referred to the appropriate government.
The petitioner argued that since the employer company was considered a controlled industry, the appropriate government according to Section 2(a)(i) would be the Central Government, not the State Government.
However, the court held that the labor court judge had not erred, as in the case of the respondent company, the appropriate government would, in fact, be the Central Government, not the State Government.
As a result, the petitioner's case was dismissed.
