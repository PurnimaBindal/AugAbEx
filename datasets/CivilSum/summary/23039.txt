The petitioner in a Public Interest Litigation (PIL) challenging appointments to the High Court Judgeship must disclose public interest, as well as their locus standi and the source and authenticity of the allegations that the cause of action is founded upon.
To maintain such a cause, there must be a real and genuine public interest involved.
Failure to meet these necessary requirements implies that the petitioner is a busybody focused on self-publicity rather than any sense of responsibility, and who is heedless of the adverse impact on public interest by such frivolous petitions.
The jurisdiction of the court to entertain PIL cannot be allowed to be invoked by a person or body to further their personal causes or satisfy their personal grudges and enmities, either directly or through a proxy.
If such a petition is submitted, the court may lift the veil and dismiss it with exemplary costs to send the message that such petitions are not entertained and frivolous litigation is curtailed, thereby saving the precious time of the court.
The meaning of "public interest" is explained.
