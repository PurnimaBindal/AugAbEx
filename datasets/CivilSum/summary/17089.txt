The petitioner in this case was terminated from service due to absence from duty, but they argued that they were hospitalized after an accident on their way to work.
The medical certificates provided by government hospital doctors confirm their injuries.
The respondents failed to provide proof of sending a registered letter for the petitioner to return to work, and the notice published in two newspapers was not served to the petitioner due to their hospitalization.
As a result, the order of termination violates principles of natural justice and is invalid.
[Para 5]

The petitioner in this case was appointed as a permanent Class IV employee under the Dying-in-harness rules, which means their appointment can only be terminated after providing an opportunity for a hearing.
The impugned order treating the petitioner as a temporary employee and terminating their service without a hearing cannot be upheld.
[Para 4]
