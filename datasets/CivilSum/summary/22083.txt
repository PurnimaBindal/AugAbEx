The appellant applied for the Assam Industrial Development Corporation (AIDC) Voluntary Retirement Scheme in 1995 and was recommended for acceptance by the recommending authority.
When there was no response from the respondent-Corporation, the appellant wrote letters to the Managing Director and the Chairman of the respondent-Corporation to urge them to accept her voluntary retirement.
Instead of accepting the request, the Managing Director issued a show cause notice for disciplinary action, alleging that the appellant was participating in political activities.
The appellant filed a writ petition against the show cause notice, seeking relief and retirement benefits.
The learned Single Judge granted relief, but the Division Bench of the High Court set aside the order.
The discretion under Clause 81 of the Scheme is not unfettered or absolute and must be exercised judiciously.
The Corporation being a State under Article 12 must act fairly not arbitrarily.
The show cause notice was only on extraneous considerations.
The subsequent indulgence of the appellant in politics was not germane to consideration of application of appellant for retirement nor a relevant factor in Clause 81 of the Scheme.
The MD was obliged to accept the request for retirement once there was no impediment.
The petition against the show cause notice cannot be termed pre-mature because instead of giving benefits due to an employee, issuing a show cause for disciplinary proceedings is not reasonable.
