The Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923, Section 3(1)(b) - Compensation - A workman was injured while blasting stones, lost three fingers and suffered a 50% disability; a criminal case was also filed against the applicant.
It was found that the appellants (contractor and principal employer) were negligent, despite the appellant's acquittal in criminal case, the workman could not be denied "just and reasonable compensation" on this ground.
The Commissioner awarded compensation against both appellants.

The Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923, Section 3(1)(b) - Compensation - In order to determine whether an accident has arisen out of and in the course of a person's employment, three tests need to be satisfied: (i) the workman must have been employed on the duties of his employment at the time of the accident; (ii) the accident must have occurred at the place where he was performing those duties; and (iii) the immediate act which led to the accident must not be so removed from the sphere of his duties as to be regarded as something foreign to them.
