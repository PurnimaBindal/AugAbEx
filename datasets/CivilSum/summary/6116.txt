Constitution of India, Article 226 - Education - Shortage of attendance - Condonation of - Petitioner was a bright student who couldn't attend classes due to the death of his father and financial issues - Academic Council did not make a decision regarding petitioner's application for condonation of attendance shortage - Shortage of attendance occurred due to circumstances beyond control of the petitioner - Therefore, the shortage of attendance should be condoned - Respondent instructed to allow petitioner to take the examination.

Constitution of India, Article 226 - Education - Condonation of shortage in attendance - Writ petition - If the rules allow for the condonation of attendance shortage by the Vice Chancellor/Academic College for cogent reason, then it is the authority's responsibility to exercise the power reasonably - The Academic Council failed to make a decision on the petitioner's representation for condonation of attendance shortage - The authorities neglected to fulfill their duty - The court has two options: to order the Academic Council to decide or to assess whether the petitioner provided cogent reasons - The latter option is limited to cases where the purpose would be thwarted if the court fails to take action - The examination is about to begin - The High Court, in the exercise of its power under Article 226, can determine if cogent reasons for condonation of attendance shortage existed or not.
