Article 226 of the Indian Constitution – Judicial review of orders of reference – Orders of reference passed by the government under Section 10(1) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 are subject to judicial review on limited grounds.
Relevant considerations showing application of mind to reach a conclusion as to existence or apprehension of an industrial dispute and a prima face case for reference are required to be taken into account by the appropriate government.
The adequacy or sufficiency of material for forming such an opinion is outside the purview of judicial review.
The court can only see the existence of some relevant material and the application of mind by the competent authority as well as the scope of applicable statutory provisions.

Section 10 of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 - Section 11 of the Civil Procedure Code – Principles of res judicata – When parties and issues for determination differ from an earlier dispute, the principles of res judicata do not apply.
[Para 21] C. Sections 10(1) and 25-FF of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 – Liability of employer to pay compensation on transfer of undertaking – Payment of compensation does not affect validity of reference order, especially when received by the workmen under protest.
Such grounds may only be used as defence by the management before the Tribunal.
