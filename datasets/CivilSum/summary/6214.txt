Constitution of India, Articles 162 and 311 - Executive instructions state that no reporting officer should record remarks in a confidential report without having observed the officer's work and conduct for a minimum of three months.
Despite this, the SSP recorded adverse remarks in the petitioner's report.
The petitioner raised a representation that was rejected, and an order of retirement was subsequently passed.
However, since the SSP had not worked with the petitioner at the time, he had no occasion to assign any remarks.
Therefore, the court struck down the impugned order and ordered the reinstatement of the petitioner, with continuity of service and benefits.
[Paras 9 and 11]

Constitution of India, Articles 226/227, 311 - When appellant authorities dismiss representation, they need not record detailed reasons.
However, when the petitioner questions the jurisdiction of an officer to record adverse remarks and cites rules and instructions in his representation, the authority must respond to the point.
A simple order rejecting representation without a speaking order cannot be sustained.
[Para 8]

Constitution of India, Articles 226/227, 311 - The petitioner alleged that the SSP recorded adverse remarks in violation of instructions, as he had no occasion to see the petitioner's work.
The order passed by the SSP, in this case, was done out of malice to protect himself, as there was no evidence that the petitioner was unfit for service.
The order was struck down and the petitioner ordered to be compensated with posts valued at Rs. 5000/-[Paras 10 and 11]
