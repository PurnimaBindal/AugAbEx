The Constitution of India's Articles 14 and 226 provide for transfers that are necessary for good administration.
If an employee is consistently posted in one position, vested interests may emerge.
It is vital to transfer doctors in medical colleges, even though they are not exempt from being transferred, as transfers are a direct result of service and an essential part of ensuring good governance.
But transfers made in bad faith, for reasons that do not benefit the public or good administration, or that go beyond the transferable cadre, can be judicially reviewed.
Mala fide transfers are also governed by the Constitution of India's Articles 14 and 226.
If a transfer lacks a connection with administrative necessity, it is considered mala fide.
Mala fides are not limited to personal animosity but also apply to wilful malice and the law.
If a transfer is made without any rational basis, it can be held mala fide and quashed.
Administrative exigencies in the context of transfers imply that good governance must be at the forefront of all decision-making processes.
However, appointing an unqualified person to a post intended for a qualified individual is not an example of good governance.
