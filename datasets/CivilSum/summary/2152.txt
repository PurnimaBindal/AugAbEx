Indian Administrative Service (Regulation of Seniority) Rules, 1987, Rule 3(3)(ii) as amended in 2005 - Indian Administrative Service (Appointment by Promotion) Regulations, 1955, Regulations 3 and 5 as amended in 1997 - Seniority - Year of allotment - The yearly meeting of the Selection Committee is now mandatory.
If it cannot meet every year, the consequences of non-convening the meeting of Selection Committee cannot disadvantage promotee officers.
If a meeting is not convened due to a court stay, the maxim that "an act of the Court shall prejudice no man" will apply.
The allotment year will relate back to the year for which the selection is made in the later years.

Indian Administrative Service (Regulation of Seniority) Rules, 1987, Rule 3(3)(ii) as amended in 2005 - Indian Administrative Service (Appointment by Promotion) Regulations, 1955, Regulations 3 and 5 as amended in 1997 - Seniority - Year of allotment - The 2005 amendment to Rule 3 does not adversely affect the seniority or any vested right of direct recruits.
It only clarified the existing rule to determine the year of allotment with reference to the year for which the meeting of the Selection Committee to make selection was held, and not the current year of the meeting.
The amendment did not introduce new rules and even if it was retrospective, it did not affect any right of direct appointees.

Indian Administrative Service (Regulation of Seniority) Rules, 1987, Rule 3(3)(ii) as amended in 2005 - Indian Administrative Service (Appointment by Promotion) Regulations, 1955, Regulations 3 and 5 as amended in 1997 - Interpretation of Statute - Seniority - Year of allotment - The 2005 amendment only clarified the existing rule to determine the year of allotment with reference to the year for which the meeting of the Selection Committee to make selection was held, and not the current year of the meeting.
The amendment was made to avoid the anomalous situation in the existing rule and is therefore retroactive in nature and by necessary implication.
Such an interpretation is preferred over one that could cause hardship, anomalous results, or absurdities.
