The membership of a union may be cancelled if a member qualifies as a defaulter.
This cancellation is deemed if the member is in arrears of subscription for more than three calendar months.
The phrase "period of more than three calendar months" includes at least four months within a block of a period of six calendar months immediately preceding the month in question.
A member is not a defaulter for more than three months if they are paying the subscription far exceeding one month, even if they have not made up the deficiency of one month during a number of subsequent months.
[Paras 10 to 12]  B.
The proviso to Section 3(25) of the Bombay Industrial Relations Act contains a deeming fiction to dismember a person.
This provision must be strictly construed, not liberally.
Any provision of the Act catering to the needs of illiterate or disabled workmen has to be interpreted to subserve the legislative intent underlying the principle of industrial democracy and collective bargaining guaranteed by the Act.
An interpretation that guarantees continuance of membership of such workmen has to be preferred over one that frustrates the benevolent enactment.
