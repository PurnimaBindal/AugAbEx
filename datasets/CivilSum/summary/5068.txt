Section 11-A of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, outlines the procedure for conducting domestic enquiries.
However, if the Enquiry Officer also acts as the Presenting Officer, the enquiry proceedings are considered biased.
Therefore, the report submitted by such an officer will have no value and any punishment order resulting from the enquiry is also considered void.
[Para 17]

When an objection is raised by an employee that an enquiry was not fair at the preliminary stage of disciplinary proceedings, it cannot be ignored.
In such cases, the labour court cannot proceed assuming that the domestic enquiry was valid and only focus on the employee's conduct.
The award cannot stand in such a situation, and the petitioner's rights would have been denied as a result.
[Para 18]

Pension regulations can be used to address cases where an employee's punishment is set aside.
In this case, there has been thirty years of litigation, and as the nature of the job has changed considerably in that time, it is doubtful that the employee on reinstatement will be able to contribute.
As such, the petitioner should be permitted to retire under Regulation 29, with all associated benefits and half of the back wages awarded.
