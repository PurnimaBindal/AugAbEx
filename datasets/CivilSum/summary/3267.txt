The Industrial Disputes Act of 1947, Sections 10 and 2(s), permit State Governments to refuse to refer disputes for adjudication where employees seeking reference are not defined as workmen according to Section 2(s) of the Act.
[Para 5]
In accordance with the Industrial Disputes Act of 1947, Sections 2(s) and 10, the position of Production Incharge, a supervisory post, which the petitioner holds, does not fall under the definition of a workman.
Thus, the demands raised do not merit reference and the dispute has been declined for adjudication.
[Para 9]
If an employer claims that an employee had resigned from his job, but there is a dispute about the fact of resignation, the government can check the original resignation letter to see if it bears the workman's signatures, and if so, the government can decline reference.
This only means that the government has formed an opinion that resignation was tendered prima facie, but it cannot be said that the government has decided a lis between parties.
[Para 9]
In relation to jurisdiction, Article 226 of India's Constitution stipulates that the Court cannot instruct the State Government to refer a matter.
Instead, the Court can only order a reconsideration of the matter, taking into account the relevant factors, in accordance with the Industrial Disputes Act of 1947, Section 10.
