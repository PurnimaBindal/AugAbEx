Appointment of a qualified teacher on ad-hoc basis against a permanent existing vacancy on a consolidated salary much lower than the actual salary of the post amounts to unfair labor practices.
The petitioner is fully eligible to be appointed on a regular basis.
When similar situations arise, the same corrective actions should be applied in court.
The respondent school is directed to take the petitioner on duty, pay her arrears of full salary of the post from the date of her initial appointment, excluding already paid, and treat her in regular service from that date.
[Paras 1, 10 to 12, 15 to 17]

Even with an interim order, the service of the petitioner terminated only on the ground that the court did not order stay to continue.
It cannot be accepted that her rights have come to an end, and she is no longer qualified to enforce her right to continue in service and regularisation.
The court is not powerless and can order composite corrective action.
[Paras 10 & 11]

Regularisation is a corrective measure of all illegalities that the court does not approve of.
The court can exercise overriding powers when the employee is fully qualified and is not at fault.
When appointing contrary to the rules or with ad-hoc appointment without the approval of the competent authority, regularisation is necessary.
[Para 13]

In appropriate cases, the court must exercise its writ jurisdiction in order to enforce the rights of those which have accrued in favor of the employee as vested rights and which cannot be technically relegated to the realm of mere contractual obligations.
The court can also declare the conditions of appointment as unconscionable and void and order regularisation.
[Para 15]

A petition cannot be held belated when the petitioner is appointed in 1988 as ad hoc, and the petition filed in 1994 for regularisation as a last resort because of a fear of termination always prevailing.
[Para 15]
