The Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16 and 226, along with the Apprentices Act of 1961, Section 22, state that the appointment to the post of helper must not be made without issuing an advertisement or without calling the names from employment exchange.
The petitioner raised a grievance that for the post of helper, the names were not called from the employment exchange nor were the vacancies advertised, which is a violation of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
Thus, the respondents are restrained from having any type of reservation for the wards of its employees, and any agreement or standing order is to be ignored as illegal.
There is no preferential right granted to any person except those covered by the reservation policy, and no application must be made without calling names from the employment exchange in addition to public advertisement.
