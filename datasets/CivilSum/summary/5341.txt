Section 41 of the Specific Relief Act, 1961 is in tandem with Articles 14, 16 and 226 of the Indian Constitution as well as Section 9 of the Civil Procedure Code.
The civil court bears the power to verify whether a departmental inquiry is performed as per the required norms and principles of equity and fair play.
If yes, the court will maintain the punishment handed down by the executive authority.
The court cannot review and assess the evidence brought forth.
[Paras 8, 9 and 14]

Under Punjab Police Rules, 1934 Rule 1624, a police constable's unlawful actions such as giving a patta blow to an individual at a police station without solid grounds and showing disrespect to a senior officer under the influence of liquor is classified as grave misbehavior and indiscipline.
[Para 15]

The gravest form of misconduct under Punjab Police Rules, 1934 Rule 1624 is taking liquor while on duty for a police constable.
[Para 15]

The Punjab Police Rules, 1934 Rule 1624 demands that an inquiry be conducted and natural justice be observed.
However, disciplinary authority can dispense with the inquiry if they determine that a witness is unlikely to depose against the officer's apprehending causing injury.
Dispensing with the inquiry is a measure taken in the public interest and is therefore proper and reasonable.
[Para 12]
