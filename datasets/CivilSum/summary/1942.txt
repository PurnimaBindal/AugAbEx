The Industrial Disputes Act of 1947, Section 2(s) - Workman - A question arises as to whether the respondent is a workman under Section 2(s) of the ID Act.
- The concept of workman is central to an industrial dispute - The court must determine whether the order or award passed by the tribunal is without any valid basis - If the question is one of law and writ jurisdiction is invoked, the court cannot refuse writ jurisdiction on the ground that the question of workman is a question of fact and cannot be entertained by a Writ court.

The Industrial Disputes Act of 1947, Section 2(a) - Appropriate Government - 'De facto Doctrine' - Principle of - In a decision by the Apex Court, it was held that the appropriate government would be the Central Government with regard to IISCO, the petitioner Corporation - The judgment is reported in 1996 - The respondent was dismissed in 1982 - It was held that any subsequent change in the law will not render any act done by a person or authority invalid at the relevant time who was then competent to make the issue of such an order - The principle of 'de facto doctrine' applies.
