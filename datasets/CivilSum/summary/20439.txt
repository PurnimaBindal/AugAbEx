According to the Constitution of India, specifically Articles 14 and 16, along with the Karnataka Village Officer's Abolition Act of 1961, the claim to posts of Village Officers on the basis of hereditary cannot be considered a right.
In cases where custom has been recognized by law as allowing for hereditary claim, such claim must give way to fundamental rights which are ultra vires.
Since the 1961 Abolition Act came into effect and was upheld by the Court, appointments cannot be made on a hereditary basis and no policy for the regularization of those continuing on contract basis can be established.
However, given that the job was part-time and seasonal, individuals holding such posts were entitled to carry out other jobs including cultivation work.
