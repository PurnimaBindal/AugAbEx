Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 226 - Enquiry - Dismissal - Show cause notice - The petitioner, a chowkidar, is calling for an impartial enquiry after being dismissed on the mistaken assumption that he was involved in the theft of a jeep from an officer's kothi.
The petitioner argues that the officer who is a party in the matter and an enquiry conducted by his subordinate cannot serve justice.
Despite the petitioner's appeal against his dismissal, the enquiry concluded without the involvement of any officer other than the one accused of the theft.
The enquiry officer submitted his report quickly and with a clear bias, rendering the enquiry invalid.
As settled principle of administration of justice, no man can be the judge of his own cause.
The petition is allowed.

Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 226 - Enquiry - Dismissal - The petitioner has claimed that his dismissal was made by an officer of the rank of Divisional forest officer who is not the appointing authority, whereas he was appointed by the Divisional forest officer Bhatinda.
However, since the petitioner was not dismissed by an officer below the rank of Divisional forest officer, his contention cannot be sustained.
