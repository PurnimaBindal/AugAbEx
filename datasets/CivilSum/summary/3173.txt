The Labour Court does not have the power of review under Constitution of India, Articles 226/227 or the Industrial Disputes Act, unlike the power of review under Section 47 of CPC.
Therefore, the Labour Court cannot pass a fresh award to review its earlier award.
The only remedy available to a workman is to challenge the award.
Labour Court is not justified in giving itself powers that were not granted by legislation.
The impugned award is quashed.
[Paras 5 and 15 to 18] B.
The application for setting aside an award is different and separate from the review of the case merits.
Procedural difficulties can be remedied by the Labour Court, but a review on merits is distinct and such difficulties cannot be remedied by the Labour Court under the guise of review powers.
[Paras 15 and 16] C. Review is not an inherent power and must be conferred by law either specifically or by necessary implication.
The Industrial Disputes Act is a special legislation in which there is no specific power of review.
The Labour Court has no right to give itself powers that were never granted by the legislation.
