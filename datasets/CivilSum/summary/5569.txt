The Co-operative Societies Act's provisions excluding jurisdiction of Industrial Tribunal and Labour Court are unconstitutional and contrary to provisions of Article 14 of the Constitution Jurisdiction of Labour Court is not excluded.
[Paras 6 and 8(a)]
A question of jurisdiction based on facts cannot be raised for the first time in a writ petition when no plea of jurisdiction was raised before the Labour Court and no Rules or material were brought on record.
[Para 8]
A workman, appointed as a Carpenter, had his services terminated without complying with the provisions of Sec. 25(F) of the ID Act.
Despite the work of Carpenter being important, the employer appointed a new person instead of complying with the law.
The findings of the Labour Court holding the termination illegal were upheld.
[Para 12]
Although the termination order was passed in the Head Office at Hansi and served upon the workman at Meerut, the territorial jurisdiction of Court at Hissar was not excluded.
The workman could have invoked the jurisdiction of the Court at Meerut as well as at Hissar.
[Para 14]
The change of status of a workman from casual to 'Badli' without any opportunity given to him adversely affected his conditions of service.
The change was made in violation of principles of natural justice, even if it was made at the instructions of the Labour Department.
The change in status was held to be illegal and unoperative.
