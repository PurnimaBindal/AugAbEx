Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 - The contention that the person issuing the charge sheet was named as a witness and the officer passing the removal order was much lower than the appointing authority, combined with the hasty manner in which proceedings were conducted, implies that there was bias.
It is against the principle of law that a delegatee cannot sub-delegate, in this case, the officer who issued the order of removal was redelegated power, thereby rendering it bad, illegal, and liable to be quashed.

Constitution of India, Article 226 - A workman who has been ordered to be removed and whose order of removal is ex facie illegal can have recourse to writ jurisdiction.
Writ court can interfere if it is found that the proceeding is vitiated by procedural irregularity or violation of principles of natural justice, or if the proceeding is ex facie bad and illegal.
There is no provision in the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 that lays out the ouster of jurisdiction of the writ court under Article 226; therefore, a Writ petition is maintainable.
