Article 226 of the Constitution of India specifies that in case of Court Martial procedures, the delinquent may not object to the Court on any grounds not raised at the time of the proceedings.
This prevents seeking a judicial review against the final orders and findings of Court Martial, unless the violation of mandatory provisions of the Act and Rules leads to great prejudice or miscarriage of justice.

When the order of sentence is passed, it must be promulgated as per Rule 71, and cannot be considered effective until such confirmation is complete.
Therefore, the finding and sentence cannot be held to be confirmed until promulgated as per Rule 71, and the cause of action arose to the petitioner when it was communicated to him after being confirmed and promulgated.
As such, the writ petition at Chandigarh was held to be maintainable.
