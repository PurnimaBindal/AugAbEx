Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 311 - Termination - Natural justice - An employee may be terminated without an enquiry if given an opportunity to explain and all documents and correspondence are considered and no defence is placed before the authority.
The termination order may not be challenged on the grounds of a violation of principles of natural justice or due process when no submission of any defence on merit exists.
When an individual admits to a violation of an undertaking, no enquiry is necessary.
The order of termination must be upheld.
[Paras 7 and 8]

Constitution of India, Article 226 - Termination - An employee herself found instrumental in inviting the termination of her services.
She has been employed elsewhere but did not return to duty despite repeated reminders in violation to her own undertaking.
The Tribunal and High Court should not have set aside the order of termination.
[Para 9]

Constitution of India, Articles 14 and 311 - Termination - Stigma - Termination on the ground that an employee did not return to her duties in violation to her own undertaking and despite reminders cannot be held to be stigmatic but a termination simpliciter.
[Para 10]
