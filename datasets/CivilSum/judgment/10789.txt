JS Khehar, J - Petitioner Ranbir Singh was appointed as a Carpenter on 16.4.1986.
His services were dispensed with on 6121986.
The present controversy has nothing to do with the engagement of the petitioner as a Carpenter for the period referred to above.
After the services of the petitioner Ranbir Singh were terminated, he was offered an alternative appointment as Mazdoor.
Since the post of Mazdoor is lower in status than the post of Carpenter, he was required to confirm in writing whether or not he was willing to accept the lower category post, on or before 2241987, through a letter dated 1141987.
A perusal of the affidavit dated 142004 filed by the respondents before this Court, reveals that the petitioner submitted his willingness to accept the lower post on 2141987 i.e. well before the period stipulated in the letter dated 1141987.
2.
In order to complete the pre-requisite requirements of recruitment before a formal letter of appointment could be issued to the petitioner, he was required to submit character certificates issued by two gazetted officers, on or before 2461987, through a letter dated 1961987 (Annexure P.7).
The aforesaid letter was addressed to the Garrison Engineer, Patiala, who received the same only on 2661987 i.e. well after the last date of acceptance of the certificates, to be supplied by the petitioner, in case he was desirous of accepting the offer of appointment to the post of Mazdoor, expressed in the letter dated 1961987.
The petitioner, on coming to know of the same, on 2961987 submitted all necessary certificates along with his acceptance to the Commander Works Engineer on 2961987 itself.
Since the said certificates had not been supplied by the petitioner before 2241987, in terms of the requirement of the letter dated 1961987, his candidature for appointment to the post of Mazdoor was not considered any further.
3.
In order to press his claim for appointment to the post of Mazdoor, on account of his selection for the same, the petitioner invoked the provisions of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 (hereinafter referred to as `the Act').
The Central Government referred the following dispute for adjudication to the Central Government Industrial Tribunal-cum-Labour Court, Chandigarh (hereinafter referred to as `the Labour Court') :-  "Whether the management of MES is justified in cancelling the offer of an alternative job ?
If not, to what relief the workman Shri Ranbir Singh is entitled to ?"
By the impugned award dated 19.11.2001, (which was published on 2562002), the Labour Court rejected the claim of the petitioner for appointment against the post of Mazdoor because he had not furnished the required particulars i.e. character certificates issued by two gazetted officers on or before the stipulated date i.e. on or before 2261987.
In this behalf, reliance was placed on the letter dated 1961987 wherein it was expressly noticed that the candidature of a selected candidate would not be considered beyond 2461987, if all the formalities had not been complied with before the said date.
4.
The claim projected by the petitioner before this Court during the course of arguments on 932004 was, that the communication by which the Garrison Engineer, Patiala, was required to ensure the submission of necessary certificates by 2261987 was received in the office of the Garrison Engineer, Patiala, to whom it was addressed, only on 2461987 i.e. two days after the last date stipulated in the letter dated 1961987.
If the letter dated 1961987 had been received in the officer of the Garrison Engineer to which it was addressed after 2461987, surely the petitioner could not be held blame-worthy for not supplying the required certificates on or before 2461987.
5.
The aforesaid grievance of the petitioner was noticed by this Court in its order dated 932004, whereupon the learned counsel for the respondents sought an adjournment in order to obtain instructions from the Commander of Works Engineer.
Rather than obtaining instructions, learned counsel for the respondents filed an affidavit dated 142004 again clarifying the factual position which had transpired in respect of the petitioner's claim for appointment to the post of Mazdoor.
A perusal of the aforesaid affidavit does not clarify, whether or not, the communication which required the petitioner to furnish the certificates on or before 2461987, was received by the Garrison Engineer, Patiala, only on 2661987.
6.
So as to clarify the aforesaid factual position, learned counsel for the respondents required us to hear Col. PS Suresh.
We accordingly, afforded a hearing to Col. PS Suresh, the author of the affidavit dated 142004.
During the course of hearing, we questioned him about the aforesaid factual aspect, namely whether the communication requiring the petitioner to submit necessary certificates on or before 2461987 was received in the office of the Garrison Engineer, Patiala, only on 2661987.
In response to the aforesaid querry we were only informed that the petitioner was required to submit the necessary certificates on or before 2461987, and since he had not done so, it was not open for the respondents to consider the claim of the petitioner for appointment to the post of Mazdoor.
From the aforesaid sequence of facts it is clear that the allegation of the petitioner that the communication addressed to the Garrison Engineer, Patiala, through which the petitioner was required to furnish necessary character certificates on or before 2461987, has remained unrefuted.
7.
The present controversy will, therefore, have to be adjudicated by accepting the factual position that the letter requiring the petitioner to submit the required character certificates on or before 2461987 was received in the office of the Garrison Engineer, Patiala, on 2661987.
The petitioner cannot be held blame-worthy of not submitting the necessary certificates by the stipulated date i.e. on or before 2461987.
Since the communication requiring him to do so was received well after the prescribed date for supplying the stipulated certificates, the respondents themselves are blameworthy for the non-communication of the certificates by the petitioner by the appointed date.
It is clear from the narration of facts in the pleadings, that the aforesaid communication dated 1961987, was received in the officer of the Garrison Engineer, Patiala, on 2961987, and the petitioner furnished the said certificates in the office of the Commander Works Engineer on 2961987 itself.
The fact that the said certificates were furnished in the office of the Commander Works Engineer on 2961987 has been acknowledged at the end of paragraph 3 in the reply (on merits) filed by the respondents to the claim statement submitted by the petitioner.
In the aforesaid circumstances, it is apparent that it was wholly unjustified for the respondents in cancelling the candidature of the petitioner for appointment against the post of Mazdoor.
It would be just and fair in the facts and circumstances of the instant case to direct the respondents to issue a formal order of appointment to the petitioner against the post of Mazdoor.
Ordered accordingly.
In view of the aforesaid conclusion, the award of the Labour Court dated 19.11.2001/2562002 is hereby set aside.
8.
The appointment of the petitioner shall relate back to the appointments made in furtherance of the selection process in response to the communication dated 1961987 (Annexure P.7).
The name of the petitioner in the seniority list will figure at the same position as it was depicted in the process of selection.
9.
The only remaining issue is the quantum of back wages payable to the petitioner-workman, for the period which has lapsed for no fault of his.
Insofar as the instant issue is concerned, the facts narrated in the instant order reveal, that the petitioner was initially inducted into the service of the respondents against the post of Carpenter.
A person well versed in the trade of carpentry cannot be expected to have remained idle.
The petitioner must have been gainfully employed for most of the period.
Thus viewed, was consider it just and appropriate to direct the payment of back-wages to the petitioner limited to 25% wages which he would have drawn, had he not been deprived of his rights as noticed above.
Petition allowed.
