Satpal, J - By this judgment, we propose to dispose of three Writ Petitions.
C.W.P.
No. 2787 of 1986, C.W.P.
No. 1667 of 1986 and C.W.P.
No. 4009 of 1986, as the point of law involved in all these writ petitions is common.
2.
In all these writ petitions, the petitioners at the relevant time, were holding the post of Ziledars, and they have challenged the order, dated March 18, 1986, passed by respondent No. 1 whereby four other Ziledars, namely Sarvshri Banwari Lal, Mohinder Singh, Har Narain Singh and Dharamvir Vir Mittal, were selected as `B' Class Tehsildars on temporary basis for six months.
The selection of the aforesaid Ziledars has been challenged mainly on the ground that they were not eligible for consideration and case of the Writ Petitioners was not considered by the respondents.
It has also been contended in the writ petition that the respondent has no right to raise the upper age limit for the purpose of selection.
3.
These writ petitions were dismissed without any order as to costs vide judgment, dated 23rd August, 1994.
Thereafter, Review applications bearing Nos. RA No. 245 of 1994, 246 of 1994 and 247 of 1994, were filed on behalf of the writ petitioner on the ground that the arguments could not be addressed on their behalf and the absence of their counsel on the date of hearing was bona fide.
These Review applications came up for hearing on 7th October, 1994 and after hearing the learned counsel for the parties, the judgment dated 23rd August, 1994 was recalled and the writ petitions were directed to be listed afresh for arguments.
Thereafter, elaborate arguments were addressed on behalf of the petitioners.
4.
We have given our thoughtful consideration to the arguments made by the learned counsel for the parties and have perused the record.
The impugned order, dated 18th March, 1986, shows that the private respondents were selected as `B' Class Tehsildars on temporary basis for six months subject to their being declared suitable by the Haryana Public Service Commission and declared medically fit.
The upper age limit for selection to the said post was 40 years vide Annexure P1 (in CWP No. 2787 of 1986), but the same was raised to 45 years vide Annexure P2 and it was further raised to 50 years vide Annexure P3.
5.
Mr. Gopi Chand, learned counsel appearing on behalf of the petitioners, submitted that the raising of the upper age limit vide Annexures P2 and P3, was bad as no special reasons as required under the rules were given when the age was raised.
The further submitted that the age could not be relaxed when the candidates within she age limit were available in sufficient number.
In support of his contention, he placed reliance on a judgment of this Court in Sukhdarsan Pal Sehgal v. State of Punjab, 1994(2) SCT 551(P&H)(DB) : (1994) 2 Serv LR 228.
We, however, do not find any substance in the contention of learned counsel for the petitioner.
In para 5 of the written statement, filed on behalf of respondents Nos. 1 and 2, it has clearly been stated that in view of the upper age limit of 40 years, no recommendations were received from the Commissioners of Divisions, Director Land Records and Director Consolidation of Holdings because no eligible candidates were available.
It has further been stated that representations from some Suprintendents and Assistant Superintendents for enhancing the prescribed age limit of 40 years were also received.
In reply to ground (c) of the petition, it has further been submitted that even earlier also in the year 1974, upper age limit was enhanced to 48 years.
Another reason which weighed with the Government for raising the age limit was that a clerk working in Deputy Commissioner's office could not become Assistant Superintendent Revenue, at the age of 40 and, therefore, none of them could compete for the post.
We are, therefore, of the view that all these were valid reasons for the Government for raising the upper age limit.
6.
We also do not find any merit in the contention raised by the learned counsel for the petitioners that they were not considered by the respondents and selections were made without any guidelines.
Admittedly, the criteria for selection was seniority-cum-merit.
From the written statement filed on behalf of respondents Nos. 1 and 2, it is clear that the case of the petitioners and the private respondents, who were holding the posts of Ziledars, has been duly considered on the basis of the service record before the impugned order was passed.
From the length of service and assessment of the service record, we find that the private respondents were senior to the petitioners and they were fit for selection to the post of `B' class Tehsildars.
