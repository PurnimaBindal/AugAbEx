GS Singhvi, J - This appeal is directed against order dated 19121996 vide which the learned Single Judge allowed the writ petition filed by respondents No. 3 and 4 and quashed the appellant's appointment on the post of General Supervisor w.e.f.
15121983 and her promotion as Assistant Superintendent (Juvenile Home).

4.
While working as Lady Attendant (Hostel Warden) the appellant filed a suit in the Court of Senior Sub Judge, Jalandhar for directing the official respondents to appoint her as Hostel Warden/General Supervisor on regular basis by creating a post.
During the pendency of the suit, the government counsel made a statement that the appellant's case will be duly considered by the Competent Authority.
The suit was accordingly disposed of by learned Senior Sub Judge, Jalandhar.
Thereafter, respondent No. 2 issued order dated 2211991 (Annexure P.3) for appointment of the appellant as General Supervisor on regular basis w.e.f.
15121983 in the pay scale of Rs. 400-600 (old) and Rs. 950-1800 (revised).
After two months, he issued order dated 2131991 (Annexure P.4) and revised the date of the appellant's appointment from 15121983 to 2211991.
The appellant represented against order dated 2131991.
The State Government accepted her plea and in pursuance of the decision taken by it, which was conveyed to respondent No. 2 vide memo No. 1/95/90-1 Sub./3877 dated 1351993 (Annexure P.7) order dated 22111993 (Annexure P.9) was issued by respondent No. 2 restoring the retrospective appointment of the appellant on the post of General Supervisor.
Consequently, she was treated senior to respondent No. 3 and was promoted as Assistant Superintendent vide order dated 4121993 (Annexure P.10).
5.
Respondents No. 3 and 4, who were duly selected and regularly appointed as General Supervisors w.e.f.
9.7.1987 and 24.3.1987 respectively filed C.W.P.
No. 5447 of 1994 for quashing orders dated 2211991, 1351993 and 22111993 and order dated 4121993 vide which she was promoted as Assistant Superintendent.
They averred that the decision of the State Government to regularly appoint the appellant as General Supervisor with retrospective effect from 15121983 was violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India and was also ultra vires to the Punjab Civil Services Rules.
They further averred that in the seniority list of General Supervisors circulated vide order dated 2621990 (Annexure P.1), the name of the appellant had not been shown but by virtue of retrospective appointment, she was being treated senior to them.
6.
Respondents No. 1 and 2 justified the regular appointment of the appellant on the post of General Supervisor w.e.f.
15121983 by asserting that decision to that effect was taken keeping in view the assurance given on behalf of the government in the court of Senior Sub Judge, Jalandhar.
According to them, the State Government had sanctioned retrospective creation of the post of General Supervisor to facilitate the appointment of the appellant.
They also relied on the provisions of the Punjab Social Welfare Department Class-III Service Rules, 1973 (for short, `the Rules') and pleaded that respondent No. 2 did not commit any illegality by regularising the appointment of the appellant as General Supervisor.
According to them, the posts of Hostel Warden and General Supervisor form part of the same cadre and, therefore, no illegality was committed by appointing her as General Supervisor w.e.f.
15121983.
7.
The appellant defended the retrospective regularisation of her service on the post of General Supervisor by claiming that her initial appointment as Hostel Warden was regular and there was no difference between the qualifications, pay and job responsibilities of Hostel Warden and General Supervisor which form part of the common cadre in Group `C' of Appendix `A' attached to the Rules.
8.
The learned Single Judge quashed the orders vide which the appellant was regularly appointed as General Supervisor and then promoted as Assistant Superintendent.
He referred to orders dated 2791982 and 24.6.1983 and observed that by virtue of those orders, the appellant cannot be deemed to have been appointed to any of the post mentioned in the rules, much less on regular basis.
He further observed that the appellant was never selected for appointment as General Supervisor and, therefore, there was no question of treating her as regularly appointed with retrospective effect from 15121983.
He also rejected the theory of deemed relaxation of the Rules by observing that Rule 19 of the Rules had neither been invoked by the State Government nor it was available for regularising the appointment of the appellant.
9.
Shri PS Patwalia, Senior Advocate appearing for the appellant argued that the order of the learned Single Judge is liable to be set aside because it is based on a misreading of the pleadings and documents produced by the parties.
He laid emphasis on the fact that the appellant had been appointed as Hostel Warden on the basis of selection made by respondent No. 2 by considering the candidature of those who had applied in response to advertisement issued in 1982 and argued that the State Government did not commit any illegality by regularly appointing her as General Supervisor w.e.f.
15121983.
Shri Patwalia referred to Appendix `A' attached to the rules to show that the posts of General Supervisor and Hostel Warden fall in the same group and argued that regularisation of the appellant's service on the post of General Supervisor cannot be treated illegal simply because it was not preceded by advertisement in papers.
He pointed out that the qualifications, pay scale and job responsibilities of the two posts are identical and submitted that the appellant's appointment on the post of General Supervisor will be deemed to have been made by the State Government by relaxing the implicit requirement of advertising the posts.
In the end, he argued that even if the Court comes to the conclusion that the appellant could not have been retrospectively appointed as General Supervisor by invoking the provisions of the Rules, the decision of the State Government may not be disturbed because she was otherwise entitled to be regularised in terms of the policy framed by the State Government for regularising the services of ad hoc/temporary employees.
Shri Patwalia invited our attention to the instructions contained in circular letters dated 2831985 and 881985 and submitted that the appellant's service should be deemed to have been regularised w.e.f.
141985 because she fulfilled the conditions of eligibility enshrined therein.
10.
Ms. Rita Kohli, learned Deputy Advocate General, Punjab and Sarv Shri Ramesh Kumar and Kapil Kakkar, counsel representing the private respondents supported the order under challenge and argued that the learned Single Judge did not commit any error by quashing the patently illegal, arbitrary and unconstitutional orders passed by the State Government for retrospective appointment of the appellant on the post of General Supervisor and her promotion to the post of Assistant Superintendent.
Ms. Kohli referred to the records maintained by the department to show that before issuing advertisement, which was published in The Tribune dated 1191982, respondent No. 2 had made a reference to the government for creation of various posts including the post of Hostel Warden but his suggestion was rejected.
She argued that initial appointment of the appellant as Hostel Warden was purely temporary and fortuitous and did not create a right in her favour to seek regular appointment.
Ms. Kohli submitted that the appellant had been appointed as Hostel Warden without there being any sanction for the post and this was the reason why respondent No. 2 had sanctioned her continuance by invoking Rule 196 of the Punjab Financial Rules Volume-I She further submitted that the appellant cannot be treated as regularly appointed on the post of General Supervisor under the Rules because the procedure prescribed therein had not been followed.
11.
Sarv Shri Ramesh Kumar and Kapil Kakkar referred to Rule 9(vii-A) of the Rules to show that the posts of General Supervisors and Hostel Wardens can be filled only by direct recruitment and argued that respondent No. 2 did not have the power to appoint the appellant with retrospective effect without advertising the post so as to enable other eligible candidates to compete for selection.
They further argued that the so-called selection made by respondent No. 2 in 1982 cannot be equated with direct recruitment made under the Rules and, therefore, the appellant cannot be treated to have been regularly appointed to the service.
12.
We have thoughtfully considered the respective arguments.
A conjoint reading of the advertisement issued by respondent No. 2, orders dated 2791982, 24/2861983 and Annexures P2/A, P2/B, P.12/2 to P.12/14 reveals that the appellant was selected and initially appointed as Hostel Warden on purely temporary basis on consolidated salary.
The advertisement issued by respondent No. 2 was not meant for making recruitment within the meaning of Rule 9 of the Rules.
If he had intended to make regular recruitment, there was no reason to advertise the post for appointment on consolidated salary and call upon the candidates to directly appear for interview without even making application.
The fact that respondent No. 2 knew about the fortuitous nature of the appellant's appointment on the post of Hostel Warden is evident from the sanctions issued by him under Rule 196 of the Punjab Financial Rules, Volume-I for different periods commencing from 131984.
A reading of the sanction orders shows that the same were meant for Class-IV posts and not for the posts encadered in the Rules.
Therefore, the appellant cannot be treated to have recruited under the Rules so as to enable the State Government to regularise her service with retrospective effect.
13.
The matter deserves to be examined from another angle.
Admittedly, the post of General Supervisor had not been advertised by respondent No. 2 and no one including the appellant had applied for that post.
Therefore, there was no occasion, much less legal justification, for appointing her as General Supervisor on regular basis w.e.f.
15121983.
In view of the above, we are in complete agreement with the conclusion recorded by the learned Single Judge that appointment of the appellant as General Supervisor w.e.f.
15121983 was violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India.
13.
We also agree with the learned Single Judge that Rule 19 of the Rules had neither been invoked by the State Government nor that Rule was available for regularising the appointment of the appellant on the post of General Supervisor.
No material was placed before the learned Single Judge to show that the State Government had applied mind to the conditions enshrined in Rule 19 of the Rules and relaxed the requirement of making appointment by direct recruitment.
Therefore, the appellant's retrospective appointment as General Supervisor cannot be treated to have been made with the aid of Rule 19 of the Rules.
14.
The argument of Shri Patwalia that orders dated 2211991 and 22111993 should be saved by giving the appellant benefit of policy contained in circulars dated 2831985 and 881985 which have been approved by the Supreme Court in State of Haryana v. Piara Singh, 1992(4) SCC 118 : 1992(3) SCT 201 (SC) sounds attractive but lacks merit.
A reading of the two circulars (photostat copies of which were produced during the course of hearing) shows that the policy contained therein envisaged regularisation of the services of ad hoc/temporarily appointed employees appointed to Class-III services or posts who had completed minimum of one year's service on 1st April, 1985.
This was subject to the condition that they must have been appointed through the employment exchange or by open advertisement.
The appellant was appointed as Hostel Warden on consolidated salary and not on ad hoc or temporary basis.
Therefore, she cannot get the benefit of regularisation in terms of the policy instructions contained in circular letters dated 2831985 and 881985.
Hence, the appeal is dismissed.
However, we give liberty to the appellant to represent to the government for regularisation of her service, if her case is covered by any other policy decision circulated by the State Government after 881985.
We also give liberty to the appellant to seek voluntary retirement and direct that if she makes an application for that purpose within 3 months from today, then the government may retire her from the post of Assistant Superintendent.
Appeal dismissed.
