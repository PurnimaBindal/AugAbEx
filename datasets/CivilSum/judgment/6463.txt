HS Bedi, J. (Oral) - The petitioner joined as a Daftri in the year 1978 and was confirmed on the said post in the year 2001.
During his interregnum, the petitioner received a pre-mature increment on 2431990.
Consequently, a proficiency step up increment was granted to him on the completion of 18 years of service on 371997 and he was promoted as an officiating Restorer on 16.9.1998.
During this period, the petitioner was also awarded a minor penalty of stoppage of one increment without cumulative effect vide order dated 2191995.
The petitioner is aggrieved by the order Annexure P-12 dated 242003 whereby the Chief Justice has pre-maturely retired him from service in public interest and the order Annexure P-15 dated 2352003 made by the Chief Justice on the representation filed by him.
The present writ petition has been filed impugning the aforesaid orders.
In the written-statement filed by the High Court, the stand taken is that an absolute right vested in the appointing authority to retire an official on the basis of the record and as such no interference was called for.
It has also been pleaded that after the order Annexure P-12 had been made, the petitioner had filed a representation which had been put up before a Committee of three Judges and they too had recommended the rejection of the representation and the order Annexure P-15 dated 2352003 has been passed by the Chief Justice thereafter.
It has further been pleaded that the petitioner had withheld some relevant information in the writ petition as in addition to a stoppage of one increment without cumulative effect which had been imposed in the year 1996, another penalty of censure too had been imposed on 541994 vide Annexure R-1/1 and as such the writ petition was liable to be dismissed on the ground of withholding of relevant facts.
During the course of hearing before us, it has been argued by the learned counsel for the petitioner that the stoppage of one increment and one censure during a span of about 25 years of service did not justify the pre-mature retirement of an employee.
In these circumstances, we have thought it fit to peruse the record of the High Court qua the petitioner and the summary thereof has been produced before us.
We reproduce the summary hereinafter :  Year  Remarks 1978  B+(Good) 1979  B+(Good) 1980  B+(Good) 1981  B+(Good) 1982  B+(Good) 1983  B+(Good) 1984  B+(Good) 1985  B+(Good) 1986  B+(Good) 1987  B+(Good) 1988  B(Satisfactory) 1989  B+(Good) 1990  B+(Good) 1991  B+(Good) 1992  B+(Good) 1993  B(Satisfactory) 1994  B+(Good) 1995  B+(Good) 1996  B+(Good) 1997  B+(Good) 1998  B+(Good) 1999  B+(Good) 2000  B+(Good) 2001  B+(Good) 2.
We find that except for two reports one of 1989 and other of 1993 which were satisfactory, the petitioner has had a consistently B+ good record.
It is also relevant that from the year 1978 to 1997 the petitioner was working as a Peon/daftri.
The last 4 reports of year 1998 to 2001 after his promotion as a Restorer were consistently Good.
Two minor penalties of the nature described above do not justify an order of retirement.
The petitioner cannot be dubbed as a piece of dead wood which ought to be chucked out.
We accordingly allow the writ petition and direct the reinstatement of the petitioner forthwith with all consequential benefits.
Ipso facto the petitioner is allowed to retain the house he is presently occupying without penal interest.
No order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
