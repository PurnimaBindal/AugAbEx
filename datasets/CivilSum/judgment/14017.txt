Mehtab S Gill, J - By this judgment, I shall be disposing of both these petitions bearing Nos. 5267 and 5268 of 1988 as common questions of law and facts involved therein are identical in nature.
For the sake of convenience, facts are being extracted from Civil Writ Petition No. 5267 of 1988.
2.
The petitioners have prayed for issuing a writ in the nature of certiorari for quashing the Punjab State Assistants Grade Examination Rules, 1984.
The petitioners have further prayed for issuing a writ in the nature of mandamus, directing respondent Nos. 3 and 4 to promote them to the posts of Assistants, from the date on which respondent Nos. 5 to 7 were promoted.
3.
The petitioners have averred, that they were Clerks in the office of Deputy Commissioner, Ludhiana.
All the petitioners belong to the Punjab Subordinate Services of District Establishments and were appointed under the Punjab District Subordinate Service Rules, 1942 (hereinafter called "1942 Rules").
They joined service between years 1959 to 1965.
On November 1, 1966, the Punjab Reorganisation Act, 1966, came into force and Punjab was split into three States, i.e, Punjab, Haryana and Himachal Pradesh.
4.
As per original provisions under 1942 Rules, there was no condition of clearing Assistants Grade Examination for promotion from the post of a Clerk to that of an Assistant.
Relevant rules, i.e. 8(iv)(c) of 1942 Rules, are reproduced as under :-  (i) "By Selection from among senior clerks in the same district; or (ii) By selection from among stenographers in the grade of Rs. 35-1-1/2-65-2-75, or (iii) By selection from among junior clerks or apprentice clerks in the same office; or (iv) By direct appointment."
5.
In the year 1976, fresh rules were framed by the Governor of Punjab, in exercise of the powers conferred under Article 309 of the Constitution of India.
These rules were called the Punjab District Service (Class III) Rules, 1976 (hereinafter called "1976 Rules").
They came into force on June 24, 1976.
As per rule 19, 1942 Rules were repealed.
Rule 9(e) of 1976 Rules provided for appointment to 75% of the posts by promotion from amongst Clerks and Steno-typists of the respective cadres of the district concerned and 25% of the posts by direct recruitment.
These 1976 Rules did not provide for any test or examination for appointment to the post of Assistant.
6.
In the year 1984, a new set of rules was enforced, i.e., the Punjab State Assistants Grade Examination Rules, 1984 (hereinafter called "1984 Rules").
These rules were framed in exercise of powers conferred under Article 309 and clause (b) of Article 313 of the Constitution.
They came into force on April 11, 1984.
7.
It has been further averred that the petitioners have rendered satisfactory service and earned good remarks.
In spite of good service records, the petitioners were denied promotion for not complying with 1984 Rules and for not clearing the Assistants Grade Examination, as prescribed by these Rules.
8.
Petitioner Nos. 1 to 3 were promoted as Assistants in September 1985, May 1984 and July 1984 respectively.
In the year 1987, they were demoted for not having passed the said examination.
9.
Notice of motion was issued.
10.
Learned counsel for the respondents has stated that petitioner Nos. 1 to 25 were Clerks in the office of the Deputy Commissioner, Ludhiana.
All the petitioners joined service somewhere between years 1959 to 1965 i.e., before new State of Punjab came into being, i.e, on November 1, 1966.
Learned counsel has further stated that in 1984 Rules, there is a condition of passing Assistants Grade Examination for promotion from the post of a Clerk to that of an Assistant.
Learned counsel has further stated that 1942 Rules were repealed and new 1976 Rules came into force.
11.
During the course of arguments, learned counsel for the petitioners has stated that all the petitioners have retired from service.
12.
Learned counsel for the petitioners has stated that, as the services of the petitioners were governed by 1942 Rules, thus, they could not have been compelled to clear Assistants Grade Examination, as provided in 1984 Rules.
Learned counsel has further stated that service conditions of the petitioners could not have been changed unilaterally and that too retrospectively.
13.
I have heard the learned counsel for the petitioners and the learned counsel for the respondents and gone through the relevant Rules.
14.
Learned counsel for the petitioners has drawn my attention to a judgment of the Hon'ble Supreme Court rendered in the case of State of Haryana and others v. Shamsher Jang Bahadur and others, 1972 S.L.R.
441, wherein it has been held that change in conditions of service by administrative instructions - if a test is prescribed for promotion, relating to change in conditions of service, the Government is not competent to prescribe examination for promotion by administrative instructions.
It has been further held that by adding the qualifications already prescribed by the rules, the Government has altered the existing conditions of service.
It has been further held that any rule, which affects the promotion relating to conditions of service, approval of the Central Government is necessary, if conditions of service changed after reorganisation of the States.
15.
Learned counsel for the petitioners has further drawn my attention to a judgment of the Hon'ble Supreme Court rendered in the case of TR Kapur and others v. State of Haryana and others, AIR 1987 Supreme Court 415, wherein it was held that promotion to the post of Executive Engineer in Class I service - Notification by State Government under Article 309, making a degree in Engineering, essential for such promotion, and making diploma holder members of Class II service ineligible for promotion.
Notification is ultra vires the State Government, being contrary to Section 82(6) of the Punjab Reorganisation Act.
It also violates Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution (Punjab Reorganisation Act 31 of 1966).
16.
Citations, as referred to above, squarely cover the case of the petitioners.
17.
It is an admitted fact that Rules 1976 and 1984 were framed, without the approval of the Central Government.
Proviso to Section 82 of the Punjab Reorganisation Act, 1966 is reproduced as under :-  "Provided that the conditions of service applicable immediately before the appointed day to the case of any person referred to in sub-sections (1) (2) shall not be varied to this disadvantage except with the previous approval of the Central Government."
From this proviso, it is clear that the service conditions of the petitioners could not have been changed due to their disadvantage except with the previous approval of the Central Government.
18.
1984 Rules were made by the President of India.
These rules were framed at the time, when the State was under the President's rule.
The President of India was making these rules on behalf of the Punjab Government.
Prior permission of the Central Government, as per Section 82 of the Punjab Reorganisation Act, 1966, as still essential.
Rules could not have been framed without the prior permission of the Central Government.
19.
Punjab State Assistants Grade Examination Rules, 1984, are set aside partly to this extent that they shall not apply to the petitioners for appointment/promotion to the post of an Assistant.
20.
Further, respondent Nos. 3 and 4 are directed to give all the consequential benefits accruing from partly setting aside the 1984 Rules.
Both these petitions are allowed to this limited extent only.
Petitions partly allowed.
