17.
Mr. RN Das, the learned counsel for Railway Administration would contend that the amendments are prospective in nature which would also appear from Rule 2045 of the Railway Manual.
It has been submitted that in between the period 1966 to 1989 several changes had taken place and if additional benefits proposed in 1989 are to be given with retrospective effect, the same would cause a great financial hardship to the Railway Administration.
The learned counsel submits that although the Railway Board has granted the said benefit to Group `B' employees with retrospective effect, the said order of the Board should not be considered to be applicable in relation to Group `C' employees, as the employees of both Groups `B' and `C' do not belong to the same category.
18.
Mr. Ganguly, the learned counsel appearing on behalf of the Respondents, however, has drawn our attention to the decision of Central Administrative Tribunal (Madras Bench) which is to the following effect:  "This Tribunal has considered the clarification issued by the Railway Board dated 4990, which would marked as A.5 before that Tribunal.
The Ernakulam Bench after considering the respective contentions quashed the Railway Board's letter dated 4990 and directed increments."
19.
According to the learned counsel as the 1990 Circular Letter has been quashed, the benefit of the said Judgment must be held to be equally applicable to the petitioners.
20.
Our attention in this connection has also been drawn to the recommendations of the 4th Pay Commission which is in the following terms :  "Recommendation of IVth Central Pay Commission.

Efficiency of Administration : The question of granting incentive to officers & staff who acquire higher qualification as also engaged our attention.
Railways have suggested a scheme for giving such incentives in the context of the need for updating the skills of the employees for the more efficient discharge of their duties in these days when modernisation and adoption of advanced technology is being undertaken in different fields of Railway working.
Suggestions have also been made for grant of post graduate allowance to veterinary surgeons and special allowance to EDP personnel.
Some such schemes are in existence in the defence services.
We suggest that some incentive should be given to employees who acquire qualification are useful for their work and contribute to their efficiency."
21.
The learned counsel submits that in view of the order dated 29th April, 1991, the benefit with retrospective effect had been granted to both Group `B' and `C' officers and thus, there is no reason as to why those who are working in Group `C' will have to wait till they are promoted to Group `B'.
22.
In the instant cae, the Railway Board advisedly has used the word `Substitute' in its Order dated 2951989.
Normally although a Circular Letter is construed prospectively, if the same grants benefit to an employee the same may have retrospective effect.
In fact despite Rule 2045 of the Railway Manual the Railway Board itself has construed the Order dated 25.9.90 as retrospective.
Subject to certain exceptions, the term `substitution' either may mean amendment which will have a prospective effect, but having regard to the text and context of the substituted provision, the same may have to be read as if the same was in existence from the very beginning.
In the latter case it would be retrospective.
23.
Railway Board in terms of its circular letter gave retrospective effect to the said Circular in respect of Group-`B' employees.
Despite a bar that once an increment had been granted, the same would not be claimed for the second time, the Board by an order dated 14.5.1986 directed that those who are promoted from Group `C' to Group `B' would be entitled to the additional benefit.
24.
We really fail to see any rationality in such approach inasmuch as in terms of the Circular Letters issued by the Railway Board, the benefit which is sought to be denied to the petitioners herein so long as they hold the present post would be granted on their promotion to Grade `B'.
As the petitioners would not be entitled to double benefit, there is no harm if such benefit is granted to the concerned officers at the stage when they are holding the post of Grade `C'.
25.
Therefore, it is also necessary to consider the reason for grant of such incentive.
The aforementioned incentive scheme dated 14.5.1966 as contained in Annexure `L' reads thus :-  "A number of directives have been issued from time to time by the Board in regard to the grant of incentives to Class III serving Railway employees/apprentices for acquiring higher or additional professional qualifications.
As references keep on pouring from Railways as to whether passing of a particular examination will make a person eligible for the grant of incentives, the following comprehensive instructions embodying all the relevant instructions issued from time to time on the subject are sent herewith, in supersession of all the previous orders for guidance :- (a) Incentives should be granted to such of the Scientific/Technical Accounts Staff, who pass technical/professional examination as in (b) below and for whom a decree or equivalent in Science or Engineering (in case of Scientific/Technical Staff) and Membership of the Institute of Cost and Works Accountants (in the case of Accounts staff) is not obligatory as a condition of recruitment.
(b) Such qualifications must be equivalent to Degree examinations and must have been recognised by the Central Government for the purpose of recruitment to posts and services under the Central Government.
For this purpose, reference should be made to the qualifications prescribed for recruitment of Superior Posts and services in the pamphlet "List of Technical and Professional Qualifications recognised by the Government of India" issued by the Ministry of Scientific Research and Cultural Affairs in March 1963 as amended from time to time by the Ministry of Education, Government of India, New Delhi.
(c) If in a grade posts are filled both by promotion from lower grades and also by direct recruitment of persons possessing a specified professional or equivalent qualification the promotees in that grade will be eligible; to the incentives/or cash award if they acquire the same qualification while employed in that grade.
(d) On passing the relevant examinations the following awards shall be given to the Railway employees/apprentices.
(i) For passing Part-I or `A' Intermediate Rs. 200/- as cash award.
(ii) For passing Part-II or `B' or Final Two advance increments.
Examination : (e) Incentives are to be granted only once and not twice over.
(f) Except what is provided in Clause (g) the benefit of incentive scheme will be made available to all the staff, permanent or temporary, irrespective of their working in the parent department or any other Department or Railways in a cadre or ex-cadre post.
(g) Non-Accounts staff who are temporarily working in the Accounts Department may also be granted the benefits of incentive scheme for passing the Cost Accountants Examination so long as they continue to work in the Accounts Department."
26.
The reason for grant of incentive for acquisition of higher qualification, thus, was that such higher knowledge would be applied by the concerned employees which would help them in better performance of their job.
The employees who had acquired a high er qualification whether they work in Grade `B' or Grade `C' would utilise their higher knowledge while performing their job; the logical corollary whereof would be that the railway administration would derive the benefit of the knowledge and experience of a more academically qualified person although minimum qualification for holding such post was lower.
27.
Furthermore, the order impugned before the Tribunal had been struck down by the Madras Bench.
A Special Leave Petition taken there against has been dismissed by the Supreme Court of India.
Admittedly, the facts in OA No. 1403/92 are similar to the case of the petitioners.
Article 14 of the Constitution of India mandates that if a benefit has been granted to a section of the employees by reason of a Judgment passed by a competent court of law, the same be extended to all other employees who are similarly situated.
28.
Benefit of Judgment, thus, should be granted to all persons similarly situated (See : Virendra Kumar & Ors. v. Union of India & Ors, reported in AIR 1981 SC 1775; Gopal Krishna Sharma v. State of Rajasthan & Ors. reported in 1992(3) SCT 726 (SC) : 1993 Supp.
(2) SCC 375 at 376 and Lt.
Governor of Delhi v. Dharampal & Ors. reported in 1994(4) SCC 13.
29.
In KC Sharma & Ors. Union of India & Ors., reported in 1997(3) SCT 641 (SC) : 1997(6) SCC 721, the Apex Court directed condonation of delay in a case where even the application was barred by limitation, inter alia, on the ground that benefits as claimed in such application had been granted by the Tribunal to other employees who are similarly situated.
30.
It is further well known that an incentive scheme granted to an employee should be liberally construed.
31.
Having regard to our aforementioned findings, we are of the opinion that the learned Tribunal was correct in holding that the incentive scheme continued by reason of the order dated 29th May, 1989 and the petitioners are entitled to the similar benefit as granted to the other Officers, who are similarly situated with retrospective effect.
32.
In view of our findings aforementioned, it is not necessary to consider the effect of the decision of the Apex Court in Parsion Devi & Ors. v. Sumitri Devi & Ors., reported in 1977(89) Supreme 25, for the purpose of considering the effect of an order passed on a review application.
33.
