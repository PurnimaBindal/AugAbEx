THB Chalapathi, J - This Writ Petition has been filed to quash the order of the Presiding Officer, Labour Court, dated September 21, 1994, holding that the enquiry conducted by the management against the workman-respondent No. 2 has not been proved to be valid.
2.
Respondent No. 2, who was a workman of the petitioner, raised an industrial dispute as his services was terminated without valid and proper enquiry.
The Labour Court framed a preliminary issue as to whether fair and proper enquiry was held against the workman as to the charges framed against the workman.
The workman had examined himself and he stated that no witness was examined during the enquiry.
The management has not adduced any evidence with regard to the enquiry held.
Therefore, the Presiding Officer, Labour Court, came to the conclusion that the management has not proved that the enquiry is valid.
Challenging the said order, the Writ Petition has been filed.
Learned counsel for the respondent-workman contended that several opportunities had been given to the management to produce evidence but the management failed to avail the opportunities given to it and, therefore, the evidence for the management was closed and the impugned order was passed by the Labour Court and, thus, the management is not entitled to any indulgence from this Court.
Therefore, the Writ Petition is liable to be dismissed.
3.
It can be seen from the record that the matter was originally adjourned to April 25, 1990, for evidence but the management did not adduce any evidence on that date.
Therefore, it was adjourned to September 6, 1990, on which date there was a strike by the members of the Bar.
Thereafter, it was adjourned to November 7, 1990.
On November 7, 1990, the statement of Sham Sunder Batra was recorded and the matter was adjourned for cross-examination to December 12, 1990.
Again on that date there was strike and the case was adjourned to February 20, 1991.
On that day, at the request of the parties, the case was adjourned to March 21, 1991.
There was strike on March 21, 1991, also, therefore, the matter was adjourned to May 14, 1991, on which date the Presiding Officer was on leave.
Therefore, the matter was adjourned to May 20, 1991.
Again the matter was adjourned to August 13, 1991, on which date the management examined Sham Sunder Batra and closed his evidence and the matter was posted on October 22, 1991, for workman's evidence.
Thereafter, the statement of the workman was recorded on May 5, 1992, and the matter was posted for arguments on May 21, 1992, and again it was adjourned to June 3, 1992.
As June 3, 1992, was declared holiday the matter was adjourned to July 15, 1992, and again the matter was adjourned to July 22, 1992, for arguments.
Again it was adjourned to August 12, 1992, for arguments, but due to strike the members of the bar, matter was adjourned to September 2, 1992, but at the request of the parties, the matter was adjourned to October 6, 1992.
The matter was taken up on May 11, 1993, and the matter was adjourned to September 15, 1993, at the request of the counsel for the petitioner.
Then the matter was adjourned to December 23, 1993, and January 21, 1994, and February 24, 1994, and March 18, 1994, and March 30, 1994, for arguments.
Thus, it can be seen that the matter had been coming up for arguments but for one or the other reason, the arguments could not be heard by the Presiding Officer.
On March, 30, 1994, nearly 2-1/2 years after closing of the evidence by the management, the petitioner-management filed an application for adducing additional evidence.
That application was allowed on payment of Rs. 150/- as costs and the matter was adjourned to June 2, 1994.
The petitioner could not adduce any evidence on that day and then the matter was adjourned to July 20, 1994, and again on September 14, 1994.
On September 1, 1994, also the management could not adduce evidence.
Therefore, the Presiding Officer closed the evidence for the management and posted the matter for arguments to September 16, 1994.
Learned counsel for the petitioner contended that the members of the bar were on strike on September 14, 1994, therefore, the management could not produce its evidence.
I am unable to accept this contention.
Even if the members of the bar were on strike, nothing prevented the petitioner to place on record the enquiry report for which the matter was re-opened in March, 1994.
When the matter was adjourned to September 16, 1994, the counsel for both the parties argued the matter.
The Presiding Officer closed the evidence of the petitioner on September 14, 1994.
No objection was raised by the counsel for the petitioner before the Labour Court on September 16, 1994.
On September 16, 1994, the Presiding Officer heard the arguments and posted the matter to September 21, 1994, for orders.
Accordingly, the orders were passed on September 21, 1994.
There is nothing on record to show that either the petitioner or his counsel were present on September 14, 1994.
4.
The learned counsel for the petitioner stated before me that the petitioner was not present before the Labour Court on September 14, 1994.
From a narration of the facts as detailed above, it is clear that ample opportunity had been given to the petitioner to adduce evidence but the petitioner had not taken advantage of the several opportunities given to him.
In fact, after closing the evidence of Sham Sunder Batra, on August 13, 1991, the matter was posted for evidence of the workman and the evidence of the workman was recorded on May 5, 1991.
Since then it was coming up for arguments only till March 30, 1994, when a request was made by the petitioner to adduce additional evidence.
Thus, there is a long delay on the part of the petitioner even in seeking to adduce additional evidence.
Even after his application was allowed on March 30, 1994, the matter was adjourned thrice but the management did not adduce any evidence.
I am, therefore, of the opinion that the Labour Court rightly closed the evidence and came to the conclusion that the management failed to prove that the enquiry was fair, proper and valid.
I do not, therefore, find any grounds warranting interference with the orders of the Labour Court.
The Writ Petition therefore fails and is accordingly dismissed.
No order as to costs.
