ML Liberhan and HS Bedi, JJ.
- Five seats were reserved by the State in Diploma in Education course, for the genuine.permanent residents of village Mohra, District Ambala, who have donated the land for the establishment of the Institute.
Concededly, the petitioners are not residents of the said village.
2.
The petitioners competed against the general seats advertised and could not get admission on the basis of the merit list prepared.
In case this reservation is struck down, cause of education would suffer irreparably particularly when the State cannot afford to open institutions on its own and provide education to people, as envisaged by the Constitution of India.
If the villagers who donated their land and resources are not permitted to get their wards admitted, against a limited number of seats reserved for them no body would come forward to donate land and resources for establishment of such institutions and the reservation of five seats for permanent residents of the village in these circumstances, cannot be called arbitrary or unreasonable, specifically when the five seats are over and above the requisite seats.
Moreover, if the reservation of these seats for the villagers is quashed, it would result in depriving more than 100 students from getting education which would be a counter productive and would defeat the object of proving education to the rural persons.
In view of the above discussion, we do not find it a fit case to interfere in exercise of writ jurisdiction.
Dismissed.
