JS Khehar, J - Learned counsel for the petitioner-management does not dispute the conclusion drawn by the Presiding Officer, Labour Court, Gurdaspur (hereinafter referred to as the Labour Court), to the effect that the respondent-workman has rendered service in excess of 240 days in the twelve calendar months preceding his termination w.e.f.
15101992.
2.
The only contention advanced by the learned counsel for the petitioner- management is, that the respondent-workman himself abandoned his duties.
In order to establish that the respondent-workman had himself abandoned his duties, learned counsel for the petitioner-management extensively relied on a communication dated 991992 (Ex.M-2), which has been placed on the record of this case as Annexure P-4.
A perusal of Annexure P-4 reveals that the respondent-workman had remained absent from duty w.e.f 1381992 to 1781992.
The aforesaid communication, however, also reveals that the respondent-workman had resumed his duties on 27.8.1992.
In view of the acknowledgment in the letter Ex.M-2 that the respondent-workman had resumed his duties on 27.8.1992, it is not possible for us to accept, on the basis of the aforesaid communication, that the respondent-workman had abandoned his duties w.e.f.
15101992.
3.
Learned counsel for the petitioner-management, in order to authenticate the plea of the petitioner-management in respect of abandonment, also placed reliance on Ex.M-1 i.e. order dated 15101992.
The aforesaid order has been placed on the record of this case as Annexure P-1.
The aforesaid order is indeed an order of termination.
In the background of the fact that the petitioner-management itself termination the services of the respondent- workman, it is not possible for petitioner-management to plead that the respondent-workman himself had abandoned his duties.
4.
In view of the above, we find not merit in the instant writ petition.
The same is, accordingly, dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
