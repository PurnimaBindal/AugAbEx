RL Anand, J - State of Haryana has filed the present Regular Second Appeal and it has been directed against the judgment and decree dated 19.11.1993, passed by the Court of Additional District Judge, Faridabad, which dismissed the appeal of the State by affirming the judgment and decree dated 331992, passed by the Court of Sub Judge 2nd Class, Faridabad, which decreed the suit of the plaintiff-respondent Shri Lakhender Parkash for declaration that verbal orders of the termination of the service of the plaintiff of 11111987, were illegal, unlawful, mala fide and plaintiff continues to be in service.
It was also declared by the Court that the plaintiff would be entitled for back pay and allowances from the date of his termination and that he would be entitled to reinstatement into service.
2.
Some facts can be noticed in the following manner.
The case set up by the plaintiff in the trial Court was that he joined the service of Haryana State as Patwari in Sub Division No. 1 P.W.D.
(B&R) w.e.f.
2211986 from where he was transferred to Land Acquisition Department under the supervisory control of Executive Engineer.
According to the plaintiff, he was in continuous employment of the State with effect from 2211986.
His services were interrupted by showing notional breaks on account of holidays.
He was shown in service from 2211986 to 1191986 and again from 1.12.1986 to 11111987.
His services were orally terminated on 11111987.
According to the plaintiff, this order of termination is unlawful and mala fide.
He was appointed as Patwari and was qualified for such appointment.
According to the plaintiff the appointment of Shri Bijender Singh and Lal Singh, Ziledar as Patwaris, was illegal and mala fide as they did not possess the requisite qualifications.
The services of the plaintiff were terminated as the Department wanted to accommodate Shri Lal Singh.
Moreover, Bijender Singh was junior to the plaintiff and he was allowed to continue in the Department.
There was a regular post of Patwari which existed at the time of the appointment of the plaintiff as well as his termination.
The plaintiff further stated that he remained in the service of the defendants for more than 240 days continuously and, therefore, he will be deemed to have been appointed against a regular post.
He also alleged that he served a notice under Section 80 CPC before filing the suit.
3.
Notice of the suit was given to the defendants who filed the written- statement and took the preliminary objections such as that the suit of the plaintiff was not maintainable; that it was barred by limitation; that the civil Court has no jurisdiction to try the suit.
4.
On merits, the stand of the defendants was that plaintiff was engaged on short term basis on 2211986 which term expired on 3131986.
It was further submitted that the service of the plaintiff was never continuous and he was engaged from time to time on short term periods basis as per details in Annexure-A, filed with the written-statement.
Sanctions were given for a work-charge Patwari for different periods but not exceeding 89 days.
No appointment letter was given to the plaintiff.
The last sanction of the plaintiff was up to 31101987 and, therefore, beyond 31101987, the plaintiff could not remain in service.
The defendant admitted that notice under Section 80 CPC was given.

6.
The parties led oral as well as the documentary evidence support of their case.
7.
The learned trial Court held that oral termination of the service of the plaintiff took place on 11111987 and it is illegal, ultra vires and arbitrary.
Issues No. 1 to 6 were decided in favour of the plaintiff.
Issues No. 7 to 9 were decided against the defendants.
Resultantly, the suit of the plaintiff was decreed vide judgment and decree dated 331992.
8.
The State of Haryana filed the first appeal in the Court of Additional District Judge, Ferozepur, which vide judgment and decree dated 19.11.1993 dismissed the appeal by affirming the judgment and decree of the trial Court.
9.
So far as the date of engagement of the plaintiff in service is concerned, it is dated 2211986 and this aspect of the case is not disputed by the parties.
10.
Now, it is to be seen whether the appointment of the plaintiff was contractual or that he continued in service up to 11111987 as alleged by the plaintiff.
The case set up by the defendants in the trial Court is that plaintiff remained in service up to 31101987 but his appointment was contractual from time to time and the contract of period was never extended beyond 89 days at one stretch.
The Civil Court can always lift the veil in order to ascertain as to whether the service of a public servant was continuous or that a notional break has been given by the department in the service of the plaintiff so that plaintiff may not be able to get the advantage of regularisation etc. As per the showing of the defendants itself, the plaintiff was appointed as Patwari on 2211986 and at the first instance his service continued up to 1191986.
Again the plaintiff was re-appointed on the same day i.e. 1191986 and this time he continued up to 11111987.

A critical reading of Annexure P-5 would show that the department has given notional breaks of one day on 141986, 1.6.86, 1886, 1187, 1287, 1.4.87, 1687 and 1887.
This act on the part of the Department was nothing but a mala fide practice just to give a colour of pure contractual post which was unwarranted.
Even otherwise, it will be presumed that plaintiff was in service from 2211986 to 11111987.
12.
The next aspect of the case is whether the plaintiff was working against a regular post or not ?
13.
In this regard, I can refer to the statement of DW-1 Atam Parkash who categorically stated that the plaintiff was working as a Patwari under him from August 1987 to October 1987 and there was a regular post of Patwari under him.
He further stated that after the appointment of the plaintiff, Shri Lal Singh was appointed as Patwari and similarly Shri Bijender Singh was also appointed as a Patwari much after the appointment of the plaintiff.
It gives two inferences that plaintiff was working against a post and Shri Bijender Singh and Lal Singh were juniors to the plaintiff.
In these circumstances, the principle of "last come first go" has to be adopted.
There is no evidence on the record to suggest that there was any allegation of misconduct against the plaintiff.
It is not the case of the defendant-State that any regular departmental inquiry was conducted against the plaintiff before terminating his services.
If the junior employees have been retained in service by violating the principle of natural justice and fair play such oral order of termination of the service of the plaintiff cannot be sustained in the eye of law on the test of judicial scrutiny being arbitrary and against the principle of natural justice.
Rather the evidence which is coming on the record is that as many as 20 persons were appointed as Patwaris and this is so stated by Shri Atam Parkash, Tehsildar DW-1.
In this view of the matter, the termination of the service of the plaintiff is nothing but an abuse of the process of law.
Such action on the part of the defendant-State has to be held arbitrary, mala fide, unconstitutional and unlawful.
14.
The second point which survives for determination is whether the plaintiff is entitled to the back wages with effect from 11111987 onwards or not ?
15.
The counsel appearing on behalf of the State submitted that plaintiff has not served the department since 11111987 and, therefore, he is not entitled to the back wages.
The learned counsel appearing on behalf of the respondent-plaintiff submitted that it is not the fault of the plaintiff.
He was thrown out from the service for arbitrary reasons.
Since then the plaintiff is unemployed.
Moment a declaration is granted to the plaintiff that the termination of his service was bad, illegal, the plaintiff stands relegated to his original position and he would be entitled to all the consequential benefits not only in the matter of pay but also in the matter of seniority and regularisation etc.
He submitted that there is no illegality in the judgments of the Courts below when a declaration has already been granted in favour of the plaintiff that he would be entitled to the back wages etc.  16.
After considering the rival contentions of the parties, I am of the opinion that plaintiff is entitled to the benefit of seniority, regularisation, continuity in service w.e.f.
11111987 onwards.
So far as back wages are concerned he will be entitled to back wages of 38 months of his last pay drawn when he was discharging the duties of patwari.
To this extent, the declaration granted by the Courts below is modified.
17.
The net result is that this appeal is partly allowed; the judgments and decrees of the Courts below are modified to a limited extent.
The plaintiff is granted a declaration that termination of the service with effect from 11111987 is illegal, unlawful, mala fide and arbitrary.
Further relief is granted to the plaintiff-respondent that he will be entitled to the benefit of the service seniority, regularisation, continuity of service but he would only be paid back wages/pay of 38 months which shall be calculated on the basis of his last pay drawn because the plaintiff did not serve the Department after the institution of the suit and he never prayed to the Court that he may be allowed to join the duty.
The appellant shall also pay Rs. 500/- by way of costs to the plaintiff of this appeal.
18.
In all other respects, the appeal of the State is hereby dismissed.
19.
Before parting with this judgment, I direct the State-appellant to fix the seniority of the plaintiff within three months from the receipt of the copy of this order and further grant him ancillary benefits on account of the dismissal of the appeal of the State.
I further direct the State to release the arrears after making the calculations as stated in the terms of my judgment, within two months from the receipt of the copy of this order, failing which the State shall pay interest to the plaintiff at the rate of 18 per cent on the amount due to the plaintiff.
Appeal dismissed.
