3.
In Civil Writ Petition Nos. 4844 of 2004 titled Duni Chand Walia v. The State of Punjab and another and 14834 of 2006 titled Mohinder Parkash v. State of Punjab and others, the employees of the Board have been paid gratuity under the Civil Services Rules and they have sought directions to the Board to make the payment of gratuity under the Act of 1972.
4.
Since the question involved in all the above-mentioned Civil Writ Petitions is whether the employees of the Board are entitled to receive gratuity according to the Act of 1972 or under the Civil Services Rules, as such all these Civil Writ Petitions are being disposed of by this common judgment.
The facts have, however, been extracted from Civil Writ Petition No. 5126 of 2004 titled Balwant Singh and others v. State of Punjab and another.
5.
As mentioned above, the controversy in the present lis is whether the employees of Punjab State Electricity Board ( in short the Board) are entitled to gratuity under the Act of 1972 or are entitled to gratuity under the Civil Services Rules.
Notification dated 812004 passed by respondent No. 1 exempting the Board from payment of gratuity under the Act of 1972 has been challenged.
6.
It is pleaded that employees of Board were receiving gratuity under the Act of 1972 from the day it had come into force.
Prior to the enforcement of Act of 1972, the employees of the Board were governed by the provisions of Civil Services Rules for payment of gratuity.
The Board in 1996 applied for exemption from payment of gratuity.
It was declined by the Government on the ground that the gratuity payable to the employees under the Act of 1972 was more beneficial to the employees than gratuity payable under the Civil Services Rules, vide Annexure P-1.
Thereafter, many cases were filed by the Board claiming that Act of 1972 was not applicable on the Board but the Board lost all the cases up to the Hon'ble Apex Court which held that Act of 1972 was applicable on the Board.
The Board again vide letter dated 1712002 asked for exemption from payment of gratuity.
The Worker Unions of the Board were not informed and the State Government vide order dated 612003 exempted the Board from payment of gratuity under the Act of 1972.
At the request of Board, the Government of Punjab made amendment in the notification dated 612003 so as to include gratuity or pensionary benefits instead of gratuity alone and fresh notification dated 812004 was issued by Government of Punjab substituting notification dated 612003.
The Government, vide amended notification dated 812004, Annexure P-4, was of the opinion that employees of establishments of Board who were in receipt of gratuity or pensionary benefits under the Punjab Civil Services Rules, Chapter II, is not less favourable than the benefits conferred under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 and in exercise of powers conferred by sub-section (1) and (2) of Section 5 of the Act of 1972, the Punjab Government was pleased to exempt the Board and its employees from the operation of the provisions of the Act of 1972 with effect from 17th January, 1972 the date on which Act of 1972 came into force in the State of Punjab.
Vide notification, Annexure P-5, the State of Punjab clarified that the payment made by the establishment to its employees under the Act of 1972 will not be recovered.
The order dated 812004 exempting the Board from payment of gratuity under the Act of 1972 has been challenged on the following grounds :-  (i) That the State Government is not competent to issue notification exempting the Board from payment of gratuity under the Act of 1972 as the Board has branches in more than one State and for that purpose the appropriate Government is the Central Government and not the Punjab Government, in view of Section 2(a) of the Act of 1972.
(ii) That the State Government has given exemption with retrospective effect which had adversely affected the employees of the Board prejudicially.
(iii) That the gratuity payable under the Act of 1972 is more beneficial than the gratuity payable under the Punjab Civil Services Rules and according to Section 5(3), no notification under Section 5(1)(2) can be issued retrospectively prejudicially affecting the interest of any person.
7.
In reply to the above noted submissions, the stand taken by the Board is that the Board has no branches outside the State of Punjab.
The Board is financed and controlled by the State of Punjab.
The regular employees of the Board are governed by the Main Service Rules/Punjab Civil Services Rules.
The Board applied to the Government of Punjab which is appropriate Government seeking exemption from applicability of provisions of Act of 1972.
The Government of Punjab, after considering the fact that pensionary benefits under the Main Services Rules/Punjab Civil Services Rules are more beneficial to the employees than those under the Act of 1972 has exempted the Board from operation of Act of 1972 vide notification dated 612003.
Subsequently, the Government of Punjab, in exercise of powers conferred in it under Section 5(1)(2)(3) of the Act of 1972 granted exemption to the respondent No. 2 vide notification dated 812004 from the operation of the provisions of Act of 1972, with retrospective effect i.e. from the date of commencement of the Act.
The Government of Punjab, later on, made an addition in the notification to the effect that the amount already paid to the employees under the Act of 1972 shall not be recovered from them.
It was further clarified that respondent No. 2, Board has its offices within the State of Punjab and Chandigarh which is the capital of State of Punjab and does not have any office in any State other than Punjab.
However, the Board has a factory (Hydro Electric Power Project) namely Shanan Power House in Joginder Nagar in Himachal Pradesh.
The Labour Department of the Government of Himachal Pradesh has registered it and granted a licence to work as a factory under the Factories Act, 1948 and the rules made thereunder.
The Board has Guest Houses in Chandigarh and Delhi but these Guest Houses are only meant for the use of the officials of the Board and not profit making and as such are not covered by the definition of 'establishment' or 'shop'.
Some administrative offices are in Chandigarh but that is the capital of Punjab.
The Board has supported the exemption made to it from payment of gratuity under the Act of 1972 vide notification dated 812004.
8.
We have heard both sides and have carefully gone through the record of the case.
9.
To fortify their first contention raised above, learned counsel for the employees of the Board has relied upon authority of Division Bench of our own High Court in Civil Writ Petition No 12419 of 2005, titled Punjab State Electricity Board and others v. The Regional Labour Commissioner and others, and on the strength of same, it is argued that appropriate Government for issuance of notification under the Act of 1972 is the Central Government and not the State Government as the Board has different offices in whole of India.
It is further submitted that the appeal preferred by the Punjab State Electricity Board was also dismissed by the Apex Court in Special Leave to Appeal (Civil) No. 24971 of 2005.
10.
We have considered the above submissions and have gone through the judgment in Civil Writ Petition No 12419 of 2005.
However, from the perusal of the said judgment, it is revealed that the counsel for the Board has not contested the fact that the Board had branches in more than one State.
It was held that appropriate Government under Section 2 of the Act of 1972 is the Central Government.
However, the Board preferred an appeal i.e. Special Leave to Appeal (Civil) No. 24971 of 2005 and the Hon'ble Apex Court has kept the question of law open.
So, the question whether the appropriate Government for grant of exemption under Section 5 of the Act of 1972 is the State Government or the Central Government is still open in view of order passed by the Hon'ble Apex Court.
11.
Mr. Mattewal submitted that the Punjab State Electricity Board can operate only within the State of Punjab.
It has no power to operate in other State.
It is further submitted that every State Government has its own Electricity Board to regulate the supply of electricity and no Board of one State can operate within the jurisdiction of other State.
It has been further submitted that so far as Shanan Power House situated in Himachal Pradesh is concerned, that has been registered under the Factories Act and the Act of 1972 is applicable to the employees of that factory.
That Project cannot be said to be a branch of the Board but has separate entity under the Factories Act.
It is further submitted that the Guest Houses situated in Chandigarh and Delhi do not fall within the ambit of having a branch office in any other State.
So, it is submitted that the appropriate Government in the case of Board is the State of Punjab and not the Central Government.
12.
To properly appreciate the above-said fact, the relevant provisions of Section 2(a) are reproduced hereunder :-  "2.
Definitions.
- In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires, - (a) "appropriate Government" means, - (i) in relation to an establishment - (a) belonging to, or under the control of, the Central Government.
(b) having branches in more than one State, (c) of a factory belonging to, or under the control of, the Central Government, (d) of a major port, mine, oil field or railway company, the Central Government.
(ii) in any other case, the State Government; 13.
From the bare perusal of above provisions of law, it is revealed that in case an establishment has branches in more than one State, in that case, the appropriate Government is Central Government and in all other cases, under Section 2(a)(ii), the State Government is the appropriate Government.
Every State has its own Electricity Board to regulate the supply of electricity.
No Electricity Board can operate within the jurisdiction of other State .Mere purchasing electricity from different Boards for supplying the same to the residents of Punjab, cannot be said to be having branch offices in different States.
Having some Guest Houses at Delhi or any other State does not lead to the conclusion that the Board has its branches in more than one State.
The word having branches has to be construed legally.
An establishment can be said to have a branch office if that establishment is operating or doing any business from that State.
There is nothing on the file to warrant the conclusion that Punjab State Electricity Board is operating in any other State for the supply of electricity.
No other State would allow the Punjab State Electricity Board to operate.
14.
So far as the office of Shanan Power House situated in Himachal Pradesh is concerned, that cannot be said to be a branch office of the Board as it is registered under the Factories Act.
So, it cannot be said that the Board has branch office in different States.
So, we have no hesitation in holding that appropriate Government for issuance of notification under Section 5 of the Act, 1972 is State Government and not the Central Government.
15.
So far as point Nos. 2 and 3 raised by the petitioners are concerned, they are inter-connected and are being taken up together.
In this context, the counsel for writ petitioners have submitted that the order cannot be passed retrospectively and that since the payment of gratuity under the Act of 1972 is beneficial than the Civil Services Rules, as such in view of Section 5(3) of the Act of 1972, no order prejudicial to the employees can be passed.
16.
To properly appreciate the said contention, relevant provisions of Section 5 of the Act of 1972 are reproduced as under :-  "5.
Power to exempt.
- (1) The appropriate Government may, by notification, and subject to such conditions as may be specified in the notification, exempt any establishment, factory, mine, oilfield, plantation, port, railway company or shop to which this Act applies from the operation of the provisions of this Act, if in the opinion of the appropriate Government, the employees in such establishment factory, mine, oilfield, plantation, port, railway company or shop are in receipt of gratuity or pensionary benefits not less favourable than the benefits conferred under this Act.
(2) The appropriate Government may, by notification and subject to such conditions as may be specified in the notification, exempt any employee or class of employees employed in any establishment, factory, mine, oilfield, plantation, port, railway company or shop to which this Act applies from the operation of the provisions of this Act, if, in the opinion of the appropriate Government, such employee or class of employees are in receipt of gratuity or pensionary benefits not less favourable than the benefits conferred under this Act.
(3) A notification issued under sub-section (1) or sub-section (2) may be issued retrospectively a date not earlier than the date of commencement of this Act, but no such notification shall be issued so as to prejudicially, affect the interests of any person."
17.
So, from bare reading of the provisions of law, it is crystal clear that exemption can be issued retrospectively but order prejudicial to the employees cannot be passed.
However, from a bare perusal of the Act, it is revealed that while determining the fact regarding the receipt of gratuity or pensionary benefit has to be taken into account.
In case, the payment of gratuity under the Civil Services Rules and pension to the Board employees is taken together, in that case, the same is beneficial for the employees than the gratuity payable under the Act of 1972.
So, the factum of pension has to be taken into account while making the payment of gratuity to the employees.
18.
So far as reliance of petitioners on notification, Annexure P-1, vide which the Government has opined that the payment of gratuity under the Punjab Civil Services Rules is not more beneficial than gratuity to be paid under the Act of 1972 is concerned, the Government, at that time, has not considered the factum of pension payable to the Board Employees.
However, when Government of Punjab has considered the effect of payment of pension and gratuity together under Civil Services Rules and then realized that the employees of the Board are benefited by making payment of gratuity under Civil Services Rules.
Notification, Annexure P-3, was issued keeping in view the beneficial interest of Board Employees.
The payment of gratuity under the Act of 1972 is meant to employees in any establishment, factory, mine, oil field, plantation, port, railway company or shop as envisaged by Section 5(1) of the Act.
Generally, those employees do not get pension and only gratuity is paid to the employees of those establishments.
It is an admitted case of the parties that the conditions of services of employees of the Board are governed by Civil Services Rules, Chapter II.
Board Employees get pension only on account of applicability of Civil Services Rules.
19.
So far reliance on authority Municipal Corporation of Delhi v. Dharam Prakash Sharma and another, reported in 1999(2) SCT 297 : 1998 II LLJ 15 is concerned, that authority is distinguishable as in that case, the appropriate Government has not exempted the establishment from operation of the Act.
20.
The other contention raised by the Board employees that it cannot be made operative retrospectively is concerned, from bare perusal of Section 5(3) of the Act of 1972, it is clear that the Government has been empowered to issue the notification exempting the establishment from operation of Act of 1972 but not earlier than the date of the commencement of this Act.
The vires of that provision of the Act have not been challenged.
Moreover, as discussed above, if the cumulative effect of pensionary benefits and payment of gratuity under the Civil Services Rules is taken together, in that case, the same is favourable to the Board Employees than the simple payment of gratuity under the Act of 1972.
21.
The State Government has taken into account that the Act may not be prejudicial to the Board employees and, on that count, it has been clearly laid down in Annexure P-5 that the payment already made to the employees shall not be recovered.
So, due protection has been given to the Board employees who have already received the amount.
22.
The Controlling Authority under the Act of 1972 has ordered the payment of gratuity.
In some cases, the said amounts have been deposited with the Controlling Authority but have not been paid to the Board Employees due to pendency of present writ petitions.
Since we have categorically held that the notification dated 812004, is legal, valid and in accordance with the provisions of Section 5 of the Act of 1972, as such the amount, if any deposited by the Board shall be refunded to the Board.
23.
In authority reported as Beed District Central Co-op.
Bank Ltd v. State of Maharashtra and others, 2006(4) SCT 564 (SC), it has been laid down that the employee has to opt for one of the Scheme or the Statute.
The Board employee can get gratuity under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 or can opt for payment of gratuity and pension under the Civil Services Rules and they cannot opt for payment of gratuity under the Act of 1972 and payment of pension under Civil Services Rules.
24.
In authority reported as Janak Prasad Singh v. Bihar State Electricity Board, 2004-II, LJJ 1080, it has been held that power to exempt establishment from the Act depends on two conditions (a) benefit available to employees not less favourable than benefit under the Act and retrospective exemption, if any, not to cause prejudice to interest of any person.
In that case, it was held that since both the conditions were fulfilled, the Act of 1972 , was not applicable and the employees of the Board will be governed by Bihar Pension Rules.
Claim for payment of gratuity was held to be not sustainable under the Act of 1972.
