Satyabrata Sinha, J - This appeal is directed against a judgment and order dated 31st August, 1988 passed by a learned Single Judge of this Court in Matter No. 843 of 1986, whereby and whereunder the said learned Judge allowed the writ application filed by the Ist respondent herein questioning the order of dismissal dated 18th January, 1986.
2.
The admitted fact of the matter lie in a very narrow compass.
3.
The petitioner was appointed as a Lighting Inspector by the appellant Corporation on 181967.
He was placed under suspension pending initiation of departmental proceedings.
On 981975 questioning the said order he filed a writ application and by a judgment dated 24.8.77 this Court allowed the said writ application upon directing the appellant corporation to permit the writ petitioner to join his post with all benefits in terms of the Rule 55A of the Service Regulations framed by the appellant.
The first respondent, however, was again placed under suspension on 16/17th March, 1981.
The following articles of charges were framed against him :-  "Article-I.
That the said Shri Santosh Kumar Nath while serving as Lighting Inspector under the Lighting Department Corporation of Calcutta was in direct charge of supervision and maintenance of electric installations of the SS Hogg Market.
In course of his official business it has been revealed on inquiry that the said Shri Santosh Kumar Nath during the period from March 1980 to November 1980 used to erase, interpolate or tamper the records kept for electrical charges to be paid by the stall holders of SS Hogg Market unauthorisedly which is not only irregular but also is tantamount to gross violation of departmental rules and misconduct.
Article-II.
That the said Shri Santosh Kumar Nath while functioning as Lighting Inspector under the Lighting Department was in direct charges of supervision and maintenance of the electric installations of the SS Hogg Market deliberately replaced the actual bills for the electric charges by bills of lesser amount during the material period in question causing loss to Corporation fund.
The said Shri Santosh Kumar Nath thus acted in a manner which was highly unbecoming of a civic servant and this conduct and mis- behaviour was thus most objectionable as unworthy of a man of trust and the act on his part was an act of mis-appropriation of Corporation Revenue during the material period in question constituting gross irregularity in the discharge of the official duties with a dishonest motive, breach of trust and misconduct.
Article-III.
That the said Shri Santosh Kumar Nath while servicing as Lighting Inspector under the Lighting Department, Corporation of Calcutta, has acquired assets disproportionate to his known sources of income.
Article-IV.
That Shri Santosh Kumar Nath, Inspector Lighting Department, Corporation of Calcutta, while in direct charge of supervision and maintenance of electrical installation of SS Hong Market, has acquired Stall No. Writer-I at SS Hogg Market since 21679 in the "Benami" of his brother-in- law Shri Tanmoy Nath and engaged himself in carrying on trade of typewriting in the said Stall in contravention the Official rules, discipline and procedures which is unbecoming of a civic servant."
4.
He submitted a statement of defence and also was given inspection of documents.
Although, disciplinary proceeding was pending, a writ petition was filed by the delinquent officer which was marked as Matter No. 589 of 1982, wherein he again prayed for quashing of the order of suspension.
By an order dated 861982 a learned Single Judge of this Court issued certain directions.
The petitioner was ultimately dismissed from service on 16111982.
The petitioner filed another writ application questioning the said order of dismissal without availing the alternative remedy as provided for under Section 21(4) of the Calcutta Municipal Corporation Act, 1980.
The said matter was marked as Matter No. 163 of the 1983.
By an order dated 16.6.1983 the learned Single Judge of this Court set aside the enquiry report and consequently set aside the order of dismissal allegedly on the ground that the Despatch Book and the Meter Reading Book were not made available before the conclusion of the enquiry proceeding.
The matter was remanded back to the Enquiry Officer with a direction to proceed with enquiry after giving the petitioner an opportunity to inspect the said documents.
Yet again a writ petition was filed by the writ petitioner on 1191984 which was marked as Matter No. 1225 of 1984 with a prayer for quashing the order of suspension and the charge sheet on the ground that the aforementioned order dated 16.6.1983 had not been complied with.
By an order dated 1011985 the appellant Corporation was again directed to give inspection of the said documents i.e. only those documents whereupon the Corporation intends to rely on.
The writ petitioner preferred an appeal against the said order and by a judgment and order dated 29885 the appeal was disposed of with the following directions:-  (i) Mr. SB Dasgupta, Chief Municipal Officer was appointed Enquiry Officer: (ii) Enquiry Officer will decide as to whether writ petitioner was entitled to have inspection of documents asked for by him; (iii) Documents not given inspection so far Calcutta Municipal Corporation is entitled to say that such inspection was not relevant or the same was lost or unavailable.
Dispute, if raised, will be decided by the Enquiry Officer with a reasoned order; and (iv) Enquiry Officer will conclude the proceedings by 31121985, if possible."
5.
The Enquiry Officer pursuant to or in furtherance of the said direction of the Division Bench submitted his report on 31121985 whereupon the disciplinary authority passed an order of removal on 18.1.1986.
As indicated hereinbefore, the learned Single Judge allowed the writ petition principally on the question that the appellant had not complied with the direction about the documents which were relied upon by the appellant Corporation during the preliminary enquiry and the order of dismissal was also vitiated as the Municipal Service Commission had not been consulted as was mandatorily required under Section 21(5) of the Calcutta Municipal Corporation Act, 1980.
Mr. LC Bihani, learned counsel appearing on behalf of the appellant, inter alia, submitted that the question of supplying of any documents which were not relied upon by the Corporation, does not arise.
According to the learned Counsel it is not the case of the writ petitioner Ist respondent that the documents relied upon by the Corporation before the Enquiry Officer had not been given inspection of and in that view of the matter the principles of natural justice had been fully complied with.
The learned Counsel contends that the writ petitioner was, therefore, not prejudiced at all.
Reliance in this connection has been placed on Chandrama Tewari v. Union of India reported in AIR 1988 Supreme Court page 117.
As regard the second ground stated by the learned trial judge the learned Counsel submits the provision as contained in Section 21(5) of the Calcutta Municipal Corporation Act being a provision relating to consultation with another authority, the same is directory in nature and in support of the aforementioned contention strong reliance has been placed on a recent decision of the Apex Court in State of AP and another v. Dr. Rahimuddin Kamal reported in Supreme Court Cases 1997 Vol. No. 3 page 505 : 1997(2) SCT 25 (SC)..  6.
Mr. PK Roy, learned Counsel appearing on behalf of the writ petitioner/Ist respondent on the other hand took us through the judgment and submitted that from a perusal thereof it would appear that there has been no allegation that either the meter reading was wrong or the bills had not been correctly prepared.
The learned Counsel in this connection has drawn our attention to the duties which were required to be performed by the delinquent Officer.
The learned counsel further submits that so far as the charge No. 3 is concerned, in view of the concession made by the learned Counsel appearing on behalf of the appellant that the Enquiry Officer was not competent to go into the question of benami, the same cannot be permitted to be raised in this appeal.
As regard the second contention, the learned Counsel submits that the interpretation of a provision of a statute has to be read in the context thereof.
The learned Counsel submits that the Supreme Court in its decision in State of AP (supra) has mechanically followed the decision in State of UP v. Manbodhan Lal Srivastava reported in AIR 1975 SC 912, although the said decision has subsequently been explained in Narayana Sankaran Mooss v. State of Kerala and another reported in AIR 1974 Supreme Court 175.
Mr. Roy in particular has drawn our attention to paragraphs 20 and 21 of the aforementioned judgment.
7.
Keeping in view the rival contentions as noticed herein before, in our opinion two questions arise for consideration in this appeal:-  (1) Whether the learned Trial Judge was correct in coming to the conclusion that the impugned order of dismissal stands vitiated by reason of non- complaince of principles of natural justice; (2) Whether the provisions of Section 21(5) of the Calcutta Municipal Corporation Act is mandatory in nature.
8.
It is a well settled principle of law that High Court in exercise of its jurisdiction under Article 226 of the Constitution of India, would not ordinarily reappreciate the evidence.
The Court will consider the materials placed before the Enquiry Officer only in a case if the findings arrived at by the Enquiry Officer are questioned on the ground of perversity.
It is also well settled that the Court while exercising its power of judicial review is not concerned with sufficiency of evidence but is only concerned with illegality, irrationality and procedural irregularity in the decision making process.
In other words, the Court is not concerned with the merit of the decision.
9.
It was not and could not have been the case of the writ-petitioner Ist respondent that he had not been given an opportunity to inspect the documents upon which reliance has been placed by the appellant Corporation.
If for the purpose of arriving at a preliminary finding as to whether a disciplinary proceeding should be initiated against the Officer, the employer conducts a preliminary investigation, the materials relied upon therein are not relevant when a full-fledged enquiry is initiated against him.
The records clearly show that the Enquiry Officer considered the entire materials on records including the stand taken by the first respondent.
In his statement of defence, he admitted that he was entrusted with the work of meter reading and preparation of bill.
The question as to whether he was guilty of causing any erasing interpolation or tampering with the official records connected with his official duties and responsibility was the subject matter of enquiry.
The Enquiry Officer in his report, as contained in Annexure H to the writ petition, has dealt with all the materials on records and analysed the same.
Apart therefrom, we may also note that in various minutes of the meetings held in the chambers of the Enquiry Officer he had dealt in details as to how and in what manner the inspection of documents had been sought for and was granted and/or refused on the ground that the same was not relevant.
10.
It has not been stated before this Court as to how and in what manner the writ petitioner has been prejudiced by non-grant of inspection of any documents.
Although the principle of natural justice is embedded in an enquiry proceedings which would include a fair procedure, there cannot be any doubt whatsoever that the same is not a rigid rule.
Substantial compliance of a procedural provision would serve the purpose and would meet the ends of justice.
If despite such substantial compliance, the order of punishment is questioned on the ground of the non-compliance of the principle of natural justice it is incumbent on the delinquent officer to show that he has been prejudiced thereby.
Reference in this connection is made to State Bank of Patiala v. SK Sharma reported in AIR 1996 Supreme Court page 1669 : 1996(2) SCT 568 (SC) equivalent to 1996(3) SCC page 364.
Unfortunately the learned Trial Judge has usurped the jurisdiction of the Enquiry Officer and the disciplinary authority.
The learned Trial Judge, in our considered view, committed an error in coming to the conclusion that there has been a violation of principles of natural justice without taking into consideration the Enquiry Report.
11.
It was not within the domain of this Court to analyse the evidence so as to arrive at a finding that the charges 1 and 2 has not been proved.
As indicated herein before, the Enquiry Officer has analysed the materials on record in great details and came to the conclusion that the Corporation had produced various materials to show that the charges against him had been proved.
Upon consideration of the said documents in paragraphs 8, 9 and 10 of the said report the Enquiry Officer had come to the conclusion that the delinquent was guilty of the said charge.
While arriving at the said finding, the Enquiry Officer has also taken into consideration the fact that the delinquent himself has clarified his duty as Lighting Inspector attached to SS Hogg Market.
Such being the position, it cannot be said that the findings arrived at by the learned Enquiry Officer who was an independent Officer and having been appointed by this court was perverse in any manner whatsoever.
12.
So far as the charge No. 3 of Article of charges is concerned the learned Trial Judge held as follows :-  "Article 111 being the charges alleging that the petitioner acquired assets disproportionate to his own source of income proceeds on the basis that the property which stands in the name of the wife of the petitioner being a three-storeyed house with six flats approximately really belongs to the petitioner and the petitioner could not disclose the source of income of his wife.
It is well settled that the person who alleged benami is to prove the same.
In any event whether the property is a benami property or not can be declared by a Court of law and not by the municipal authority or by Enquiry Officer appointed by the Muncipal Authority.
The learned Advocate for the Calcutta Municipal Corporation also concedes that this charge cannot be sustained for the reasons as aforesaid."
Enquiry Officer while dealing with Charge No. 3 considered the defence of the Ist respondent to the effect that the property belonged to his wife.
It was observed as follows :-  "In course of argument the delinquent wanted to make me understand that his wife had her own business in electrical goods, that she received money from her mother, that she utilised her 'Srtidhan' property and that he (delinquent) personally took loan of Rs. 38,000/- from the employer and helped his wife to complete the construction.
The delinquent was not agreeable to accept that rupees three lakhs would be the estimated cost of construction of three- storeyed house with six flats within the jurisdiction of Municipality of Behala which has since come within the jurisdiction of C.M.C.
The building was raised some time in 1978-79.
The cost of construction of a three-storeyed building with six flats in any body's guess.
Since the delinquent did not claim ownership of the building.
I repeatedly asked him as to whether he was willing to give evidence, either oral or documentary, to prove that his wife had sufficient means to raise a three-storeyed house with six flats at the relevant time.
I asked the delinquent if he was willing to produce papers to show that his wife runs a shop in electric goods and the statement of accounts so maintained in course of the business.
The answer of the delinquent was that since the business is owned by his wife he is incompetent to produce any papers in their connection.
As to the liquid money allegedly received by his wife from her mother the delinquent gave the same answer.
He was not even prepared to give evidence that he gave her loan of Rs. 38,000/- to help his wife in raising the building.
In fact, the delinquent took up the stand that his wife might be competent to and disclose and prove her source of income in the construction.
He wanted to utilise the return filed by his wife before the Income Tax Department as evidence as to the ownership of the property.
Since the delinquent preferred not to claim ownership of the property standing in the name of his wife, it is necessary to see whether charge in Article 111 in the matter of alleged acquisition of property disproportionate to known source of income has been proved."
13.
Thereafter the enquiry officer considered the materials on records.
It also stand admitted that the delinquent officer made contribution towards the construction of the building.
14.
The appellant in ground Nos. 6 and 7 of the grounds of appeal has, inter alia, questioned the statement recorded by the learned Trial Judge to the effect that the learned Counsel appearing on behalf of the appellant had conceded that there existed the question of benami and the same could not have been gone in by the Inquiry Officer.
Whether such a concession was in fact made or not is not for us to consider but there cannot be any doubt whatsoever that when a wanton concession is made, the party will not be bound thereby.
Reference in this connection may be made to Union of India and anr. v. Babu Singh and ors. reported in 1996(1) Supreme Court Cases page 477 : 1996(3) R.C.R.
(Civil) 195, in any event the question as to whether a delinquent officer can be charged for having assets disproportionate to his known source of income, when a plea of benami is raised, is a pure question of law.
15.
In a departmental preceding, the provisions of the Evidence Act have no application.
16.
Apart from the fact that at the relevant time the Benami Transactions (Prohibition) Act was not in force, the law, as it then stood, clearly postulated that persons in India frequently acquire properties in the name of their wives or sons even for no ostensible reason.
A plea was taken by the first respondent to the effect that the property in question, which was a three-storeyed building, was in fact constructed from the stridhan of his wife and he had also contributed therefor, it was, thus, incumbent on his part to prove the said fact.
In any event, he could not have refused to bring the materials on records and in particular to have examined himself or his wife.
It may be true that the burden of proof was on the appellant Corporation but the question as to whether the said burden has been discharged or not is essentially a question of fact and the findings on such a question would vary from case to case.
In a disciplinary proceeding, the standard of proof is not that a charge has to be proved beyond any shadow of doubt but the rule is that such a charge may be proved by applying the doctrine of preponderance of probability.
17.
In that view of the matter, we are of the considered opinion that it cannot be said that Enquiry Officer was precluded from going into the said charges or the said charges were illegal or invalid on the face of it.
In any event, as noticed hereinbefore, the petitioner had filed a writ application on earlier occasions questioning the finding of the Enquiry Officer on the aforementioned charges as also the order of dismissal.
It is not his case that the aforementioned question was earlier raised.
If he had to raised the said question, which he could and ought to have raised in the earlier writ application, in our opinion, he could not have been permitted to raise the said question once over again.
Keeping in view the materials on record brought on by the parties before the Enquiry Officer and his report on the basis thereof, we are satisfied that the third charge could not have been brushed aside by the learned trial Judge only on the said ground, and particularly in view of the fact that the question as to whether the properties were purchased by his wife from her stridhan property or not was within the special knowledge of the delinquent officer.
18.
Let us now consider the question of interpretation of Section 21 of the Calcutta Muncipal Corporation Act.
It deals with control, discipline and conduct of the Officers and employees.
Sub-section (2) of Section 21 provides for framing of regulations relating to discipline, control and conduct of the employees of the Corporation.
It is accepted that such a regulation has been framed.
Sub-section (3) of Section 21 prohibits the Corporation from passing any order of reduction of any rank, removal or dismissal for any breach of departmental rules or of discipline or for negligence of duties or for other misconduct of the authority by a person other than by whom such officer or employee has been appointed.
Sub-section (4) of Section 21 provides for an appeal.
Sub-section (5) which is material for this appeal, reads thus :-  "S. 21.
Control, Discipline and Conduct of officers and employees.
- (5) No officer or employee of the Corporation appointed on the recommendation of the Municipal Service Commission (or the Public Service Commission, as the case may be,) shall be reduced in rank, removed or dismissed except after consultation with such Commission."
19.
In State of AP and Anr. v. Dr. Rahimuddin Kamal, 1997(3) SCC 505 the relevant rule interpretation of which arose for consideration read thus :-  "We affirm an employee of the Corporation appointed on the recommendation of the Municipal Service Commission or the Public Service Commission, as the case may be, shall not be reduced in rank, removed or dismissed except, after consultation with such Commission."

(2) ...
But before taking a decision, the Government shall consult the Andhra Pradesh Vigilance Commission."
20.
The Apex Court considered the phraseology used in the said Rule upon comparison thereof with Clause 3 of the Article 320 of the Constitution of India which reads thus :-  "The (sic) Union Public Service Commission, as the case may be, shall be consulted."
21.
There cannot be any doubt whatsoever as has been submitted by Mr. Roy that a provision of Act has to be construed in the light of the context thereof.
The Rules of purposive and contextual interpretation should be resorted to in interpreting the provisions in question upon taking into consideration the contextual matrix.
In other words provision of a statute has to be interpreted keeping in view the text and context thereof.
22.
The Supreme Court in Narayana Sankaran Mooss v. State of Kerala and another reported in AIR 1974 Supreme Court page 175 has clearly distinguished its earlier decision in State of UP v. Manbodhan Lal Srivastava reported in AIR 1957 SC 912, upon which reliance has been placed by the Apex Court in paragraph 21 which reads thus :-  "Counsel for the Board has relied on 1958 SCR 533: AIR 1957 SC 912 (supra), Ram Gopal Chaturvedi v. State of Madhya Pradesh, 1970 SCR 472 : AIR 1970 SC 158 and State of Bombay v. DA Korgaonkar, Civil Appeal No. 289 of 1958 D/d.
65.1960 (SC).
He has also relied on Rallo v. Minister of Town and Country Planning, 1948 (1) AII ER 13 and Derham v. Church Commissioners for England, 1954 AC 245 : (1970) 1 SCR 472 : AIR 1970 SC 158 (supra) and Civil App.
No. 289 of 1958 D/d.
65.1960 (supra) have followed Manbodhan Lal Srivastava (supra).
In Manbodhan Lal, 1958 SCR 533 : AIR 1957 SC 912 this Court held that Art. 320(c) of the Constitution is directory and not mandatory.
A Government employee was dismissed from service after complying with the provisions of Art. 311(2) of the Constitution.
The UP Public Service Commission was consulted as to the punishment to be imposed on him.
But it was consulted before the explanation of the employee was received by the Government.
The argument was that as the Commission did not have the opportunity of considering his explanation, there was no real consultation as required by Art. 320(c).
It was also argued that Art. 320(c) was mandatory and that accordingly the order of dismissal was void.
This Court pointed out several reasons for the view that Art. 320(c) is directory.
The proviso to Art. 320 empowers the appropriate Govt. to issue directions as to the classes of cases in which consultation of the Commission will not be necessary.
The proviso therefore indicates that the provision is directory.
Another reason given by the Court is that Art. 320(c) does not occur in the Chapter in which Art. 311 occurs.
It finds place in the Chapter dealing with the Public Service Commission.
Accordingly it cannot be said that it confers any right on Government employees.
The third reason given is that consultation of the Commission is not binding on the Government.
The first two reasons do not apply in our case.
We have shown earlier that the condition of consulting the Board has been inserted by an amendment of Section 4 with the object of creating a safeguard in favour of the licensee.
There is no provision in the Act authorising the Government to waive the condition of consultation in any case.
It is true that the third reason given by the Court in Manbodhan Lal 1958 SCR 533 (supra) applies in this case.
As there, so here the opinion of the Board is not binding on the Government.
In spite of the Board advising against revocation, the Government may, if satisfied that it is necessary to revoke the licence, may revoke it.
But having regard to the object and context of Section 4, we are of the opinion that it should not be regarded as an over-weighing consideration.
It will normally be difficult for the Government to ignore the Board's expert advice.
We are satisfied from the object and context of Section 4 that Parliament intended to make consultation of the Board an imperative condition to revoking a licence."

In the instant case, however, Section 21 of the Calcutta Municipal Corporation Act does not deal with the modalities of recruitment nor does it deal with the modalities of the dismissal, removal or reduction in rank of service.
The provisions of Section 21 of the said Act cannot, therefore, be treated to be similar to Rule 4(2) of the DPT Rule as was in the case of Dr. Rahimuddin Kamal or Clause 3 of Article 320 of the Constitution of India.
Sub-section 5 of Section 21 of the said Act must be read as an exception to sub-section 3 thereof.
It uses negative words whereas both Rule 4(2) of DPT Rules and Clause 3 of Article 320 of Constitution of India are couched in positive language.
The distinction in the phraseology in the aforementioned provision is absolutely clear and unequivocal.
Use of such negative words as is well known must be held to be mandatory in nature.

24.
For the aforementioned reasons, there cannot be any doubt that it was incumbent on the part of the appellant-Corporation to consult the Municipal Service Commission before an order of dismissal could be passed.
25.
Although in view of our findings aforementioned, the order of dismissal has to be set aside but keeping in view the peculiar facts and circumstances of the case and particularly the gravity of charges levelled against the petitioner, we direct that the question of payment of back wages will depend upon the ultimate decision of the Municipal Service Commission.
Such a direction given by this Court in exercise of its jurisdiction under Article 226 of the Constitution of India can neither be denied nor disputed.
This court is not only a court of law but also a Court of equity.
The Court, in our considered opinion, may sometimes refuse to give entire reliefs to which the writ petitioner Ist respondent might have been entitled to in law.
In this connection we may refer to a decision of the Apex Court in M/s Orissa Cement Ltd v. State of Orissa reported in AIR 1991 Supreme Court page 1717 wherein the law is stated as follows :-  "It is well-settled proposition that it is open to the Court to grant, mould or restrict the relief in a manner most appropriate to the situation before it in such a way as to advance the interests of justice.
It will be appreciated that it is not always possible in all situations to give a logical and complete effect to a finding.
Many situations of this type arise in actual practice.
For instance, there are cases where a Court comes to the conclusion that the termination of the services of an employee is invalid, yet it refrains from giving him the benefit of "reinstatement" (i.e. continuity in service) or "back wages".
In such cases, the direction of the Court does result in a person being denied the benefits that should flow to him as a logical consequence of a declaration in his favour.
26.
It is stated that a sum of Rs. 1,20,000/- and odd together with the interest accrued thereon had been deposited with the Registrar (AS).
The appellant shall be entitled to withdraw the said amount subject to the condition that in view of our aforementioned direction in the event the Commission disapproves the order of dismissal passed by the appellant Corporation, in that event, the entire sum by way of arrears shall become payable to the Ist respondent by the appellant.
27.
This appeal is, therefore, disposed of with the aforementioned directions but in the facts and circumstances of this case, there shall be no order as to costs.
