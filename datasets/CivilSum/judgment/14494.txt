GS Singhvi, J - This petition has been filed for quashing of order, Annexure P-1, dated 24th of January, 1994 passed by respondent No. 1 under rule 5(ix) of the Punjab Civil Services (Punishment and Appeal) Rules, 1970 dismissing the petitioner from service.
2.
The petitioner joined service on 19111970 as Sectional Officer in Public Works Department.
Later on he was designated as Junior Engineer.
While he was posted at Nabha in the year 1979, the petitioner was placed under suspension.
A departmental inquiry was held against him.
The inquiry was entrusted to Shri BR Bansal, Superintending Engineer, Public Health.
As a result of the inquiry, order dated 9101987 issued by the Government in the name of the President of India and penalty of stoppage of four grade increments without cumulative effect was imposed on the petitioner in addition to punishment of recovery of Rs. 3,32,86108, for the financial loss caused to the Government.
The petitioner did not file any appeal or review against the order of punishment.
However, after about two years and two months the Government suo moto reviewed the order dated 9101987 and vide its order dated 2511990, the Government withdrew the order dated 9101987 but without prejudice to a fresh order which could be passed after holding fresh inquiry or serving fresh charge-sheet upon the petitioner.
Thereafter, the Chief Engineer, Public Health, Patiala issued a notice dated 2121992 and while furnishing a copy of the inquiry report prepared by Shri BR Bansal, the Chief Engineer called upon the petitioner to submit his explanation within one month.
The petitioner submitted a reply dated 651992 and pleaded that after withdrawal of the order of punishment, no fresh order could be made.
This was followed by the impugned order dated 2411994, whereby the Government imposed the penalty of dismissal from service upon the petitioner.
3.
When the case was taken up for hearing on 6121995, the Court noted that the petition as well as the reply were wholly laconic because either side did not produce copies of the orders dated 9101987 and 2511990 and further that the copy of the inquiry report and show cause notice served upon the petitioner had also not been produced before the Court.
Therefore, the learned Assistant Advocate General, Punjab, was directed to produce these orders before the Court.
In compliance of this direction, CM No. 15451 of 1995 has been filed alongwith the relevant orders.
That CM has been allowed by us today.
4.
Perusal of the inquiry report prepared by Shri BR Bansal shows that he found the petitioner guilty on various charges levelled against him.
Shri Bansal recorded a finding that the Government had suffered loss amounting to Rs. 1,66,43049.
Thereafter the Government passed order dated 9101987 and imposed penalty of stoppage of four increments without future effect and recovery of Rs. 3,32,81608.
This order was treated as final by the petitioner inasmuch as he did not file any appeal or review petition.
However, the Government suo moto passed order dated 2511990 and withdrew the order dated 9101987.
At the same time, it reserved the right of passing a fresh order after holding fresh inquiry or serving fresh charge-sheet on the petitioner.
Thereafter a show cause notice was issued and finally the impugned order was passed.
5.
Argument of Shri Rajput, learned counsel for the petitioner, that after withdrawal of order dated 9101987 the Government could not pass fresh order of punishment dated 2511990 is based on a wholly misconceived assumption that the Government had unconditionally withdrawn the order dated 9101987.
A bare look at the order dated 2511990 shows that the order dated 9101987 was withdrawn subject to a rider that fresh order could be passed by the Government after holding a fresh inquiry or serving a fresh charge-sheet, if considered necessary.
This rider incorporated in the order dated 2511990 empowered the Government to pass a fresh order and, therefore, it is not possible to hold that the order dated 2411994 is vitiated due to lack of jurisdiction.
However, there is a serious lacuna in the impugned order of punishment.
It appears to us that the Government suo moto withdrew the order dated 9101987 because of non-supply of the copy of the inquiry to the petitioner.
Whether there was any justification for suo moto action or not, but one thing is evident from the record that before passing the order of major penalty of dismissal from service, the Government did not give any indication to the petitioner that it has proposed to impose a more severe penalty on the petitioner.
The petitioner was never made aware of the intention of the Government to dismiss him from Service and simply by giving a copy of the inquiry report alongwith the notice dated 2121992 issued by the Chief Engineer, the Government imposed extreme penalty of dismissal from service upon the petitioner.
In our opinion, this action of the Government is liable to be declared as unlawful due to violation of principles of natural justice.
We are conscious of the legal position that after amendment of Article 311 (2) of the Constitution of India, it is not necessary for the employer to give a notice to the employee about the proposed punishment, but in a case like the present one where the Government had already punished the petitioner with a minor penalty of stoppage of four increments without further effect and the recovery of the loss suffered by the Government it was impossible for respondent No. 1 to have straight-way awarded a penalty of dismissal from service to the petitioner.
The minimum fairness required was that the Government should have given a notice to the petitioner of its proposal to dismiss him from service and an opportunity to make a representation against the same.
This having not been done, the impugned order dated 2411994 (Annexure P-1) cannot be sustained.
6.
In the result, the writ petition is allowed.
The impugned order dated 2411994 (Annexure P-1) is quashed.
It is, however, made absolutely clear that respondents No. 1 to 3 shall be entitled to take fresh proceedings against the petitioner by giving him a show cause notice and pass a fresh order after considering the reply, if any, submitted by the petitioner.
The parties are left to bear their own costs.
