Sham Sunder, J - The petitioner was initially appointed as untrained Matric teacher on 7.02.1955, by the District Inspectors of School, Gurdaspur, in CP Primary School, Udhowali Khurd, Tehsil Batala, District Gurdaspur.
However, w.e.f.
11061956, the services of the petitioner were terminated.
Still later, the petitioner was appointed as JBT Teacher in Govt. Primary School Hakimpur, in the pay scale of Rs. 60-120 w.e.f.
20.11.1957.
He continued to serve, as such, in the spells of three months up till 30.05.1959.
There after, he was appointed as a regular JBT Teacher w.e.f.
16.7.1959.
While serving in the Education Department, the petitioner improved his qualifications.
In the first instance, he passed BA and BT Examinations.
Thereafter, he passed the MA Punjabi Examination on 26.08.1971 in 2nd Division.
The petitioner was promoted as Master w.e.f.
29.12.1975.
It was stated that by virtue of passing of MA Examination in 2nd Division, the petitioner became entitled to get three advance increments, in accordance with the Punjab Government Instructions dated 23071957.
Accordingly, three advance increments of Rs. 8/- each w.e.f.
2661971, were granted to the petitioner, for possessing higher qualification.
It was stated that the advance increments, on account of higher qualification, being given to the employees of the Education Department of the Punjab Government, were withdrawn by the Government, vide instructions dated 19021979.
After 111978, when the pay scales were revised and before 19021979, the rate of increment was Rs. 30/-.
As per the seniority list (copy Annexure P-1) of the cadre of Masters as on 30061987, the name of the petitioner figured at No. 3663, whereas, the name of Kartar Singh figured at No. 4868.
Since, said Kartar Singh was junior to the petitioner, and had acquired higher qualification of passing the MA Examination in 2nd Division, after 111978, but before 19021979, he was granted three increments of Rs. 30/- each.
On account of this, said Kartar Singh, was drawing more pay, than the petitioner, as the petitioner, had been granted three advance increments @ Rs. 8/- each, in the year 1971.
The petitioner made a representation for stepping-up of his pay, at par with his junior Kartar Singh, but his pay was not stepped up, and brought at par with the pay of his junior Kartar Singh.
The petitioner also filed Civil Writ Petition No. 6721 of 1989.
The other masters, whose pay had not been stepped up, to bring the same, at par with their juniors, also filed a number of writ petitions.
A common judgment dated 131995 was passed by this Court, in C.W.P.
No. 17005 of 1989, allowing the same.
The other writ petitions bearing Nos. 12141 of 1989, 4878, 6418, 6721, 9941, 9944, 9945, 13858, 13926, 17064, 6791 of 1989 and 3564, 4730, 8403, 3274, 11156 & 16623 of 1990 were also allowed, vide the same judgment, with a direction, that the pay of the petitioners in those writ petitions, be stepped up equal to the one being drawn by other Masters and Mistresses, immediate junior to them irrespective of the fact, that they joined service with Post- graduate qualifications before 111978, or acquired Post-Graduate qualification, before that date.
The petitioners, in those writ petitions, were also granted the benefit of arrears of pay for a period of three years and two months, prior to the date of filing the same.
The respondents were directed to comply with the directions, given in the judgment dated 131995, within a period of six months.
Since the petitioner also filed one of the writ petitions, bearing No. 6721 of 1989, referred to above, which was allowed, his pay was ordered to be stepped-up to bring the same, at par with his junior.
In compliance with the said judgment, the benefit of stepping-up of pay, was granted to the petitioner, in pursuance of the order dated 13101997 passed by the District Education Officer (Schools), Gurdaspur.
All of a sudden, after the retirement of the petitioner, the Accountant General (A&E), Punjab- respondent no. 5, raised an objection vide his letter dated 27082001 (Annexure P-7) alleging therein that the benefit of stepping-up could not be given and, thus, it was liable to be withdrawn.
On an explanation, having been called, the petitioner submitted a detailed reply, vide representation dated 2792001 (Annexure P-8).
However, the Accountant General (A&E), Punjab vide order dated 21112001 (Annexure P-9) reiterated his earlier stand and consequently respondent No4, vide his letter dated 21122001 (Annexure P-10) informed the petitioner that his pay was required to be re-fixed, on a lower side, and the amount over paid to him, was required to be recovered.
Against this, the petitioner made a detailed representation dated 212002, but his explanation was not accepted.
Thereafter, left with no alternative, a legal notice dated 12032002, was served upon the respondents, by the petitioner.
In response thereto, respondent No. 5, reiterated vide letter dated 1652002 ( Annexure P-12) that explanation tendered by the petitioner, was not acceptable, stating that Kartar Singh was not junior to him, but was senior to him and, as such, his pay had been wrongly stepped-up at par with him.
It was further stated that such a stance taken up, by the respondents, was clearly against the seniority list ( Annexure P-1 ), referred to above.
It was further stated that according to the aforesaid seniority list, Kartar Singh was much junior to the petitioner.
It was further stated that memo (Annexure P-10), issued to the petitioner, asking him to furnish explanation, failing which recovery shall be effected from his pay and, at the same time, directed him to deposit the excess amount paid to him as also, Memo (Annexure P-12) issued by the Accounts Officer, to the petitioner, through his counsel, to get anomaly in pay removed with Master, who was junior and was drawing lesser pay than him before promotion as SS Master, were illegal and against the judgment dated 131995 (Annexure P-13), which had attained finality.
On the final refusal of the respondents, to withdraw these memos, left with no alternative, the instant petition for issuance of a writ in the nature of certiorari quashing the impugned orders dated 21122001 (Annexure P-10) and dated 1652002 (Annexure P-12) ordering the re-fixation of pay and recovery of the alleged overpayment, was filed.
A prayer for issuance of a Writ of Mandamus was also made.
2.
Respondent Nos. 1 to 4, filed their joint written statement.
The appointment of the petitioner, as a Teacher, acquisition of higher qualifications by him, his promotion; and grant of three advance increments @ Rs. 8/- each, on account of acquisition of higher qualification, were not denied.
It was also admitted that the petitioner earlier filed C.W.P.
No. 6721 of 1989 titled as Manohar Lal and others v. State of Punjab and the same was decided in his favour on 131995, by this Court.
It was also admitted that, in pursuance of that judgment, vide order dated 13101997, the pay of the petitioner was stepped up, at par with the juniors irrespective of the fact, that the petitioner joined service with Post Graduate qualifications before 111978, or acquired Post-Graduate qualification, before that date.
It was further stated that the pay of the petitioner was correctly stepped up, to bring the same, at par with his junior Kartar Singh.
It was further stated that the Accountant General of Punjab, Chandigarh, raised an objection with regard to stepping-up of the pay of the petitioner, with his junior.
It was further stated that the reason of withdrawal of such stepping up, was on account of the objection raised by the Accountant General of Punjab, Chandigarh.
3.
Respondent No. 5, filed a separate written statement, wherein, it was stated that the stepping up of pay of the petitioner, to bring at par with Kartar Singh , was not in order.
It was further stated that Kartar Singh was drawing more pay, from time to time, than Gurdial Singh in the lower cadre.
It was further stated that memo Ex.P-12 was correctly issued by respondent No. 5.
4.
I have heard the learned Counsel for the parties, and have gone through the record of the case, carefully.
5.
The Counsel for the petitioner, vehemently contended that once the pay of the petitioner, was stepped up, to bring at par with his junior Kartar Singh, vide order dated 13101997, by respondent Nos. 1 to 4, in implementation of the judgment dated 131995 (Annexure P-13), which according to his information, had attained finality, the Accountant General, Punjab, Chandigarh, could not direct respondent Nos. 1 to 4, to re-fix the pay of the petitioner.
He further contended that, accordingly, the order Annexure P-10 passed by respondent Nos. 1 to 4, for re-fixing the pay of the petitioner and ordering the recovery of alleged over-payment, was illegal.
He further contended that passing of the orders Annexures P-10 and P-12, amount to violation of the directions, given in the judgment dated 131995 (Annexure P-13) and prima facie amount to contempt of Court.
He further contended that these orders are, thus, liable to be set aside.
6.
The Counsel for the respondents, also submitted that the pay of the petitioner was correctly fixed, in pursuance of the judgment dated 131995 ( Annexure P-13 ).
He further contended that re-fixation of pay and recovery of the alleged over payment, was ordered by respondent Nos. 1 to 4, in view of the objection, raised by the Accountant General, Punjab, Chandigarh.
7.
Ex.P1 is the extract of the seniority list of Master Cadre in Punjab Education Department as on 30061987.
According to this document, Gurdial Singh, petitioner figures at Sr. No. 3663, whereas Kartar Singh, figures at Sr. No. 4868.
According to this document, which has not been disputed, by the learned Counsel for the respondents, the petitioner is certainly senior to Kartar Singh.
The dispute with regard to the stepping up of pay of the petitioner, to bring the same, at par with his junior, Kartar Singh, was finally decided, by this court, vide judgment dated 131995 (Annexure P-13).
As stated above, the judgment dated 131995 (Annexure P-13) has attained finality, as submitted by the Counsel for the petitioner, and not disputed by the Counsel for the respondents.
It was, in pursuance of this judgment, that respondent Nos. 1 to 4 stepped up the pay of the petitioner, to bring the same at par with his junior, Kartar Singh, vide order dated 13101997.
The Accountant General (A & E), Punjab, Chandigarh, thus, could not question the judgment dated 131995.
His act of questioning the judgment (Annexure P-13), prima facie, amounts to contempt of Court.
Accordingly, the order dated 21122001 (Annexure P-10 ) for re-fixation of his pay, recovery of the alleged over-payment from the petitioner, and the memo dated 1652002 (Annexure P-12), issued by the Accountant General (A&E), Punjab, Chandigarh, are totally illegal and arbitrary.
Accordingly, the same are liable to be set aside.
8.
For the reasons recorded hereinbefore, the writ petition is accepted, with no order as to costs.
The impugned order dated 21122001 (Annexure P-10) and the memo dated 16052002 (Annexure P-12), are quashed.
The respondents, are further directed not to re-fix the pay of the petitioner, resulting into consequential reduction of his pension, and also not to effect recovery of the alleged over payment.
