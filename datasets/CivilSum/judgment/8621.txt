VS Aggarwal, J - The Haryana State Co-operative Land Development Bank Ltd by virtue of the present petition filed under Articles 226 and 227 of the Constitution of India seeks quashing of the award dated 8.6.1992 passed by the Labour Court, Union Territory, Chandigarh,  2.
Respondent Sanjiv Srivastava (hereinafter described as 'the workman') had been appointed as a Peon for a period of 89 days on 22101986.
He continued to work as per his assertions till 2211989 with notional breaks.
His services were terminated without complying with the provisions of Section 25-F of the Industrial Disputes Act (for short 'the Act').
The workman made representations against illegal and arbitrary retrenchment contending that he had worked for more than 240 days and thus his services should be regularised and his retrenchment is illegal.
3.
In the reply filed before the Labour Court, the petitioner's claim was that the workman was initially appointed on 22101986.
His services were extended uptil 2351987.
Thereafter, no extension was given.
Fresh appointment was given only on 2681987 for 60 days.
The workman had filed an application on 2071988 for taking a sympathetic view.
He was given appointment on ad hoc basis.
He worked till 5111988.
Thereafter there was no extension.
It was denied, therefore, that he had completed 240 day.
The learned Presiding Officer, Labour Court, Union Territory, Chandigarh held that the workman was appointed on 22101987.
His services were first terminated on 27101987.
He was re-appointed on 2171988.
He worked for 178 days.
The Labour Court felt that a right had already accrued to the workman and it could not be undone by simply offering the workman service for less than number of days which would not entitle him to claim benefit of the Industrial Disputes Act.
The right so vested could not be taken away.
4.
Learned counsel for the petitioner urged that respondent No. 1 was appointed as peon for 89 days on 22101986 by the petitioner-Bank.
His services continued till 27101987.
The contractual period of his service expired.
At that time, he did not raise any industrial dispute.
After a long gap, he was given fresh appointment as a Peon on 2171988.
The services of respondent No. 1 on the expiry of contract period came to an end on 2211989.
Thus, he had not worked for 240 days preceding the date with reference to which the calculation has to be made and thus the award passed by the Labour Court cannot be sustained.
5.
The said contention of the learned counsel is not without merit.
The facts show that when contract of service of respondent No. 1 expired on 27101987, he did not raise any dispute.
He did not challenge the non-renewal of the contract before an appropriate forum.
After a long gap the was given fresh appointment.
The previous appointment has no nexus with the subsequent appointment.
There is nothing on the record to establish that second appointment was made by the petitioner because of some reason connected with the earlier appointment.
The Labour Court could not club the appointment when there was a long gap between the two.
It was a fresh contract of service.
6.
The period of 240 days is to be counted during the period of 12 calendar months preceding the date with reference to which calculation is to be made which in the instant case is the date of termination.
In other words, we have to see whether the workman worked for 240 days in one year before the termination order.
In the present case it is not so.
The same question came up before a learned Single Judge of this Court in case Punjab State Electricity Board v. Presiding Officer, Labour Court, Gurdaspur and others, 1995(6) Services Law Reporter 803 : 1995(4) S.C.T.
442.
In paragraph 4 it was held :-  "4.
The termination of services of the workman, no doubt, amounts to retrenchment within the meaning or Section 2(oo) of the Act as it does not fall in any of the exceptions mentioned therein but he would be entitled to claim retrenchment compensation in terns of Section 25-F of the Act only if he had been in continuous service with the management for not less than one year preceding the date of termination.
According to Section 25-B(2)(a) of the Act, the workman will be said to be in continuous service for a period of one year, if he has actually worked under the employer for not less than 240 days.
This period of 240 days is to be counted during a period of 12 calendar months preceding the date with reference to which the calculation is to be made which in the instant case is the date of termination.
In other words, we have to see whether the workman had worked for 240 days in a period of 12 months immediately preceding December, 1988.
When so calculated the workman had put in only 105 days service.
In fact he had not put in 240 days during any period of 12 calendar months as is clear from the aforesaid chart and he had been working as a daily wager intermittently.
In this view of the matter, it has to be held that he had not put in one year of continuous service with the management at the time when his services were terminated.
The provisions of Section 25-F of the Act were not required to be complied with.
The Labour Court was, therefore, not right in holding that the termination was illegal or unjustified.
The impugned award has thus to be quashed."
Similar is the position herein and, therefore, the learned Labour Court was patently in error in holding to the contrary.
7.
In fact the facts indicate further that there was nothing to show that there was any unfair labour practice to circumvent the provisions of the Industrial Disputes Act.
When it is not so, the award in question cannot be sustained.
8.
For these reasons, the writ petition is allowed.
The respondent- workman had not completed 240 days in a period of 12 months prior to the date of termination and, therefore, the provisions of Section 25-F of the Act were not required to be complied with.
Consequently, the award is set aside.
Petition allowed.
