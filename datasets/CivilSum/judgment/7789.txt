Viney Mittal, J - The present petition under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India has been filed by the petitioner Balbir Singh for issuance of a writ of certiorari for quashing the order dated July 18, 2000 passed by the Executive Engineer, Jagadhri Provl.
Division (B&R) P.W.D.
Yamunanagar-respondent No. 2, whereby he has declined to regularise the services of the petitioner.
A further prayer has been made for issuance of the writ of mandamus for directing the respondents to regularise his services.
Certain facts may be noticed.
2.
The petitioner joined the services of PWD B&R Department, Haryana as a Beldar w.e.f.
September 4, 1986.
His services were terminated on February 1, 1994.
He raised an industrial dispute.
A reference was made to the Labour Court, Ambala.
Vide an award dated September 15, 1999, the learned Labour Court held his termination to be illegal and bad and in violation of the provisions of the Industrial Disputes Act.
A copy of the award dated September 15, 1999 has been appended as Annexure P-1 with the petition.
3.
The case of the petitioner is that the aforesaid award was modified subsequently by this Court when the petitioner filed a CWP No. 9020 of 2000.
This Court vide order dated January 11, 2001 modified the relief and allowed full back wages to the petitioner which had been restricted to 50% by the Labour Court.
The award of the Labour Court ordering the reinstatement of the petitioner has attained finality.
4.
The State Government of Haryana issued a policy dated March 7, 1996 for regularisation of certain employees.
The aforesaid policy was subsequently modified on March 18, 1996.
As per the aforesaid policies, any person who was working on daily wages and had completed three years of service as on January 31, 1996 and did not have a break of more than 30 days and had 240 working days in one year, was entitled to be regularised.
5.
The case of the petitioner is that he was entitled to be regularised under the aforesaid policies since he was working since September 1986.
Since his services were not being regularised, therefore, he served a legal demand notice dated February 13, 2001.
No action was taken by the respondents.
He approached this Court through CWP No. 5850 of 2001.
The aforesaid Civil Writ Petition was disposed of by a Division Bench of this Court vide order dated April 25, 2001.
The respondents were directed to pass a speaking order upon the legal demand notice served by the petitioner within a period of four months from the date a certified copy of the aforesaid order was received by them.
6.
In complaisance with the directions of this Court, the Executive Engineer-respondent No. 2 considered the demand of the petitioner.
However, vide order dated July 18, 2001, the demand of the petitioner was rejected.
The order dated July 18, 2001 has been appended as Annexure P-4 with the petition.
7.
The petitioner is aggrieved against the order Annexure P-4 and has approached this Court through the present writ petition.
8.
Upon receipt of the notice of the writ petition, the respondents had put in appearance.
A written statement contesting the claim of the petitioner has been filed on behalf of the respondents No. 1 and 2.
The respondents have stated that since the petitioner did not fulfil the instructions issued by the Haryana Government, vide the aforesaid policies, therefore, they were not entitled to the regularisation of the services.
The order Annexure P-4 was sought to be defended.
It was further pointed out that there have been many breaks in his service each year and thus he was not covered under any policy of the regularisation.
Along with written statement, the respondents have also attached the details of man days of the petitioner from the year 1987-88 to 1993-94.
The aforesaid details have been appended as Annexure R-1 with the petition, which is as follows :-  Detail of Mandays of Shri Balbir Son of Sugan Year Total 1987-88 122 1988-89 304 1980-90 201 1990-91 189 1991-92 97-1/2 1992-93 239-1/2 1993-94 242-1/2 9.
I have heard Sh. JS Maanipur, learned counsel for the petitioner and Sh. Amol Rattan, learned Assistant Advocate General, Haryana appearing for the respondents.
The record of the case has been perused by me with their assistance.
10.
Sh. JS Maanipur, learned counsel for the petitioner has submitted that a persual of the order Annexure P-4 as well as the stand taken by the respondents in the written statement and the detail of the man days provided in Annexure R-1, clearly shows that the respondents had not taken into consideration the award passed by the learned Labour Court.
It has further been pointed out by Sh. Maanipur that learned Labour Court had categorically given a finding that the workman had continuously served from September 4, 1986 to February 1, 1996.
On the strength of the aforesaid finding, Sh. Maanipur submits that the rejection of the claim of the petitioner was totally without any basis.
11.
On the other hand, Sh. Amol Rattan, learned Assistant Advocate General, Haryana has submitted that while passing the order Annexure P-4, the Executive Engineer has taken into consideration the various dates on which the petitioner was working in any one year from the date of his employment.
A further reliance has been placed by him upon the details given in annexure R- 1.
On the strength of the aforesaid details, Sh. Amol Rattan submits that the petitioner was not eligible for regularisation of his services as per the policies dated March 7, 1996 and March 18, 1996.
12.
I have given my thoughtful consideration to the entire matter.
In my view, the petition filed by the petitioner deserves to succeed.
13.
The categoric stand of the petitioner is that he was working on daily wages w.e.f.
September 4, 1986.
Although his services were terminated w.e.f.
February 1, 1994, but the Labour Court upon a reference made on behalf of the workman, ordered his reinstatement vide award dated September 15, 1999.
While ordering the reinstatement, it was also directed that the petitioner would be entitled to continuity of his service.
A further categorical finding was given by the Labour Court to the effect that "in these circumstances, the plea of the workman that he had continuously served from 491986 to 121994 has to be accepted and in this manner the workman had completed the continuous service of 240 days........" The necessary details of the man days served by the petitioner in the various years, have been given in Annexure R-1 by the respondents themselves, an noticed by me above.
However, I find that the respondents have given the details only upto the year 1993-94.
As per the award of the Labour Court, which is Annexure P-1, the services of the petitioner were terminated on February 1, 1994, but were ordered to be reinstated w.e.f.
September 15, 1999.
Thus w.e.f.
February 1, 1994 till January 31, 1996 (which is the cut off date in the policies), the petitioner would be deemed to be in service.
Thus, it is apparent that the petitioner has served for 304 days for the year 1988-89.
He has again served for 242-1/2 days in 1993-94.
Because of the award of the Labour Court, he shall be deemed to be in service for the entire period wef February 1, 1994 till January 31, 1995 in the year 1994-95 and again for the entire year for the period w.e.f.
February 1, 1995 to January 31, 1996.
If we take the period of his man days in the years 1988-89, 1993-94, 1994-95 and 1995-96, it is apparent that the petitioner has rendered service of more than three years for a period which is more than 240 days in each years.
In this view of the matter, the claim of the petitioner cannot be denied.
14.
Faced with this situation, Sh. Amol Rattan, Assistant Advocate General, Haryana says that for the application of the policies, a period of three years immediately preceding the cut off date i.e. January 31, 1996 is to be taken into consideration.
Sh. Amol Rattan submits that since in the present case, the aforesaid requirement was not completed and the petitioner did not actually render physical service, therefore, he was not entitled to make any claim under the aforesaid policies.
15.
I do not find that submissions of Sh. Amol Rattan have any merit.
As far as, the question whether a person who was ordered to be reinstated by the award of the Labour Court was entitled to the consideration of the aforesaid period when he was actually out of service, to be counted as a period of service rendered by him, the matter stands duly adjudicated by a Division Bench of this Court in the case of Attar Singh v. State of Haryana, 2002(2) SCT 186, wherein it was held as follows :-  "We may also usefully refer to a judgment of another Division Bench of this Court in CWP No. 14165 of 2001, titled as Deep Chand v. State of Haryana, decided on 1022002, where while dealing with somewhat (identical) situation, the Bench held as under :- "It is a deemed fiction of law that the period in case such award would have to be treated as a period actually spent on duty.
Initially, the services of the petitioner were wrongly terminated by the respondent State and subsequently the State compelled him to remain out of service for no fault of the workman.
Even after the award of the Labour Court, the petitioner was not permitted to join the duty.
In other words, the petitioner was not physically present at the work as a result of action of the respondents and no fault was attributable to the petitioner on any ground whatsoever.
The physical absence of the petitioner from duty was result of compulsion of the petitioner and not his choice.
In these circumstances, we are unable to sustain the reasons given in the impugned letter that the petitioner was not entitled to regularisation because he did not physically work during the period in question.
The learned counsel for the State was unable to support his argument by any precedent or documents to show that the petitioner was called upon to join his duties.
Once the Court finds it as a matter of fact that services of the employees were wrongly terminated, who in the eyes of law, would be deemed to be in service, cannot be even deprived of his back wages or arrears of salary subject to just exceptions and the law of limitation.
The consequence of setting aside an order of dismissal or termination given rise to a consequential relief of reinstatement and payment of back wages a necessary corollary to the setting aside of such order.
As a logical consequences of this declaration (award), it is manifest that the petitioner would be entitled to the benefits or reliefs which was natural results of such relief.
(In this regard reference can be made to a full Bench Judgment of this Court in the case of Radha Ram v. Municipal Committee, Barnala and another, 1983(1) SLR 151 and the judgment of the Supreme Court in the case of State of Madhya Pradesh v. State of Maharashtra, 1977(2) SCC 288).
The above admitted facts support the case of the petitioner and are indicative of the fact that the respondent-State has taken this objection only to deny a benefit legally and reasonably due to the petitioner."
"In view of the above settled position of law and the admitted facts in the present case, we are not able to persuade ourselves to hold that the petitioner was not in service as on 3131993.
Once award in favour of the petitioner attained finality and the petitioner was entitled to continuity in service in terms thereof, it was neither just nor fair for the respondents to deny the benefit of continuity of service on the ground of physical absence of the workman, which in fact was own creation of the respondents."
16.
Thus the contention raised by Sh. Amol Rattan that actual physical service and not the deemed continuity, was required under the policies has absolutely no force.
17.
As regards the other contention raised by Sh. Amol Rattan, that petitioner has not completed three years service consecutively prior to the cut off date i.e. January 31, 1996, the aforesaid matter has also been concluded by the authoritative pronouncement by a Division Bench in Kavita Gandhi v. State of Haryana and others, 1997(4) RSJ 670, wherein it has been held as follows :-  "In view of the judgment of the Full Bench of this Court in Jagdish Lal and others (supra) it is abundantly clear that in order to become eligible for consideration for regularisation of the service under a particular notification of the State Government the prescribed years of completed service with reference to the cut-off date mentioned in the notification applies to such employees also who have completed those number of years of service prior to the cut off date and not necessarily, immediately preceding the said date.
In other words, if the employee has completed, with reference to the notification dated 731996/1831996, three years of completed service and was in service on cut off date i.e. 3111996, he would become entitled for regularisation of service provided he fulfilled the other conditions mentioned in the notification.
It is not necessary that the period of three years should be taken immediately preceding the cut-off date i.e. 3111996.
If the case of the petitioner is examined from this angle, she rendered more than three years completed service without any break prior to 3111996 and she was in service on the said date.
The break of 48 days in service of the petitioner in the year 1994 would, thus, be not relevant to be considered for regularisation of her service.
Resultantly, the petition is allowed.
The impugned order Annexure P4 is quashed and the respondents are directed to regularise the services of the petitioner within a period of three months from the date of production of the copy of this order.
No order as to costs."
18.
As noticed by me above, the petitioner has already rendered more than three years of service, though not consecutively and not immediately preceding the cut off date i.e. January 31, 1996.
However, in view of law laid down in Kavita Gandhi's case (supra), the case of the petitioner is fully covered under the aforesaid policies.
19.
Thus, I allow the present writ petition and while quashing the order dated July 18, 2001, Annexure P-4 passed by respondent No. 2, direct the respondents to regularise the services of the petitioner w.e.f.
February 1, 1996.
The petitioner would be entitled to all consequential benefits arising from such regularisation.
The respondents are directed to issue the necessary orders, regularising the services of the petitioner w.e.f., February 1, 1996 and release him all consequential benefits arising therefrom within a period of four months the date a certified copy of the order is received by them.
No costs.
Petition allowed.
