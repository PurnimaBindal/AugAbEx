THB Chalapathi, J - The petitioner filed this writ petition for issuance of a writ of mandamous directing the respondents to regularise his services as per the decision of the Supreme Court and also the instructions of the State Government.
2.
The petitioner has been appointed on daily wages in the office of respondent No. 3 on muster rolls without issuing any order of appointment during the years 1980 to 1994.
According to him, he remained in service till 31st of May, 1994 and thereafter he has not been given any muster roll.
Some other similarly situated persons approached this Court and their writ petitions were allowed.
The services of the petitioner was not regularised, but the services of the persons junior to him have been regularised.
In order to seek regularisation of his services, the petitioner filed a writ petition bearing No. 1695 of 1989.
By an order dated 4111992 this Court passed the following order :-  "It is pleaded that the petitioners had put in more than two years services prior to the filing of the present writ petition.
If that be so, the petition is allowed only to the extent that the respondent shall consider the case of the petitioner for regularisation in view of the judgment rendered by the Supreme Court in State of Haryana v. Piara Singh, 1992(5) S.P.J.
1 : 1992(3) SCT 201 (SC) and the instructions issued by the State Govt. in this behalf.
However, till such time this exercise is done, the services of the petitioner(s) shall not be dispensed with.
No order as to costs."
On behalf of the respondents, no written statement has been filed.
3.
There is no dispute of the fact that the petitioner has been working as Casual Labourer on daily wages.
The Government of Haryana on the basis of the decision of the Supreme Court in State of Haryana v. Piara Singh, 1992(3) RSJ 235 : 1992(3) SCT 201 (SC) issued instructions on 2751993 vide Annexure R-1 in regard to regularisation of work charge/casual/daily rated employees.
According to those instructions, the casual and daily rated employees who have completed 5 years of continuous service on 31st March, 1993 and were in service on that date shall be regularised.
On regularisation, they shall be put in the time scale of pay applicable to the lowest Group `D' cadre in the Government and they would be entitled to all other allowances and benefits available to regular Govt. servants of the corresponding grade.
Subsequently, a clarification was issued by the Chief Secretary to Government, Haryana in his circular letter dated 18.3.1994 which is marked as Annexure R-2.
According to this letter, the Government has decided that those daily wages employees who have worked for 240 days in a year should be regularised, but there should not be a break in service for more than 30 days at a time in a year.
Thus it is clear that for getting the benefit of regularisation of services in the Policy of the Government, the employee must have worked for a period of 240 days in a year without any break of more than 30 days.
4.
It is, therefore, to be seen whether the petitioner fulfilled the conditions laid down in the circular letter referred to above so as to enable him to have the benefit of regularisation.
The petitioner asserts that he has worked for more than 240 days during the last five years prior to 3131993.
Since the respondents has not filed any written statement, I hereby direct the respondents to consider the case of the petitioner for regularisation of his services in accordance with the instructions.
If the petitioner worked for 240 days in each year during the last five years prior to 3131993 his services has to be regularised in view of the decision of this Court in Giri Raj and others v. State of Haryana and others, 1997(2) RSJ 506, Ram Dhari v. The Chief Wild Life Warden, Haryana and another, 1996(1) RSJ 246 : 1996(1) SCT 615 (P&H)(DB).
and Anand Kumar v. Haryana Urban Development Authority etc., 1995(1) RSJ 230 : 1995(1) SCT 677 (P&H)(DB).
5.
The writ petition is, accordingly, disposed of with the above directions.
Petition allowed.
