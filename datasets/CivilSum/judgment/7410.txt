SS Nijjar, J - This judgment will dispose of the aforesaid Civil Writ Petitions as the facts and questions of law involved are identical.
2.
This petition has been filed by the Sr.
Vocational Staff Masters Association, Punjab, as the Association represents Vocational Masters who have been appointed on the posts of Vocational Masters from 1975 onwards.
Punjab Public Service Commission advertised on 24111978, 132 posts of Vocational Masters, in the PES-III (School Cadre).
According to the petitioners, the qualifications for being appointed on the posts of Vocational Masters were a degree in Automobile Engineering or 3 years diploma in Mechanical Engineering with three years teaching or practical experience.
Both the qualifications were treated as equal.
Petitioner Nos. 2 to 16 were selected and recommended for appointment by the Punjab Public Service Commission.
Thereafter the appointments were made by the State of Punjab in the subsequent years by sending requisitions to the Employment Exchange, as and when required.
Although the petitioners had been working as Vocational Masters for a number of years, they were not being promoted to Punjab Education Service (Class-II) (hereinafter referred to as the "PES Class-II").
Promotions to PES Class-II are made under Rule 10 of Punjab Education Service Class-II (School and Inspection) Rules, 1976 (hereinafter referred to as the "1976 Rules").
The Vocational Masters, therefore, filed CWP Nos. 746 and 2157 of 1987.
They prayed for the issuance of a writ in the nature of Mandamus directing the respondents to (a) grant a pay-scale on the basis of the qualifications possessed by them; (b) two advance increments; (c) make 20% posts in the selection grade, available to the petitioners; (d) frame statutory rules or issue instructions regarding the services of the petitioners; (e) consider the petitioners for promotion to PES Class II posts under Rule 10(1) of the 1976 Rules.
Both the aforesaid petitioners were allowed by this Court on 1041991.
In the written statement filed by the respondents, it was pleaded that the posts held by the petitioners are not governed by the 1976 Rules as the posts of Vocational Masters are not included in the Punjab Education Service Class III (School Cadre) Rules.
This Court took notice of the law laid down by the Supreme Court in the case of Council of Scientific and Industrial Research and another v. Mr. KGS Bhatt and another, 1989(4) SLR 252 and directed the State Government to finalise the rules within four months from the receipt of the order and then to consider the claim of the petitioner for promotion under those rules.
It was also directed that the State Government would be at liberty to treat the draft rules as Executive Instructions so that the petitioners' claim for promotion may be considered immediately.
It was further observed as follows :-  ".....
If for some reason, the Rules are not finalised, within a period of four months from the receipt of this order or the draft rules are not treated as Executive Instructions, then the petitioners would be entitled to consideration for promotion to Class II Rules under Rule 10 of the Class II Rules w.e.f.
today.
In that eventuality, the State Government would fix the quota for promotion of or Vocational Masters under Rule 10 of the Class II Rules."
3.
As usual, the four months time expired and the judgment of this Court was not implemented by the State.
The applications filed by the State for extension of time were also dismissed.
The Letters Patent Appeal filed against the judgment of the Single Judge was also dismissed on 31.10.1991.
The petitioners filed contempt petitions as the judgment of the learned Single Judge was not being implemented.
Around that time, the Punjab Government issued an order on 14111991 providing that the Draft Rules titled as Punjab Vocational Education Service Class-II (School and Inspection) Rules, 1991 would be treated as Executive Instructions and further provided that the Executive Instructions would be operative w.e.f.
September 16, 1991.
On 15111991, some of the petitioners in the aforesaid writ petitions were actually promoted, on the basis of the Draft Rules treating the same to be executive instructions.
Thereafter, 18 Senior Vocational Masters filed CWP No. 7036 of 1992 challenging the inaction of the respondents in not framing the rules.
After the admission of the writ petitions, the State of Punjab issued an order on 30.11.1993.
Under this order, the posts of Vocational Masters were included in Punjab Education Service Class-II (School and Inspection) Cadre.
It was, therefore, observed that there is no necessity of framing separate Punjab Vocational Education Class-II Rules.
According to the petitioners, the net result of this is that the petitioners in the present writ petition are in the same position as the petitioners in CWP No. 746 of 1987.
4.
Apart from this, the petitioners are claiming the pay-scale of Rs. 6400- 10640 w.e.f.
111996 which would be at par with the scales granted to the Lecturers.
The petitioners claim that when they were initially appointed as Vocational Masters on ad hoc basis in 1975, they were placed in the pay-scale of Rs. 300-600.
A degree in Engineering was the necessary qualification for teaching students in the Engineering trade.
For non-Engineering Trade, a candidate was required to have the qualification of BA with ITI Diploma.
These qualifications were at par with Lecturers under the PES Class-III Rules.
In fact, at that time, the posts of Lecturer were in the lower scale of Rs. 250-550.
The scale which had been given to Vocational Masters 300-600 was equivalent to the scale which had been enjoyed by Head Masters.
In the year 1978, the Pay Commission recommended the pay-scale of Rs. 700-1300 both for lecturers as well as for vocational masters.
Thus, the vocational masters and lecturers were placed in the same scale.
The parity in pay continued even in the subsequent pay revision and both the categories were placed in the pay- scale of Rs. 1800-3200.
The nature of duties, according to the petitioners, of the Lecturers and vocational masters have not undergone any change.
The Vocational Master and Lecturers are all teaching 10+1 and 10+2 classes.
In the meantime, the posts of the Vocational Masters were encadered in Class-III Rules by notification dated 871995.
They were placed in the pay-scale of Rs. 1800-3200.
However, when the pay-scales were revised in the year 1998 w.e.f.
111996, a disparity was created between the pay-scales of the lecturers and vocational masters.
Whilst the lecturers were granted the pay-scale of Rs. 6400-10640, the petitioners were fixed in the converted pay-scale of Rs. 5800-9200.
The petitioners who were appointed earlier to 871995, claimed that they cannot be granted a pay-scale lesser than the Lecturers, i.e. Rs. 6400-10640.
On 7112002, a Notification was issued clarifying the aforesaid Notification to the effect that the Vocational Masters/Mistresses appointed on or after 871995, neither can be designated as Vocational Lecturers based upon the educational qualifications nor can the revised scale of Rs. 6400- 10640 be granted to them w.e.f.
111996.
It was clarified that the higher scale will be admissible to those who are in service prior to 871995.
Accordingly, the higher scale was granted to the petitioners, pay fixed and arrears of salary given.
Subsequently, the State issued yet another instruction on 1652003 regarding implementation of revised scale of Rs. 6400-10640 to Vocational Masters/Mistresses.
In this Notification, it was directed that "all the Vocational Masters/Mistresses appointed before 8.7.95 either they are degree holders or diploma holders have been granted the scale of Rs. 6400-10640".
5.
In between the petitioners have also been agitating that they should be given the designation of Vocational Lecturers.
The State Government passed an order on 3131995 (Annexure P-12) designating only those vocational masters as Lecturers, who had degree in Engineering and Post-graduate qualification in the non-engineering traders.
It was also provided that diploma holder vocational Masters will be redesignated as Vocational Lecturers as and when they acquire degree/post-graduate qualifications.
On the basis of the amendment in Class-III Rule vide notification dated 871995, the petitioners claim that they should have been encadred with effect from the date of their initial appointment.
However, the State Government issued a Memorandum on 2571995 (Annexure P-13) that their seniority should be determined under Rule 11 from the date of eacadrement.
As a result, a joint seniority list was circulated.
The petitioners were still aggrieved against the selected designation and their non-promotion with effect from due dates and, therefore, filed CWP No. 7517 of 1995.
The petitioners also filed CWP No. 4182 of 1995 claiming the same relief for promotion to Class II.
When the aforesaid writ petition came up for hearing, this Court reserveed 170 post in PES Class II Service for the petitioners.
These posts are still lying vacant.
Subsequently, however, by order dated 1322003, the State Government withdrew letters dated 1311995 and 2571995 regarding the grant of seniority and the change of designation.
Aggrieved against the aforesaid decision, the petitioners again filed CWP No. 5437 of 2003.
In the written statement filed to the aforesaid writ petition, the State of Punjab admitted that lecturers and vocational masters all teach 10+1 and 10+2 Classes, but nature of duties of both the categories is different.
It was also pleaded that the pay-scale of both the categories are not identical w.e.f.
111996.
According to the respondents, the pay-scale of Lecturers on general side was Rs. 6400-10640 whereas the pay-scale of Vocational Masters who were appointed on or after 871995 is Rs. 5800-9200.
It was stated that the vocational masters appointed prior to 871995 were in the scale of Rs. 6400-10640.
During the pendency of the aforesaid writ petition, the State Government passed an order on 2152003 granting a quota of 15% to the Vocational Masters for being considered for promotion to PES Class II (Annexure P-15).
The petitioners, therefore, decided not to pursue the aforesaid writ petition.
The writ petition was disposed of as infructuous.
It was, however, observed by the Division Bench that the petitioners shall be at liberty to pursue the claim with regard to the re- designation as Lecturers with the respondents.
The petitioners were permitted to make a representation within one month.
It was also directed that if such a representation is made, the respondents shall pass appropriate orders within one month thereafter, after hearing the petitioners, if necessary.
The petitioners duly submitted their representation.
However, the Government again passed an order on 1672003.
It was now decided that designation and pay- scale of Rs. 6400-10640 w.e.f.
111996 will be admissible to only those Vocational Masters/Mistresses who were appointed as Masters/Mistresses prior to 871995 and those who acquire the qualification of Post Graduation or Degree in Engineering by 871995.
It was further provided that if any other Vocational Masters/Mistresses/Lecturers had been allowed this pay-scale, that is not as per government directive issued in consultation with the department of Finance.
So, the DPI was directed to recover the excess amount, if paid to ineligible vocational Masters/Lecturers, after following the proper procedure under the Rules.
Letters dated 7112002 (Annexure P-10) and 1652003 (Annexure P-11) were to be treated as cancelled/withdrawn.
6.
It is this order which is challenged by the petitioners in the present writ petition under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India.
The petitioners pray for the issuance of a writ in the nature of Certiorari quashing the order dated 1672003 (Annexure P-17).
The petitioners also pray for the issuance of a writ in the nature of Mandamus directing the respondents to continue to pay to the petitioners their salary in the pay-scale of Rs. 6400-10640.
On 1872004, this Court issued show-cause notice to the respondents as to why the writ petition be not admitted returnable on 2272003.
When the matter came up for hearing on 2472003, it was stated by the learned counsel appearing for the respondents that the State does not press to recover the amount at this stage.
On 3172003, when no written statement was forthcoming, this Court stayed the operation of the impugned order dated 1672003.
Hence the petitioners have been receiving the salary which was being paid to them prior to the issuance of the impugned order dated 1672003.
7.
The respondents have filed a written statement.
It is stated that pay fixation and job evaluation is the task of Pay Commission.
Therefore, the writ petition is not maintainable.
The Vocational Masters/Mistresses/Lecturers were in receipt of pre-revised pay-scales of Rs. 1800-3200, Rs. 2000-3500 (after 8 years) and Rs. 2100-3700 (after 18 years).
Keeping in view the recommendations of the 4th Pay Commission made in para 5710 of the report, a higher pay-scale of Rs. 6400-10640 has been made admissible to the School Lecturers.
So far as the case of the petitioner is concerned, the recommendation contained in para 5728 of the report, the revised pay-scales to be granted are as per he General Conversion table.
The petitioners never put forward their claim before the Anomalies Committee.
No order effecting the recovery have been issued under orders dated 1672003.
The same will be effected after complying with rules of natural justice.
The pay of the petitioners had been wrongly fixed and only mistake has been corrected.
On 3131995, the Government had taken a decision (Annexure P-12) that Vocational Masters/Mistresses appointed in Education department, who acquired educational qualification upto Degree in Engineering or Post Graduation in any subject would be designated as Vocational Lecturers.
This provision was incorporated when the Punjab State Education Class III (School Cadre) Service Rules, 1978 were amended.
In Appendix-B, Note below Item No. 2(i) was inserted, which reads as under :-  "The existing vocational Masters having post graduation degree in any subject or having degree in Engineering shall be deemed to have been appointed as Lecturer for vocational Education."
8.
Keeping in view the aforesaid amendment, the scheme notified by letter dated 3131995 was done away with.
Therefore, only Vocational Masters appointed prior to 871995 were entitled to be given designation of Vocational Lecturers based on educational qualifications.
The Office of respondent No. 1 i.e. Secretary to Government of Punjab, Department of Education had inadvertently awarded the pay-scale of Lecturers to those Vocational Masters who were appointed prior to 871995, without verifying the facts as to whether they had acquired higher qualifications or not by 871995.
The 4th Pay Commission recommended a scale of Rs. 6400-10640 for Lecturers.
Vocational Master were allowed general conversion of pre-revised scale of Rs. 1800-3200 w.e.f.
111996 i.e. pay-scale of Rs. 5800-9200 was made admissible to the Vocational Masters.
The State Government inadvertently, allowed higher pay-scales of Rs. 6400-10640 to the Vocational Masters by letters dated 7112002 and 1652003 (Annexure P-10 and P-11).
Therefore, the order dated 1672003 was issued to rectify the error.
With the aforesaid distinction, on merits, facts narrated by the petitioners are admitted.
9.
A separate written statement has been filed on behalf of Government School Lecturers Union, Punjab-respondent No. 5 subsequently impleaded by orders of this Court on 992003 passed in CM No. 1993 of 2003.
It is stated that the Vocational Scheme was introduced in Government Schools in the year 1975 and the posts of Vocational Masters were created.
No posts of Vocational Lecturers were created.
The Class III Rules were notified by the State Government on 1181978.
It was provided in Rule 3 of the Class III Rules that the service shall have two branches, namely, Men Branch and Women Branch.
The posts in the service were listed in Appendix A of the Rules, which were Masters/Mistresses as well as Lecturers.
Vocational Masters and Lecturers were not included in Appendix "A".
In Class III Rules, Masters and Mistresses are entitled to promotion on the posts of Lecturers and on the posts of Headmasters/Headmistresses.
25% of the posts of Headmasters/Headmistresses and Lecturers are to be filled by direct recruitment and 75% from amongst the Masters/Mistresses.
Thus, the posts of Headmasters/Headmistresses and Lecturers are not highest channel of promotion from the post of Headmasters/Headmistresses as well as that of Lecturers to PES Class-III.
The appointments and conditions of service in Class II are governed by 1976 Rules.
Rule 10 deals with the method of recruitment.
25% of the posts are to be filled by direct recruitment and 75% by promotion from Class III.
The posts of Vocational Lecturers were mentioned for the first time in the Punjab State Education Class III (School Cadre) Service (Ist Amendment) Rules, 1995.
The posts of Vocational Masters as well as that of Vocational Lecturers were included in Appendix A w.e.f.
871995.
In all 1225 posts of Vocational Lecturers/Masters were created.
In the Note given in the Appendix to the Rules, it was provided that the existing Vocational Masters having the Post Graduate Degree in any subject or degree in Engineering, shall be deemed to have been appointed as Lecturer in Vocational Education.
There were only 170 existing vocational masters who were having the Post Graduate degree of degree in Engineering.
Thus, it becomes evidence that out of 1225 posts, only 170 posts were created for Vocational Lecturers and the remaining 1055 as Vocational Masters.
In implementation of the recommendations of the 4th Pay Commission, the State of Punjab promulgated Punjab Civil Service Revised Pay Rule, 1998.
These rules were notified on 1611998 and the pay-scales were revised w.e.f.
111996.
The posts of Vocational Lecturers/Masters were not granted the same scale as the School Lecturers who had been granted revised scale of Rs. 6400-10640.
All categories of Masters were granted pay-scale of Rs. 5480-8925.
They were placed in the Ist Schedule of the Rules i.e. according to the General Conversion Table.
The Vocational Masters as well as Vocational Lecturers were in the pre-revised scale of Rs. 1800-3200 and as per the Ist Schedule, they were granted the pay-scale of Rs. 5800-9200.
The 1998 Rules were never challenged by the petitioners.
Therefore, they are only entitled to the pay-scales as provided under the aforesaid Rules.
Any instructions issued by the State Government would not automatically amend the statutory rules.
Furthermore, it is further stated that under the Class III Rules, the members of the service are entitled to the pay-scales prescribed therein from time to time.
Therefore, the instructions issued by the State Government on 7112002 and 1652003 (Annexures P-10 and P-11) were contrary to the statutory rules.
Similarly, orders dated 3131995 and 2571995 were contrary to the provisions of the Class III Rules.
For the same reasons, the Notification dated 2152003 (Annexure P-5) is contrary to Class III Rules.
According to the respondents, under Class III Rules, only Vocational Masters appointed prior to 871995 having the post-graduate qualification/degree in Engineering would be deemed to have been appointed as Lecturers in Vocational Education.
The remaining Vocational Masters who were appointed prior to 871995, but were not possessing the Post Graduate qualification or Degree in Engineering as well as the Vocational Masters appointed after 871995, even though they may possess the post-graduate qualification or degree in Engineering shall remain Vocational Masters only.
Therefore, in order to remove the confusion caused by Memo dated 7112002 (Annexure P-10) and 1652003 (Annexure P-11), the respondents issued a clarificatory memo dated 1672003 (Annexure P-17).
10.
In connected writ petition (CWP No. 7527 of 1995, titled as The Senior Vocational Staff Masters Association and others v. The State of Punjab and others), also the facts pleaded by the parties are virtually identical.
In this writ petition, the petitioners have additionally pleaded that at the time when the petitioners were appointed as Vocational Masters, the post of Lecturer was in lower scale of Rs. 250-550 and the petitioners were given the scale of Rs. 300-600.
This was also the scale given to Headmasters.
It was only in the year 1978 that the Pay Commission recommended the pay-scale of Rs. 700-1300, both for the Lecturers as also Vocational Masters.
It was at that stage, for the first time that the petitioners and Lecturers were placed in the same pay-scale.
At that time, the qualification to be appointed as Lecturer was MA whereas the qualifications for Vocational Masters were Degree in Engineering or BA with ITI Diploma.
From 1978 onwards, the pay- scale of the petitioners and that of the Lecturers covered under the PES Class III Service Rules, remained the same.
So far as the nature of working is concerned, the petitioners are discharging their duties in Senior Secondary Schools in Engineering/non-Engineering trades.
The petitioners, like the Lecturers teach classes 10+1 and 10+2.
The petitioners seek the issuance of a writ in the nature of Mandamus directing the respondents to grant the designation of Lecturer to all the Vocational Masters in the State of Punjab and to extend the benefit of the order dated 3131995 to all the Vocational Masters.
11.
On the basis of the aforesaid pleadings, it is submitted by Mr. Patwalia that there can be no disparity in the pay-scales of vocational lecturers and vocational masters.
In view of the judgment rendered by this Court in CWP No. 746 of 1987, the petitioners could not have been denied the relief.
Positive direction had been issued by this Court on 1041991 to finalise the rules within four months.
The State of Punjab has been deliberately delaying the promulgation of the rules.
Even though the petitioners had been encadered in the Class III Rules on 871995, they were still denied the relief of promotion to Class II on the ground that there is no corresponding amendment in the Class II Rules.
The petitioners have been denied the relief, according to the learned counsel, due to the strong lobby of Class III Lecturers.
The claim of the vocational masters to be designated as Vocational Lecturers had been recommended on 2171992.
However, no action was taken by the respondents even after the Vocational Lecturers/Masters/Mistresses were encadered in the Class III Rules on 871995.
The benefit of the Lecturers Grade was sought to be illegally restricted to the Degree Holder Vocational Masters who were in service prior to 871995.
It is not disputed that Lecturers/Vocational Lecturers/Vocational Masters teach 10-1 and 10+2 classes.
The only distinction is that the Lecturers teach students in General Studies whereas vocational lecturers/masters teach vocational/trade subjects.
Since the duties performed by the Degree-holder/Diploma-holder Vocational Masters are identical, there can be no disparity in the pay-scales.
Learned Sr Counsel for the petitioners submits that even otherwise, after 1975, the Vocational Masters were in the scale of Rs. 300-600 whereas Lecturers were in the scale of Rs. 250-550.
So far as the qualifications are concerned degree was required for teaching the students in the Engineering Trade.
For the non-engineering trade, a candidate was required to have a qualification of BA with ITI Diploma.
Both the categories of Vocational Masters were enjoying the scale of Rs. 300-600 which was equivalent to the scale of Head Masters.
In the subsequent pay revision in the year 1978, the Pay Commission had recommended the scale of Rs. 700-1300 both for the Lecturers and Vocational Masters.
This parity in the pay-scales continued even in the subsequent pay revision.
Both the categories were placed in the scale of Rs. 1800-3200.
Even at the time when the Vocational Masters were encadred in the Class III Rules, they were granted the same pay-scale as Lecturer i.e. Rs. 1800-3200.
A disparity in the pay-scale of the Lecturers and Vocational Masters was sought to be created for the first time w.e.f.
1.1.96 between Lecturers and Vocational Masters.
While the Lecturers were granted the scale of Rs. 6400-10640, the petitioners were fixed in the converted pay-scale of Rs. 5800-9200.
This disparity was, however, removed by the Government by issuing a clarification that Vocational Masters/Mistresses appointed before 871995 shall be entitled to the higher pay-scale of Rs. 6400-10640.
The petitioners have been in receipt of the aforesaid scale.
Learned counsel submits that disparity having been once removed, the petitioners cannot now be put in a lower scale.
In support of the submission, learned counsel relies on a number of judgments viz. in the cases of GK Nagpal v. Punjab State Electricity Board, 1998(1) SCT 694, State of Andhra Pradesh v. G Ramakishan, 2001(1) SCT 347, Pritam Dass v. State of Punjab, 2000(4) SCT 987, Bhagwan Shukla v Union of India, 1994(4) SCT 406 (SC) : AIR 1994 Supreme Court 2480 and MP Rural Agriculture Extension Officers Association v. State of M.P. and another, JT 2004(4) SC 446.
12.
Mr. RK Chopra, learned Sr. counsel appearing for the Lecturers submits that the petitioners cannot be granted the same scales as the other Lecturers.
The pay-scale of the petitioners have to be fixed in accordance with the Punjab Civil Services (Revised Pay) Rules, 1998 which came into force w.e.f.
111996.
Since the petitioners were in the scale of Rs. 1800-3200, they would be entitled only to the converted scale of Rs. 5800-9200.
13.
Ms. Tuli, learned counsel for the State argues that the higher scale of Rs. 6400-10640 has been granted only to Graduates in Engineering.
Otherwise all the petitioners would have got only Rs. 5800-9200.
The higher scale has been granted only on the basis of higher qualification which, according to the learned counsel, is a permissible classification.
She further argues that now the Punjab Education Service (School and Inspection) Group A Service Rules, 2004 have been promulgated.
Under these Rules, the Vocational Lecturers/Masters/Mistresses have been granted a quota of 15% for promotion from Class III to Class II.
The promotion chances of the petitioners have not been affected in any manner by grant of two different pay-scales.
14.
I have considered the submissions of learned counsel for the parties.
15.
It is not disputed that Vocational Masters/Mistresses and Lecturers were in receipt of pre-revised pay-scales of Rs. 1800-3200, 2000-3500 (after 8 years) and 2100-3700 (after 18 years).
This equation of pay had continued for a long period of time, in three successive pay revisions.
I am unable to hold as projected by Mr. Chopra that higher pay-scale had been recommended for Lecturers in paragraph 5710 of the 4th Pay Commission Report.
A perusal of this paragraph shows that the scale of Rs. 2000-3500 was recommended for Lecturers.
It was further provided that instead of three grade pay structure, the School Lecturers shall be eligible for payment in higher scale, after 8 years service under the Assured Career Progression Scheme.
From the aforesaid, it is not possible to hold that only the general conversion scale was granted to Vocational lecturers, Vocational Masters and Vocational Mistresses.
In any event, it is not disputed that inspite of the aforesaid recommendations, the petitioners were granted the pay-scale of Rs. 6400-10640 on the basis of a clarification issued by the State of Punjab on 7112000.
In the aforesaid order, it was directed that the higher scale will be admissible to all Vocational Masters/Mistresses/Vocational Lecturers, who were in service prior to 871995.
If there was any doubt, it was further clarified by letter dated 1652003 in which it was observed as follows :-  "....... All the Vocational Masters/Mistresses appointed before 8.7.95 either they are degree holders or diploma holders have been granted the scale of Rs. 6400-10640.
You are advised that as per the letter issued by the Government dated 7112002, action may be taken immediately and in this regard report may be sent to the Government.
In case of violation of the orders, disciplinary action would be taken against you.
Reasons for not implementing the same, may be informed within one week."
16.
Pursuant to the aforesaid orders, the petitioners had received the arrears of salary also.
Having accepted the pay parity between Vocational Lecturers and Vocational Masters since 1975, the same is now sought to be withdrawn by order dated 1672003.
The order now passed by the State Government is as follows :-  "2.
Government has considered this case afresh and has decided that designation and scale of pay of Rs. 6400-10640 w.e.f 111996 will be admissible to only those vocational Masters/Mistresses who were appointed as Masters/Mistresses prior to 871995 and those who acquired the qualifications of Post Graduation or degree in Engineering by 871995.
If any other Vocational Masters/Mistresses/Lecturers has been allowed this pay-scale, that is not as per Government directive issued in consultation with the department of Finance.
So you are requested to recover the excess amount, if paid to uneligible vocational Master/Lecturers, after following the proper procedure under the relevant rules.
Intimation in this regard may be sent to Govt. within a week positively after collecting the same from the field offices.
3.
All other letters on the subject dated 7112002 an 1652003 may be treated as cancelled/withdrawn.
4.
This letters issues on the advice conveyed by OSD (lit) Department of Finance and Principal Secretary Finance vide their DO No. 1/149/01-IFPI-4808 dated 2752003 and DO No. 1/149/01-FPI/5846 dated 27.6.2003 respectively."
17.
In my opinion, the impugned order dated 1672003 deserves to be quashed on the short ground that it has been passed without complying with rules of natural justice.
Undoubtedly, it is an order which causes civil consequences.
The same could not have been passed without giving an opportunity of hearing to the concerned employees.
It is by now well settled that no orders causing civil consequences can be passed, without observing rules of natural justice.
18.
Mr. Patwalia has rightly relied on the judgment on Bhagwan Shukla (supra).
In the aforesaid case, it has been observed as follows :-  "3.
We have heard learned counsel for the parties.
That the petitioner's basic pay had been fixed since 1970 at Rs. 190 pm is not disputed.
There is also no dispute that the basic pay of the appellant was reduced to Rs. 181/- pm from 190/- pm in 1991 retrospectively w.e.f.
18121970.
The appellant has obviously been visited with civil consequences but he had been granted no opportunity to show-cause against the reduction of his basic pay.
He was not even put on notice before his pay was reduced by the department and the order came to be made behind his back without following any procedure known to law.
There, has, thus, been a flagrant violation of the principles of natural justice and the appellant has been made to suffer huge financial loss without being heard.
Fair play in action warrants that no such order which has the effect of an employee suffering civil consequences should be passed without putting the concerned to notice and giving him a hearing in the matter.........." 19.
Apart from this, once the posts had been equated, the petitioners cannot be given a pay-scale different from the Lecturers.
This view of mine will find support from a Division Bench judgment of this Court rendered in the case of GK Nagpal (supra).
In that case, the appellants, Laboratory Assistants/Shift Chemists were seeking the same pay-scale as the Line Superintendents, Auxiliary Plant Attendants/Sub-Station Operators and Bulldozer Operators.
Prior to January 1, 1978, all these categories of posts were in a uniform scale.
In successive pay revisions, the posts were equated.
However, with effect from 111978, the Laboratory Assistants and Shift Chemists were not put in the uniform scale of Rs. 570-1080.
The Division Bench has clearly held as follows :-  "5.
It is undoubtedly true that the posts of Laboratory Assistants are different from those of Line Superintendents etc.
It is also correct that they carry different qualifications as well as different duties.
However, the fact remains that all these posts had been treated as "equal" initially.
They were even treated as "equal" even at the time of the revision of the pay- scales.
If at a subsequent stage, they were to be treated differently, some reason should have been disclosed......... 6.
We are conscious of the fact that a differential treatment in the matter of pay-scales on the basis of educational qualifications and the nature of duties is permissible.
However, it is equally clear to us that if two categories of employees are treated as equal initially, they should continue to be so treated unless a differential treatment is justified by some cogent reason.
In a case where the nature of duties is drastically altered, a differential scale of pay may be justified.
Similarly, if a higher qualification is prescribed for a particular post, a higher scale of pay may be granted.
However, if the basic qualifications and the job requirements continue to be identical or as they were initially laid down, then the Court shall be slow to accept the action of the authority in according a differential treatment unless some good reason is disclosed.
In the present case, the only reason pointed out in the written statement is based on the qualifications and the job requirements.
There has been admittedly no change in either of the two parameters.
That being so, no new ground for creating a classification was made out."
20.
In the case of State of Andhra Pradesh v. G Ramakishan (supra), it has been held as under :-  "7.
It is our considered view that on the pleadings of the parties, the learned Single Judge rightly came to the conclusion that the State Government failed to show that there was any reasonable basis or intelligible differentia for giving effect to the enhanced rates of stipend for the Post- Graduate students of the Agricultural University from a date different from the effective date of the Post-Graduate students of the Medical Colleges, particularly when it was the specific case of the writ petitioners that the State Government had all along maintained parity in the matter of stipend paid to the Post-Graduate students of both the institutions.........." 21.
In the case of Pritam Dass (supra), the Single Bench of this Court considered the law with regard to equation of posts and pay scales.
JS Narang, J considered the objections raised by the State of Punjab and tabulated the same as follows :-  "6........
Learned counsel for the respondents had laid emphasis by projecting four reasons :- i) The scales of Sectional Officer in Public Works Department (B&R Branch) had been subsequently agreed to be revised from Rs. 570-1080 to Rs. 700-1200 keeping in view the technical nature of duties and responsibilities and that while revising the pay-scales, the Government had decided that this revision of pay-scales shall not be made the ground of revising the scales of other categories, enjoying the same un-revised scales; ii) The posts of Well Supervisors were really equated though both the posts were not provided the same pay-scale; iii) The duties and responsibilities provided for both the posts are not similar;  iv) By accepting the same scales of Well Supervisors would have a wide repercussion as there are other similar categories which would also raise similar demands."
22.
After considering the same, the reasons put forth were rejected with the following observations:-  "6.....
The Government cannot lose sight of the fact that right upto 1978, the Government had equated these posts and same pay-scales have been given to both the posts after the revision suffered on account of the recommendations made by the Pay Commission.
No difference or reasons have been spelt out as to in what manner the duties are different and so far as the qualifications are concerned, they remain the same and the Government has admitted itself while submitting reply to para No. 4 that these two posts had been equated way back in 1928 and the copy Annexure P-1 i.e. letter issued by the Government of Punjab has also been admitted.
Nothing has been brought on record that after this communication, any other communication had been issued to the effect that these two posts are not equated or equitable on any other ground.
In fact on the basis of this communication, the pay revision was made in respect of both the posts right upto 31.12.1977.
A very weak defence has been put up by stating that the duties and responsibilities attached to these posts are different and that by revising the pay scales of Supervisors, the repercussions would be very wide as if the revised pay-scales of Sectional Officers have not created any kind of repercussion.
I am not impressed by this argument, as the duties remained the same when pay parity was maintained upto 1978.
The duties are not at variance except that "buts" and "ifs" may have been added to give that colour.
But in that also the State has miserably failed."
23.
I am of the considered opinion that the aforesaid observations of the learned Single Judge are fully applicable to the facts and circumstances of the present case.
24.
Mr. Chopra has, however, relied on a judgment of the Supreme Court in the case of MP Rural Agriculture Extension Officers Association (supra).
In this case, the Supreme Court reiterated the law laid down earlier in the case of State of Mysore and another v. P Narasinga Rao (1968(1) SCR 467), in which it was observed as under :-  "16..........
It is well settled that though Article 14 forbids class legislation, it does not forbid reasonable classification for the purpose of legislation.
When any impugned rule or statutory provision is assailed on the ground that it contravenes Article 14, its validity can be sustained if two tests are satisfied.
The first test is that the classification on which it is founded must be based on an intelligible differentia which distinguishes persons or things grouped together from others left out of the group, and the second test is that the differentia in question must have a reasonable relation to the object sought to be achieved by the rule or statutory provision in question.
In other words, there must be some rational nexus between the basis of classification and the object intended to be achieved by the statute or the rule.
As we have already stated, Articles 14 and 16 form part of the same constitutional code of guarantees and supplement each other.
In other words, Article 16 is only an instance of the application of the general rule of equality laid down in Article 14 and it should be construed as such.
Hence there is no denial of equality of opportunity unless the person who complains of discrimination is equally situated with the person or persons who are alleged to have been favoured.
Article 16(1) does not bar a reasonable classification of employees or reasonable tests for their selection."
25.
These observations would not be applicable in the facts and circumstances of this case.
As noticed earlier, the Vocational Lecturers/Masters had been granted the same pay-scale as the Lecturers.
Initially, when the Vocational Masters/Lecturers were recruited, it had been mentioned in the advertisement that the recruitment shall be in Class III Education Service.
However, the posts of Vocational Lecturers/Masters/Mistresses were not included in the Class III Rules.
After persistent litigation, the respondents were directed by this Court to frame proper rules for permission (promotion ?)
of Vocational Lecturers/Masters to Class II Education Service.
These directions were issued on 1041991 in the judgment rendered in CWP No. 746 of 1987.
After considerable amount of delay, the posts of Vocational Lecturers and Masters were included in the Class III Rules.
The pay-scale granted to the Vocational Masters/Lecturers was identical to the pay-scale granted to other Class III Lecturers.
As noticed earlier, subsequently, the benefit granted to the petitioners was sought to be withdrawn.
The only justification for granting a different pay-scale to the petitioners is that some of the petitioners do not possess the Degree qualification which are necessary for being appointed on the post of Lecturer in Class III.
It is a matter of record that Vocational Lecturers and Vocational Masters all teach students of Class 10+1 and 10+2.
The only distinction between the two categories is that Class III Lecturers teach General Classes whereas Vocational Lecturers/Masters teach different trades to the students.
The State of Punjab was aware of the different qualifications possessed by the petitioners which were specialised in nature.
After considering the entire matter, the posts of Vocational Lecturers/Masters and other Lecturers were treated as equal.
In fact even now the Vocational Lecturers have been equated with Lecturers.
During the pendency of CWP No. 5437 of 3003, the respondents had issued a Notification on 2152003 providing a quota for promotion of Vocational Lecturers/Masters to Class II Irrigation Service.
Consequently, the aforesaid writ petition was withdrawn.
Clearly, the claims of the petitioners for equation had been accepted.
The writ petition was, therefore, disposed of as having become infructuous.
However, the Division Bench permitted the petitioners to make a representation for designation of their posts as Lecturers particularly, in the light of Notification dated 2152003.
Ultimately, the respondents have promulgated the Punjab Educational Service (School and Inspection) Group A Service Rules, 2004.
In these Rules, 15% posts have been reserved for promotion form amongst the Vocational Lecturers, Masters, Mistresses having an experience of working as such for a minimum period of seven years.
26.
The aforesaid provision makes it abundantly clear that all the Vocational Masters/Mistresses and Vocational Lecturers are entitled to the same treatment.
As noticed earlier, Degree in Engineering was required only if a Vocational Master was to teach the discipline of Engineering.
For other trade, the essential qualification was BA with ITI Diploma.
The qualifications were treated at par with Lecturers under the Class III Rules.
In fact the Lecturers were in a lower scale of pay of Rs. 250-550 whereas Vocational Masters were in the scale of Rs. 300-600.
This was a scale which had been enjoyed by the Head Masters.
In view of the above, I am of the considered opinion that the letter dated 1672003 (Annexure P-17) is wholly arbitrary and violates Article 14 of the Constitution.
It has the effect of undoing the legal position which had been affirmed by this Court in the judgment rendered in CWP No. 746 of 1987.
27.
I am of the considered opinion that the claim of the petitioners in CWP No. 7527 of 1995 would have also to be accepted in view of the findings recorded in CWP No. 10928 of 2003.
By order dated 3131995, the benefit of designation of Lecturers is sought to be withdrawn from Vocational Masters, although appointed earlier to 871995, but possess the qualification of BA and ITI Diploma.
It is a matter of record that the qualifications of Degree in Engineering and BA with ITI diploma were accepted as equal to Lecturers and Vocational Masters had throughout enjoyed the same pay-scale.
In fact initially when the Vocational Masters were recruited, they were placed in the scale of Rs. 300-600 whereas the Lecturers were in the lower scale of Rs. 250-550.
Even otherwise the qualifications even for appointment on the post of Vocational Lecturer were relaxed in the case of Vocational Masters only for a short period and were restored in the year 1991.
The diploma holder petitioners were recruited only during that short period.
There is no distinction in the duties which are performed by the Vocational Masters having degree in Engineering and the Vocational Masters teaching trades other than Engineering and having qualifications of BA + ITI diploma.
Therefore, it would not be possible to uphold the distinction which is sought to be made in the order dated 3131995 between degree holders and diploma holders.
Such a distinction would totally destroy encadrement of the petitioners in Class III Education Service brought about by the amendment dated 871995.
Order in CWP No. 10928 of 2003  28.
In view of the above, the letter dated 1672003 (Annexure P-17) is hereby quashed.
The petitioners are held entitled to the scale of Rs. 6400-10640.
Since the petitioners have been receiving the aforesaid scale on the basis of the interim orders of this Court, no further orders are required.
No costs.
