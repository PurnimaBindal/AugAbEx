Dr. Sarojnei Saksena, J - Appellant has challenged the Insurance Court's order dated 1041995, whereby its petition filed under Section 75 of the Employees' State Insurance Act, 1948 (in short the `Act') was dismissed.
2.
Adumberated facts of the case are that respondent-Employees' State Insurance Corporation passed the assessment order on 18/1911990, Exhibit R- 9, vide which the appellant was directed to deposit insurance contribution amounting to Rs. 546540 for the period from 1121984 to 30121985 and Rs. 1,364/- as interest upto 20101989.
As Shri MS Rana Insurance Inspector, visited the appellant's factory on 1621989, the appellant's factory was earlier being run in Bhagwati Building, Sector II, Parwanoo and production activity was shifted w.e.f.
March 1986 to plot No. 15-C, Sector II, Parwanoo.
The construction on plot No. 15-C was started by the appellant while its factory was still running in Bhagwati Building.
Shri MS Rana reported that as on 1121984 the petitioner had employed 10 persons for wages and was, therefore, covered under the provisions of the Act with effect from that date.
The appellant contended that it had not employed more than 9 workers during the disputed period, i.e., from, 1984 to 30121985.
The appellant also objected that before passing this assessment order, list of employees alleged to have been employed by the appellant on 1121984 showing their names, father's names, addresses, designation, length of service and their signatures or thumb impressions was not supplied to the appellant.
Even the list of those persons who were found present by Shri MS Rana on 1621989 in appellant's premises was not sent to the appellant.
It is also objected that the appellant cannot record after 12 months from the date it employed less than 10 workers.
The maximum period of coverage under the circumstances cannot be beyond 30.11.1985 and contribution more than Rs. 3,174 cannot be claimed.
The appellant also objected that it was a new construction which had nothing to do with the existing Bhagwati Building.
Therefore, the construction works cannot be clubbed with the works of the Bhagwati Building.
The construction work was also being made outside the appellant's factory located at that building.
The loading charges amounting to Rs. 1,723/- and Rs. 125/- cannot be covered for the said purpose.
No opportunity of hearing was given to the appellant before passing the impugned order.
3.
Respondent-Corporation filed written statement and denied all the allegations.
It inter alia contended that as a result of the inspection conducted by Shri MS Rana, Insurance Inspector, it was found that on 1121984 the appellant had employed 10 persons for wages.
His report was based on the attendance-cum-wages register and ledger produced by the petitioner at the time of his visit and therefore, there was no need for preparing a list of employees with full particulars, as alleged by the appellant.
4.
The Insurance Court recorded partie's evidence and came to the conclusion that on 1121984, 10 persons were employed by the appellant- applicant on 1121984 and since the appellant-factory is being run with the aid of power, it is liable to pay the amount claimed by the Corporation.
The appellant's learned counsel vehemently argued that the provisions of Section 45-A of the Act were wrongly invoked by the respondent-Corporation as no such blanket assessment could have been made by Shri MS Rana, Insurance Inspector.
Under section 45-A of the Act, if the Corporation intends to proceed under Section 45-A, before fixing the liability for contribution, it is required to record the names of the employees, name of the employer and wages payable to the employees.
Their list should be prepared giving their names and all other details specifically showing wages paid to them and the period for which the payment is made.
A rough and ready calculation of wages after deducting probable profits and then fixing the rate of contribution on the basis of the bills submitted by the contractors is not proper method of such assessment.
To support this contention, he has relied on Hindustan Times Ltd v. Employees' State Insurance Corporation, Delhi, 1988 Vol. 73 Factories Journal Reports 327.
6.
The appellant's learned counsel also contended that under the head of `Building, Loading and Unloading' could not have been included in the said list as they were not employees of the appellant.
They were employed by the contractor in the course of construction of building of the factory.
He also pointed out that the Insurance Court has wrongly held that two persons who were paid on 7121984 were employed by the appellant on 1121984.
The Insurance Court also fell into an error in including three persons who were paid wages on 1121984.
According to him, these three persons were paid wages for the work that they did not the appellant in November, 1984.
On 1121984 the three persons and those two persons, who were paid under the head `land development' on 7121984 were not in the employment of the appellant.
7.
Respondent's learned counsel submitted that no such blanket assessment was made by the Corporation on the basis of report of Shri MS Rana, Insurance Inspector.
The report of the Inspector is based on the ledgers and vouchers submitted by the appellant itself.
The details were given which are noted by the Insurance Court in the impugned order in para 7.
As per the attendance registers three person were employed for wages on 1121984.
One person employed for wages was booked under Labour Charges; two persons were paid under the head `Land Development Account' on 1121984, though the payment was made on 7121984 and payment was made under the head `Building Account' to 4 persons on 1121984.
Thus, it is evident that 10 persons were employed by the appellant on 1121984 for wages.
8.
Respondent's learned counsel relying on Employees' State Insurance Corporation v. K Ramchandran and others, 1986(3) SCC 239, contended that though two persons were paid under `Land Development Account' and 4 persons were paid under `Building Account' that will not make any difference because they were employed in the construction of additional building for expansion of the appellant's factory and therefore, they also fall within the definition of the employees under the Act.
He also pointed out that during the evidence, the appellant's witnesses have not stated that two persons who were paid on 7121984 were not in the appellant's employment on 1121984 and further 3 persons who were paid wages on 1121984 were discontinued from the appellant's employment from that date and this payment was made to them for the work which they did for the appellant in the month of November, 1984.
9.
After hearing the rival contentions of both the parties, in my considered view the appeal has not merit.
10.
In K Ramchandran's case (supra), the Apex Court has held that``Section 2(9)(i) of the Act comprehends casual workers as well.
Workers employed for construction of additional buildings for expansion of factory are employees.
Work of construction of additional factory buildings is work incidental or preliminary to or connected with the work of the factory.''
Thus, it is obvious that four persons who were paid under the head of `Building Account' on 7121984; 2 persons who were paid under the head of `Land Development Account' on 1121984 and 1 person who was paid wages as `Labour Charges' should be considered to be the employees of the appellant.
The appellant has not proved that 3 persons who were paid wages on 1121984 were paid for the work done by them for the appellant in the month of November and their services were discontinued with effect from 1121984.
The appellant has also not proved that 2 persons who were paid on 7121984 under the head of Land Development Account' were not in its employment on 1121984.
Similarly, the appellant also failed to prove that four persons who were paid on 7121984 under the Head of `Building Account' were not doing any work for the appellant on 1121984.
Thus, in my considered view, the Insurance Court has rightly held that on 1121984 10 persons were employed by the appellant.
11.
So far as invocation of the provisions of Section 45-A of the Act is concerned, from the lower Court's judgment, it is obvious that Shri MS Rana, Inspector, submitted his report on the basis of the entries made in the ledger and vouchers of the appellant's factory.
These documents were placed before him at the time of the inspection.
Since these details were found in the account-books of the appellant, it was not obligatory on the respondent- Corporation to prepare any list of workers showing all their details to fix their identity as well as the amount of wages paid to them.
If the details would not have been collected from the appellant's documents, the position would have been otherwise.
Therefore, the judgment relied on by the appellant's counsel in Hindustan Times case (supra) is of no help to the appellant.
12.
Thus, finding no merit in the appeal, it is hereby dismissed with costs, quantified at Rs. 500/-.
Appeal dismissed.
