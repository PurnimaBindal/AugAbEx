Gokal Chand Mital, J - This judgment will dispose of Civil Writ Petition No. 8736, 6006, 3547 and 9318 of 1987, and Civil Writ Petition Nos. 103, 486, 966, 892, 1000, 1102, 712, 3161, 1945, 2164, 1419, 3352, 4159, 3940, 3941, 3562, 1362, 1325, 3070 and 2618 of 1988, as in all these the main point to be decided is as to whether the State Government, Haryana Agricultural University, Kurukshetra University, Maharishi Dayanad University, Private Colleges and the Institutions in which teachers from Haryana Government are working on deputation in Union Territory, Chandigarh, were justified in making deduction of Rs. 45/- per month from the gross salary payable to the teachers on the basis of order Annexure P2.
For facility of reference, the facts of Civil Writ Petition No. 966 of 1988 will be taken notice of.
2.
The petitioners joined as teachers in the Education Department of Haryana, or the University mentioned above.
From the Education Department of Haryana, some of them were sent on deputation to Union Territory, Chandigarh.
Most of them joined after 1st January, 1973 and very few had joined before that date.
3.
On 6th September, 1974 the Secretary to Government, Haryana Finance Department, issued letter Annexure P2, to all the heads of Departments, informing that the Government had decided to sanction additional dearness allowances to its Class-I and Class-II officers.
The teachers, with whom we are concerned, are Class-II officers, and they are governed by items (4) and (5) shown at page 2 of the Annexure P2, at page 80 of the paper book.
For those teachers, who were drawing emoluments above Rs. 300/- and upto Rs. 900/-, between 111974 to 3111974, additional dearness allowance at the rate of 12 per cent of the emoluments subject to minimum of Rs. 48/- and maximum of Rs. 108/- was allowed and for the teachers drawing emoluments upto Rs. 1600/- per month, with effect from 121974 onwards, 15 per cent of emoluments subject to the minimum of Rs. 60/- and a maximum of Rs. 135/- was allowed.
According to para 2 of order Annexure P2, the additional dearness allowance had to be calculated on the emoluments which were to be total sum of pay, dearness allowance and the two ad hoc reliefs granted, with effect from 141972 and 1121972.
Para 4 of Annexure P2 mentions that the additional dearness allowance, which was being sanctioned was subject to the deductions to the extent indicated in Col. 5 of the table given below it in the Annexure.
Against 4th and 5th items of this table, Rs. 45/- was mentioned as the amount to be deducted as part of ad hoc relief to be adjusted.
In the Education Department, from the pay of teachers whether in service from 141972 or 111973, or those who joined after the issue of letter, Annexure P2, deduction of Rs. 45/- per month was made after the issue of letter Annexure P2, right till the writ petitions were filed in this Court in 1987 and onwards.
Since common questions were involved they were ordered to be heard.
4.
Surprisingly, more than 50 per cent of the teachers have joined service after the issue of the Annexure P2 and even from them Rs. 45/- were deducted probably on the basis of letter Annexure P2.
The petitioners kept quiet all through.
Similar treatment was meted out to the Punjab teachers.
Some of the Punjab teachers filed a suit and a Subordinate Judge vide his judgment dated 16111983 granted a decree of declaration that deduction of Rs. 45/- per month out of the additional amount was being wrongly deducted and thus directed the refund of the amount so recovered.
On appeal, the Additional District Judge upheld the decision of the trial Court in regard to deduction being illegal but held that the plaintiffs in that suit were entitled to relief only for a period of three years and two months, that is, which was within limitation, and allowed relief with effect from 1211980 onwards in regard to the arrears besides a declaration to the authorities not to deduct Rs. 45/- in future.
This judgment dated 12121984 is Annexure P4.
The State of Punjab came up in appeal to this Court, which was dismissed in limine, vide Annexure P5, and it is averred in para 8 of the writ petition that against the decision of this Court, Annexure P5, Special Leave Petition (Civil) 126761/1985 was dismissed by the highest Court of the land on 16th December, 1985.
Thereafter, representation Annexure P/6 is alleged to have been made to the State Government for not making deduction of Rs. 45/- per month and to pay arrears of the amount deducted to Haryana teachers but there is no date either on the Annexure or in the writ petition as to when it was filed.
When no action was taken by the State Government on the representation, these writ petitions have been filed from time to time in 1987 and 1988.
5.
After reading the judgment of the Civil Court contained in Annexure P/4 and the facts given in the writ petitions, I am of the view that the matter is identical and since the decision regarding the Punjab teachers has been upheld upto the Supreme Court, the same view deserves to be followed in these cases.
The matter can be disposed of in this manner but all the same I would like to say a few words as emerged from the un-controverted allegations contained in the writ petition as the State Government has remained content by not filing reply.
6.
The crucial document is Annexure P/2 and it mentions the grant of additional dearness allowance to Class-I and Class-II officers.
In para 1 the percentage of the additional amount has been classified for different emoluments.
Para 2 provides that for calculation of additional dearness allowance, the emoluments will be the sum total of the pay, dearness allowance and the two ad hoc reliefs granted with effect from 141972 and 1121972.
Para 4 of this Annexure seems to have created the problem.
It says that the additional dearness allowances which is being sanctioned, would be subject to the deductions to the extent indicated in column 5 of the table given below it.
In column No. 5 the amount to be deduct/adjusted shown is Rs. 45/- and the heading of column No. 5 is as follows :  "part of ad hoc relief to be adjusted" 7.
In view of this letter issued in September, 1974 benefit of the additional dearness allowance was granted to the teachers already in service at that time, whether before 111973 or after that and also to the teachers who joined after the issue of letter Annexure P/2.
Para 2 of this Annexure clearly shows that ad hoc relief granted with effect from Ist April, 1972 and Ist December, 1972, would be included in calculating the emoluments, and once that is so it does not appear to reason as to how deduction of Rs. 45/- was being made on the basis of table shown under para 4.
If there was some logic in the reasoning that the additional dearness allowance was being allowed subject to deduction of Rs. 45/- from the ad hoc reliefs granted with effect from Ist April, 1972 and Ist December, 1972, then deduction could only be made from those employees who had got the benefit of the aforesaid two ad hoc relief but in case of those employees who joined on 111973 or after Annexure P2 was issued, there could be no deduction of over payment of additional dearness allowance.
Yet this was being done.
8.
There is yet another important matter to be taken notice of, vide Annexure P3, an order issued on 2711976, pay scales of teachers were revised with effect from 111973.
In respect of all those employees who joined on or after 111973, or those who joined after Annexure P2 came into force, or those who joined after the issue of Annexure P3, there was no problem regarding the fixation of their pay and in regard to the additional dearness allowance.
Yet in spite of fixation of pay on the basis of Annexure P3, an order issued on 2711976 to be effective from 111973, the deduction of Rs. 45/- per month was being made right upto the date of filing of the writ petitions.
For making the deduction after the issue of order Annexure P3, there is no justification whatsoever.
Accordingly, I am in agreement with the decision taken in identical matter in Annexure P4 regarding Punjab teachers, which has been upheld by this Court and the Supreme Court and on that basis I hold that Rs. 45/- per month was being deducted wrongly from the pay of the petitioners in the writ petitions before me.
9.
The next question which calls for determination is for how much period the refund can be allowed to the petitioners.
In Annexure P4 payment of arrears has been allowed only for the period which would be within limitation and I am of the view that here also the petitioners cannot get the arrears beyond the period of limitation.
Their claim for the time barred period would be barred by laches and I would not exercise my extra-ordinary jurisdiction to award them the amount which would be beyond limitation in case they had filed the suits on the day the writ petitions were filed.
Accordingly, the relief for arrears would be limited to the period of three years and two months, two months being notice period, besides for the future.
10.
For the reasons recorded above, all these writ petitions are allowed with costs, counsel's fee being Rs. 250/- in each writ petition, and it is ordered that since the deduction of Rs. 45/- per month was illegal, the State or the other concerned authorities would pay to the petitioners the arrears for the period of three years and two months prior to the date of filing of the writ petitions in this Court, in each case, within a period of six months from today.
As regards the future, the State Government or the other concerned authorities are directed by issue of a writ of mandamus not to deduct Rs. 45/- per month from the total emoluments of the petitioners.
Petition allowed.
