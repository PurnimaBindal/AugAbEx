GS Singhvi, J - These four writ petitions involve determination of connected issues and while admitting these writ petitions the Court had directed hearing of the cases together.
Therefore, all these petitions are being disposed of by a common order.
2.
In CWP.
8719 of 1988 petitioner Gulab Singh has prayed for the quashing of orders Annexures P1 and P.3 by which respondent No. 4 has been assigned seniority over him and his representation against the seniority of respondent No. 4 has been rejected.
Petitioner has stated that he joined service as Constable on 341961.
He was promoted as Head Constable, Assistant Sub Inspector, Sub Inspector and then as Inspector of Police on the basis of his merit and seniority.
Petitioner has pleaded that respondent No. 4 joined service as Constable on 2.8.1958 and was promoted on various posts after the dates of his promotions.
On the basis of comparative statement of service particulars given out in para No. 3 of the writ petition, the petitioner has claimed that he is senior to respondent No. 4.
However, in the gradation list of Inspectors showing the position as on 111984, respondent No. 4 was shown senior to the petitioner.
By order Annexure P1 dated 28.8.1986 respondent No. 4 was given retrospective service benefits of confirmation and promotion and was, thus, made senior to the petitioner.
The representations submitted by the petitioner against the seniority assigned to respondent No. 4 came to be rejected by the Director General of Police, Haryana, vide Annexure P.3 dated 2641988.
Petitioner has questioned Annexures P1 and P.3 on the ground of arbitrariness and violation of his constitutional right of equality before law.
3.
In their reply, respondent Nos. 1 to 3 have stated that one Mohinder Singh had superseded the petitioner as well as respondent No. 4 at the time of preparation of seniority list of Head Constables.
This action was challenged by Gurdev Singh by way of a civil suit which was decreed by the trial Court and the appeals filed by the Government were dismissed by the Additional District Judge as well as the High Court.
This necessitated the passing of order Annexure P1 to implement the judgment and the decree passed by the learned Senior Sub Judge on 1261975.
Respondents have pleaded that failure of the petitioner to challenge higher seniority assigned to Mohinder Singh disentitles him from claiming any relief at this belated stage.
They have also pleaded that the writ petition be dismissed because the petitioner has not impleaded all those persons as parties to the writ petition who are likely to be affected by the acceptance of his claim of seniority over respondent No. 4.
4.
CWP.
No. 14648 of 1991 has been filed by Dalip Singh with the allegation that he joined service as Constable on 1071957 and he is senior to respondent No. 4 Gurdev Singh in the cadres of Constables, Head Constables, Assistant Sub Inspectors and Sub Inspectors.
However, on the basis of order Annexure P2 issued by the Deputy Inspector General of Police, Haryana Armed Police, Madhuban, respondent No. 4 has been placed above him.
Petitioner Dalip Singh has stated that during the pendency of writ petition No. 8719 of 1988 the claim made by Gulab Singh has been accepted by the Government and he has been granted seniority over and above respondent No. 4 vide order Annexure P4 dated 1521991.
Similarly, CWP.
No. 5517 of 1991 filed by one Raghbir Singh has been allowed by this Court on 4.9.1991 and, therefore, there is no justification for not giving benefit of seniority to the petitioner.
In their reply, respondent Nos. 1 to 3 have once again made reference to the seniority assigned to Mohinder Singh over and above the petitioner as well as Gurdev Singh as also the factum of civil suit filed by Gurdev Singh, which was decreed by the trial Court and against which the appeals were dismissed by the Additional District Judge as well as the High Court.
The respondents have pleaded that in view of the judgment and the decree passed by the competent Court the Government had to issue the order in favour of respondent-Gurdev Singh.
Regarding Annexure P4 dated 1521991 issued in favour of Gulab Singh, respondent Nos. 1 to 3 have admitted the facts mentioned in para 11 of the writ petition but have insisted that the petitioner is not entitled to any relief.
CWP.
No. 56 of 1992 has been filed by Harbans Singh.
Gulab Singh, who is the petitioner in CWP.
No. 8719 of 1988 has been impleaded as respondent No. 3 in this writ petition.
Petitioner Harbans Singh has stated that he joined service as Constable on 1.10.1955 and got his promotions on the posts of Head Constable, Assistant Sub Inspector, Sub Inspector and Inspector of Police on the basis of merit and seniority and that he was ranked senior to respondent No. 3 in the gradation list of Inspectors Class III.
He has pleaded that ignoring his seniority the Director General of Police has issued order dated 1521991 Annexure P.6 and has given benefit of seniority to respondent No. 3 over and above Gurdev Singh and has, thus, made him senior qua the petitioner.
This action of respondent No. 2 has been characterised as arbitrary, unreasonable and unjustified.
In their reply, the respondents have repeated those facts which have been incorporated in their reply to CWP.
No. 14648 of 1991.
They have pleaded that Gulab Singh was given benefit of higher seniority after due consideration of his representation but in so far as the petitioner is concerned, the writ petition deserves to be dismissed because of delay and laches.
CWP.
No. 1199 of 1992 has been filed by Gulab Singh with the allegation that he joined service as Constable on 24.1.1961 and that he is senior to respondent-Gurdev Singh in the cadre of Head Constables, Assistant Sub Inspectors, Sub Inspectors and that without any reason or rhyme respondent Nos. 1 to 3 have placed Gurdev Singh over and above him.
He has claimed seniority over Gurdev Singh and consequential benefits.
The reply filed by respondent Nos. 1 to 3 is similar to their reply to CWP.
No. 14648 of 1991 and, therefore, detailed reference to this reply is not necessary.
5.
From the facts which have come on record, it is clearly revealed that petitioner Gulab Singh claimed seniority over respondent Gurdev Singh.
Other petitioners have claimed seniority over Gulab Singh as well as Gurdev Singh.
One of the contentions raised in all the petitions is common, namely, that while re-fixing the seniority of Gurdev Singh the departmental authorities have not taken into consideration the fact that persons senior to Gurdev Singh had a preferential right to be assigned seniority in the various cadres.
The grievance made by Gulab Singh petitioner in CWP.
No. 8719 of 1988 has been redressed by respondent No. 2 with the issue of order dated 1521991 Annexure P4 in CWP.
No. 14648 of 1991.
Therefore, it can be said that the writ petitioner in CWP.
No. 8719 of 1988 has been given relief by the departmental authorities and his grievance no more survives.
However, other writ petitioners, who are senior to Gurdev Singh as well as Gulab Singh, continue to remain aggrieved on account of the failure of the respondents Director General of Police, Haryana, and Inspector General of Police, Haryana Armed Police, Madhuban, to assign them proper seniority.
In this context, it is significant to note that CWP.
No. 5517 of 1991 (Raghbir Singh v. State of Haryana and others) in which Gurdev Singh was one of the respondents, was allowed by a learned Single Judge and a direction was given to restore the seniority of petitioner Raghbir Singh over respondent Gurdev Singh with a further direction that the said petitioner Raghbir Singh be brought on List `F' atleast with effect from 3011985.
Of course, the Court directed that if it becomes necessary to ante-date his seniority in other ranks, the petitioner Raghbir Singh shall not be entitled to claim arrears of salary.
While CWP.
No. 5517 of 1991 was allowed on 4.9.1991, CWP Nos. 816 of 1990, 5127 of 1992, 10780 of 1992 and 19871 of 1992 have been dismissed by a common order dated 1491993.
In the second order a learned Single Judge of this Court held that the petition suffers from laches and also that the petitioners have not impleaded the affected persons as parties to the writ petition and on these counts the petitioners are not entitled to any relief.
6.
Ordinarily, there may have been justification for rejecting the writ petition Nos. 14648 of 1991, 56 of 1992 and 1199 of 1992 on the ground of delay and laches and non-impleadment of necessary parties.
However, a look at the averments made in these writ petitions show that petitioner Dalip Singh had submitted a representation on 2511991 and that representation of Dalip Singh has not been decided even though after about one year of the filing of the representation by Dalip Singh.
Relief has been given to Gulab Singh (Petitioner in CWP.
No. 8719 of 1988) with the issue of order dated 1521991.
Petitioner Harbans Singh did make a representation immediately after the issue of order in favour of Gurdev Singh.
His representation was rejected by respondent No. 2 on 2641988.
However, when respondent No. 3 decided to assign seniority to Gulab Singh over and above petitioner Harbans Singh again made a representation Annexure P.5 dated 411991.
Respondent No. 2 did not decide that representation but issued order dated 1521991, fixing seniority of Gulab Singh over and above Gurdev Singh and in the process made him senior to the petitioner also.
Gulab Singh, who is petitioner in CWP.
No. 1199 of 1992 submitted his representation dated 25.11.1990 and filed this petition soon after the issue of order dated 1521991.
All the three writ petitioners have claimed that they are senior to Gulab Singh who is petitioner in CWP No. 8719 of 1988.
Their grievance is that while Gulab Singh has been assigned seniority vide order dated 1521991 even when his writ petition was pending, but their cases have not been considered by respondent No. 2 for proper fixation of seniority.
The respondents have not chosen to explain as to under what circumstances the order dated 1521991 was issued in favour of Gulab Singh even while his writ petition was pending before this Court.
There can be no manner of doubt that Gulab Singh had claimed relief for ante-dated promotion with effect from 3.6.1985 and respondent No. 2 issued the order in his favour after about six years of that date.
If respondent No. 2 found legitimacy in the grievance made by Gulab Singh, in the absence of any satisfactory explanation for non-consideration of the cases of the petitioners who are admittedly senior to Gulab Singh, it is not possible to overlook the grievance of the petitioners.
In the context of order dated 1521991 the writ petitioners cannot be non-suited on the ground that they have approached the Court after long delay, particularly when their representations were not decided by respondent No. 2.
Rejection of representations of one or the other petitioner prior to 1521991 cannot be of any consequence because after rejection of such representation the respondent No. 2 himself thought that justice should be done to one of the similarly situated persons.
If Gulab Singh's case warranted re-fixation of his seniority and ante-dating of his promotion, the minimum expected of respondent No. 2 was that he should have considered the cases of all the similarly situated persons before the issue of order dated 1521991.
Since that has not been done, it is reasonable to conclude that grave injustice has been done to the petitioners.
Rejection of the writ petitions of these petitioners would amount to perpetuation of injustice done to them, particularly when respondent No. 2 has not even decided their representations.
7.
The plea that delay defeats equity and that the High Court should not exercise its certiorari jurisdiction under Article 226 of the Constitution of India has to be applied in the light of the facts brought on record of a particular case.
Relief cannot be denied to similarly situated persons merely because some have approached the Court at a particular point of time and others have not.
It is not possible to recognise the principle that litigious perseverance should pay in each and every case.
Therefore, I am of the opinion that the writ petitioners deserve to succeed to a limited extent, namely, that respondent No. 2 should consider the grievance made by the petitioners in their representations and take a decision in the light of the order dated 1521991.
8.
Order passed by this Court on 1491993 cannot appropriately be pressed into service for defeating the claim of the petitioners.
A look on that order shows that the Court has not considered the question of delay in the light of the order dated 1521991 and has not held that even though the writ petitions have been filed in the years 1991 and 1992, i.e. soon after the issue of the order dated 1521991, the petitioners should be non-suited on the ground of delay and laches.
9.
In the result, the writ petition No. 8719 of 1988 is declared as having become infructuous and is dismissed as such.
CWP Nos. 14648 of 1991, 56 of 1992 and 1199 of 1992 are allowed and a direction is given to respondent Nos. 1 to 3 to consider the representations made by the petitioners for refixation of their seniority and ante-dating their promotions in the light of the order dated 1521991.
If the respondents come to the conclusion that notwithstanding re-fixation of seniority and ante-dating of promotion of Gulab Singh vide order dated 1521991, the petitioners are not entitled to such relief, the competent authority should pass a speaking order.
While undertaking this exercise the competent authority shall consider the cases of all persons according to their original seniority and decide as to who are the persons entitled to relief of re-fixation of seniority and ante-dating of promotions.
This exercise shall be undertaken and completed by the respondents within four months of the submission of a certified copy of this order before the Director General of Police, Haryana, Chandigarh.
Parties to bear their own costs.
