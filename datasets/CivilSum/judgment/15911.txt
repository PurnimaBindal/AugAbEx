THB Chalapathi, J - The petitioner joined as Sub Inspector in the Central Excise Collectorate on 541962.
He was promoted as Inspector, Central Excise in the year 1972.
In the year 1977, the Central Government framed a scheme of Voluntary Retirement according to which the goverment employees, who have put in not less than 20 years of service, can seek voluntary retirement.
The petitioner made a representation on 1811982 seeking voluntary retirement under Voluntary Retirement Scheme and requested the authority to treat his representation as Three Months' Notice as required by the scheme, as he was completing 20 years of service on 441982.
The petitioner was informed that Three Months' Notice for voluntary retirement should be given only on completion of 20 years of service.
Thereafter the petitioner made another request on 2141982 for seeking voluntary retirement with effect from 441982.
In pursuance of the request of the petitioner, he was allowed to retire voluntarily under the said scheme with effect from 971982 vide Annexure P.9.
Before 971982 disciplinary proceedings were initiated against the petitioner on 371982.
The Articles of Charge were enclosed with the Memorandum dated 371982 (Annexure P13).
The first charge against the petitioner was that during the period from 2581980 to 1951981 the petitioner while functioning as Inspector, Central Excise, failed to maintain absolute integrity and devotion to duty and it was alleged that he visited premises of certain Gold Dealers and threatened them with ulterior motive and he also harassed and threatened an assessee.
The second article against the petitioner was that he levelled baseless allegations against the Senior Offices and refused to co-operate in the enquiry conducted by Shri DC Bhatia, Superintendent.
On these two charges, the petitioner was asked to submit his explanation by the Assistant Collector in his Memorandum bearing No. C.No.II-10(4)Vig/Sec/82/2441 dated 3081982.
It is an admitted fact that this charge was served on him on 571982 and thereafter he was allowed to retire voluntarily under the Voluntary Retirement Scheme with effect from 971982.
2.
In this writ petition, the petitioner is seeking a writ of mandamus declaring that he was deemed to have retired voluntarily from service on 371982 when the period of three months' notice expired and to direct the respondents to make final settlement of his dues and for a writ of certiorari to quash the disciplinary proceedings initiated against him on 371982 (vide Annexure P13) and also for a writ of prohibition for forebearing the respondents from taking action against the petitioner in pursuance of the said Memorandum of chargesheet.
3.
There is no dispute that the petitioner completed 20 years of service and he is entitled to seek voluntary retirement under the voluntary Retirement Scheme framed by the Central Government.
The order permitting him to retire from service (Annexure P.9) reads as follows:-  ``In pursuance of the Ministry of Home Affairs, Department of Personnel and Administrative Reforms, New Delhi's O.M.
No. 25013/7/77- Estt.
(A) dated 2681977, Shri Kartar Singh, Inspector, Hqrs.
Office, Chandigarh is hereby allowed to retire on retiring pension under the voluntary retirement scheme with effect from 971982 (AN) as requested by him vide his notice dated 2141982.''
Thus, in the said order, there is to reference to disciplinary proceedings initiated or started against the petitioner on 371982.
4.
When once an employee was allowed to retire, the master and servant relationship comes to an end and no disciplinary action can be taken against him unless the rules governing the employment permit such an action to be taken.
It is no doubt true that a chargesheet was issued to the petitioner before he was allowed to retire on 971982, but there is no stipulation in Annexure P.9 i.e. the order allowing him to retire voluntarily that he was permitted to retire voluntarily subject to the result of the disciplinary proceedings already initiated against the petitioner.
As held by the Supreme Court in State of Punjab v. Khemi Ram, AIR 1970 SC 214, there cannot be any doubt that when disciplinary action is sought to be taken against the Government servant, it must be done before he retires as provided by the rules.
If a disciplinary inquiry cannot be concluded before the date of such retirement, the course open to the Govt. is to pass an order of suspension and refuse to permit the concerned public servant to retire and retain him in service till such inquiry is completed and a final order is passed therein.
5.
Once the relationship of master and servant has come to an end by termination, no disciplinary action could be taken in respect of any misconduct relating to that period.
In the case on hand, though the chargesheet dated 371982 was served on the petitioner on 571982 but he was allowed to retire w.e.f.
971982.
When the order allowing the petitioner to retire voluntarily was passed on 3171982, it was in the knowledge of the authorities that a chargesheet has already been issued to the petitioner on 371982.
In such a case the authorities should not have allowed the petitioner to retire voluntarily unconditionally.
Allowing the petitioner to retire from service voluntarily amounts to waiver of the disciplinary proceedings initiated against him.
6.
In this view of the mater, it is not necessary to go into the question whether the petitioner is deemed to have retired on 371982 when Three Months' Notice period expired.
Even otherwise, it is difficult to accept the contention of the petitioner that he should be deemed to have retired on 371982.
It is a benefit conferred on a Govt. Servant under the scheme to seek voluntary retirement only with the permission of the authorities.
The acceptance of offer seeking voluntary retirement is a condition precedent.
The petitioner's request for voluntary retirement was accepted on 3171982, with effect from 971982.
Therefore, the petitioner is deemed to have retired only on 971982, but not with effect from 371982 as contended by him.
7.
Even assuming for a moment that the disciplinary proceedings against the petitioners can be continued irrespective of the fact that he was allowed to retire voluntarily w.e.f.
971982 as the disciplinary proceedings initiated before his retirement.
In my view, it will be a futile exercise at this stage.
This Court, at the time of admission of the writ petition, granted stay of further proceedings in pursuance of the Memorandum dated 371982 and about 15 years have elapsed.
As already stated above, the charges against the petitioner are to effect that he failed to maintain absolute integrity and devotion to duty and he harassed and threatened an assessee whose name was not mentioned and that he did not co-operate in the enquiry proceedings.
No specific instances have been given in any one of the articles of charges.
The witnesses were not mentioned.
The articles are vague.
It is difficult to prove the said vague charges after lapse of 15 years.
No useful purpose will be served if the disciplinary proceedings are allowed to continue at this stage.
In my view, it will be a futile exercise only.
I am, therefore, of the opinion that it is un-necessary wastage of time and public exchequer to conduct the enquiry at this stage.
In this view of the matter, I am also of the opinion that there is no point in allowing in the disciplinary proceedings to continue against the petitioner.
8.
The writ petition is, accordingly, allowed and the respondents are directed not to proceed with the inquiry initiated against the petitioner in pursuance of the Memorandum dated 371982.
The respondents are further directed to settle all the retiral benefits that have accrued to the petitioner on his seeking voluntary retirement within three months from today if they are not already settled.
However, there will be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
