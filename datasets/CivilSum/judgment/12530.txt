VK Bali, J (Oral) - After conclusion of the arguments, Mr. Masih the learned Assistant Advocate General, Punjab, states on the basis of record that so far as Petitioners Nos. 1, 2, 7, 8, 16, 18 to 31, 33 to 36, 38 to 40, 42 to 50, 54 to 58, 64, 65, 66, 69, 63 and 78 are concerned, they have since been confirmed on the same date by the impugned order (Annexure P-9) itself and that being the position, they had no cause of action and in any case, the matter on their behalf has become infructuous.
Mr. Vinod Sharma, learned counsel for the petitioners was unable to controvert the position as noticed.
That being so, the writ petition on behalf of the aforesaid petitioners is disposed of with the observation that since they had already been confirmed as Head Constables on 171989 and the relief asked for by them has already been granted to them, there is no cause of action for them to come to this Court.
This Court is now left to decide the cause of other petitioners for which facts need necessary mention.
2.
The prayer in the petition filed under Article 226 of the Constitution of India on behalf of the petitioners, other than mentioned above, is to issue a writ in the nature of certiorari quashing order dated 1681989 (Annexure P-9) vide which respondents 6 to 25 were confirmed as Head Constables without considering the case of the other Head Constables like the petitioners.
It is pleaded that the petitioners after having passed the Lower School Course as Constables were brought on the promotion list C-I and on the basis of seniority, they were promoted as officiating Head Constables on different dates.
The date of promotion of some of the petitioners is reflected from Annexures P-1 to P-6 attached to the petition.
The promotion in the Police Department is governed by Chapter XIII of the Punjab Police Rules, 1934, Volume II and according to Rule 131, promotion from one rank to another and from one grade to another in the same rank is made by selection tempered by seniority and while promoting efficiency and honesty are to be seen as the main factors governing the selection.
The petitioners plead that for the purpose of regulating promotion amongst the enrolled police officers, six promotion lists i.e. A, B, C, D, E and F are to be maintained.
The aforesaid lists are to be maintained in each district in view of the provisions contained in Rules 136, 137, 138 and 139.
This is for regulating promotion to selection grade Constables to the rank of Head Constables and Assistant Sub-Inspectors.
Petitioners, it is further pleaded, were brought on list 'C' under Rule 138, after passing the Lower School Course from Police Training College, Phillaur.
Annual Confidential Reports prepared under Rule 1317 of the petitioners are stated to be good, very good and excellent.
Under Rule 1318 of the Punjab Police Rules, Police officers promoted in rank are to be on probation for a period of two years and the Appointing Authority by a special order in each case can permit the period of officiating service to count towards the period of probation.
Under the rules, the maximum period of probation is two years.
Under Rule 1319, a Constable receiving President's Police award and First Services Medal is entitled to be promoted to the first substantive vacancy of Head Constable which occurs in the district where he is serving subsequent to the award of the medal being gazetted.
He is also entitled to the selection grade on promotion.
The case of the petitioners is that none of the respondents was recipient of President's Police Medal or Fire Services Medal.
Inspector General of Police issued instructions vide letter No. 12614-74/B dated 22.5.1964 regarding confirmation of promoted police officers for regulating the exercise of discretion given under Rule 1318 for treating the officiating period for probation for the purposes of confirmation.
Following guiding principles have been laid for confirmation officiating officers in their ranks :  "(a) Where an officer has been officiating for at least 3 years and has an outstanding record of service, he may be confirmed straightway.
(b) Where an officer has a good record of service, he may also be confirmed without being placed on probation, if he has put in 4 years officiating service.
(c) Where an officer has an average record of service, he may be confirmed without being placed on probation if he has put in 5 years' officiating service.
(d) As regard the officers falling under categories (a) (b) (c) above, the period of probation may be fixed, if necessary, keep in view the extent to which the officiating service falls short of 3, 4 and 5 years respectively.
(e) Officiating officers whose honesty or moral character is suspected even once during their officiating service should, if at all considered for confirmation, be placed on two years' probation.
3.
Instructions aforesaid have been placed on record as Annexure P-7.
The petitioners further plead that in view of the instructions Annexure P-7, number of persons were getting undue importance by confirming them before their seniors.
This, obviously, affected their legal right and realising this condition, the Director General of Police, Punjab, Chandigarh vide Memo No. 83893-8433/E-2 dated 21st June, 1989, issued instructions to the effect that out of turn confirmation could be ordered in exceptional circumstances and that too with the prior approval of Director General/Inspector General of Police, Punjab.
Instructions referred to above have also been placed on record as Annexure P-8.
233 vacancies of Head Constables were made permanent vide Punjab Govt.
Memo No. 4/4/87-2HC (I)671 dated 611989 and Director General of Police endorsement No. 2589-2675/E-2 dated 3031989 and vide order dated 1681989, Senior Superintendent of Police, Amritsar, confirmed a number of Head Constables by giving them the benefit of officiating service towards the probationary period.
Copy of the order is Annexure P-9 and it is reiterated that it is this order which is sought to be quashed by issuing a writ in the nature of ceriorari.
4.
It is pleaded by the learned counsel appearing for the petitioners that while confirming the Head Constables, a pick and choose method was adopted, where juniors have been confirmed without assigning any reason or even considering the case of seniors.
It is further pleaded that an arbitrary and discriminatory attitude was applied by the respondents and the seniority of the Head Constables was not taken into consideration.
The Head Constables who were confirmed vide impugned order did not have exceptional outstanding reports.
Pleadings as reflected above would, thus, reveals that the main point praying setting aside of Annexure P-9, is confirmation of the junior persons without considering the petitioners despite the fact that the seniors are fully eligible and had no blemish whatsoever in their service record.
5.
The cause of the petitioners has been opposed and two identical written statements have been filed, one by respondents No. 1 to 5 and the other by some of the private respondents.
It is pleaded that Deputy Inspector General of Police, Jalandhar Range, has only called for the service particulars of Head Constables upto 111988 vide his T.P.M.
No. 23423-30/JR-A-I, dated 1181989 copy of which is Annexure R-1.
So the Head Constables confirmed or put on probation with effect from 171989 do not merit selection for Intermediate School Course at Police Training College, Phillaur which commenced from 1101989.
The cases of petitioners Nos. 3 to 6, 9 to 11, 15, 17, 32, 37, 41, 51, 52, 60, 62 and 70 to 76 were duly considered and their service record was found to be average.
Since they have not completed five years' officiating service, they were not eligible for straightaway confirmation.
As regards petitioners No. 12 to 14 and 17, they were put on probation for a period of two months ie a period which falls short for five years of their officiating service.
Vacancies for petitioners No. 53, 59 and 61 were kept reserved for want of upto date Annual Confidential Reports and as soon as these were to be received their cases for confirmation were to be considered with effect from 171989.
Regarding petitioners No. 63 and 68, it is pleaded that they stood confirmed with effect from 171989 at Sr. No. 75 and 76 respectively.
6.
A perusal of Annexure P-2 would, however, demonstrate that cases of petitioners No. 3 to 6, 9 to 11, 15, 17, 32, 37, 41, 51, 52, 60, 62, 67, 68 and 70 to 76 were not considered at all.
Assuming it that the aforesaid petitioners and others whose names were not considered had only average record, as is the case pleaded by the respondent-State, in considered view of this Court, they could not be given a treatment different to the one which was given to the persons having an average record and whose case was admittedly considered by passing order Annexure P-9.
The case of two Head Constables Jaswant Singh and lqbal Chand who had crossed the efficiency bar and against whom nothing adverse was found was dealt with while confirming them first in point of time.
It was thereafter that the case of Head Constables who had outstanding record of service and who had more than 3 years' service as Officiating Head Constables came for consideration and they were straightaway confirmed.
The Head Constables who had good record of service and had more than four years of officiating service as Head Constables were also confirmed straightaway with effect from 171989.
Thereafter the case of those Head Constables who had average record of service came into consideration and this is how the matter was dealt with :  "The following Head Constables who have average record of service and have put in more than 5 years officiating service as Head Constables are confirmed with effect from 171989 in the vacancies noted against each.
Their two years' officiating service as Head Constables is counted towards the probation period under PPR 1318."
16 Head Constables were under consideration for this category.
It is thereafter that those whose case was identical with that of the petitioners on any count came for consideration.
It was observed while dealing with their case :  "The following officiating Head Constables have average record of service but have not completed five years' officiating service as Head Constables and as such they are placed on probation period w.e.f.
171989 for the period which falls short of 5 years' as indicated against their names against 233 vacancies made permanent vide Punjab Govt.
Memo No. 4/4/87-2 - HC(I) 671, dated 6189 and DGP/Punjab's endst.
No. 2589-2675/E-2 dated 3031989.
Their officiating service as indicated against each is counted towards the period of probation under Punjab Police Rules 1318."
There were 25 such persons whose case was considered in the manner as indicated above.
The matter does not rest there as thereafter the case of Head Constable Balwinder Singh with regard to whom it is stated that he did not merit straightaway confirmation due to indifferent record of service came for consideration.
He was placed on six months' probation with effect from 171989 against one of the 233 vacancies made service as Head Constable was counted towards the period of probation under P.P.R.
138.
7.
The relevant part of Annexure P-9 which has been extracted would, thus, manifest beyond doubt that petitioners who are similarly situate were given a differential treatment, thus, straightaway offending Article 14 of the Constitution Learned Counsel appearing for the respondent-State could not distinguish the case of those Head Constables who had an average service record and who had not completed five years' of service on the post of Head Constable.
That being so, this Court is left with no choice but to direct the respondents to give the same treatment to the petitioners, which was given to the Head Constables who had not completed five years' officiating service and had average service record, and place them on probation with effect from 171989 for the period which falls short of five years' against 233 vacancies which were made permanent vide Punjab Government memo No. 4/4/87-2HC(I) 671, dated 611989 and DGP Punjab's endst No. 2589-2675/E-2 dated 3031989.
Their officiating service would be counted towards the period of probation under the Punjab Police Rule 1318.
8.
Petitioner Nos. 53, 59 and 61 were not considered for confirmation in view of non-availability of their A.C.Rs.
However, in their case a direction is issued to the respondents to trace their A.C.Rs.
and consider their case from the date the cases of orders were considered.
This direction is applicable only if the aforesaid exercise has not been done earlier.
9.
The petition is, thus, allowed the manner indicated above leaving, however, the parties to bear their own costs.
Petition allowed.
