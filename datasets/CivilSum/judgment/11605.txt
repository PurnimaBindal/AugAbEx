Mehtab S Gill, J - The petitioner has prayed for the issuance of a writ in the nature of Certiorari for quashing Resolution dated 2262000, Annexure P- 12, vide which he was proposed to be compulsorily retired under the provisions of Rules 326 and 532 of the Punjab Civil Services Rules, as applicable to the State of Haryana and the adverse remarks recorded in the Annual Confidential Report (in short A.C.R.)
for the year 1990-91, Annexure P-2 as having been recorded on account of extraneous considerations and mala fide intentions of respondent No. 4 and has further prayed to issue a writ in the nature of Prohibition/Mandamus directing respondent Nos. 1 and 2 to immediately reinstate him on the post of Superintending Engineer and to allow him to retire at the age of 58 years i.e. on attaining the age of superannuation in accordance with the provisions of Punjab Civil Services Rules as applicable to the State of Haryana.
2.
The petitioner has averred that the Haryana State Agricultural Marketing Board (hereinafter called the Board) is under the administrative control of the State of Haryana.
The service conditions of the employees of the Board are governed by the Haryana State Agricultural Marketing Board Service Rules, 1974 (hereinafter called the "1974 Rules").
Respondent No. 1, Commissioner and Secretary to Government Haryana, Department of Agriculture and Marketing is also the Chairman of the Board.
Pay scales of the employees of the Haryana State are applicable to the employees of the Board.
For all intents and purposes, the Board is "Other Authority" within the ambit of Article 12 of the Constitution of India Rule 14 of the 1974 Rules pertains to leave and other matters concerning the service conditions of the employees of the Board and it has been stated in the said Rule that the Board shall be governed by the Punjab Civil Service Rules as applicable to the State of Haryana.
Since no specific Rule has been framed in the 1974 Rules for compulsorily retiring an officer/employee, the Punjab Civil Services Rules, as applicable to the State of Haryana are applicable to the petitioner.
3.
The petitioner has further averred that he was promoted as an Executive Engineer with effect from 6.9.1972 on the basis of his outstanding service record and seniority.
He was given the charge of the post of Superintending Engineer on 3151978 and thereafter promoted on regular basis with effect from 2191978.
His work and conduct throughout his service career had been either Good, Very God or Outstanding and on the basis of his meritorious service record, he was promoted as Chief Engineer on 5.12.1990.
Thereafter, he worked on various senior posts like Chief Project Engineer, Nodal Officer of Project and Environmental Planning.
He was serving as a Superintending Engineer (Headquarters) in Panchkula.
His service record except for the years 1990-91 and 1991-92 has been Good, Very Good, Outstanding and unblemished.
In view of the his performance, the Chairman of the Board had written various letters of appreciation (Annexure P-1).
In the year 1990-91, the Board made entries in his ACR without disclosing any substantial material against him.
In his ACR for the year 1991-92, average entries were made by the Chief Engineer which have been accepted by the Chief Administrator of the Board (Annexure P-2) and the same were conveyed to him vide letter dated 1761998 after a gap of six years.
Representations dated 591991 and 9.7.1998 (Annexure P-3) were made by the petitioner against the entries made in his ACRs for the years 1990-91 and 1991-92.
In his ACR for the year 1990-91, the Board made entry "doubtful integrity" without disclosing any material and the basis for making such entry.
The adverse entries were conveyed to him on 4.9.1991.
A representation was made by him to the Board i.e. competent authority on 591991 but this representation is still pending with the Board.
Resolution dated 12121990 makes it clear that in case of Superintending Engineer, the appropriate authority to decide the representation, regarding ACRs is the Board.
Vide letter dated 3111998, the Chief Administrator of the Board requested Shri Dharamvir IAS, then Commissioner of Hisar Division to record ACRs of petitioner for the period of 7121994 to 3131995 and 1.4.1995 to 28.11.1995 as Shri Dharamvir was posted as Chief Administrator of the Board during the said period.
From the above letters, it is clear that the accepting authority of the ACRs of Superintending Engineer is the Chief Administrator of the Board.
His representation against the adverse remarks recorded in the ACR for the year 1990-91 should have been decided by the Board being competent authority but this was not done and the Commissioner and Secretary to the Government of Haryana without giving any fair opportunity rejected his representation made against the adverse remarks recorded in the ACR for the year 1990-91.
Both the orders dated 151998 and 481995 have been termed by the petitioner as illegal, null and void and without any jurisdiction.
Copies of orders dated 151998 and 481995 have been collectively attached as Annexure P7.
The Board was going to hold a meeting on 23111998 and the petitioner being apprehensive that he may not be compulsorily retired at the age of 55 years after taking into consideration his ACRs for the years 1990-91 and 1991-92 filed a suit in the Court of Civil Judge (Senior Division), Panchkula against the respondent Nos. 3 and 4 to protect his interest.
The Civil Judge (Junior Division), Panchkula passed the following order dated 23111998 :-  "Anand Parkash v. Haryana State Agricultural Marketing Board.
Present : Sh. VK Verma, counsel for the plaintiff.
Ms. Uma Gupta, counsel for the defendant.
Power of attorney on behalf of the defendant filed who seeks adjournment for filing written statement.
Now, to come up on 11121998 for filing written statement.
Heard on the application under Order 39, Rules 1 & 2 read with Section 151 CPC.
It is directed that the defendants before taking any decision regarding the retirement of the plaintiff at the age of 55 years shall thoroughly consider and examine the case of the plaintiff strictly in accordance with the service rules as applicable including representation, if any, made by the plaintiff."
4.
The Chairman of the Board called the petitioner to his office on 2262000 and told him that he was going to be compulsorily retired despite the fact that his representations have not been decided.
A decision was taken by the Board on 2262000 to compulsorily retire the petitioner (Annexure P-12).
The petitioner filed an application under Section 151 CPC before Civil Judge (Junior Division), Panchkula for restraining respondent Nos. 2 and 3 for compulsorily retiring him without obeying the orders of the Court dated 23111998.
The petitioner was not granted stay.
the respondents were served in the civil suit and while putting in appearance in the Court, they showed the order of the compulsory retirement of the petitioner.
5.
Notice of motion was issued.
6.
Written statement was filed on behalf of the respondent Nos. 2 and 3.
7.
I have heard counsel for the petitioner and the counsel for the respondents and perused the petition, written statement and the annexures attached therewith.
8.
Counsel for the respondents has stated that four ACRs of the petitioner in which adverse remarks have been recorded relate to the years 1987-88, 1988-89, 1990-91 and 1991-92.
In the ACR for the year 1987-88 the reputation of the petitioner for integrity remained poor.
In the ACR for the year 1988-89, it was written that the petitioner was a intriguer.
In the ACR for the year 1990-91, it was written that his integrity was doubtful and for the year 1991-92, his reputation for honesty was recorded to be average.
Apart from this, the conduct of the petitioner was not satisfactory.
The service career of the petitioner had been controversial as borne out from Annexures R2/2 to R2/7.
He has further stated that reliance cannot be placed on letters dated 1141991 and 2241991, (Annexure P1) as these are not available on the official record.
These letters seem to have been manipulated.
He further stated that the petitioner before the joining service in the Board was involved in making excess/fraudulent payment worth Rs. 154 lacs in the item of earth work while working as SDF, PWD (B&R) in the name of Shri AP Singla during the year 1969-70.
A civil suit for the recovery of Rs. 51,452.62 is pending in the Court of Civil Judge (Senior Division), Ambala.
In fact, Shri AP Singla is no other person than Shri Anand Parkash, the present petitioner.
I do not find any force in the arguments put forth by the counsel for the respondents.
9.
In the ACR of the petitioner for the year 1990-91, he has been adjudged as below average and in the column for reputation for honest, "Doubtful integrity" has been written.
In the ACR for the year 1991-92, against the column of reputation for honesty, the petitioner has been adjudged as "Average".
These ACRs are collectively attached as Annexures P-2 with the writ petition.
Representation was submitted by the petitioner to the competent authority on 591991 (Annexure P-3) but the same has not been decided till date.
Annexure P-5 is the resolution in which it has been clearly stated that the Board has decided to delegate the powers to the Committee consisting of Chairman of the Board, Chief Administrator and the Secretary qua recording of annual confidential reports of the officers and the employees.
The Chief Administrator has been made accepting authority.
10.
It has been held in State of UP v. Yamuna Shanker Misra and another, 1997(4) Supreme Court Cases 7 : 1997(2) SCT 234 (SC) that the reporting/reviewing officer should confront the reported officer with his knowledge before forming an opinion to make adverse remarks so that the reported officer gets an opportunity either to improve himself or to explain his conduct.
This principle of natural justice was not followed in this case qua the petitioner.
11.
The counsel for the petitioner has drawn my attention to the civil suit filed by the petitioner before the Civil Judge (Junior Division) on 20111998 and the Civil Judge in his order dated 23111998 had specifically directed that the defendants before taking any decision regarding retirement of the plaintiff (petitioner) at the age of 55 years shall thoroughly consider and examine the case of the plaintiff strictly in accordance with the Service Rules as applicable including representation, if any, made by the plaintiff (petitioner).
This order was not taken into consideration by the Board before passing order of compulsory retirement of the petitioner.
It is also clear that the adverse remarks which was recorded in the ACRs for the years 1990-91 and 1991-92 were communicated to the respondent on 1761998.
No decision was taken till the filing of the writ petition on the representation made by the petitioner against the adverse remarks recorded in the ACR for the year 1990- 91 though in the order passed for compulsory retirement of the petitioner, Annexure P-12, it has been mentioned that this representation has been rejected.
This has also been done after elapse of about nine years.
12.
Rule 326(a)(c)(1) of the Punjab Civil Services Rules, Volume I, Part I, as applicable to the State of Haryana is reproduced below :-  "326(a) Except as otherwise provided in other clauses of this rule, every Government employee shall retire from service on the afternoon of the last day of the month in which he attains the age of fifty-eight years.
He must not be retained in service after the age of compulsory retirement, except in exceptional circumstances with the sanction of the competent authority in public interest, which must be recorded in writing : Provided that the age of compulsory retirement for class IV Government employees shall be sixty years : Provided further that a Government employee whose date of birth is the first of month shall retire from service on afternoon of the last day of the preceding month on attaining the age of fifty-eight or sixty years, as the case may be.
(b) xx xx xx (c) The following are special rules applicable to P.W.D.
Officers :- (1) Except as otherwise provided in this sub-clause, Government employees in the Haryana Service of Engineers, Class I (B&R, IB and Electricity) must retire on reaching the age of 58 years, and may be required by the competent authority to retire on reaching the age of 50 years if they have not attained the rank of Superintending Engineer."
13.
Sub-rule (c) states that except otherwise provided, Government employees in the Haryana Service of Engineers, Class (B&R, IB and Electricity) must retire on attaining the age of 58 years and may be required by the competent authority to retire on reaching the age of 50 years if they have not attained the rank of Superintending Engineer.
In this case, when the petitioner was retired, he was Superintending Engineer and, thus, could not have been retired at the age of 55 years but as per this Rule he could have been retired at the age of 58 years.
14.
Counsel for the petitioner has stated at the Bar that the petitioner was not retired at the age of 55 years but was retired compulsorily vide order dated 2262000, Annexure P-12, when he had attained the age of 56 years and about five months.
The date of superannuation of the petitioner is 30.11.2001.
Thus, this clearly shows that the Rules have been violated and decision regarding adverse entry, Annexure P-2, of doubtful integrity cannot be taken now into consideration.
The counsel for the respondent could not convince me as to what were the grounds and reasons for making entry of doubtful integrity.
As already discussed above, the Board has given appreciation letters to the petitioner.
15.
Counsel for the respondents has contended that Rule 326(a)(c)(1) as reproduced above was amended on 2381988 and the petitioner has deliberately concealed this fact.
Counsel for the petitioner has stated that since the petitioner was appointed as Superintending Engineer on 3151978, the said amendment cannot be made applicable to him retrospectively and the same will not have any bearing on the rights of the petitioner.
He has further stated that Service Rules of an employee cannot be amended to his detriment and the rule as existed on the date the petitioner was taken in the cadre would be applicable.
In support of his contention, he has relied upon the judgment of Hon'ble Supreme Court in the case of titled P. Mohan Reddy v. E.A.A.
Charles & others, JT 2001(3) SC 1 : 2001(2) SCT 542 (SC).
16.
On consideration of respective contentions of the counsel for the parties, I am of the view that the amendment made in the year 1988 is not applicable to the case of petitioner who was appointed on the post of Superintending Engineer on 3151978 and thereafter promoted on regular basis on 2191978.
The amendment cannot operate retrospectively and Rule 326(a)(c)(1) as existed on 3151978 is applicable to the petitioner.
17.
The counsel for the respondents has stated that the petitioner embezzled an amount of Rs. 51,45652 before he had been appointed in the Board and when he was working as Sub Divisional Engineer in the Haryana, PWD, B&R Branch on ad hoc basis.
the learned counsel for the respondents further stated that the petitioner represented his name as AP Singla and, thus, when the charge was given, it was found that it was the petitioner who had embezzled the amount and that he used the name of other person viz. AP Singla.
This argument of learned counsel for the respondents does not cut any ice.
As per appointment letter and the service record from the year 1972 onwards, it has been found that the name of the petitioner has been mentioned as Anand Parkash son of Shri Babu Ram.
18.
No other point was raised by the counsel for the petitioner or the respondents.
19.
In the result, the respondents are directed to reinstate the petitioner with all consequential benefits.
The respondents are further directed to retire the petitioner on his attaining the age of superannuation.
The ACRs for the years 1990-91 and 1991-92 qua the column "integrity doubtful" and "Integrity Average" and Annexure P12 and quashed.
20.
With the above directions and observations, this writ petition is allowed.
Petition allowed.
