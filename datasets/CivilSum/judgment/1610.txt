GS Singhvi, J - Shri SP Gupta and four others sought this Court's intervention by filing Civil Writ Petition No. 15652 of 1992 for quashing the letter dated September 6, 1990 written by the Home Secretary, Chandigarh Administration to the Secretary, House Allotment Committee to take action for eviction of those Journalists/Press Correspondents who had attained the age of 60 years or who were transferred outside Chandigarh.
They also prayed for quashing orders passed by the House Allotment Committee for cancellation of allotments of houses made to them and the notices issued by the Sub Divisional Magistrate, Chandigarh-cum-Estate Officer under the Public Premises (Eviction of Unauthorised Occupants) Act, 1971 (hereinafter referred to as the Act).
By an order dated September 6, 1993, a Division Bench dismissed the Writ Petition on the ground that the Journalists/Press Correspondents are not entitled to allotment of residential houses under the Government Residences (Chandigarh Administration Pool) Allotment Rules, 1972 (hereinafter referred to as 1972 Rules).
While doing so, the Division Bench referred to the decision taken in the meeting held on July 5, 1968 under the Chairmanship of the then Chief Commissioner, Chandigarh, Shri MS Randhawa as well as 1972 Rules.
Some of the observations made in the order dated September 6, 1993, are extracted below :-  "It will be seen that as per this decision the quota of houses for Press Correspondents had been fixed at 33, a number which has since been exceeded by almost three times, inasmuch as according to the affidavit of the Secretary, House Allotment Committee, Chandigarh, the number of houses already allotted to Journalists or Press Correspondents is 94.
There is no material on record to show how, why and when any decision was taken raising the limit of the number of houses to be allotted to Journalists or Press Correspondents to this figure.
To the pointed query put to the Standing Counsel for the Chandigarh Administration, in this behalf, no answer was forthcoming.
It was the plea of the petitioners that allotment of Government Residential Houses to Journalists or Press Correspondents was governed by the provisions of Government Residences (Chandigarh Administration Pool) Allotment Rules, 1972 (hereinafter referred to as the "Allotment Rules") while the Standing Counsel for the Chandigarh Administration, Mr. Ashok Aggarwal, sought to trace the exercise of the power of allotment of such houses to Journalists or Press Correspondents, to rule 26 thereof.
Neither of these contentions can, however, stand scrutiny.
A plain reading of the allotment rules would show that they deal with and are concerned merely with the allotment of Government Residential Accommodation to government servants.
Journalists or Press Correspondents clearly do not come within their ambit.
As regards to rule 26 of the Allotment Rules, this reads as under :- "Relaxation of Rules - S.R.
317 - A.M. 26 - The Chief Commissioner may, for reasons to be recorded in writing, relax all or any of the provisions of these rules in the case of any Government servant or residence or class of Government servants or types of residence."
Here too, it will be seen that the power of relaxation contained therein is only concerning and with regard to Government servants and residences for them.
Journalists and Press Correspondents cannot possibly be brought thereunder.
Such being the situation, there can be no escape from the conclusion that the allotment of Government Residential Accommodation to Journalists or Press Correspondents is not a matter covered either by the allotment rules or even by the power of relaxation contained in rule 26 thereof.
In other words, there are no rules, regulations or guidelines governing such allotment.
Such being the settled position in law and the circumstances being as narrated, no other conclusion is possible except that the allotment of government residential accommodation, by the Chandigarh Administration, to Journalists and Press Correspondents has been an exercise of unbridled power and discretion and cannot, therefore, be sustained.
Journalists and Press Correspondents are not entitled to allotment of government residential accommodation and, consequently, it follows that all such allotments made, lack legal sanction and therefore, no further allotment be made to Journalists or Press Correspondents.
The Chandigarh Administration would, no doubt, now take all the necessary consequential action in the matter.
In making this order, we are conscious of the fact that persons to be adversely affected by it, namely, Journalists and Press correspondents, who have been allotted government residential accommodation, are not before us.
The issue raised being of general public importance could not, in our opinion, brook undue delay, which would inevitably have occurred if all the 90 odd allottees were to have been personally served.
In order, however, to get their view point, in a representative capacity, we impleaded the Press Club of Chandigarh, which unfortunately came forth with no such view.
Be that as it may, we leave it open to any aggrieved allottees to seek review of our order, if they can show any legal infirmity in it, warranting a different view being taken.
In the context of what has been held, no occasion survives to grant to the petitioners the relief claimed.
At any rate, both the petitioners have vacated the houses allotted to them and neither of them appeared later either in person or through counsel to press their claim.
Mr. RS Surjewala, counsel for the petitioner Shri SP Gupta did appear but stated that he had no instructions.
Be that as it may, the challenge of the petitioner Shri SP Gupta to the policy decision of the Chandigarh Administration regarding cancellation of the allotment of the allottees on attaining the age of 60 years or being transferred out of Chandigarh, stands rendered infructuous by the very entitlement of Journalists and Press Correspondents to allotment of government residential accommodation being held to be contrary to law.
Similarly, Waryam Singh Dhotian, the other petitioner cannot now be heard to complain of allotment not being made in his favour.
As regards penal rent charged from him, he having continued in occupation beyond the permissible period, clearly rendered himself liable to it."
2.
Several issues relating to allotment of residential houses in Chandigarh were considered by a Full Bench of this Court while deciding Civil Writ Petition No. 16863 of 1994 which was registered on suo motu cognizance taken by the Court.
By a detailed judgment dated June 1, 1995, the Full Bench gave directions to the Administration to make allotments in terms of directions given by the Court.
The conclusions recorded by the Full Bench are extracted below :-  "I) The judgment in KG Verma's case (CWP No. 12688 of 1993 decided on February 4, 1994) is overruled and it is held that allotment of residential accommodation in Chandigarh is to be made under rule 5 of the Rules and only in exceptional cases out-of-turn under Rule 26; II) In making allotments to the State Government employees the recommendation or concurrence of the respective State Government would not be required; III) Only eligible persons, as defined in Rule (2)(e), would be given government accommodation but those who are already occupying such accommodation will not be disturbed with the exception of Journalists and Press Correspondents who are covered by SP Gupta's case (supra); IV) Rule 7 is struck down as being arbitrary; (V) The allotment in favour of Mr. AR Talwar is quashed and a direction is issued that he will vacate H No. 11, Sector 7 Chandigarh within three months but in the light of his security considerations, he is allowed allotment of a type 6 house instead of category 8 which is his actual entitlement; VI) Some guidelines be framed to ensure that the budget for maintenance of government accommodations in Chandigarh is spent fairly amongst the various types of accommodation covering different strates of the population; VII) Rule 3-A of the Rules is to be strictly enforced as it remains on the statute book; VIII) No allotment be made on the basis of instructions in favour of a government employee who is a member of the family of a superannuated government servant occupying a government accommodation; IX) Steps be taken to ascertain and if necessary, to recover government accommodation that has been sub-let by the allottees and to ensure that residential accommodation is not used for any other purpose; X) The directions of the Supreme Court in the All India Judges' case be strictly complied with within the time frame fixed by the Court; XI) High Court Judges who have not been allotted government residential accommodation so far be provided such accommodation on a priority basis."
3.
Shri Ramesh Gautam and others, Journalists/Press Correspondents as also Shri BR Jatley, whose Writ Petition (Civil Writ Petition No. 75 of 1996) is being disposed of by this order, who were likely to be affected by the order of the Division Bench dated September 6, 1993 filed petitions for Special Leave to Appeal questioning the correctness of the directions given by the High Court in SP Gupta's case.
These appeals were disposed of by the Supreme Court on May 7, 1996 ie the day on which the Petition for Special Leave to Appeal filed by the Chandigarh Administration against the order of Full Bench was disposed of.
Their Lordships reversed the order of the Division Bench on the ground of violation of principles of natural justice and remitted the case to the High Court for deciding the larger question, whether the Administration was justified in allotting Government residential quarters to the Journalists/Press Correspondents on the basis of discretionary quota.
The relevant portion of the order of the Apex Court and the directions given by the Apex Court are reproduced below :-  "We are, therefore, of the opinion that the order passed by the Division Bench of the High Court in SP Gupta's case which we have extracted earlier was clearly in violation of the principles of natural justice.
Reserving to the Journalists the right to seek review of the order, after expressing strongly against the allotment of quarters to them, would hardly serve the principles of natural justice.
The Full Bench of the High Court has merely endorsed the view of the Division Bench and interpreted it to mean that the allottees are liable to be evicted forthwith from the premises occupied by them.
If the main order passed by the Division Bench suffers from the infirmity pointed out by us, the decision of the Full Bench would also suffer from the same infirmity.
We, therefore, set aside the decision of the Full Bench also in this behalf.
We may mention that the learned Solicitor General appearing on behalf of the Chandigarh Administration stated before us that no fresh allotments have since been made or shall be made hereafter to Journalists or Press Correspondents.
We any also state that we have, in Civil Appeals arising out of S.L.P.
(Civil No. 16919, 18950-18951 and 23225/95) disposed of today, finalised the revised rules for allotment of Government quarters and those rules do not provide for allotment of Government residential quarters to Journalists.
Therefore, the question of any further allotment to Journalists from Government residential quarters is ruled out.
Secondly, the rules regarding eviction from Government residential accommodation of the Journalists attaining the age of sixty years has not been set aside since both the Division Bench and the Full Bench went into the larger question regarding allotment of Government residence to Journalists/Press Correspondents.
That rule stands and can be implemented unless there is any stay in that behalf from any court.
So far as the appellant BR Jaitely is concerned, we are told that he has not paid the occupation charges which exceed a lakh and he has also not vacated the quarters, even on attaining the age of sixty years.
It will, therefore, be open to the Administration to proceed to evict him from the quarter unless he pays up the entire dues within two weeks from today and unless there is any order in his favour to continue in occupation even after attaining the age of sixty years.
On the larger question whether the Administration was justified in allotting Government residential quarters to Journalists/Press Correspondents on the basis of a discretionary quota, the High Court may consider the same de novo after issuing a public notice in two local newspapers, one in English and the other in the regional language and thereafter proceed to consider whether the existing Journalists allottees should be allowed to continue in occupation upto or beyond the age of sixty years as per the policy and if any Journalist questions the policy, to consider whether the policy, is supportable in law.
We must make it clear that for the present since there is no interim order which has been brought to our notice prohibiting eviction after the Journalists have completed the age of sixty years, it would be open to the Administration to proceed to enforce that policy unless precluded from doing so.

ARGUMENTS.
8.
Shri SP Jain, learned Senior Counsel appearing for Shri Ramesh Gautam and others Journalists/Press Correspondents strenuously urged that the allotments made prior to September 6, 1993 ie the date on which the writ petition filed by SP Gautam and others was dismissed should be treated as regular in view of the decision taken on July 5, 1968 in the meeting held under the Chairmanship of the Chief Commissioner of Chandigarh for allotment of Government Residential Accommodation to the Press Correspondents.
Shri Jain relied on the minutes of the said meeting (Annexure R4/2) and memo No. 1590- F.II (9)-77/17802 dated 18.10.1977 written by the Finance Secretary, Chandigarh Administration and submitted that the allotments made to the Journalists/Press Correspondents in accordance with the policy decision taken by the Chandigarh Administration should not be treated as illegal.
An alternative argument made by Shri Jain is that even though the letters of allotment issued to the Journalists/Press Correspondents do not show that the same have been issued under Rule 26 of 1972 Rules, the Court should presume that the provisions of Allotment Rules were relaxed by the competent authority and, therefore, the allottees should be allowed to continue in occupation of the government accommodation.
He further submitted that the allotments already made should be saved by invoking Rule 22 of the Government Residences (Chandigarh Administration General Pool) Allotment Rules, 1996 (hereinafter referred to as `1996 Rules').
In the last, he pleaded that in view of the total ban imposed on the allotment of Government Residential Houses is to the Journalists/Press Correspondents under 1996 Rules, the allotments made prior to the enforcement of those rules should be left undisturbed, else great hardship will be suffered by the allottees.
9.
Shri Ashok Aggarwal, Senior Counsel appearing for the Chandigarh Administration, argued that the allotments of the houses, which constitute part of Chandigarh Pool made to the Journalists/Press Correspondents without following any policy or norms, are per se illegal and, therefore, the same should not be sustained.
He controverted Shri Jain's argument about the deemed relaxation of the Allotment Rules and urged that the action taken by the various authorities as a measure of favouritism should not be regularised by an order of the Court.
Shri Aggarwal made a statement that the eviction proceedings have already been initiated against some of the persons who have been found in unauthorised occupation of the Government Residential Accommodation.
In support of this statement, he relied on the affidavit dated 2491997 of Shri Abhey Singh Yadav, Secretary, House Allotment Committee.
10.
Before we deal with the respective contentions urged by the learned counsel, it is appropriate to mention that in the meeting held on 571968 to consider the allotment of Government Residential Accommodation to Press Correspondents, it was decided to fix the quota of 33 for Correspondents.
It was also decided that the allotment will be made in accordance with the following criteria :  "(i) Priority for allotment would be free (fixed ? )
from the date of application.
(ii) No recommendation from a State Government for allotment would be accepted unless arrears of rent due from them are first cleared.
(iii) Only one house will be allotted to one news agency or newspaper."
11.
In the letter dated 18.10.1977 written by the Finance Secretary, Chandigarh also it has been indicated that the number of houses earmarked for the press pool is 33.
Notwithstanding this, the number of Journalists/Press Correspondents to whom Government Residential Houses have been allotted, is far in excess of the quota of 33, as is evident from the list reproduced hereinabove.
That apart, learned counsel for the Chandigarh Administration was not able to explain as to how those shown at serial numbers 7, 26, 30, 31, 32, 36, 39, 44, 50, 52, 54, 60, 61, 69, 71 and 72 have been allotted houses, even though they are not even accredited by the State Governments of Punjab and Haryana and the Administration of Union Territory.
12.
Shri Jain's argument that the allotment of government houses to the Journalists/Press Correspondents should be deemed to have been made in relaxation of the conditions of eligibility prescribed by 1972 Rules pre- supposes that Rule 26 of 1972 Rules confers unbridled and unchecked power on the competent authority to waive the essential conditions of eligibility.
In our considered view, this argument is wholly meritless and deserves to be rejected.
Rule 26 has to be read in conjunction with other Rules including Rules 5, 6 and 9 which contain substantive provisions for allotment of government houses to eligible persons and if so read, the power vested in the Chief Commissioner to relax the provisions of 1972 Rules can, at the best, be read as empowering him to lighten the rigour of substantive Rules relating to allotment in exceptional cases in favour of eligible persons and that too for reasons to be recorded in writing.
If we interpret Rule 26 in the manner suggested by Shri Jain, the very object of framing the Rules for regulating allotment of government houses to eligible persons will be rendered otiose and there is no plausible reason for accepting an interpretation which will lead to such consequences.
13.
There is one more reason why the submission of Shri Jain should not be accepted.
The material produced on the record of these petitions does not show that the competent authority had recorded reasons for relaxing the provisions of 1972 Rules for allotment of Government Residential Houses to Journalists/Press Correspondents.
14.
However, we find considerable merit in the argument of Shri Jain that allotments to the Journalists/Press Correspondents should be deemed to have been made in accordance with the policy framed by the Administration on July 5, 1968 and reiterated in the letter of the Home Secretary dated October 18, 1977.
Though, the policy framed by the Central Government has not been placed on record, a bare perusal of Annexures R4/2 and R4/3 shows that the Government of India had created Press Pool for allotment of houses to Journalists/Press Correspondents.
In our opinion, the Central Government which has the power to make Rules for regulating allotment of residential houses to its employees, could legitimately issue directions for allotment of a particular number of government houses to Correspondents and such of the allotments, which have been made as per the criteria laid down in the meeting held on 571968, do not deserve to be disturbed.
However, we wish to make it clear that this conclusion of ours shall not be treated as a judicial recognition to the allotments made in excess of the quota of 33 fixed by the Government of India and in any case, no allotment can be made to Journalists/Press Correspondents after the commencement of 1996 Rules because the decision taken by the Central Government to create the Press Pool will be deemed to have superseded with the promulgation of 1996 Rules.
We are also of the view that out of 33 houses, the allotments made in violation of the policy framed by the Government/Chandigarh Administration cannot be sustained and in no case, the allottees of these houses can be allowed to retain the accommodation unless he/she pays the licence fee/rent and on transfer outside Chandigarh.
That apart on attaining the age of 60 years, he/she will automatically lose right to retain the accommodation.
15.
In the premise aforesaid, we dispose of the writ petitions in the following manner :-  (1) No Journalist/Press Correspondent shall be allotted Government Residential Accommodation after the coming into force of 1996 Rules.
(2) The allotment in favour of Journalists/Press Correspondents in accordance with the policy decision taken by the Government/Administration on 571968 can be treated valid only qua 33 persons.
All allotments made beyond the quota of 33 will have to be treated as illegal and occupants thereof will have to be treated as unauthorised occupants of public premises.
(3) The Administration shall scrutinize the allotments and determine which of the 33 allotments are to be treated as regular keeping in view the conditions of eligibility laid down in the decision taken in the meeting held on July 5, 1968.
(4) Those who have not paid licence fee/rent fixed by the Chandigarh Administration will forfeit their right to retain the accommodation unless they pay the arrears, if any found due, within two months of the communication of the details of such arrears.
(5) All such allottees who are transferred out of Chandigarh or attain the age of 60 years will automatically become dis-entitled to retain the accommodation and they will be treated in unauthorised occupation of the public premises and the competent authority will be free to proceed against them under 1971 Act.
Petition disposed of.
