Jawahar Lal Gupta, J. (Oral) - The petitioner, who had joined PCMS-II on November 19,1960 and was promoted to PCMS-I with effect from April 1, 1980 claims that he is, in fact, entitled to be considered and promoted to the Class-I Service w.e.f.
April 21, 1976.
The Government had, in fact, considered him suitable for promotion w.e.f.
June 15, 1978 and sought the approval of the Punjab Public Service Commission vide letter dated September 18/201989.
The recommendation of the Government having been declined by the Public Service Commission, the petitioner had approached this Court through the present writ petition.
2.
The petitioner avers that he had been communicated adverse reports for the years 1966-67, 1971-72, and 1974-75.
He claims that he submitted a representation against the adverse report for the year 1971-72, which has still not been decided by the Government.
His representation against the report for the year 1974-75 was accepted and the remarks were expunged by the Government vide its order dated August 17, 1978, in spite of that the Public Service Commission took these remarks into consideration and has declared him unsuitable for promotion on the ground that the remarks have been expunged after 15 years.
It is, inter alia claimed that the remarks for the year 1971-72 could not have been taken into consideration by the respondents as the petitioner's representation against the adverse report is still pending.
It is further claimed that the petitioner having been allowed to cross the efficiency bar vide orders dated July 22, 1983 with effect from the due date, viz. September 18, 1972, the action of the respondents in not promoting the petitioner w.e.f.
April 21, 1976 when the persons junior to the petitioner were promoted, is violative of the provisions of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
3.
Two separate written statements have been filed on behalf of the respondents.
In the written statement filed on behalf of the Government, it is inter alia averred that the representations against the adverse reports for the years 1971-72 and 1974-75 were submitted by the petitioner.
Vide order dated August 17, 1978, the Government had decided to expunge the remarks in the confidential report for the year 1974-75.
It is further averred that the remarks for the year 1971-72 were conveyed to the petitioner by the Director, Health Services vide his letter dated March 1, 1973.
The petitioner, it is averred, had not submitted any representation within a stipulated period of 3 months.
However, a representation dated December 10, 1990 was received and the same is under consideration.
It has been admitted that the petitioner had been permitted to cross the second efficiency bar w.e.f.
September 18, 1972.
The respondent further avers that the petitioner's claim for promotion from PCMS-II to PCMS-I on April 21, 1976, when his juniors were promoted, was also considered by the Government, but he was not found fit for promotion as he was under suspension w.e.f.
October 27, 1975.
Ultimately, vide orders dated March 11, 1976, the petitioner was charge-sheeted for having remained absent from duty w.e.f.
August 21, 1975.
Vide orders dated November 30, 1977, the petitioner was awarded the punishment of 'Censure', but the period of suspension was treated as duty for all intents and purposes.
He was accordingly reinstated on December 1, 1977.
The respondent further avers that after April 21, 1976, persons were considered for promotion in February, 1978 and June, 1978.
The petitioner's claim was also considered but he was not found fit for promotion on account of the remarks contained in his ACRs for the years 1971-72 and 1974-75.
Thereafter, in view of the expunction of the remarks for the year 1974-75, the Government vide order dated June 29, 1979 promoted the petitioner to the Class-I Service.
Approval of the Public Service Commission was sought.
The Commission declined the approval vide their letter dated June 30, 1983.
As a result the petitioner was reverted vide order dated September 13, 1983.
The petitioner was then promoted w.e.f.
April 1, 1980 vide order dated January 23, 1986.
On receipt of an approval from the Commission, the services of the petitioner were regularised in PCMS-I w.e.f.
April 1, 1980.
It is further admitted that the petitioner's case for promotion w.e.f.
June, 1978 was recommended to the Commission vide letter dated September 18/20, 1980.
Since the Commission had declined the approval, no relief whatsoever could be granted to the petitioner.
4.
A written statement has been filed on behalf of respondent No. 2 during the course of hearing.
Normally, the Commission should have filed its return well in time.
Under the rules, it is required to be filed 3 days before the date of hearing.
However, keeping in view the interest of justice, the request of the learned counsel for the respondent for permission to place the written statement on record was granted and the reply has been taken on record.
It has been inter alia averred that the petitioner was not promoted w.e.f.
April 21,1976 as he had remained under suspension at that time and also because there were adverse remarks in his A.C.R.
of 1974-75.
The Government on receipt of representation of the petitioner had expunged the adverse remarks vide order No. 1/25/II/79-3 (RBI), dated 541989 (copy attached with CWP vide annexure P-8).
It was also requested by respondent No. 1 that the claim of petitioner for promotion in PCMS-I w.e.f.
1561978 may be reconsidered as his record for the last five years was satisfactory for giving promotion with retrospective effect.
The answering respondent reconsidered the proposal of respondent No. 1 and did not find any justification to approve his suitability with retrospective effect viz. 1551978 as the adverse remarks contained in his A.C.R.
for the year 1974-75 was expunged by the State Government after a lapse of 15 years.
On this premises, the action of the Commission in refusing to accord approval to the recommendation of the Government is sought to be justified.
5.
I have heard Mr. Ashu M Punchhi, learned counsel for the petitioner and Mr. Arihant Jain for the respondents.
Learned counsel for the petitioner has raised a two-fold contention.
It is contended that the petitioner is entitled to be considered for promotion w.e.f.
April 21, 1976 without taking-into account the adverse report for the year 1971-72.
This is sought to be sustained on the twin ground that the representation against the adverse report is still pending as also that the petitioner had been permitted to cross the efficiency bar w.e.f.
September 18, 1972 vide orders dated July 22 1983.
It is further submitted that the action of the Commission in declining approval to the recommendation of the Government is based on a wholly wrong assumption of facts.
It is contended that the adverse remarks for the year 1974-75 had been expunged by the Government vide its letter dated August 17, 1978 and that the averment in the affidavit of the respondent that the remarks had been expunged in the year 1989 ie after a lapse of 15 years is wholly incorrect.
On behalf of the respondents, it is contended that the petitioner was actually under suspension in the year 1976 and consequently he cannot claim a right to be considered w.e.f that date.
It is further submitted that the action of the Commission in declining approval is in strict conformity with the rules and deserves to be sustained.
6.
Admittedly, the persons junior to the petitioner were promoted on April 21, 1976.
The petitioner's claim was ignored only on the ground that he was under suspension.
Ultimately, he was reinstated in the year 1977 and the period of suspension was ordered to be treated as duty for all intents and purposes.
Once the petitioner had been reinstated, it was incumbent on the respondents to reconsider his claim w.e.f.
the date the persons junior to him were promoted.
A perusal of the official records indicates that the claim was actually considered.
However, the petitioner was not found suitable primarily on the ground that there was an adverse report for the year 1971-72.
The original report has been produced to me.
The Reporting Officer had assessed the petitioner's performance as `above average'.
His reputation for honesty was described to be 'not good'., The Reviewing Authority had assessed the petitioner as an average officer.
It was further observed that "he has been too keen to get food powers under the PFA Act which is not very proper."
These remarks were conveyed to the petitioner vide letter dated March 1, 1973.
The petitioner maintains that he had submitted a representation within the prescribed period of three months.
However, there is some controversy on this aspect of the matter.
It appears that in December 1984, the petitioner had requested the authorities to decide his representation.
Thereupon, vide letter dated January 15, 1985, the Director Health Services, Punjab called upon the petitioner to furnish a copy of the representation which he has submitted against the report for the year 1971-72.
The petitioner claims that vide his letter dated January 24, 1985 he had not only sent a representation, but also an affidavit in support thereof averring that he had submitted the representation within the prescribed period and that he shall be responsible for any consequence if the statement was found to be false.
The respondents have not been able to trace that representation or the affidavit.
They were called upon by me to produce the Receipt Register(s) for the relevant period.
Even the Receipt Register(s) have not been produced.
However, it appears that the petitioner had again sent a reminder in response to which the Director, Health Services wrote a letter to the Government with reference to an earlier letter dated February 19, 1990 requesting that the decision taken by the Government "on the representation of Dr. KC Khosla forwarded vide this office letter under reference may kindly be conveyed to this office."
Still nothing was done.
The petitioner then wrote a letter dated 'Nil' which appears to have been received in the office of Director, Health Services on October 23, 1990.
He requested that his representation be decided.
He then submitted a detailed representation on December 10, 1990.
Along with this representation he produced a copy of the affidavit dated January 24, 1985 which he claims was submitted earlier.
This matter is still pending with the Government and has not been finally decided so far.
The basic claim made by the petitioner in these representations is that his efforts to seek more power were in strict conformity with the rules and that he could not be accused of any impropriety on this account.
In any event, it is admitted that the petitioner's representation against the adverse report for the year 1971-72 is still pending.
7.
Further a perusal of the letter dated September 18, 1989 shows that the Government while recommending the petitioner's case for promotion to Public Service Commission had clearly observed that "accepting the appeal of Dr. Khosla expunged on 17878, the adverse remarks contained in the ACR."
It is thus apparent that the adverse remarks for the year 1974-75, had been expunged by the Government on August 17, 1978 and the assumption of the Commission while declining approval that the remarks have been actually expunged after 15 years on April 5, 1989 is wholly contrary to record.
8.
It is in the light of this factual position that the contentions of the respective parties have to be examined.
9.
It has been settled that while considering the respective claims for promotion, an adverse report against which a representation is pending cannot be taken into consideration.
The principle was originally enunciated in Gurdial Singh Fijji v. State of Punjab, 1979(1) SLR 804.
It was reiterated in Brij Mohan Singh Chopra v. State of Punjab, 1987(2) SLR 54.
Their Lordships were pleased to observe as under :-  "These decisions lay down the principle that unless an adverse report is communicated and representation, if any, made by the employee is considered, it cannot be acted upon to deny promotion.
We are of the opinion that the same consideration must apply to a case where the adverse entries are taken into account interpreting an employee prematurely from service.
It would be unjust and unfair and contrary to principles of natural justice to retire prematurely Government employee on the basis of adverse entries which are either not communicated to him or if communicated representations made against those entries are not considered and disposed of.
The appellant had submitted his representations against adverse entries for the year 1971-72 and 1972-73 and admittedly those representations were not considered and disposed of and yet the appropriate authority considered those entries in forming opinion that the appellant's premature retirement was in the public interest.
We are, therefore, of the opinion that for this reason the order of the State Govt. is not sustainable in law."
10.
In view of the above the contention of the petitioner that his claim for promotion with effect from the date, when persons junior to him were promoted, viz. April 21, 1976, has to be considered is worthy of acceptance.
Accordingly, the respondents have to consider the petitioner's claim for promotion w.e.f.
April 21, 1976.
While doing so, the report for the year 1971-72 against which the petitioner's representation is admittedly pending till today shall not be taken into consideration.
11.
Learned counsel also contended that since the petitioner has been permitted to cross the efficiency bar w.e.f.
the due date, viz. September 18, 1972, the adverse reports shall be deemed to have been washed out.
I am unable to accept this contention.
Crossing of efficiency bar only proves that the officer is fit to discharge the duties of the post held by him.
The adverse report recorded against him is not such as may warrant is being held up at the stage of the efficiency bar.
However, in the matter of promotion, where the claims of various eligible persons have to be compared, the adverse report cannot be ignored.
There may be a number of officers who have crossed the efficiency bar and have a consistently good record of service.
There may be an officer, who has been allowed to cross the efficiency bar in spite of the fact that he has certain adverse reports against him.
It cannot be said that all of them are equal because they have crossed the efficiency bar.
The persons with better record of service shall have a right to be promoted in preference to a person whose performance has been assessed below the required standard.
Learned counsel contends that all persons who have crossed the efficiency bar will be at par in spite of the fact that some of them have certain adverse reports on their record.
This would be totally anomalous.
It would lead to injustice.
It would deny the equality of opportunity to the persons who have better record of service.
I am consequently not inclined to accept the contention.
It is accordingly rejected.
12.
In view of the above, I find that the Government had expunged the adverse remarks of the petitioner for the year 1974-75 vide its order dated August 17, 1978 and that the Commission has completely ignored this fact while considering the petitioner's claim for promotion w.e.f.
June, 1978.
Even the action of the Commission in rejecting the recommendation of the Government vile its letter dated February 28, 1991 (annexure P11) also cannot be sustained.
In fact, a perusal of the official file shows that the report for the year 1974-75 had been expunged in the year 1978.
In spite of that the office of Director, Health Services had omitted to take corrective steps and place the relevant letter on the file.
As a result, the petitioner has suffered since the year 1979 when the proposal of the Government for the approval of his promotion was declined by the Commission.
He had even suffered reversion during the period from 1983 to 1986.
A bit of care would have saved him from a lot of injustice and suffering.
13.
Accordingly, the writ petition is allowed.
The order at Annexure P11 is set aside.
The respondents are directed to consider to claim of the petitioner for promotion to PCMS-I w.e.f.
April 21, 1976 by ignoring the report for the year 1971-72.
In the event, if the petitioner is found suitable for promotion consequential reliefs in the nature of arrears of salary, restoration of seniority and consideration of further promotion, etc. shall ensue.
The needful shall be done within three months from the date of receipt of a copy of this order.
The petitioner shall also be entitled to his costs which are assessed at Rs. 2,000/-.
Petition allowed.
