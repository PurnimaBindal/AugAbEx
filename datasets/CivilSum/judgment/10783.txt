MM Kumar, J - This petition filed under Article 226 of the Constitution of India prays for quashing order dated 2821997 (Annexure P.2) passed by Superintendent of Police, Bhiwani imposing punishment of stoppage of two increments with cumulative effect after holding a regular departmental enquiry and a subsequent Order dated 2831997 (Annexure P.3) passed by the Deputy Inspector General, Gurgaon Range discharging the petitioner from service as a probationer ASI.
A further prayer made by the petitioner is for quashing the subsequent order dated 10.7.1998 (Annexure P.5) dismissing his review application.
The petitioner has also claimed all consequential benefits.
2.
Brief facts of the case are that the petitioner was appointed as probationer ASI on 19.4.1994 and he was deputed to the Training Centre for passing of Probationary ASI Course.
He completed the afore-mentioned course on 531995 and was posted in district Rewari.
He is alleged to have absented from duty by remaining absent for four days.
It has further been alleged that he was found absent from duty unauthorizedly on 381995 and was censured by the Superintendent of Police, Rewari.
There are further allegations that when he was undergoing training at Dharuhera he did not send his weekly diary for the weeks ending 2781995 and 1091995.
After the show cause notice, he was again censured.
The petitioner is alleged to have absented on the intervening night of 2/381995 which again resulted into inflicting the punishment of censure.
The Superintendent of Police issued him a charge-sheet and directed holding of enquiry which was entrusted to DSP HQ.
Rewari.
After examining the enquiry conducted by DSP, the Superintendent of Police, Rewari found the petitioner guilty of the afore-mentioned charges and awarded him punishment of stoppage of two annual grade increments with cumulative effect vide order dated 2821997 (Annexure P-2).
Thereafter the Deputy Inspector General of Police passed an order on 2831997 under Rule 128 and discharged him as probationary ASI.
It is admitted fact that the maximum period of probation for the post of ASI is three years and the petitioner had not completed three years.
He was probationer on the date of issuance of discharge order on 2831997 (Annexure P-3).
The petitioner filed a review application against order of discharge but his review application was summarily rejected on 10.7.1998 (Annexure P.5) which is also subject matter of challenge in this petition.
3.
In the written statement, the stand taken by the respondent is that the petitioner was guilty of a grave mis-conduct and has been awarded various punishments.
The orders of various punishments awarded to the petitioner have been appended as Annexures R/1 to R/6.
It has been claimed that the probationary ASI has been discharged during the period of three years when he was admittedly on probation.
According to the respondents once the enquiry has been held by complying with the principles of natural justice then the order of discharge could have been passed.
4.
Shri IS Balhara, learned counsel for the petitioner has argued that once for the various lapses committed by the petitioner the order dated 2821997 (Annexure P-2) has been passed then no order discharging the petitioner could have been passed because already punishment of stoppage of two annual grade increments with cumulative effect has been inflicted on the petitioner.
The learned counsel maintains that the only course open to the D.I.G.
was to call for the record and then issue a show cause notice to the petitioner under Rule 1628.
Thereafter he could have passed any other order.
The learned counsel has emphasised that under the garb of discharge order the order of dismissal has been passed by the Deputy Inspector General on 2831997 which is impermissible.
In support of his submissions, the learned counsel has placed reliance on a number of judgments of the Supreme Court as well as of this Court which are reported as Anoop Jaiswal v. Government of India and another, 1984(1) SLR 426; Dipti Prakash Banerjee v. Satyendra Nath Bose National Centre, 1999(1) SCT 861 (SC) : 1999(1) SLR 622; Radhey Sham Gupta v. UP State Agro Industries Corp, 1999(1) SCT 366 (SC) : AIR 1999 SC 609; Chandra Prakash Shahi v. State of UP and others, 2000(2) SCT 946 (SC) : 2000(2) RSJ 741; Prithipal Singh v. State of Punjab and ors., 2001(1) SCT 459 (SC) : 2000(3) RSJ 736; Rajinder Kaur v. Punjab State and another, AIR 1986 SC 1790; Krishan Kumar v. State of Punjab and ors., 1999(3) RSJ 125; Const.
Narinder Singh v. State of Haryana and ors., 1998(3) RSJ 617.
5.
Shri SS Brar, learned counsel for the State has argued that the order of discharge cannot be deemed to have any stigma and it can always be passed even when there are specific allegations of mis-conduct because it is not a penal order.
The learned counsel has placed reliance on a Full Bench judgment of this Court in the case of Sher Singh v. State of Punjab, 1994(2) SLR 100.
He has further submitted that during probation an employee can always be discharged even without holding any enquiry and the order of discharge cannot be considered as stigmatic.
For the afore-mentioned proposition, reliance is placed on the judgment of the Supreme Court in the case of State of Punjab v. Bhagwan Singh, 2002(1) SCT 253 (SC).
6.
After hearing the learned counsel for the parties and perusing the order dated 2831997 (Annexure P-3) passed by the Deputy Inspector General of Police, Gurgaon, I am of the considered view that this petition deserves to be allowed and the aforementioned order dated 2831997 (Annexure P-3) is liable to be set aside.
It is true that in this case enquiry has been held and on that basis punishment of stoppage of two increments with cumulative effect has been inflicted on the petitioner.
It is also an admitted fact that it has happened during the period of probation.
The Superintendent of Police having chosen to inflict the punishment of stoppage of two increments with cumulative effect under Rule 165 of the Punjab Police Rules, 1934 (for brevity, `1934 Rules'), the Deputy Inspector General of Police, Gurgaon range has passed the order dated 2831997 (Annexure P-3) discharging the petitioner from service as a probationer ASI, purportedly exercising power under Rule 128 of 1934 Rules.
The Deputy Inspector General of Police, undoubtedly, is clothed with the power to review the order passed by a Superintendent of Police and such a power is traceable to Rule 1628 of 1934 Rules.
The question for consideration is whether the Deputy Inspector General of Police could have passed the order of discharge after the Competent Authority like the Superintendent of Police has chosen to inflict the punishment of stoppage of two increments with cumulative effect on the basis of a departmental enquiry.
7.
The answer to the aforementioned question can be given by referring to provisions of Rule 1628 of 1934 Rules which clothe inter-alia, the Deputy Inspector General of Police with the power to review an order passed by the Superintendent of Police.
Rules 128 and 1628 of 1934 Rules are reproduced hereunder for facility of reference :-  "128 Probationary nature of appointments.
- (1) Inspectors, Sergeants, Sub- Inspectors and Assistant Sub-Inspectors who are directly appointed will be considered to be on probation for three years and are liable to be discharged at any time during or on the expiry of the period of their probation if they fail to pass the prescribed examinations including the riding test, or are guilty of grave misconduct or are deemed, for sufficient reason, to be unsuitable for service in the police.
A probationary inspector shall be discharged by the Inspector-General and all other Upper Subordinates by Range Deputy Inspector-General and Assistant Inspector-General, Government Railway Police, Assistant Inspector-General, Provincial Additional Police (designated as Commandant, Provincial Additional Police) and Assistant Inspector-General of Police (Traffic).
No appeal lies against an order of discharge.
(2) The pay admissible to a probationary Inspector, Sergeant, Sub-Inspector or Assistant Sub-Inspector is shown in Appendix 10.64, Table A.
1628 Powers to review proceedings.
- (1) The Inspector-General, a Deputy Inspector-General and a Superintendent of Police may call for the records of awards made by their subordinates and confirm, enhance, modify or annul the same, or make further investigation or direct such to be made before passing orders.
(2) If an award of dismissal is annulled, the officer annulling it shall state whether it is to be regarded as suspension followed by reinstatement, or not.
The order should also state whether service previous to dismissal should count for pension or not.
(3) In all cases in which officers propose to enhance an award they shall, before passing final orders, give the defaulter concerned an opportunity of showing cause, either personally or in writing, why his punishment should not be enhanced."
A perusal of Rule 128 of 1934 Rules shows that a direct recruit ASI is required to remain on probation for a period of three years and is liable to be discharged at any time during or on the expiry of his period of probation if he fails to pass the prescribed examination or is guilty of grave misconduct or for sufficient reason is deemed to be unsuitable for service in the police department.
The power to discharge is conferred by the Rule on Deputy Inspector General of the range in respect of an officer of the rank of ASI.
It has further been made clear that the order of discharge passed by the Deputy Inspector General of Police is not an appealable order.
However, a perusal of Rule 1628 of 1934 Rules shows that a Deputy Inspector General is competent to call for the record of award made by his subordinates and he may confirm, enhance, modify or annul the same.
Sub-rule (3) provides for an opportunity of showing cause if the punishment is proposed to be enhanced.
The rules do not permit passing of an order by the Deputy Inspector General of Police for enhancing the punishment without opportunity of showing cause for such a proposal.
Even otherwise, the principles of natural justice are so well entrenched in our Constitutional and administrative law that any violation of such principles is regarded as violation of fundamental rights guaranteed by Articles 14 and 16(1) of the Constitution.
Therefore, the impugned order dated 2831997 (Annexure P-3) is liable to be set aside.
8.
Even otherwise, the order dated 2831997 (Annexure P-3) cannot be regarded as an order of discharge as it is not an order passed under Rule 128 of 1934 Rules under which it is purported to have been passed.
A perusal of the order shows that the Deputy Inspector General of Police, Gurgaon range, has made reference to various period of absence from duty and delay in submission of weekly diary by the petitioner.
It has made a mention with regard to recording of false report on 871996 in the Daily Diary.
It is well-settled that absence from duty is a misconduct as has been laid down by a Constitutional Bench of the Supreme Court in the case of Jai Shankar v. State of Rajasthan, AIR 1966 SC 492.
It would also be a misconduct if an employee is charged with making a false entry in the police record.
Such an order can by no stretch of imagination be considered as the one based on a motive and cannot be regarded as an order of discharge under Rule 128 of 1934 Rules.
Once it is held that the order of discharge has incorporated various instances of misconduct, then it necessarily has to be regarded as an order of dismissal which could have been pased by the Deputy Inspector General of Police under Rule 1628 of 1934 Rules.
For the aforementioned view, reliance could be placed on various judgments of the Supreme Court including a seven Judges Constitutional Bench in the case of Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab, AIR 1974 SC 2192.
This view has been followed by the subsequent judgments of the Supreme Court in the cases of Anoop Jaiswal (supra), Dipti Prakash Banerjee (supra), Radhey Sham Gupta (supra), Chandra Parkash Shahi (supra), Prithipal Singh (supra), Rajinder Kaur (supra) and the other cases cited by the learned counsel at the bar.
It is not necessary to discuss in detail the judgments cited at the bar because there is no escape from the proposition that stigmatic order which is founded on a misconduct cannot be regarded as an order of discharge.
Therefore, the order dated 2831997 (Annexure P-3) is liable to be quashed.
9.
The argument of the learned counsel for the petitioner that the order dated 2821997 (Annexure P-2) is also liable to be quashed, cannot be accepted because the order has been passed after holding a regular enquiry.
Neither any argument has been addressed for attacking the order on the ground of any defect in the departmental enquiry nor any other ground has been urged.
The punishment of stoppage of two increments with cumulative effect is permitted to be imposed as is provided by Rule 165 of 1934 Rules.
Therefore, no legal infirmity could be found in the order dated 2821997 (Annexure P-2) and the argument raised by the learned counsel is liable to be rejected.
In view of the above, this petition succeeds.
The order dated 2831997 (Annexure P-3) is quashed.
The petitioner shall be entitled to all consequential benefits.
It is made clear that by virtue of quashing the order dated 2831997, the petitioner would not be entitled to become a confirmed ASI.
The respondents are at liberty to proceed against the petitioner in accordance with law by treating him as a probationer.
It is further made clear that the petitioner shall be entitled to file an appeal against the order dated 2821997 (Annexure P-2) as the right of appeal is available to him under Rule 16.29 of 1934 Rules because he was not able to file the appeal as the order of discharge was passed within one month of the order dated 2821997.
If such an appeal is filed within 30 days from today, then the respondents shall consider the same on merit without raising any objection as to limitation and pass appropriate orders in accordance with law thereon.
Petition allowed.
