GR Majithia, J - The petitioner has sought a mandate to the respondent to appoint him to the Punjab Civil Services (Executive Branch), in this petition under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India.
2.
The facts found established from the pleadings and the documents produced in record, briefly, are :  3.
The Punjab Public Service Commission, Patiala (for short, the Commission) advertised 64 posts of PCS (Executive Branch) and other Alltied Services in The Tribune, dated May 1, 1982; that the petitioner qualified in the written test and thereafter appeared for the viva voce and was selected and placed at serial No. 26 in order of merit.
Initially, there were 24 posts to be filled up in the PCS (Executive Branch), out of which 13 posts were meant for the general category.
Against these 13 posts, the candidates at Serial Nos. 1 to 13 of general category were allocated for appointment; that three of them did not join; that subsequently the number of vacancies in the PCS (Executive Branch) was increased to 40, out of which 22 vacancies fell to the share of the candidates of general category.
Since 3 selected candidates failed to join, candidates at Serial Nos. 14 to 25 in the merit list of selected candidates of general category were allocated and offered appointments.

Vijay Kumar 6394 The petitioner was placed at serial No. 26 of the Merit List.
The candidates mentioned at serial Nos. 1, 2 and 6 failed to join and the resultant vacancies were offered to the next candidates on the Merit List.
Shri Amrit Lal Garg figured at serial No. 15 of the Merit List.
The Commission, on examining the mark sheet detected that actual marks obtained by him as entered by the Examiner in the award list were not tallying with the main result statement.
On the basis of the actual marks obtained by him, his name could not figure in the Merit List as drawn by the Commission.
The Commission withdrew its recommendation in favour of Shri Amrit Lal Garg and thereafter the respondent removed his name from the list of accepted candidates from Register 'B' After serving a show cause notice on him.
Shri Amrit Lal Garg filed C.W.P.
No. 4208 of 1988 against the respondent and the Commission for quashing the order by which his name was removed from Register 'B'.
The writ petition was allowed by this Court by an order dated February 12,1990.
L.P.A. No. 1005 of 1990 was dismissed by this Court on July 31, 1990.
Petition for Special Loan to Appeal (Civil) No. 10246-47 of 1990 was also dismissed by the apex Court on September 3, 1990.
4.
Learned counsel for the petitioner submitted that Shri Amrit Lal Garg could not be placed at serial No. 15 in the Merit List.
He was so placed because of miscalculation of his total marks.
He had actually obtained 378 marks in the aggregate and was wrongly shown to have obtained 428 marks in the aggregate.
The Commission, after discovery of the mistake, withdrew the recommendation.
However, Shri Amrit Lal Garg moved this Court through C.W.P.
4208 of 1988 challenging the withdrawal of recommendation and follow-up action taken pursuant to the communication received from the Commission regarding withdrawal of its recommendation qua him on the ground that the Commission became functus officio after it had made recommendation of the selected candidates to the Government.
This Court did not hold that Shri Amrit Lal Garg was correctly placed on the Merit List.
The correctness of the statement of the Commission to the Government for withdrawing its recommendation was never assailed before any authority or in this Court.
This Court accepted the writ petition of Shri Amrit Lal Garg on legal ground.
Concededly, the total number of posts were to be filled up as a consequence of the advertisement dated May 1, 1982 in PCS (Exective Branch) was increased to 40, out of which 22 posts were to be filled up from amongst general category candidates.
Candidates at serial Nos. 1, 2 and 6 on the select list did not join.
Candidate at serial No. 15 on the select list was not eligible to be placed at serial No. 15.
He was placed at serial No. 15 on the Merit List because of miscalculation of his marks.
If these 4 names are excluded from the select list, the petitioner who is placed at serial No. 26 on the Merit List from amongst the general category candidates would have been at serial No. 22 and would have been eligible for selection to the PCS (Executive Branch).
5.
The respondent's defence is based on Punjab Government's Circular No. 391-3GS-62-1443, dated January 11, 1962, the relevant portion of which reads as under :  "In order to rationalise allocation of candidates selected on the results of such examinations for the sake of uniformity and speedy decision, the following procedure has been evolved :-  (i) The Department while intimating to the Punjab Public Service Commission the number of vacancies to be filled on the basis of a combined examination in a year shall endorse a copy of the requision to the Chief Secretary (in Department of Personnel and Adm.
Reforms).
(ii) The Commission while forwarding names of competitors in order of merit, shall clearly indicate the choice made by candidates in regard to preference for service.
The list shall also include the names of candidates belonging to the Scheduled Castes/Tribes and other Backward Classes in order of merit.
He Commission would simultaneously send to the Government the original applications of all the candidates equal to the number of vacancies including those reserved for Scheduled Castes/Tribes other Backward Classes plus five extra in each case to cover any additional vacancies.
When advertising for these posts the Commission will make it clear that the choice indicated by the candidate would be final unless changed before the final result becomes available.
(iii) In respect of services/posts for which the candidate had not indicated his choice, but are included in the category/categories for which the competition is held, the candidate will have an equal preference for those services posts.
Such candidate, if he could not be allotted to a service of his preference.
(iv) On the basis of the Commission's list, a meeting of the Departments concerned will be called and candidates earmarked in the order of merit, including reservation for the Scheduled Castes etc., for the various services according to the vacancies indicated by the Departments.
If at this stage the Departments can indicate additional number of firm vacancies, these will also be provided for.
(v) Having earmarked the candidates as above the Departments should proceed with the appointments after the formalities are completed.
If by chance any candidate falls out as a result of verification of character and antecedents, medical examination, etc., a new appointment should not be made in place thereof as that would involve a revision of the whole list, but this vacancy should be carried forward as an additional vacancy to the next year.
It is believed that the number of candidates who fail in the medical examination or whose, antecedents are found to be unsatisfactory will be very small and the risk of ignoring this number can be easily taken, than running delays involved in reallocating the whole list".
On the basis of this letter, it was stressed that the vacancy which would have arisen as a result of exclusion of the name of Shri Amrit Lal Garg from the Merit List would have to be carried forward as an additional vacancy in the next year.
The submission is devoid of merit.
The provision of this clause is not applicable to the facts of the instant case.
Here an ineligible candidate was placed on the Merit List by making miscalculation of his marks.
If the Commission had not made the miscalculation, that candidate would not have been placed on the Merit List where he was placed.
The provisions of the aforesaid letter only apply where a name has to be removed from the Merit List because of verification of character and antecedent or on medical grounds.
At any rate, the petition cannot be made to suffer because of the mistake of the Commission.
In somewhat similar circumstances, the apex Court in AV Bhogshwarudu v. Andhra Pradesh Public Service Commission, JT 1989(4) SC 130, held thus :  "We have pursued the counter affidavit filed on behalf of the State Public Service Commission pursuant to the direction of this Court.
It is not disputed that the preliminary process of selection stated in 1983 and was completed in 1987 and the vacancies that arose in between was also sought to accommodated from the recruitment list prepared by the State Public Service Commission.
The only point which requires consideration is as to whether if out of the names recommended for appointment, some candidates do not join, whether the vacancies by remaining unfilled should or should not be filled up from out of the successful candidates.
We see no justification in the stand of the State Public Service Commission that instead of filling up the vacancies by recommending the candidates next in order of merit out of the present list why a fresh selection should be made.
We accordingly dispose of this appeal by directing that the number of vacancies remaining to be filled up on account of non-joining of selected candidates for whatever reason shall be out of the present list.
This may be done within ten weeks."
In the light of this authoritative pronouncement, there is no escape from the conclusion that the 22nd vacancy on the Merit List in the P.C.
S. (Executive Branch) ought to have been allotted to the petitioner.
6.
For the reasons aforesaid, the writ petition succeeds and the respondent is directed to absorb the petitioner in PCS (Executive Branch) against the 22nd vacancy of the general category within two months from the date of receipt of copy of this judgment.
However, it is made clear that the petitioner will not to entitled to claim seniority over the candidates selected as a result of the declaration of the result by the Commission on June 25, 1985.
If any selection has been made thereafter to the PCS (Executive Branch), the petitioner will be placed below the last selected candidate on the merit list There will be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
