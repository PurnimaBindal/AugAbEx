GR Majithia and SK Jain, JJ.
- This judgment disposes of Civil Writ Petitions No. 1185 of 1993, 625, 5411, 14800, 16870 of 1990, 4212 of 1987, 8691 of 1991, 1183 of 1990 and 16643 of 1991.
2.
The petitioners have sought a mandate to the respondents to grant them benefit of military service rendered by them during the national emergency in accordance with the provisions of the Punjab Government National Emergency (Concession) Rules, 1965 for the purpose of seniority, increments and other allied benefits flowing therefrom, in these petitions under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India.
3.
A reference to relevant facts has been made from the pleadings in C.W.P.
No. 1185 of 1993 :-  Petitioner No. 1 joined the Army on March 3, 1959 and was discharged from the Army Service on November 1, 1976.
Petitioner No. 2 joined the Air Force on December 12, 1961 and was discharged therefrom on December 31, 1976.
They were selected and appointed as D.P.E.
and SS Master in the Education Department, Punjab, on December 15, 1978 and October 17, 1978 respectively.
Their services were regularised on October 1, 1980.
4.
In the wake of Chinese Aggression, the President of India proclaimed national emergency on October 26, 1962.
In order to attract young people to join the Armed Forces, the Punjab Government announced numerous concessions for Ex-Servicemen and civil employees who joined the Military Service, during the proclamation of National Emergency.
The Governor of Punjab in exercise of the powers conferred by proviso to Article 309 of the Constitution of India and all other powers conferred by proviso to Article 309 of the Constitution of India and all other powers enabling him in this behalf made the Rules called the "Punjab Government National Emergency (Concession) Rules, 1965 (for short, the rules).
Rule 2 defines the term "military service" to mean enrolled or commissioned service in any of the three wings of the Indian Armed Forces (including service) as a warrant officer rendered by a person during the period of operation of the Proclamation of Emergency made by the President of India under Article 352 of the Constitution on October 26, 1962 or such other service as may hereafter be declared as military service for the purposes of the Rules, Rule 3 of the rules provides for maximum age limit and minimum qualifications.
Rule 4 provides for the procedure for determination of benefits towards increments, seniority and pension for the period of military service.
Rule 5 provides for the manner for fixation of seniority, grant of promotion, increment, pension and leave in respect of the period spent by a government servant on military service.
A plain reading of Rule 2 indicates that a Government employee is entitled to the benefit of military service irrespective of his date of joining the military service if he has served the Armed Forces during the period of operation of proclamation of Emergency.
5.
The Governor of Haryana amended these Rules vide Notification No. GSR-182/Const/Article 309/Amd.
(2)/76, dated August 4, 1976 and for Rule 2, the following rule was substituted :-  "For the purpose of these Rules, the expression 'military service' means the service rendered by a person, who had been enrolled or commissioned during the period of operation of the proclamation of emergency made by the President under Article 352 of the Constitution of India on the 26th October, 1962, in any of the three wings of the Indian Armed Forces (including the service as Warrant Officer) during the period of said emergency or such other service as may hereafter be declared as military service for the purpose of these rules.
Any period of military training followed by military service shall also be reckoned as military service."
6.
The effect of the aforementioned amendment introduced by the Punjab Government National emergency (Concession) Haryana Second Amendment Rules, 1976, was that a person who had been enrolled or commissioned in any of the three wings of the Indian Armed Forces during the period of operation of the proclamation of emergency made by the President of India under Article 352 of the Constitution on October 26, 1962 was entitled to the benefit of military service under these Rules.
7.
The Rules were further amended by the Haryana Government by the rules called The Punjab Government National Emergency (Concession) Haryana First Amendment Rules, 1982 published vide Notification No. G.S.R.58/Const./Article 309/Amd.
(i) 82, dated April 27, 1982, whereby for Rule 4-A the following rule was substituted :-  "4-A Benefit of military service when admissible.
The benefit of military service towards increments mentioned in clause (i) of rule 4 shall be admissible only on first appointment under the Government."
8.
The apex court interpreted Rules 2 and 4-A as amended by the Governor of Haryana vide notifications dated August 4, 1976 and April 27, 1982 in Civil Appeal Nos. 2658 and 2659 of 1977 (State of Punjab & Anr.) v. Amrik Singh and Anr. and Civil Appeal No. 1073 of 1977 (State of Haryana & Ors.) v. Inderaj Singh, decided on January 30, 1991.
The Governor of Punjab did not amend Rules 2 and 4-A of the Rules.
These two judgments are thus obviously distinguishable from the facts of the instant case.
The State of Punjab refused to grant the benefit of 'military service' on the ground that executive instructions were issued to the effect that the benefit of military service as defined in the Rules will only be available to those employees who joined any of three Wings of the Armed Forces on or after November 1, 1962.
The State Government could not modify or abridge the provisions of Rule 2 of the Rules through executive instructions.
9.
The State of Punjab has defended its action of denying the benefit of military service only on the ground that under the executive instructions the benefit is allowed only to those Government employees who had joined the military service after the proclamation of National Emergency which is impermissible.
10.
This is precisely what has been laid down by the apex Court while disposing of Civil Appeals No. 2658-59 and 1073 of 1977.
The apex Court observed thus :  "Civil Appeal Nos. 2658-59 of 1977 : In view of the decisions in Rajpal Sharma v. State of Haryana, reported in 1985 (Supp) SCC 72 : [1985(3) SLR 573 (SC)] and Dhan Singh v. State of Haryana, reported in 1991(1) SCT 137 (SC) : JT 1990(4) SC 735, these cases are covered against the appellants and hence these appeals are dismissed without any order as to costs.
Civil Appeal No. 1073 of 1977 : Heard learned counsel for the parties.
The decision in Dhan Singh and others v. State of Haryana, reported in JT 1990(4) SC 735 relied on by the learned counsel for the appellants having been distinguishable on facts, the respondent's case having been under the old definition of 'military service' this appeal fails and is dismissed with costs qualified at Rs. 2,000/- (rupees two thousand)."
11.
The petitioners have been denied the benefit of military service wholly on irrelevant grounds.
12.
For the reasons stated above, the writ petitions succeed.
The respondents are directed to grant the benefit of military service to the petitioners in accordance with the rules after hearing them and the other affected parties.
Petition allowed.
