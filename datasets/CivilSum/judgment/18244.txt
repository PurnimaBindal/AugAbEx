MMS Bedi, J - Feeling aggrieved by order dated 122006, Annexure P-6 compulsorily retiring the petitioner from service under the provisions of Rule 918 (2) of the Punjab Police Rules, 1934 as amended by Haryana Government (Haryana Second Amendment) Rules, 1973 (for short `the Rules'), he has filed this petition under Article 226 of the Constitution.
The petitioner was enlisted as a Constable in Haryana Police on 111973 and was promoted as a Head Constable with effect from 12101982, as an Assistant Sub Inspector with effect from 181992 and as an Sub-Inspector with effect from 20122000.
2.
An adverse entry in the Annual Confidential Report for the period from 552001 to 3132002 was conveyed to the petitioner against which he had filed a representation, which was rejected by Inspector General of Police, Ambala Range respondent No. 3.
A mercy petition is alleged to be pending against the said order.
The petitioner has challenged the order of compulsory retirement dated 122006 (Annexure P-6) on the ground that he is entitled to serve up to the age of 58 years under Rule 326 (a) of the Punjab Civil Services Rules, Volume-I read with Rule 916 of the Rules.
3.
Mr. Rameshwar Sharma, learned counsel for the petitioner has contended that the order Annexure P-6 has been passed against the petitioner with a malafide intentions and without considering the commendation certificates, Annexures P-1, P-2 and P-3 which were granted to him during the years 2000- 2002.
The provisions of Rule 918(2) of the Rules have been wrongly applied to the petitioner and the order of compulsory retirement is not in public interest.
The petitioner is not proved to be an inefficient, dishonest or corrupt officer.
The order of compulsory retirement, Annexure P-6 is arbitrary, discriminatory and in violation of service rules and is liable to be quashed.
4.
We have heard learned counsel for the petitioner at length, gone through the averments made in the writ petition and the documents attached therewith.
We are of the opinion that in accordance with the statutory Rule 918 of the Rules, on completion of 25 years of qualifying service, the petitioner could have been compulsory retired on the basis of subjective satisfaction of the competent authority, who in the present case is not disputed to be respondent No. 2.
The petitioner had been given a show cause notice dated 3082005, Annexure P-4 for compulsory retirement on the basis of his service record.
The petitioner had filed a detailed reply dated 2772005, Annexure P-5 to the said show cause notice for compulsory retirement.
After considering the entire facts and circumstances of the case, the impugned order, Annexure P-6 has been passed by the Director General of Police, respondent No. 2.
So far as the adverse remarks recorded in the Annual Confidential Record for the period 552001 to 3132002 are concerned, the petitioner had been granted an opportunity to file a representation, which representation was dismissed vide order dated 1732003, Annexure P-7 passed by respondent No3.
5.
We have carefully gone through the order, Annexure P-6, which is a detailed order reflecting the subjective satisfaction of respondent No. 2 regarding the compulsory retirement of the petitioner on the ground of inefficiency, dishonesty and instances of corruption.
Rule 918(2) of the Rules is intended to be used against an officer in such cases where there are specific instances of inefficiency, dishonesty and corruption.
6.
After carefully going through the entire facts and circumstances of the present case, we are of the opinion that there is no scope for interference in exercise of the writ jurisdiction for the reasons :  i) the impugned order, Annexure P-6 has been passed by the competent authority after issuing a show cause notice to the petitioner under Rule 918(2) of the Rules after he has completed 25 years of qualifying service.
ii) the subjective satisfaction of the competent authority i.e. the Director General of Police regarding inefficiency, dishonesty or infamous conduct recorded in the impugned order, Annexure P-6 including the 10 specific defects and 14 punishments imposed upon the petitioner, cannot be interfered in a writ jurisdiction to exercise the powers like an appellate authority.
iii) the order of compulsory retirement Annexure P-6 has passed after taking into account the adverse remarks in the Annual Confidential Report for the period with effect from 552001 to 3132002 describing the petitioner as an unsuccessful officer, weak and unable to take action in time.
iv) the representation of the petitioner against the adverse remarks has been considered and rejected by the Inspector General of Police, Ambala Range vide order dated 1732003, annexure P-7, which is prior to the issuance of a show notice dated 792002 for compulsory retirement from service and the pendency of a mercy petition, if any, is of no consequence for the purpose of recording a subjective satisfaction while passing the order of compulsory retirement  v) the impugned order, Annexure P-6 is neither a punishment nor it implies any stigma as per the law laid down in Baikuntha Nath Dass and another v. Chief District Medical Officer, Baripada and another, AIR 1992(1) SC 1020.  vi) the commendation certificates Annexures P-1, P-2 and P-3 issued during the years 2000 to 2002 are of no relevance in diffusing the effect of the adverse remarks and penalties imposed upon the petitioner during his entire service career.
vii) the allegations of mala fide against respondent No 5 are vague, uncertain and have not been substantiated by the petitioner by placing any document on record.
viii) the order passed by respondent No. 2 does not seem to be influenced or actuated at the instance of respondent No. 5.
In view of the above, there is no merit in the writ petition and the same is hereby dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
