G R Majithia, J - This judgment disposes of Civil Writ Petitions No. 459 of 1993, 12029, 12078, 12251, 12508, 12659, 13391, 13393, 17071, 17731, 17832 and 18214 of 1991, 368, 807, 2347, 2348, 2378, 4765, 10292, 10373 all of 1992.
2.
In these petitions, a challenge has been made to the orders passed by the competent authority reverting the petitioners to their respective substantive rank.
Some of the petitioners were given out- of-turn ad hoc promotion as Assistant Sub Inspector or Sub Inspector of Police considering their achievements in the field of sports or outstanding performance of their official duties.
3.
C.W.P.
No. 459 of 1993 came up for hearing before A L Bahri and G C Garg, JJ.
on May 18, 1993.
In view of the important question of law arising in this petition, the Motion Bench admitted it to Division Bench and also directed that other connected cases be listed for hearing with this petition.
The Motion Bench observed thus :-  "An important question of law is being debated in this case which incidentally has already arisen in other cases also which stand admitted.
State of Haryana, Police Department, has been granting promotions on ad hoc basis particularly to sportsmen which, now it is represented on behalf of the State, were contrary to the provisions of Rule 138 of the Punjab Police Rules.
Such persons on their reversions have approached this Court in several writ petitions.
This writ petition is, therefore, admitted to DB and is ordered to be listed for hearing on August 2, 1993.
There are connected cases which stand already admitted, particulars of which presently available are : C.W.P.
No. 4765 of 1992, 13393 of 1991, CWP No. 17832 of 1991 and 17731 of 1991.
These cases are also ordered to be listed along with the present case.
The office may also find out, with the assistance of counsel, if on this point other writ petitions are pending and the same be also listed."
It is how these writ petitions have been placed before us for disposal.

6.
In C.W.P.
No. 13393 of 1991, the petitioner was given out-of-turn promotion as Sub-Inspector of Police on ad hoc basis by the Deputy Inspector General of Police, Ambala Range, Ambala, on the recommendations of the Superintendent of Police, Kurukshetra, for commendable job done by him while performing his official duties.
He was reverted to his original rank on administrative grounds.
7.
The petitioners in other connected cases were also given out-of-turn promotion for showing exemplary courage in performance of their official duties or for other distinctions obtained in the course of employment.
8.
The reversions have been justified by the respondents saying that the petitioners were promoted on purely temporary and ad hoc basis.
They had not passed the Lower School Course and thus had no right of promotion.
They had been rightly reverted to their substantive rank.
Their promotion is not envisaged by the Punjab Police Rules, 1934 as applicable to the State of Haryana.
9.
A perusal of Rules 137, 138 and 139 of the Punjab Police Rules, 1934 as applicable to the State of Haryana (for short, the Police Rules) does not envisage for ad hoc promotion.
An exception is carved out under Rule 1319 of the Police Rules.
It says that a Constable receiving the award of the President's Police and Fire Services Medal shall be promoted in the first substantive vacancy of Head Constable which occurs in the district in which he is serving subsequent to the award of the medal being gazetted.
The Police Rules did not provide for out-of-turn promotion for those who had shown exemplary courage or devotion to duty in extraordinary situations.
These also did not provide for out-of-turn promotion to those police officials who had achieved distinctions in other activities, viz. sports, etc. or had shown excellence in discharge of their duties.
Police is a disciplined force and when the Police Rules were framed, the framers of the Rules probably did not visualize a situation like the one which the police is called upon to combat in recent times.
The orders of reversion under challenge in these writ petitions may not be illegal but are not fair.
Out-of-turn promotions were granted to those who were found meritorious and had excelled others in discharge of their duties.
We feel the State Government should make appropriate provisions by amending the Police Rules providing for promotion to outstanding sportsmen or to those who have shown excellence in discharge of their duties.
Out-of-turn promotion should only be ordered by the Director General of Police.
The other authorities who are competent to order promotion should only make recommendation to the Director General of Police for granting out-of-turn promotion.
This amendment in the Police Rules is necessitated because the police in recent times have to perform duties to which they were unfamiliar when the Police Rules were framed.
We think the Director General of Police, Haryana should examine the merits of each case and find out if out-of-turn promotion made on ad hoc basis was justified.
He will take a decision in the matter keeping in view the observations made supra.
10.
The petitioners did not challenge the orders of their reversion in appeal, revision or memorial to the Director General of Police, Haryana, as the case may be.
We direct that the writ petitions be treated as appeals/revisions/memorials on behalf of the petitioners.
The Director General of Police, Haryana will dispose of the same after examining the merits of each individual case in the light of the observations made above.
Till final decision is taken by the Director General of Police, Haryana, the petitioners reversion is stayed.
With these observations, the writ petitions are disposed of.
Petition disposed of.
