MM Kumar, J - This petition filed under Article 226 of the Constitution challenges order dated 1082001 (P-21), passed in OA No. 129- CH/1995 and order dated 2092001 (P-23), passed in Review Application No. 107 of 2001, passed by the Central Administrative Tribunal, Chandigarh Bench, Chandigarh (for brevity, `the Tribunal').
2.
Skeleton facts may first be noticed.
The petitioner-Smt. Saroj Gupta was appointed as Lecturer in English in the pay scale of Rs. 700-1300 on purely temporary basis for 89 days, vide order dated 1391982 (P-1).
She continued in that capacity with breaks.
On 1371983, she was re-appointed as Lecturer on ad hoc basis.
Her services were regularized in the cadre of Lecturers with effect from 161984, vide order dated 25.6.1984 (P-2).
She was confirmed vide order dated 19/20111985 (P-2/A).
3.
Respondent No. 6-Smt. Saroj Bala was offered appointment as ad hoc Lecturer in Economics in the pay scale of Rs. 700-1300 on compassionate grounds on account of death of her father Shri Babu Ram Gupta, who died in harness, vide order Endst.
No. DPI-UT-S2/11(21) 82, dated 3.10.1983.
She joined the service on 7101983.
Her services were regularized with effect from the date of joining i.e. 7101983, vide order dated 161984 (P-4).
She was confirmed as Lecturer w.e.f.
20111985, vide order dated 1511986 (P-5).
4.
The Joint Secretary Finance, Chandigarh Administration, fixed the inter se seniority between the petitioner and respondent No. 6-Smt. Saroj Bala, vide order dated 2311995, while deciding the representations of the petitioner after issuing notice to respondent No. 6-Smt. Saroj Bala.
It was decided to place the petitioner senior in the seniority list above respondent No. 6-Smt. Saroj Bala.
The petitioner was assigned Seniority No. 20, whereas respondent No. 6-Smt. Saroj Bala was assigned Seniority No. 21 (P-16).
5.
Feeling aggrieved, respondent No. 6-Smt. Saroj Bala challenged her placement below the petitioner by filing OA No. 129-CH of 1995, under Section 19(1) of the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985.
The Tribunal allowed the OA vide order dated 1082001 and quashed order dated 2311995, by holding that ad hoc service rendered by the petitioner cannot be counted for the purposes of seniority and she cannot be placed above respondent No. 6-Smt. Saroj Bala.
Respondent No. 6-Smt. Saroj Bala has been held entitled to be senior to the petitioner on the basis of her regular appointment w.e.f.
7101983 as compared to the petitioner i.e. w.e.f.
161984.
The Tribunal further directed the respondent Chandigarh Administration to assign respondent No. 6-Smt. Saroj Bala correct position in the seniority list and to declare that she is entitled to all the consequential benefits as a result of such change in her seniority (P-21).
Thereafter, the petitioner filed an application bearing RA No. 107 of 2001, under Rule 17 of the Central Administrative Tribunal (Procedure) Rules, 1987, for review of the order dated 1082001, passed by the Tribunal (P-22).
The Tribunal dismissed the review application vide order dated 2092001 by holding that the petitioner was not able to pin point any factual mistake warranting review of order dated 1082001 (P-23).
Orders dated 1082001 and 2092001 (P-21 and P-23 respectively) are subject matter of challenge in the instant petition.
6.
After hearing learned counsel for the parties and perusing the record we are of the considered view that there is no merit in the instant petition and the same deserves to be dismissed.
It is apposite to notice that order dated 1082001, passed by the Tribunal (P-21) was also challenged by the Union Territory Administration-respondent No. 3 in this Court by filing C.W.P.
No. 5127-CAT of 2002 (Union Territory Administration, Chandigarh v. Smt. Saroj Bala and others), which was dismissed by a Division Bench of this Court, vide order dated 1042002.
The Division Bench has already dealt with and decided the controversy whether the period of temporary and ad hoc service rendered by the petitioner could be counted for fixation of her seniority in the cadre of Lecturers, in favour of respondent No. 6-Smt. Saroj Bala and against the petitioner.
The Division Bench has held as under :-  " In our opinion, there is no merit in the argument of the learned counsel and the writ petition is liable to be dismissed summarily because,  (a) the petitioner has not approached the Court with clean hands, inasmuch as, it has not placed on record copy of order dated 1371983 vide which respondent No. 2 was re-appointed as Lecturer on ad hoc basis and the order vide which her services were regularized in the cadre of Lecturers w.e.f.
161984 (reference to both these orders has been made in paragraph 2 of the impugned order).
This must have been done by the petitioner to avoid proper examination of its plea that respondent No. 2 had been initially appointed as Lecturer after regular selection.
In Hari Narain v. Badri Das, AIR 1963 SC 1558; Welcome Hotel v. State of Andhra Pradesh and others, AIR 1983 SC 1015; G Narayanaswamy Reddy v. Government of Karnataka and another, AIR 1991 SC 1726; S.P.
Chengalvarara Naidu (dead) by LRs v. Jagannath (dead) by LRs and others, 1994(1) RRR 253 : JT 1993(6) SC 331; Smt. Bhupinderpal Kaur v. The Financial Commissioner (Revenue) Punjab, (1968)70 PLR 169; Chiranji Lal and others v. Financial Commissioner, Haryana and others, (178) (1978?)
80 PLR 582 (FB); Smt. Harbhajan Kaur v. State of Punjab, 1995(3) RRR 28 : 1994 PLJ 287; Pawan Kumar v. State of Haryana and another, 1994(5) SLR 73; Jai Bhagwan Jain v. Haryana State Electricity Board, Panchkula, District Ambala, C.W.P.
No. 15448 of 1993 and M/s Kaka Ram Paras Ram and others v. State of Punjab and others, 1996(1) PLR 691, the Supreme Court and this Court have repeatedly held that a person who does not come to the Court with clean hands has no right of hearing on merits.
By applying the ratio of those decision to the facts of this case, we hold that by withholding material documents, the petitioner has forfeited its right to be heard on the merits of the case;  (b) the petitioner's plea that respondent No. 2 was initially appointed as Lecturer in 1982 after regular selection is negated by the fact that the tenure of her appointment was only for 89 days and the same was continued with breaks in her service and vide order dated 1371983, she was re-appointed as ad hoc Lecturer.
If she had been regularly selected in 1981, there was no occasion for her reappointment in 1983 and that too as ad hoc Lecturer; (c) It is settled that ad hoc service rendered by an employee de hors the selection made in accordance with the recruitment rules cannot be counted towards his seniority - Keshav Chandra Joshi and others v. Union of India and others, AIR 1991 SC 284; AK Bhatnagar and others v. Union of India and others, 1991(2) SCT 156 : (1991)1 SCC 544; Excise Commissioner, Karnataka and another v. Sreekanta, (1993) Suppl.
3 SCC 53; State of Gujarat v. CG Raiyani, 1995(2) SCT 541 : (1995)2 SCC 40; Chief of Naval Staff and another v. G Gopalakrishna Pillai and others, 1996(2) SCT 767 : (1996)1 SCC 521; YH Pawar v. State of Karnataka and another, 1996(3) SCT 380 : (1996)10 SCC 444; State of Haryana v. Amarjeet Singh, (1996)11 SCC 725; Ram Ganesh Tripathi and others v. State of UP and others, 1997(1) SCT 494 : (1997)1 SCC 621; Davinder Bathia and others v. Union of India and others, 1998(3) SCT 103 : (1998)5 SCC 262, Dr. Anuradha Bodi and others v. Municipal Corporation of Delhi and others, 1998(3) SCT 322 : (1998)5 SCC 293; Registrar General of India and another v. V Thippa Setty and others, (1998)8 SCC 690; RK Trivedi and others v. Union of India and others, (1998)9 SCC 58; Suraj Parkash Gupta and others v. State of J&K and others, 2000(3) SCT 34 : (2000)7 SCC 561; and Swapan Kumar Pal and others v. Samitabhar Charkraborty and others, 2001(2) SCT 1104 : (2001)5 SCC 581.
For the reasons mentioned above, the writ petition is dismissed".
7.
Mr. Sanjeev Thakur, learned counsel for the petitioner has made same submissions which have been rejected by the Division Bench while dismissing C.W.P.
No. 5127-CAT of 2002 filed by the UT Chandigarh.
He has not raised any additional or new argument which may warrant independent consideration by us.
Therefore, we find that the matter is squarely covered.
Even otherwise, it is well settled that period of ad hoc service rendered by an ad hoc employee does not qualify for seniority.
There is thus no merit in the petition and the same is liable to be dismissed.
8.
In view of above, following the same principle and precedent, we dismiss the instant petition.
Petition dismissed.
