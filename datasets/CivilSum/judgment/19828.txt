GR Majithia, J - The petitioners have impugned the order dated August 20, 1985 whereby their services were terminated by respondent No. 1, in the petitioner under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India.
2.
Reference to relevant facts is necessary to resolve the dispute in this petition : Respondent No. 1 is a statutory undertaking constituted by the Punjab Backward Classes Land Development and Finance Corporation Act, 1976 (Punjab Act No. 27 of 1976).
To fill in the vacant posts of Clerks a Committee was constituted by the Executive Director for recruitment to the posts of Clerks.
The petitioners along with others, submitted their applications and they were selected by the Committee.

Petitioners' services have been terminated principally on the ground that they were appointed as Clerks on temporary basis without notifying the vacancies to the Employment Exchange.
It is highlighted that appointments of Clerks in the Corporation are made in the same manner in which the petitioners were appointed.
"One Ashwani Kumar was originally appointed as a Clerk on ad hoc basis like the petitioners on August 7, 1981.
In view of the instructions issued by the Punjab Government on October 26, 1982, the services of Ashwani Kumar were regularised, by the Corporation on September 12, 1983.
Mrs. Sucheta Trehan, Kiran Inder Kaur, Meenu Kalija, Sukhdarshan Singh, Smt. Asha Rani, Jagjit Kaur and Kamaljit Kaur were appointed as Clerks in the Corporation in the manner in which the petitioners were appointed and they are still continuing in service.
The petitioners claim that their services be regularised in terms of the decision taken by the State Government on October 29, 1985.
4.
The principle defence taken by the respondents in their written statement is that on the basis of the circular letter issued by the Punjab Government dated March 29, 1985, the services of only those employees can be regularised who have been in service for a period of two years and were recruited through the Employment Exchange or by open advertisement.
Services of the other employees can be regularised if they have put in more than three years service.
5.
The defence taken by the respondents receives ample corroboration from the circular letter dated March 29, 1985.
The decision taken by the Punjab' Government vide circular letter March 29, 1985 was reiterated in another circular letter dated August 8, 1985 and in that letter, it was clearly mentioned that the services of those employees would be regularised who had put in one year's service as on April 1, 1985 and that the conditions for such regularisation will be the one which are contained in circular letter dated March 29, 1985.
Since the petitioners did not fulfil the conditions mentioned in the circular letter dated March 29, 1985, their services could not be regularised.
The decision taken by the respondent-Corporation is unexceptional.
For the reasons recorded above, the writ petition is devoid of merit and is dismissed but with no order as to costs.
Petition dismissed.
