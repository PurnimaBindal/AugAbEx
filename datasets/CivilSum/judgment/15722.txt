VS Aggarwal, J - Petitioner Mohit Gopal has filed the present writ petition seeking quashing of the order passed by respondent No. 1 (The Chandigarh Industrial and Tourism Development Corporation Limited) whereby the rank of the petitioner has been reduced along with salary.
Petitioner claims that he should be allowed to work at the same post and in the same scale.
2.
The relevant facts are that the petitioner had joined Diploma Course in the Hotel Management in the Institute of Hotel Management and Catering Technology and Nutrition, Chandigarh.
It is affiliated to the National Council for Hotel Management and Catering Technology, New Delhi.
The petitioner was offered appointment as Management Trainee by the respondent Corporation vide letter dated 2451993.
He joined on 161993.
On 771993 while the petitioner was working as a Management Trainee, he was given a letter wherein some conditions of absorption as regular appointee were detailed.
The training period was to expire on 3151995.
It was extended for one month.
On 881995 the petitioner was called for interview to be held on 1181995.
He was selected for the post of Guest Relation Executive in the pay scale of Rs. 1500-2700 and after two years his case for placement in the regular scale of Rs. 1800-3200 was to be considered.
The petitioner joined the service of respondent No. 1 on 2191995.
On 16101995 he was delivered another letter dated 2191995 wherein he was offered the post of Captain in the pay scale of Rs. 1365-2410.
The petitioner challenges the said order cancelling his appointment as Guest Relation Executive to be illegal, arbitrary and against the principles of natural justice.
He asserted that no enquiry has been held nor any show cause notice has been issued to him.
3.
Respondent No. 1 contests the petition.
It is asserted that in the communication dated 771993 it had been stipulated that on successful completion of the training, the petitioner would be considered for regular appointment in the available post carrying the pay scale of Rs. 1350-2400/-.
He could be appointed as a receptionist in the front office or Captain in the F&B Section.
Inadvertently, he was appointed against the post of Guest Relation Executive.
The appointment of the petitioner has raised furore because other similarly placed persons had been appointed in the scale of Rs. 1350-2400/-.
The mistake as such was corrected.
4.
Respondent No. 2 has also contested the petition.
5.
The short question agitated was that the impugned order, copy of which is Annexure P-7, had been passed without giving any opportunity of being heard to the petitioner and, therefore, it cannot be sustained.
6.
To appreciate the said controversy, reference can well be made to certain salient facts which are not in controversy.
It is admitted that the petitioner had been awarded a Diploma in Hotel Management by the National Council for Hotel Management and Catering Technology, New Delhi.
On 2451993 offer of appointment was sent to the petitioner (Annexure P-2).
The relevant portion of the same reads as under :-  "(iii) that it will not be obligatory on the part of the management to offer you regular appointment on completion of the training.
However, you will be considered for absorption in the Corporation against the available posts, if any, on the basis of merit."
An office order dated 771993 was issued, copy of which is Annexure P-3.
The same reads as under :-  "It is notified to all the management trainees that in case the Management considers them for absorption on the successful completion of their 2 years training, as per clause (iii) of the appointment letter, they will be considered for the regular apptt.
against the available posts carrying the pay scale of Rs. 1350-2400 and above, the gross emolument at the minimum of which in the scale of Rs. 1350-2400 shall be Rs. 3000/- pm approximately.
The status of their posts in front office shall be Receptionist and above, in F&B service, Captain and above and in Kitchen, Commis-II and above."
7.
It is abundantly clear from the aforesaid that after the completion of two years training, the petitioner along with others could be considered for regular appointment against the available posts carrying the pay scale of Rs. 1350-2400 and above.
However, on 2191995 on the basis of interview held on 1181995, the petitioner was appointed as Guest Relation Executive with the respondent-corpoartion.
He was held entitled to the scale of Rs. 1500-2700 plus usual allowances and that his placement in the scale of Rs. 1800-3200 to be considered on the basis of his performance on the completion of two years service.
The petitioner submitted joining report on 2191995.
8.
As mentioned above, on 2191995 the impugned order was issued and the petitioner was told that he has been absorbed as Captain in the Corporation in the pay scale of Rs. 1365-2410.
9.
On behalf of the respondents, it was highlighted that this was an inadvertent mistake that was corrected.
The petitioner's counsel has drawn the attention of the Court towards the proceedings whereby the petitioner was appointed as Guest Relation Executive.
10.
Admittedly, in the present case before passing the impugned order Annexure P-7, no notice to show cause was issued to the petitioner.
He was not given any opportunity of being heard.
Thus, the principles of natural justice was totally ignored.
11.
The sequence of the events clearly reveals that the petitioner had been appointed as Guest Relation Executive.
He took over the charge of the post.
Once such a decision has been arrived at and before the said order could be withdrawn, in all fairness, the petitioner should have been given the opportunity of being heard.
The impugned order could not be passed at the back of the petitioner particularly when a conscious decision had been taken.
Accordingly, the impugned order cannot be sustained.
12.
For these reasons, the writ petition is allowed.
It is directed that before passing any order against the petitioner cancelling his appointment as Guest Relation Executive, the management respondent No. 1 will issue a show cause notice to the petitioner.
The petitioner will have a right to represent against it and he shall be given opportunity of being heard.
Respondent No. 2 shall also be given an opportunity of being heard.
This exercise shall be completed within a period of three months.
13.
It is made clear that nothing said herein is any expression of opinion on the merits and, therefore, this Court is scrupulously avoiding to touch the topic because it is within the domain of the management to pass appropriate order in accordance with law.
Petition allowed.
