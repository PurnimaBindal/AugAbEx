GS Singhvi, J - In this petition filed on 491998, a prayer has been made for issuance of a writ of mandamus directing the respondents to pay compensation to the petitioner on account of alleged violation of constitutional provisions and the instructions/guidelines issued by the Government of Punjab resulting in denial of appointment to him as Labour Inspector Grade-II.
The petitioner has also prayed for directing the respondents to initiate appropriate action against the defaulting officers.
2.
Perusal of the record shows that the name of the petitioner is said to have been included in the waiting list prepared by the Departmental Selection Committee on the basis of the interview held on 461991.
According to the petitioner, he was not appointed on the available vacant post of Labour Inspector Grade-II although his name was placed at No. 2 in the waiting list.
But, some candidates who were interviewed subsequently were given appointment.
The petitioner alleges that he made representations and served legal notice upon the respondents but they have failed to issue order of appointment and in this manner, his fundamental right to equality has been infringed.
3.
Shri Pritam Singh Hundal vehemently argued that on the basis of inclusion of his name in the waiting list, the petitioner has acquired a valuable right to be appointed against the available vacant post and, therefore, a direction should be issued to the respondents to appoint him in the service and also to compensate him for the loss suffered during last 7 years due to denial of appointment.
4.
We have considered the submissions of Shri Hundal, but do not find any merit in them.
The petitioner's assertion regarding inclusion of his name in the waiting list has not been supported by any documentary or other contemporaneous evidence.
The petitioner has not placed any material on the record of writ petition to substantiate the statement that his name was included in the waiting list prepared in the year 1991.
Therefore, we cannot record a finding that he was seleted by the Departmental Selection Committee for appointment as Labour Inspector Grade-II.
Moreover, mere inclusion of his name in the waiting list is (not) sufficient to entitle the petitioner to be appointed as of right.
Even if his name was included in the main list, the petitioner could not have succeeded in persuading the Court to issue a writ of mandamus for his appointment because the employer has the prerogative not to appoint all the candidates recommended by the recruiting agency.
In support of this proposition, we may refer to the decisions of the Supreme Court in State of Haryana v. Subash Chander Marwaha and others, AIR 1973 SC 2216, Jatinder Kumar v. State of Punjab, AIR 1984 SC 1850 and Shankarsan Dash v. Union of India, 1991(2) SCT 194 (SC) : JT 1992(2) SC 380.
5.
We are also of the opinion that the so-called waiting list prepared by the Departmental Selection Committee could not have been used for making appointment against the posts which became available after 1991.
In Hoshiar Singh v. State of Haryana, 1993(4) SCT 291 (SC) : JT 1993(5) SC 63; State of Bihar v. Madan Mohan Singh, 1994(1) SCT 530 (SC) : JT 1993 (Suppl.)
SC 198; Madan Lal v. State of Jammu and Kashmir, 1995(2) SCT 880 (SC) : AIR 1995 SC 1088; Ashok Kumar v. Chairman, Banking Service Recruitment Board, 1996(1) SCT 660 (SC) : 1996(1) SCC 283; Prem Singh v. Haryana State Electricity Board, 1996(3) SCT 563 (SC) : JT 1996(5) SC 219 and Surinder Singh and others v. State of Punjab and another, JT 1997(7) SC 537 it has been held that the waiting list cannot be used for making appointments against the posts which are created or which become available after the initiation of process of recruitment.
Thus, on the basis of the alleged inclusion of his name in the waiting list, the petitioner cannot claim appointment on the post of Labour Inspector Grade-II as of right.
6.
We are also convinced that the writ petition deserves to be dismissed on the ground of delay and laches.
The petitioner has not offered any cogent explanation for the delay of 7 years in filing of the writ petition.
The representations made by him or the notice of demand for justice sent on his behalf cannot be treated as sufficient to explain the long time gap of 7 years and, therefore, no relief can be given to him in the view of the principles laid down by the Supreme Court in State of Madhya Pradesh v. Bhai Lal Bhai, AIR 1967 SC 1006 and PS Sadasivaswamy v. State of Tamil Nadu, 1976(1) SLR 53.
If he were to file a civil suit in the year 1998 for passing of a decree directing the respondents to appoint him, the same was bound to be dismissed being barred by limitation.
This fact cannot be ignored by the High Court while considering his plea for grant of relief under Article 226 of the Constitution.
For the reasons mentioned above, the writ petition is dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
