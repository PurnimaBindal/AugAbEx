Swatanter Kumar, J - The petitioner took the entrance examination conducted by Kurukshetra University, Kurukshetra, for admission to MBBS/BDS Course for the year 2001 for admission to the Colleges in the State of Haryana on 172001.
She was issued a Roll Number and her result of the entrance test was declared.
However, her candidature was cancelled and she was not granted admission to any of the course i.e. MBBS or BDS, in view of clause 18 of the Brochure.
As per clause 18 of the Brochure, the candidates who were already admitted to any course were considered ineligible for admission to any of the courses covered under the Information Brochure of MBBS/BDS, Entrance Examination, for Admission to The Medical/Dental Colleges in Haryana, 2001.
2.
The petitioner had already taken admission in MM College of Dental Science and Research (Mollana), Ambala, in February 2001 in the status meant for the Academic Sessions 2000-2001.
The petitioner had taken the entrance test in the previous year and was allocated scat in February 2001 in that College.
However, the petitioner claims to have left the said seat in March, 2001.
The respondent-University considered the petitioner ineligible for admission to the course in terms of clause 18 and has been denied seat in any of the courses for the current session.
3.
The petitioner, therefore, in the writ petition under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India challenges the validity of clause 18 on different grounds.
4.
We do not consider it necessary to discuss the various limbs of the contention raised on behalf of the petitioner in this petition as in a connected matter, we have already held that clause 18 of the brochure which reads as under is neither unconstitutional nor arbitrary.
18.
The candidates already admitted in any medical/dental colleges will not be considered eligible for admission to the course.
Resultantly, various contentions of the petitioner for the reasons stated in Writ Petition No. 11549 of 2001, Ashish Aggarwal v. Kurukshetra University, Kurukshetra and another, and C.W.P.
No. 8086 of 2001, Gauri Malik and others v. State of Haryana and another, 2002(2) SCT 297 (P&H).
pronounced to-day itself are hereby rejected.
5.
This Bench in the case of Ashish Aggarwal (supra) has held clause 18 of the brochure to be valid and constitutional, after following the judgment of the Full Bench of Delhi High Court in the case of Dr. Sandhaya Kabra v. University of Delhi, 1993(1) SCT 362 (Delhi)(FB) : 1992(7) SLR 31 (Delhi) and Dr. Veena Gupta v. University of Delhi, 1994(1) SCT 856 (Delhi)(FB) : 1994(2) S.L.R.
68 which has been approved by the Hon'ble Apex Court in the case of Arvind Kumar Kankana v. State of UP and others, 2001(3) SCT 1131 (SC) : JT 2001(6) SC 260.
6.
The other contention raised on behalf of the petitioner is that she had already given up the course of B.D.S.
in MM College of Dental Science and Research (Mollana), Ambala, even before the expiry of the last date for submission of applications for entrance test of 2001.
She left the course in April, 2001, while the entrance test was to be conducted on 172001.
She would suffer serious prejudice if she is not granted admission now.
We are not impressed by this contention.
Clause 18 is a specific bar to the very eligibility for admission to the course in 2001.
The petitioner was fully aware and conscious of the terms and conditions even before she submitted the application form for the entrance test.
Despite the specific condition she participated in the entrance test and opted of her own accord to give up the admission in the previous course as alleged.
She can blame none else but to herself for the unpleasant situation in which she is placed.
Clause 18 is a valid stipulation and operates as bar for admission to the course.
7.
We cannot find any fault as a matter of principle in the action of the respondents for treating the petitioner ineligible for admission to the course.
The terms and conditions of the brochure are binding and effective to all concerned and they must be adhered to strictly.
This was so held by a Full Bench of this court in the case of Amar Deep Sahota v. State of Punjab and another, 1993(4) SCT 328 (P&H)(FB) : 1993(2) P.L.R.
212.
The Hon'ble Apex Court has repeatedly held that mis-placed sympathy in the education matters should be avoided and admission contrary to the prospectus and calender of the University should not be allowed.
Even on this score we see no reason to grant the prayer of the petitioner.
8.
For the reasons aforestated we dismiss this writ petition.
However, it is left open for the petitioner to approach the authorities in terms of clause 19 of the brochure.
If such a representation is made to the competent authority, we do hope that the same shall be considered keeping in view the peculiar facts and circumstances of the case.
9.
The writ petition is accordingly disposed of.
Petition disposed of.
