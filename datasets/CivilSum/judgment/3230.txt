GS Singhvi, J - Petitioners Jaswant Singh, Joginder Pal Sharma and Tarlochan Singh, who are presently working as Senior Lecture Assistants, under the Directorate of Public Instruction (Colleges), Punjab, have filed this petition with a prayer that respondents be directed to give them the pay scale of Rs. 700-1,200 with selection grade of Rs. 950-1,400 with effect from 111978 till 31121985 and thereafter pay them salary in the pay scales of Rs. 1,800-3,200 and Rs. 2,000-3,500 with effect from 111986 and also to pay interest at the rate of 18% per annum on the arrears payable to the petitioners.
2.
Petitioners are working as Senior Lecture Assistants.
They have pleaded that the total service span of each of the petitioners is about 30 years.
On the strength of various orders passed by this Court in the writ petitions filed by Junior Lecture Assistants and Senior Lecture Assistants the Government issued order dated 861992 (Annexure P.1) and granted the scale of Rs. 570-1,080 with selection grade of Rs. 620-1,200 for 20% posts with effect from 111978 to the Senior Lecture Assistants, who were earlier in the pay scale of Rs. 510-800.
With effect from 111986 Senior Lecture Assistants have been given the pay scale of Rs. 1,500-2,640.
3.
Petitioners have pleaded that persons appointed as Laboratory Technicians have been given the grade of Rs. 700-1,200 with effect from 111978 and even though they are similarly situated qua the Laboratory Technicians, they have been given lower pay scale of Rs. 570-1,080 with limited benefit of selection grade of Rs. 620-1,200 for 20% posts and in this manner the respondents have discriminated the petitioners.
Another plea raised by the petitioners is that persons holding the posts of Assistants/Accountants, who were also drawing pay in the scale of Rs. 570-1,080, have been given the benefit of the pay scale of Rs. 1,800-3,200 with effect from 111986, whereas Senior Lecture Assistants who will be deemed to have been drawing salary in the scale of Rs. 570-1,080 with effect from 111978, have been deprived of the benefit of the pay scale of Rs. 1,800-3,200.
4.
In reply, the respondents have stated that Senior Lecture Assistants, like the petitioners, who were working in the Government Colleges of Punjab, have already been given the higher grade as well as the selection grade with effect from 111978 in accordance with the directions given by the High Court in C.W.P.
No. 4,020 of 1988 and L.P.A. No. 356 of 1989.
While conceding that the petitioners are entitled to selection grade of Rs. 620-1,200 in accordance with the instructions dated 29101984, the respondents have stated that the State Government has already granted them the benefit of selection grade of Rs. 620-1,200.
Reference in this connection has been made to the order Annexure R1 dated 15101992 by which selection grade has been granted to 33 Senior Lecture Assistants.
The respondents have stated that the petitioners had filed Civil Original Contempt Petition No. 623 of 1992 but the same was dismissed on 23111992.
In regard to the plea of the petitioners that the posts of Laboratory Technicians, which are inferior than the posts of Senior Lecture Assistants, have been given higher pay scale, the respondents have pleaded that only one post of Laboratory Technician exists in the Union Territory of Chandigarh and no such post exists in any of the colleges in the State of Punjab.
The post at Chandigarh is held by one Hardial Singh, who was getting pay in the scale of Rs. 510-940.
In accordance with the instructions dated 2910,1984 (Annexure P.3) benefit of selection grade was admissible in a single cadre post and in pursuance of the order passed by the High Court in C.W.P.
No. 1519 of 1987 Hardial Singh, who was working in the pay scale of Rs. 510-940 with effect from 111978, was given the benefit of selection grade of Rs. 680 1,120.
As against this, the petitioners, who will be deemed to have been working in the scale of Rs. 570-1,080 with effect from 111978, have been given the benefit of selection grade of Rs. 620-1,200.
This, according to the respondents, is more than what is due to the petitioners because in terms of order Annexure P.1 only 20% posts of Senior Lecture Assistants would have been earmarked for selection grade, but the Government has given this benefit to all the incumbents.
The comparison made by the petitioners between the posts of Senior Lecture Assistants on the one hand and the posts of Assistants/Accountants on the other hand has also been contested by the respondents on the ground that the posts of Assistants/Accountants have always been treated as higher than the posts of Senior Lecture Assistants.
Respondents have pleaded that the posts of Assistants/Accountants always carried a pay scale higher than that of the posts of Senior Lecture Assistants.
5.
The argument of Shri Gulshan Sharma, learned counsel for the petitioners, is that the persons holding the posts of Senior Lecture Assistants enjoy a status better than that of the Laboratory Technicians and, therefore, there is no justification, legal or otherwise, to give benefit of higher grade to the Laboratory Technicians as compared to the Senior Lecture Assistants.
Learned counsel argued that the posts of Laboratory Technician were in the grade of Rs. 510-940 as against the pay scale of Rs. 570-1,080 admissible to the Senior Lecture Assistants as on 111978 and, therefore, when the Government decided to give the grade of Rs. 680-1,120 to the Laboratory Technicians, there could be no justification for denial of that grade to the Senior Lecture Assistants.
Learned counsel argued that this action of the respondents has resulted in a serious anomaly and at the same time a hostile discrimination has been practised by the Government qua the Senior Lecture Assistants.
The second argument of Shri Sharma is that the posts of Senior Lecture Assistants have been treated at par with those of Assistants/Accountants as on 111978 because both these posts have been given the pay scale of Rs. 570-1,080.
However, by virtue of notification dated 1561990 the posts of Assistants/Accountants have been given the grade of Rs. 1,800-3,200 with effect from 111986 and the Senior Lecture Assistants have been given the grade of Rs. 1,500-2,640.
This, according to the learned counsel, has resulted in a situation where the posts carrying identical pay scale will now carry different pay scales.
Learned counsel argued that this action of the respondents is without any reason or rhyme and is, therefore, patently arbitrary resulting in violation of fundamental rights of the petitioners to be paid salary according to the principle of equal pay for equal work.
Learned Assistant Advocate-General argued that the posts of Senior Lecture Assistants always carried a lower pay scale vis-a-vis the posts of Laboratory Technicians as also the posts of Assistants/Accountants.
The learned counsel argued that when disparity in the pay scales of the various posts existed even prior to the revision of pay scales with effect from 111978 the petitioners are not entitled to plead that they have been discriminated.
Learned counsel further argued that the posts of Senior Lecture Assistants cannot be equated with the posts of Laboratory Technicians and Assistants/Accountants because these posts belong to different cadres and different departments of the Government.
Learned counsel further argued that the principle of equal pay for equal work cannot be applied without regard to the facts of the case and when employees belong to different cadres and different services, though under one employer, parity in the pay scales cannot be claimed.
6.
During the course of hearing, learned Assistant Advocate General placed before the Court a statement showing the pay scales of the posts of Senior Lecture Assistants, Assistants in 'A' Class offices, Assistants in 'B' Class Offices, and Accountants.
She has also produced before the Court a copy of the decision taken by the Government of Punjab, on the recommendations of the Anomalies Committee.
The scales of pay prescribed for different posts, to which reference has been made herein above, are as under :  Senior Lecture Assistants Assistants in 'A' Class offices Assistants in 'B' Class offices Accountants Prior to 121968 60-175 150-300 106-200 80-175 W.E.F.
121968 120-250 225-500 160-400 140-300 W.E.F.
111978 400-660 (further revised to 510-800 and 570-1,080 in the light of judgment of High Court) 600-1120 570-1080 570-1,080 W.E.F.
1.1.86 1,500-2,640 1,800-3,200 (to be designated as Senior Assistants) 1,800-3,200 1,800-3,200 (to be designated as Senior Assistant (Accounts) 7.
The posts of Laboratory Technicians were in the pay scale of Rs. 160-400 with effect from 1.2.1968 and the posts of Senior Lecture Assistants were in the grade of Rs. 120-250.
For Laboratory Technicians it was revised to Rs. 510-800 with effect from 111978 and the pay scale of Senior Lecture Assistants was revised to Rs. 400-660.
Subsequently holders of both the posts have got higher pay scale in view of the decisions of this Court.
From the above, it is clear that the posts of Senior Laboratory Assistants did not carry pay scale identical to the pay scale of the posts of Laboratory Technician.
Similarly, the posts of Assistants in 'A' Class offices, Assistants in 'B' Class Offices and Accountants carried pay scale higher than the pay scale of the Senior Lecture Assistants.
The parity in the pay scales of Assistants in 'B' Class Offices and Accountants on the one hand and Senior Lecture Assistants on the other hand was for the first time brought about on 861992 with the issue of Order Annexure P.1 in pursuance of the directions given by this Court.
Though the petitioners have claimed parity with the posts of Laboratory Technicians, no material has been placed before this Court to establish similarity between the posts of Senior Lecture Assistants and Laboratory Technicians.
Petitioners have neither given the qualifications prescribed for the posts of Laboratory Technicians and Senior Lecture Assistants nor have they disclosed the nature of duties required to be performed by the holders of the different posts.
In the absence of such material having been placed before the Court, it is not possible to record a finding that there is an identity in the qualifications and nature of duties of the two posts and, therefore, they should be placed in the same pay scale.
Similarly, qua the posts of Assistants and Accountants the petitioners have not pleaded and proved similarity in the qualifications and nature of duties.
Therefore, simply on the strength of the fact that these posts came to be given identical pay scales with effect from 111978, the petitioners cannot seek mandamus to the respondents on the anvil of the principle of equal pay for equal work.
In my considered view, the petitioners have not been able to lay foundation in the petition showing discrimination between the persons similarly situated and, therefore, it will have to be held that they have failed to discharge the primary burden which lay upon them to show that the respondents have violated their fundamental rights under Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India.
8.
The above conclusion, however, does not lead to total rejection of the claim made by the petitioners.
Undoubtedly, the Government has thought it proper to bring about parity in the pay scales of the Senior Lecture Assistants on the one hand and Assistants/Accountants on the other hand by giving the pay scale of Rs. 570-1,080 to the holders of all these posts in the light of Court orders.
The Government has subsequently implemented the recommendations of the Third Pay Commission with effect from 111978 and prescribed revised pay scales for the various posts.
At this juncture, different pay scales have come to be prescribed for the Senior Lecture Assistants on the one hand and the Assistants/Accountant on the other hand.
The petitioners did make a representation to the Government alleging that an anomaly has been brought about in the pay scales of the posts which carried identical pay scales from 111978.
The Government has not conveyed any decision to the petitioners on their representation.
Letter dated 1891991, a copy of which has been placed before the Court by the learned Assistant Advocate-General during the course of hearing, does not in any manner indicate consideration of the grievance made by the petitioners in their representation Annexure P.8, which they submitted after the issue of notification dated 1561990.
The grievance of the petitioners does require an examination by the Government in the light of the facts which have been incorporated in the representation and which may otherwise be brought to the notice of the Government.
9.
For the reasons recorded above, the writ petition is dismissed subject to a direction to the Government to consider the representation made by the petitioners on 151993 and decide the claim made by the petitioners for parity in the matter of pay scales vis-a-vis Laboratory Technicians/Assistants/Accountants.
The petitioners are free to bring additional facts to the notice of the Government by making another representation within a period of one month of the receipt of a copy of this order and the Government shall decide their representation within the next two months by passing a speaking order.
If the petitioners feel aggrieved against the decision of the Government, they shall be entitled to file a fresh petition by incorporating material facts in support of their plea of discrimination.
Petition dismissed.
