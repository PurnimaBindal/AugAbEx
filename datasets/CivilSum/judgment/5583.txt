The petitioners were engaged as seasonal workers in the Morinda Co- operative Sugar Mills Limited (respondent No. 2).
According to them, they worked from October 1986 to March 1991 as daily wage workers during the crushing seasons and they were not confirmed and their services were retrenched by the 2nd respondent without considering them for further appointments even on seasonal basis.
The petitioners raised an industrial dispute which was referred to the Labour Court, Patiala for adjudication.
On the basis of the material placed before it, the Labour Court held that the petitioners are not entitled to any relief as they had not rendered 240 days of continuous service.
In respect of some other workers, who had completed 240 days of service in a year, the relief of reinstatement was granted.
As the petitioners' claim was rejected by the Labour Court, they have filed this writ petition to quash the order of the Labour Court.

They worked during the crushing season and that too for short period.
The petitioners were taken into work for the season or during the season and after closure of the season, they ceased to work.
Therefore, it is clear that the petitioners did not work for more than 240 days in a year.
In Civil Appeal Nos. 8058-60 of 1995 the Hon'ble Supreme Court vide its order dt. Aug 25, 1996 has held as follows :  "The question is whether such a cessation would amount to retrenchment.
Since it is only a seasonal work, the respondents cannot be said to have been retrenched in view of what is stated in clause (bb) of Section 2(oo) of the Act.
Under these circumstances, we are of the opinion that the view taken by the Labour Court and the High Court is illegal.
However, the appellant is directed to maintain a register for all workmen engaged during the seasons enumerated hereinbefore and when the new season starts the appellant should make a publication in neighbouring places in which the respondents normally live and if they would report for duty, the appellant would engage them in accordance with seniority and exigency of work."
The above order of the Supreme Court came to be passed in appeal filed by the 2nd respondent i.e. Morinda Co-operative Sugar Mills Limited.
In this writ petition, I am also of the opinion that the same order has to be passed.
I do not, therefore, find any illegality and infirmity in the order of the Labour Court.
But, however, as directed by the Supreme Court, the 2nd respondent i.e. Morinda Co-operative Sugar Mills Limited is directed to maintain a register for all seasons and when the new season starts, the 2nd respondent shall make a publication in the neighbouring places in which the petitioners normally live and if they report for duty, they would be engaged in accordance with their seniority and exigency of work.
With the above directions, the writ petition is dismissed.
There will be on order as to costs.
Petition dismissed.
