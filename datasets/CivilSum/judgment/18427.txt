Surya Kant, J - This Appeal has been directed against the judgment dated 2171988 passed by the learned Single Judge whereby C.W.P.
No. 2618 of 1986 filed by Respondent No. 1 was allowed and he was declared senior to the present Appellant as well as proforma Respondent No. 4 to 6 in the Cadre of Analyst in the Department of Health and Family Welfare, Government of Haryana.
2.
The brief facts necessary to be referred for adjudication of the present Appeal are that Respondent No. 1 joined as a Chemist in the Health Department on 17.4.1959 in the erstwhile State of Punjab and was thereafter appointed as Pharmaceutical-cum-Water Chemist on 2421964 in the same department.
Vide an order dated 7101996 (Annexure P-1), the promotion of Respondent No. 1 as an Analyst was approved of.
Pursuant to this order, the Public Analyst, Punjab, issued an order dated 12101966 (Annexure P-2) thereby formally promoting Respondent No. 1 as an Analyst in the pay scale of Rs. 200-15-290/20-450.
On the re-organisation of the erstwhile State of Punjab, Respondent No. 1 was allocated to the State of Haryana with effect from 1111966 vide an order dated 27.2.1969 (Annexure P-3), passed under Section 82(2) of the Punjab Re- organisation Act, 1966.
It needs mention here that the Appellant and proforma Respondent Nos. 4 and 5 had joined as Analysts in the State of Haryana initially on ad hoc basis on 471969, 11.4.1967 and 7.7.1968 respectively.
Subsequently, they were appointed on regular basis on different dates in the month of November and December, 1970 on the recommendations of the Subordinate Services Selection Board (hereinafter referred to as the Board), Haryana.
Respondent No. 6 was promoted as an Analyst on 961972.
Apparently some doubt was entertained against the nature of appointment of Respondent No. 1 as an Analyst, hence, the department made a reference to the Board seeking approval of appointment of Respondent No. 1 as an Analyst on regular basis.
The Board accorded the aforesaid approval vide its letter dated 6101972 (Annexure P- 5).
From the averments made in the written statement filed by the official Respondents, it is evident that another reference was made to the Board vide memo dated 30121981 (Annexure R-1) seeking clarification as to from which date services of Respondent No. 1 were to be treated on regular basis.
The Board vide its reply dated 1931982 (Annexure R-2) responded that Respondent No. 1 was to be treated to have been regularly appointed as an Analyst from the date of Board's letter dated 6101972.
3.
Notwithstanding the inter se communication between the department and the Board referred to above, the Director, Health Services, issued gradation lists as the same stood on 111975, 111979 and 111981.
Respondent No. 1 was shown to be senior to the present Appellant and proforma Respondent Nos. 4 to 6 in these gradation lists in the Cadre of Analysts by taking his date of appointment as 12101966.
Not only this, the Respondent No. 1 was further promoted over and above the Appellant and proforma Respondent Nos. 4 to 6 to the post of Deputy Public Analyst initially on officiating basis vide order dated 6121979 and thereafter, on regular basis vide order dated 25.3.1981 (Annexure P-11) retrospectively from 6121979.
It seems that the Appellant submitted a representation against the gradation list as it stood on 111975, claiming seniority over Respondent No. 1 on the premise that the said Respondent No. 1's promotion as an Analyst was on ad hoc basis and his services were regularised with the approval of the Board with effect from 6101972, therefore, he was liable to be ranked junior to the Appellant who had already been recruited, on regular basis, on the recommendations of the Board.
The said representation was, however, rejected vide an order dated 981976 (Annexure P-10).
It seems that the Appellant and Respondent Nos. 4 to 6 again raked up the issue of their seniority vis-a-vis Respondent No. 1 and outcame an order dated 12.5.1986 (Annexure P-12) placing Respondent No. 1 below the Appellant and proforma Respondent Nos. 4 to 6 in the seniority list of the Analysts.
Respondent No. 1 challenged the order Annexure P-12 by way of C.P.W.
No. 2618 of 1986 which was allowed by the learned Single Judge vide the impugned judgment dated 2171988, holding that in the absence of statutory service rules governing service conditions of the parties, their inter se seniority was to be determined on the basis of the continuous length of service, therefore, Respondent No. 1 was entiteld to be ranked senior to the Appellant as well as Respondent Nos. 4 to 6.
The order Annexure P-12 was accordingly quashed.
Hence, this Letters Patent Appeal.
4.
We have heard the learned counsel for the parties and have perused the relevant record with the able assistance of Shri Randhir Singh, learned Senior Deputy Advocate General, for the State of Haryana.
5.
It has been contended on behalf of the Appellant that not only the initial appointment of Respondent No. 1 as Pharmaceutical-cum-Water Chemist but his promotion as an Analyst made vide orders dated 7101966 (Annexure P-1) and 12101966 (Annexure P-2), was also on ad hoc basis.
Strong reliance has been placed upon letters dated 951959 (Annexure A-1), 30.8.1971 (Annexure A-2) and 1721967 (Annexure A-3) which the Appellant has placed on record along with C.M.
No. 2467 of 1989.
It has been further contended that services of Respondent No. 1 were regularised with the approval of the Board vide order dated 6101972 and vide subsequent order dated 1931982 (Annexure R-3/5); the Board had clarified that the appointment of Respondent No. 1, on regular basis, shall take effect from the date of issuance of the Board's letter dated 6101972, therefore, services of Respondent No. 1 stood regularised with effect from 6101972 only.
Since the Appellant and Respondent Nos. 4 and 5 were appointed as Analysts on regular basis on the recommendations of the Board in November/December, 1970 and Respondent No. 6 having been promoted as such on 661972, they are entitled to be ranked senior to Respondent No. 1 in the Cadre of Analysts.
Supplementing the aforesaid argument, it has also been contended on behalf of the Appellant that the services rendered by Respondent No. 1 as an Analyst prior to 6101972 were on ad hoc basis, therefore, the same cannot be counted towards seniority.
Reliance has been placed on R Hariharan and others v. K Balachandran Nair and others, 2000(4) SCT 740 (SC) : JT 2000(10) SC 242, Dr. (Mrs.) Pushpa Vishnu Kumar Gurtu v. State of Maharashtra and others, 1995(3) SCT 91 (SC) : JT 1995(3) SC 518, CK Antony v. B Muraleedharan and others, 1998(4) SCT 151 (SC) : JT 1998(6) SC 11, Dr. Anuradha Bodi and others v. Municipal Corporation of Delhi and others, 1998(3) SCT 322 (SC) : AIR 1998 SC 2093 and Ashok Gulati and others v. BS Jain and others, 1966(4) SLR 4.
6.
On the other hand, learned counsel for Respondent No. 1 argued that the Respondent No. 1 was promoted as an Analyst on regular basis vide order dated 7101996 (Annexure P-1) and in compliance thereto, he was actually promoted vide order dated 12.10.1996 (Annexure P-2) on regular basis only.
The learned counsel contended that authorities in the department misdirected themselves in making reference to the Board for seeking purported approval for promotion of Respondent No. 1 whereas no such approval was required under any rules/instructions.
It has been further contended that vide order dated 7101966 (Annexure P-1), certain candidates were appointed as Analysts on ad hoc basis as well but they were directly recruited candidates appointed on the recommendations of the Employment Exchange, Chandigarh.
However, two departmental candidates, namely, Respondent No. 1 and one Shri Swaran Singh were promoted on regular basis as it is not stipulated qua them that their promotions were on ad hoc basis.
It is for this precise reason that in the formal promotion order dated 12101966 (Annexure P-2) it was nowhere mentioned that the afore-mentioned promotion was on ad hoc basis.
Thus, there was no occasion to seek approval of the Board for regularising promotion of Respondent No. 1.
Alternatively, it has been contended by the learned counsel for Respondent No. 1, that approval granted by the Board relates back to the date of original promotion of Respondent No. 1, therefore, also, he is entitled to be treated as regularly promoted Analyst with effect from 12101966 only.
Learned counsel for the Respondent No. 1 also submitted that in the gradation lists, Respondent No. 1 has been repeatedly shown senior to the Appellant as well as Respondent Nos. 4 to 6 and based upon his settled seniority only, he was promoted as Deputy Public Analyst prior to the Appellant as well as Respondents 4 to 6 and that the representation of the Appellant having been rejected way back on 981976 (Annexure P-1), his belated claim should not have been entertained by the department itself and that too after a period of almost 10 years when the impugned order dated 12.5.1986 (Annexure P-12) was passed.
7.
To appreciate the rival contentions, it will be apposite to reproduce relevant part of the promotion orders dated 7101966 (Annexure P-1) and dated 12101966 (Annexure P-2) :-  Extract from order dated 7101966  "Reference correspondence resting with your DO letter No. 6254 dated the 29th September, 1988 on above noted subject.
The approval is hereby accorded to the appointments of the following candidates recommended by the Employment Exchange, Chandigarh as Analyst in the Public Analyst Laboratory in the scale of Rs. 200-15-290/20-450, plus usual allowances from the date of their joining upto 21st Oct, 1966:- 1.
Shri Madan Lal Goyal 2.
Shri Kapil Krishan Kochhar 3.
Shri Mohan Lal 4.
Shri Mohan Singh 5.
Shri Ranbir Kumar Gupta 6.
Shri Yog Raj Sawhney  7.

Extract from order dated 12101966 "Reference Director, Health Services, Punjab letter No. TEPH-S/66/23828, dated 7th October, 1966 on the above mentioned subject.

Sd/-  Ph.D (Taxes) ARIC,  Public Analyst, Punjab,  Chandigarh."
A bare perusal of the above reproduced orders clearly shows that promotion of Respondent No. 1 was never termed on "ad hoc basis".
The afore-mentioned promotion was also not subjected to any other legally permissible rider.
It is an admitted case of the parties that there are no statutory service rules governing recruitment to the post of Analyst.
It is/was, therefore, open to the competent authority to make appointment either by direct recruitment or by promotion.
It cannot be disputed that so far as the regular recruitment is concerned, it could be made through the prescribed recruiting agency, namely, the State Public Service Commission or the Subordinate Services Selection Board, as the case may be.
On the other hand, appointment by promotion falls within the exclusive domain of the competent authority.
In our view, the order Annexure P-1 has been passed consciously inasmuch as the appointment of the directly recruited candidates on the recommendations of the employment exchange which were restricted upto a specific period and, thus, was an ad hoc arrangement.
But no such restriction was imposed while promoting the departmental candidates, namely, Shri Swaran Singh and Respondent No. 1.
8.
We have, therefore, no hesitation in holding promotion of Respondent No. 1 as an Analyst was not irregular in any manner, therefore, the same is liable to be treated on regular basis for all intents and purposes.
So far as the reliance placed by the Appellant upon letters Annexures A-1, A-2 and A-3 is concerned, it is suffice to say that A-1 relates to employment of Respondent No. 1 as a work-charge Chemist in the year 1959.
This order neither reflects the nature of his appointment of Respondent No. 1 as Pharmaceutical-cum-Water Chemist, i.e., the feedor post nor the nature of his promotion to the post of Analyst.
Similarly, the order dated 30.8.1971 (Annexure A-2) sent by the Director, Health and Family Planning, Punjab to the Director, Health Services, Haryana merely suggests that promotion of Respondent No. 1 on the ex-cadre post of Chemist could not be considered on regular basis unless the approval of Public Service Commission was obtained, as has been done by the Director of Health and Family Planning, Punjab in the case of Shri Swaran Singh (second promotee vide order Annexure P-1).
Though no rules or instructions have been referred to requiring approval of the Public Service Commission/Subordinate Services Selection Board in the matter of promotions, yet we are of the view that the so-called irregularity pointed out in the said communication stood rectified as soon as the promotion of Respondent No. 1 was approved by the Subordinate Services Selection Board, Haryana vide order dated 6101972 (Annexure P-5).
Likewise, the communication dated 1721967 (Annexure A-3) is hardly of any consequence as the same was passed during the transit period of allocation/re-allocation of officers and employees like Respondent No. 1 to one or the other State on the re-organisation of erstwhile State of Punjab.
In fact the communication dated 1721967 (Annexure A-3) stood superseded vide subsequent order dated 27.2.1969 (Annexure P-3) whereby, in exercise of the powers conferred by sub-section (2) of Section 82 of the Punjab Re- organisation Act, 1966, Respondent No. 1 was finally allocated to the State of Haryana with effect from 111966.
We cannot lose site (sight ?)
of the fact that irrespective of the communications Annexures A-2 and A-3, Respondent No. 1 was placed above the Appellant as well as Respondent Nos. 4 to 6 in the gradation lists of Analysts as it stood on 111975 or even thereafter.
Further, the claim of the Appellant for fixing his seniority above Respondent No. 1 in the Gradation list as it stood on 111975, was considered and rejected by the competent authority vide its order dated 981976 (Annexure P-10).
More so, Respondent No. 1 was further promoted as Deputy Public Analyst initially on officiating basis with effect from 6121979 and thereafter on regular basis from the same date with the approval of the Haryana Public Service Commission, as is evident from the order dated 241981 (Annexure P- 11).
It is quite queer that despite rejecting seniority claim of the Appellant on 981976 (Annexure P-10), the authorities permitted him to take up the same controversy after a period of almost 10 years when the impugned order dated 12.5.1986 (Annexure P-12) was passed placing Respondent No. 1 below the Appellant and Respondent Nos. 4 to 6 on the premise that his promotion as Analyst was approved by the Subordinate Services Selection Board since 6101972, therefore, he would be treated to have been promoted as such from the aforesaid date only.
In our view, the administrative authorities should have been circumspect in unsettling a settled seniority after a reasonably long period as it not only breeds avoidable litigation but also causes adverse affect on the efficiency and administrative performance of the officials.
9.
It has also been contended on behalf of the Appellant that since the promotion of Respondent No. 1 was approved by the Subordinate Services Selection Board from 6101972, his seniority as an Analyst is liable to reckon from the said date only.
This submission is unimpressive and does not stand any ground.
Assuming that the approval of the Haryana Public Service Commission/Subordinate Services Selection Board is mandatory for reglarising a promotee; in our view once such promotion is approved by the Commission/Board, the same shall relate back to the date on which the promotion took place and the entire service will have to be computed in reckoning the seniority according to the length of continuous officiation; the same principle may not apply in case of direct recruitment inasmuch as an ad hoc appointee, if competes before the Commission/Board for direct recruitment on regular basis, then his inter se seniority viz. other directly recruited candidates shall be determined in accordance with the service rules, if any, or on the basis of their inter se merit in the selection list.
10.
To be fair to the learned counsel for the Appellant, reference to some of the judicial precedents relied upon by him needs to be made.
There can hardly be any dispute with the settled disposition of law that when an initial appointment is on ad hoc basis, the seniority will reckon only from the date of regular appointment as ad hoc appointments are made de hors of the service rules.
However, having held that the initial promotion of Respondent No. 1 was not on ad hoc basis and is, therefore, liable to be treated as a regular promotion for all intents and purposes, we are of the view that the ratio descendi of the judgments relied upon by the learned counsel for the Appellant is not attracted towards the facts and circumstances of the present case.
In our view, the learned Single Judge rightly held that in the absence of statutory services rules governing inter se seniority, the principle of continuous length of service was required to be invoked and seniority of Respondent No. 1 in the cadre of Analysts is to reckon from the date of his promotion i.e. 12101966, therefore, he ranks senior to Appellant and Respondent Nos. 4 to 6.
We, therefore, find no merit in this Appeal.
The same is accordingly dismissed with no order as to costs.
Appeal dismissed.
