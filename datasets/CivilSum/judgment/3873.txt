RL Anand, J - By this judgment, I dispose of as many as six writ petitions i.e. C.W.P.
No. 14245 of 1997, titled Dr. Bani Singh v. Kurukshetra University Kurukshetra, and other C.W.P.
No. 8540 of 1997, titled R.N.P.
Garg and others v. Kurukshetra University, Kurukshetra and others, C.W.P.
No. 7558 of 1999, titled Dr. ML Gogana v. Kurukshetra University, Kurukshetra and others, C.WP.
No. 3107 of 1999, Himmat Singh Sinha and others v. Kurukshetra University, Kurukshetra and others C.W.P.
No. 3460 of 1998, titled HL Verma v. Kurukshetra University, Kurukshetra and others and C.W.P.
No. 1133 of 1997, Umed Singh v. Kurukshetra University, Kurukshetra and others, as in the opinion of this Court, these six writ petitions can be disposed of by one judgment as common question of law and fact arises in all the six writ petitions.
2.
I am taking the facts from C.W.P.
No. 14525 of 1997, titled Dr. Bani Singh v. Kurukshetra University, Kurukshetra and others.
This writ petition has been filed by Dr. Bani Singh under Articles 226 of the Constitution of India seeking the directions against the respondents that they be asked to grant the benefit of pension to the petitioner in lieu of the University Contributory Provident Fund for which the option has already been taken from the petitioner.
The petitioner has made a further prayer for issuing a writ of certiorari striking down the cut off date i.e. 141995 for introduction of the pension scheme in Kurukshetra University and the date may be fixed as 111992 at par with the CCS Haryana Agriculture University, Hisar.
All the petitioners are the ex-employees of the Kurukshetra University and admittedly they had retired from their respective services prior to 141995.
So far as Dr. Bani Singh petitioner is concerned, he retired from service of Kurukshetra University on 3091993.
Kurukshetra University was in existence even prior to the formation of the Haryana State in the year 1966.
It was the first University which came into existence.
Lateron Haryana Agriculture University, Hisar and MD University, Rohtak were established.
All the these Universities have their own constitutions and are statutory bodies established under separate State Acts.
The basic structure, function and purpose behind the formation of all these Universities is the same.
These Universities were formed for imparting general/professional education in the State of Haryana in order to spread literacy, higher education, vocational and professional in the State of Haryana.
The administrative staff as well as the teaching staff of all the three Universities is a part of separate independent autonomous body, yet to ensure parity in all matters of administration, Finance, Education and Welfare, all the Universities are supervised and finally controlled by the common Chancellor and a High power Co-ordination Committee.
According to the petitioners, a pension scheme was sought to be introduced for the employees of the State Government.
This pension scheme was under the active consideration of the State Government and options were sought from the employees of all the Universities in the year 1988-89.
The options were also sought from the petitioners in the year 1988 itself in order to implement the State Government Pension Scheme.
This matter was also considered at the highest level i.e. Executive Council.
The Executive Council passed resolution dated 18121992 approving the scheme.
Everything was going well but surprisingly the case of Haryana Agricultural University, Hisar was alienated and in this University pension scheme was introduced by the State Government with effect from 111992.
However, a step-brotherly treatment was given to Kurukshetra University and MD University.
Their case for granting pension to their employees remained deferred and the State Government kept considering their matter from time to time.
The matter was finally considered by the Sub Committee of HPCC in its meeting held on 2821995 and the Committee resolved and implemented the recommendation of the pension scheme in lieu of the Contributory Provident Fund in the Kurukshetra University and MD University while clearly specifying that the grant of pension would not pose any additional burden on the State Exchequer for the next 15-20 years.
3.
The case set up by the petitioners is that the pension scheme was introduced in the Kurukshetra University and MD University with effect from 141995 and this cut off date i.e. 141985 (141995 ?)
is arbitrary and had no basis.
Rather it should have been introduced with effect from 111992 as was done in the case of Haryana Agricultural University, Hisar.
Moreover, as per the decision of the Executive Council the scheme should have been introduced with effect from 111992.
The petitioners were not satisfied with this decision of the Government and they made representation on the ground that the cut off date i.e. 141995 is arbitrary, discriminatory and inequitable.
With this background, the petitioners have made the above prayer.
4.
Notices of the various writ petitions were given to the respondents.
The stand of respondent No. 1 i.e. Kurukshetra University is that it wrote to the State Government for the implementation of the pension scheme with effect from 111992 but the State Government has decided that pension scheme be implemented with effect from 141995 only.
The rules have been approved by the State Government.
These have also been put before the Executive Council which has also approved the same.
It is not within the domain of the answering University to fix the cut off date.
The State being sole funding agency, respondent No. 1 is bound by the decision of the State Government.
Moreover, the petitioners have withdrawn the Contributory Provident Fund along with other retiral benefits and, therefore, they have no locus standi to file the present writ petitions.
It was also pleaded by respondent No. 1 that it has to take prior approval in all matters concerning financial liability from the State Government.
The matter was sent to the State Government for the implementation of the scheme with effect from 141992 but the same was declined.
The petitioners have retired prior to 141995, therefore, they cannot claim the benefit of pension scheme.
The draft rules were sent for approval to the State Government and those were approved with some modification.
Rules were again put up before the Executive Council which has approved the same.
5.
The separate written statement was filed by respondents No. 2 and 3.
It is stated by the these respondents that the State Government vide letter dated 2291995 approved the proposal for the introduction of the pension scheme in Kurukshetra University with effect from 141995 with following conditions :-  (i) The scheme will be introduced with effect from 141995 and shall be applicable only to the employees of Kurukshetra University, Kurukshetra retiring on or after 141995.
(ii) The scheme will be implemented in view of the provisions under CSR Volume II which is applicable in case of Government employees.
(iii) The amendments/instructions issued/being issued by the Haryana Government from time to time on the subject will be followed by the University.
(iv) Five copies of the approved scheme will be sent to the Finance Department.
6.
It was also pleaded by respondents No. 2 and 3 that University was required to frame the scheme keeping in view the provisions contained in CSR Volume-II which is applicable in the case of the Government employees.
The respondent No. 1 did not frame the scheme is accordance with those provisions.
Resultantly, respondent No. 2, i.e.
The Director Higher Education, Haryana vide letter dated 6111995 requested respondent No. 1 to send the seven copies of the approved/final scheme strictly in accordance with the conditions laid down by the Government within 15 days for onward transmission to the State Government.
The respondent No. 1 submitted the seven copies of the scheme on 1121995.
The scheme was examined and it was found that scheme was not in accordance with the conditions laid down by the State Government.
Resultantly, respondent No. 1 was requested vide letter dated 2511996 to make necessary amendments in the scheme.
Vide letter dated 19.11.1996 the State Government again advised the Directorate to bring the discrepancies in the scheme to the notice of the University and the University be asked to prepare the pension scheme after removing the discrepancies strictly in accordance with the provisions of CSR Volume-II.
The detailed guidelines were issued to respondent No. 1.
The matter remained under correspondence and after thorough discussion with the representatives of both the parties, respondent No. 1 submitted the proposal on 2541997 and the same was not by respondent No. 2 to the State Government on 2851997.
Resultantly, the State Government approved the proposal of the University on 17101997 with certain conditions and advised the University to finalise the rules/amend the statutes accordingly.
7.
Moreover, Agricultural University, Hisar was established under the Central Act whereas the Kurukshetra University was established under the State Act.
Agricultural University is a residential University in character whereas Kurukshetra University is of affiliating character and as such two Universities are distinguishable.
Some of the employees of the University who retired prior to 141995 have already been paid their Contributory Provident Fund and other benefits.
There is no parity between the employees of Kurukshetra University and Agricultural University, Hisar, therefore, the writ petitions are liable to be dismissed.
8.
I have heard S/Shri O.P. Goyal, SS Narula.
RK Malik and Shri Tribhuwan Dahiya, learned counsel appearing on behalf of the petitioners and Shri SC Sibal and Ms Ritu Bahri, learned DAG, appearing on behalf of the respondents and with their assistance have gone through the record of this case.
9.
Two points have arisen me for determinations: whether the Government has the right to fix the cut off date i.e. 141995 in the case of the petitioners and (ii) whether this date is discriminatory and the ancillary point for determination for this Court would be whether this Court should pass the directions against the respondents that the cut off date shall be treated with effect from 111992.
10.
In their concentrated efforts the learned counsel appearing on behalf of the petitioners vehemently submitted that the move for introducing the pension scheme was started in the year 1988-89 when Kurukshetra University wrote to the Chairmans of all the University Teaching Departments, Principal, University College and Head/Branch Officers of all the University Non-Teaching Departments/Offices, and that the University itself considered the policy decision of the State Government introducing the pension scheme in the month of December, 1992, therefore, the State Government was not justified in introducing the pension scheme with effect from 141995.
It was also pointed out by the counsel for the petitioner by making a reference to Annexure P-3 that in the case of Haryana Agricultural University, Hisar the Government took a decision for the introduction of the scheme with effect from 111992.
Therefore, there cannot be discrimination vis-a-vis the teaching staff of the Kurukshetra University.
Counsel also invited my attention to the document Annexure P-4 through which the Kurukshetra University wrote to all the Chairpersons of the Teaching Department/Branch Officers of Non Teaching Offices clearly indicating their intention to introduce the scheme prior to 141995 and, therefore, there was no justification to introduce this scheme with effect from 141995.
Even the Committee which held its meeting on 28.2.1995 under the Chairmanship of the Financial Commissioner Education took a decision of the implementation of the pension scheme.
In was also contended that the source of money is the same i.e.
The State Government and Financial implications are not much in this case, therefore, the Government has acted in an arbitrary manner in introducing the pension scheme with effect from 141995 instead of 111992 as it has been done in the case of CCS Agricultural University, Hisar.
11.
In support of their contentions, the learned counsel appearing on behalf of the petitioners referred to Several decisions reported as 1999(2) SCT 164 (P&H) : 1999(1) RSJ 735 Harivansh Bhandari v. State of Punjab and another, 1997(1) SCT 438 (P&H)(DB) : 1997(2) RSJ 124 Raj Pal and others v. State of Haryana, 1996(3) SCT 496 (Gujarat) : 1996(4) RSJ 186 Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation Nivruti Karamchari Sangh v Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation and another, 1996(2) SCT 304 (SC) : 1996(1) RSJ 629 MC Dhingra v. Union of India and others, 1997(4) SCT 33 (SC) : 1997 Supreme Court Cases (L&S) 1613 Union of India and others v. Lieu (Mrs.) E Icats, 2000(3) SCT 645 (SC) : JT 2000(10) SC 334 Bank of India v. Indu Rajagopalan and others, 1998(2) SCT 337 (SC) : JT 1998(2) SC 590 Dhan Raj and others v. State of Jammu and Kashmir and others.
12.
I have considered the contentions raised by the learned counsel for the petitioners and in my opinion, these are totally devoid of any merit.
13.
Initiation ofthe pension scheme by the Government in the year 1988-89 does not create any right in the employees of Kurukshetra University or MD University.
It was only the intention of the Government that it wanted to introduce the pension scheme.
That scheme could go through or it could flop in the mid way.
Moreover, principle of parity cannot be made applicable when the establishment of the Universities are different.
The employees of Kurukshetra University are totally governed by different sets of service conditions than that of the employees of the Haryana Agricultural University, Hisar.
Merely that the funds to these two Universities come from the State Government is not the rigid formula to hold that the pension scheme must be introduced unilaterally from a particular date.
So far as Kurukshetra University was concerned, the proposal of the Scheme itself started somewhere after 1992.
There was proposal and objection in the years 1993 and 1995.
The Government had been asking the University to frame a proper scheme or rules in accordance with the C.S.R.
The scheme which was submitted was not approved.
Rather it was modified.
In these circumstnaces, it was not obligatory upon the Government to introduce the pension scheme with effect from 111992 as desired by the petitioners.
Moreover, it is a case of financial implications.
The Government has to see whether they can bear the financial burden if, so, from what date.
When nothing tangible was in the pipe in the year 1992 before the Government, how the Government can introduce the pension scheme with effect from 111992 vis-a-vis the employees of the Kurukshetra University.
It has been observed in 1997(1) SCT 699 (SC) : 1997(2) Supreme Court Cases 342, State of Rajasthan and another v. Amritlal Gandhi and others, that while giving the benefit ofpension under a pension scheme, paying capacity is a relevant consideration.
The Hon'ble Supreme Court was pleased to hold that the High Court had no justification to substitute the date of scheme as 1.1.1986 instead of 111990.
The Hon'ble Supreme Court was pleased to hold that as introducing a pension scheme involves financial implications, the approval of the State Government was required.
The Government has to see whether the pension scheme is wholly economical or not and if the Government has given a different cut off date such cut off date cannot be struck down being arbitrary.
The ratio of this judgment is fully applicable to the facts in hand.
The reliance was also placed by the learned counsel for the respondents on the judgment reported as 2000(2) SCT 8 (SC) : JT 2000(2) SC 265, State of Punjab and another v. JL Gupta and others and it was observed that the principle of parity cannot be made applicable to those employees who are governed by different rules.
In the cited case also the Government gave the benefit of dearness allowance etc. to those employees who retired after 3131985.
The employees who retired prior to 3131985 claimed the same benefit but their prayer was rejected on the ground that they cannot claim the benefit as they cannot be equated with those who retired on or after 3131985.
In 1999(2) SCT 589 (SC) : JT 1999(2) SC 429, Tamil Nadu Electricity Board v. R Verrasamy and others it was held that the employees who retired before the cut off date i.e. 171996 and who are governed by Contributory Provident Fund Scheme cannot compel that the scheme should be introduced retrospectively and not prospectively.
The Hon'ble Supreme Court was pleased to obseve that such retired employees could not claim retrospective introduction of the pension scheme because such former employees form a separate class in itself.
The ratio of this judgment is again helpful to me in deciding the points formulated by me in the earlier portion of this judgment.
In 1997(4) SCT 33 (SC) : JT 1997(7) SC 279 Union of India v. Lieu (Mrs.) E Iacats it was observed that the benefit of the pension scheme which was introduced in the month of October 1983 cannot be claimed by those who retired prior to this date.
14.
The judgments which have been relied upon by the counsel for the petitioners are distinguishable on facts.
Had the Government introduced the pension scheme on two different dates with regard to the employees of the Kurukshetra University, there would have been merit in the contention of the learned counsel for the petitioners but in the present case if the benefit of scheme has been granted to the employees of a particular University with effect from 111992 the employees of other Universities cannot claim as a matter of right or course that this benefit should be extended to them also with effect from 111992.
15.
Faced with this difficulty, the learned counsel Shri RK Malik, appearing on behalf of the petitioners submitted that let the pension scheme mayalso be introduced with effect from 141995 and the petitioners are ready to deposit the benefits which they had earlier received under the Contributory Provident Fund Scheme along with the market rate of interest or as may be ordered by the Court and they will exercise the option to switch over the pension scheme witheffect from 141995.
The submission of the counsel for the petitioners Shri RK Malik, cannot be accepted on the short ground that on 141995, the petitioners were no longer the employees of the Kurukshetra University.
This Scheme is beneficial and is applicable to the employees who were on the strength of the University as on 141995.
Moreover, the petitioners had already received the benefits under the Contributory Provident Fund Scheme and therefore, this Court is not inclined to come to their rescue.
In this view of the matter all the points formulated above are decided against the petitioners.
16.
The net result is that all the aforesaid six writ petitions are totally devoid of any merit and the same are dismissed with no order as to costs.
Petition dismissed.
