GS Singhvi, J - Order dated 26.6.1992 passed by the General Manager, Haryana Roadways, Chandigarh, has been made the subject-matter of challenge in this petition.
The petitioner has prayed for quashing of order Annexure P-4 and for issue of a direction to the respondents to pay him salary for the period between 1.7.1986 and 26.6.1992 and to count the said period towards pensionary benefits.
He has also prayed for award of interest and for refund of Rs. 18390/- recovered from him.
2.
Petitioner joined service of the Punjab Roadways on 2411964.
He was allocated to the State of Haryana on 1111966.
At the time of appointment he gave out his date of birth as 17.6.1928.
On 6.2.1990 he was suspended and charge-sheeted for making interpolation in the service book in the column of 'date of birth'.
It was alleged that he was instrumental in getting his date of birth changed from 17.6.1928 to 1761938.
A notice was issued to the petitioner proposing termination of service.
After considering his reply the General Manager, Haryana Roadways, Chandigarh, passed an order dated 1361990 terminating the service of the petitioner.
On an appeal filed by the petitioner, against the order of termination of his service, the Transport Commissioner, Haryana, passed order Annexure P2 dated 9.10.1991 and gave direction to the General Manager to retire the petitioner with effect from his normal date of superannuation with a further direction that the period beyond his superannuation till the actual retirement be regarded as re-employment and pension for this period should be kept in abeyance.
He was ordered to be paid pension from the date of normal retirement.
Thereafter, the General Manager issued order Annexure P.3 dated 2051992 retiring the petitioner with effect from 3061986 (AN).
He also passed order Annexure P-4 for reemployment of the petitioner as driver in the service of the Haryana Roadways with effect from 1.7.1986 in the pay scale of Rs. 1200-2040 with basic pay of Rs. 1200/- Subsequently, the respondents deducted a sum of Rs. 18390/- from the gratuity payable to the petitioner.
The petitioner has assailed the legality of the order Annexure P4 on the ground of violation of the principles of natural justice and also on the ground that respondent No. 3 has acted arbitrarily in not giving benefit of total length of service to the petitioner for the purpose of computation of his pension and also that he has arbitrarily withheld the benefit of annual grade increments etc. payable to the petitioner with effect from 1.7.1986.
3.
The writ petition has been opposed by the respondents.
It has been pleaded by the respondents that the petitioner was responsible for manipulating a change in the recorded date of his birth and that the order passed by the Transport Commissioner on 9.10.1991 cannot be construed as an order for retirement of the petitioner from a date subsequent to 3061986.
Respondents have also pleaded that excess payment of salary was made to the petitioner and therefore deduction was necessitated from the amount of gratuity payable to the petitioner.
Respondents have denied the claim of the petitioner that he is entitled to the grant of benefit of total length of service towards computation of his pension.
They have also denied that the petitioner is entitled to annual grade increments for the service rendered by him after 3061986.
4.
A perusal of the order passed by the Transport Commissioner shows that while accepting the appeal filed by the petitioner the Transport Commissioner gave the following directions to the General Manager :  "(i) He should be retired w.e.f.
his normal date of superannuation.
(ii) The period beyond his superannuation and till the date of his retirement may be regarded as re-employment and pension for this period, should be kept in abeyance.
He should start drawing his pension from the date his retirement orders are issued simultaneously terminating his re-employment.
(iii) Action should be initiated against the Traffic Manager and other officials who were responsible for attesting a cutting, wrongly made in the service book which was not based on facts."
5.
Annexure P.3 passed by respondent No. 3 on 2051992 effected the retirement of the petitioner with effect from 3061986.
The petitioner has not challenged the legality of the order dated 2051992 nor has he pleaded that he was entitled to continue in service after 3061986.
If at all the petitioner had challenged that order or had made a claim that he was entitled to continue in service beyond 3061986, that claim of the petitioner would have been rejected by the Court simply on the ground that his date of birth has been treated as 17.6.1928.
In fact, this was the date of birth declared by the petitioner at the time of his entry in the service of the Punjab Roadways on 2111964.
The petitioner had a right to continue in service up to the date of attaining the age of 58 years and as he had attained the age of 58 years on 1661986, the respondents cannot be said to have committed any illegality in effecting his retirement with effect from 3061986.
This being the factual position regarding the date of retirement of the petitioner, he is entitled to get post-retirement benefits in respect of the service rendered by him between 2411964 and 3061986.
Learned counsel for the petitioner has not brought to the notice of the Court any rule on the basis of which an employee can claim that the period of re-employment should be counted for the purpose of pension or other service benefits.
In the absence of any rule providing for counting the period of re-employment for the purpose of computation of pension or for grant of annual grade increments, this Court cannot issue a writ of mandamus to the respondents that the service rendered by the petitioner beyond 3061986, i.e. the date with effect from which the petitioner has been treated as retired, should be counted for the purpose of pension and other post-retirement benefits.
6.
Argument of the learned counsel for the petitioner that the petitioner ought to have been given the benefit of increments etc. during the period of re-employment from 1.7.1986 to 1361990 is clearly based on misinterpretation of the directions issued by the Transport Commissioner, Haryana.
In view the fact that the petitioner had to be retired with effect from the date of his normal date of superannuation, i.e. 3061986, the direction given by the Transport Commissioner for re-employment of the petitioner cannot but be construed as a direction given on compassionate grounds.
If the Transport Commissioner had not issued that direction, it may have become necessary for the employer to effect the recovery from the pay of the petitioner of the entire salary paid to him between 1.7.1986 and 1361990.
In order to save the petitioner from this agony, the Transport Commissioner directed that the period subsequent to the petitioner's superannuation with reference to his normal date of retirement, should be treated as the period of re-employment.
Though para (ii) of the directions given by the Transport Commissioner, Haryana, is not happily worded but no other interpretation of that direction can possibly be taken.
In my considered opinion, the Transport Commissioner had intended that the period during which the petitioner had physically served after 3061986 should be treated as period of re-employment and this is precisely what the respondent No. 3 has done by issuing order Annexure P4.
7.
Fixation of pay of the petitioner in the scale of Rs. 1200-2040 with basic pay of Rs. 1200 cannot also be termed as arbitrary or capricious.
It is not the case of the petitioner that the pay scale of the post of Driver was higher than the scale of Rs. 1200-2040 as on 1.7.1986.
This being the position, the petitioner who was re-employed with effect from 1.7.86 could not have been paid wages higher than those mentioned in the order Annexure P4.
He was not entitled to count his service up to 3061986 for the purpose of fixation of his pay with effect from the date of re-employment.
Therefore, the order Annexure P4 cannot be termed as arbitrary, or illegal.
8.
Rejection of the plea of the petitioner for being treated as retired with effect from 26.6.1992 as also the rejection of his claim for counting the total period of service, i.e. the service rendered by him up to 1361990, for the purpose of computation of post-retirement benefits, however, does not lead to total rejection of the writ petition.
The petitioner has made a grievance that respondents have deducted a sum of Rs. 18390/- from his gratuity and this they have done without following the basic principles of natural justice.
In substance, his plea is that he has been condemned unheard.
The allegations made by the petitioner in the context on deduction of Rs. 18390/- have not been denied by the respondents.
In view of this factual position, it must be held that respondents have penalised the petitioner without giving him any action oriented notice or an opportunity of hearing and, therefore, the action taken by the respondents cannot but be termed as contrary to principles of natural justice.
9.
In the result the writ petition is partly allowed.
Deduction of Rs. 18390/- from the gratuity of the petitioner is declared illegal.
The respondents are directed to reconsider the issue of deduction of Rs. 18390/- from the gratuity paid to the petitioner.
If at all the respondents think that the said amount is recoverable from the petitioner, they should pass necessary order in consonance with the principles of natural justice.
Any such action must be taken by the respondents within a period of three months from the date of receipt or submission of a certified copy of this order.
If no fresh order is passed by the respondents within the aforesaid period, they shall refund the amount of Rs. 18390/- to the petitioner with interest at the rate of 12% per annum calculated from the date of deduction.
Writ petition of the petitioner qua other reliefs is rejected.
Parties are left to bear their own costs.
Petition partly allowed.
