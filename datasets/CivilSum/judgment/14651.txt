Jawahar Lal Gupta, J The petitioner who is working as Economic Adviser to the Govt. of Punjab and has voluntarily opted for being governed by the Punjab Civil Services (Revised Scale of Pay-Rules, 1979, prays for the issue of a writ in the nature of mandamus directing the respondent to give him "the benefit of Punjab Civil Services (Revised Pay) (5th Amendment) Rules, 1989 in the old scale of pay."
A few facts relevant for deciding this claim may be noticed.
2.
The petitioner joined the Economic & Statistical Organisation Punjab as a District Statistical Officer on November 15, 1961.
On February 11, 1975, he was promoted as a Deputy Economic Adviser.
On October 16, 1980 he became a Joint Director.
On June 13, 1985, he was promoted to the post of Director.
On December 1, 1990 he was appointed as Economic Adviser.
He continues to hold this post and is due to retire shortly.
3.
The post of Director originally carried a lower scale of pay which was revised and raised to Rs. 1775-2000/100-2300.
This revision was ordered in pursuance to the recommendations of the Pay Commission.
The fixation of pay etc. in this scale of pay was governed by the provisions of the Rules called the Punjab Civil Services (Revised Scale of Pay) Rules, 1979, (hereinafter to be referred as the 1979 Rules).
The petitioner on his promotion as Director was placed in the scale of Rs. 1775-2300.
It may be noticed here that under rule 7(2) of the 1979 Rules, it was provided as under :-  "7(2).
In cases where a Government employee is held up at the maximum after completing the time scale, selection grade or extended scale as the case may be, on or after the first day of January, 1978, he shall be allowed ex gratia biennial increments, unless it is withhold, not exceeding five such increments, at the rate of last increment in the relevant scale subject to the condition that in no case the pay shall exceed two thousand and four hundred rupees."
Vide notification dated January 29, 1985, the maximum pay was fixed at Rs. 2750 - instead of Rs. 2400/-.
For the purposes of the present case, it would suffice to notice that under the above provisions a Govt. employee who was held up at the maximum of the scale was entitled to the grant of ex gratia biennial increments.
4.
The State Govt. appointed the Third Pay Commission.
On receipt of the recommendations of the Commission, the pay scales were revised.
It promulgated the rules called the Punjab Civil Services (Revised Pay) Rules, 1988.
Even though the Rules were gazetted in September 1988, these were enforced with effect from January 1, 1986.
The pay scale of Rs. 1775-75-2000/100-2300 was revised and raised to Rs. 3000-100-4000-125-5000.
In rule 5 of these rules it was inter alia provided that "a Govt. employee may opt to continue to draw pay in the existing scale until he vacates this post or ceases to draw pay in that scale."
Rule 6 inter alia provided for the period during which the option could be exercised.
Rule 7 laid down the method of fixation of initial pay in the revised scale.
In these rules as initially promulgated no provision similarly to the one contained in rule 7(2) of the 1979 Rules was made.
5.
On June 6, 1989, the Govt. amended the 1988 rules though rules called the Punjab Civil Services (Revised Pay) (5th Amendment) Rules, 1989 which were enforced with effect from January 1, 1986.
In rule 2, a Master Scale of Rs. 750-20-950-25-1200-30-1560-40-2000-50-2400-60-2700-75-3000-100-4000-125-5000-150-5900-200-6700 was provided.
Further the existing provision of rule 9 was renumbered as rule 9(1) and the following provision was added as Clause (2) :-  "(2) Where a Government employee is held up at the maximum after completing the time scale, senior scale or selection grade, as the case may be, on or after the first day of January 1986, he shall be allowed ex gratia annual increments, unless it is withheld, at the same rates as are given in the master-scale after the stage at which he was held up, subject to the condition that in no case the basic pay of the Government employee shall exceed seven thousand and three hundred rupees."
6.
The petitioner avers that he had reached the stage of Rs. 2300/- on June 1, 1986 in the existing pay scale.
He exercised his option to remain in that scale "as the revised scales of pay were not beneficial."
He represented to the Govt. on May 11, 1990.
A copy of the representation has been produced as Annexure P-3 with the writ petition.
In this representation, it has been stated that in accordance with the provisions of rule 7(2) of the 1979 Rules, he had been granted ex gratia biennial increment in the existing scale on June 1, 1988, and that the next biennial increment was due on June 1, 1990.
He complained that no increment was granted on June 1, 1987 and June 1, 1989.
Referring to the provisions of rule 9(2) for the 1988 rules, the petitioner pointed out that the Govt. has "now allowed ex gratia annual increment at the same rates as given in the master scale after reaching the maximum of the revised time scale."
According to the petitioner, this resulted in a recurring loss to him.
He, therefore requested the Govt. to grant "necessary relaxation in the relevant rules," and to give him ex gratia annual increments which fell due on June 1, 1987 and June 1, 1989.
This representation was rejected by the Govt. and the order was conveyed to the petitioner vide letter dated July 11, 1990.
Averring that the petitioner's representation had been rejected without any proper consideration, the petitioner has challenged the action of the Govt. as being violative of Articles 18 and 16 of the Constitution.
7.
In the written statement filed on behalf of the respondent, it has been pointed out that the benefit under rule 9(2) is not admissible to a person who opts to be governed by the 1979 Rules.
In his case the provisions of rule 7(2) of the 1979 Rules are applicable and the biennial increments as admissible has been duly granted to the petitioner.
Further, it has been stated that a person who opts for the old pay scale like the petitioner earns more than a person whose pay is fixed under the 1988 Rules.
It has been averred that in the case of a person who opts to remain in the old scale of pay, the emoluments rise from Rs. 345940 on January 1, 1986 to Rs. 5200/- on January 1, 1993.
There is thus an increase of Rs. 174060 giving an average annual value of increment of Rs. 24866.
In the revised scale of pay however the emoluments rise from Rs. 3700/- to Rs. 4500/- giving an increase of Rs. 800/- only for the same spell of time which gives an average annual increase of Rs. 11429 only.
It has been further averred that if instead of the biennial ex gratia increments as now permitted, the officer was to be granted annual ex gratia increments, the annual value of the increase would be Rs. 34812.
It has thus been stated that the claim made by the petitioner is wholly unjustified and has been rightly rejected.
It has also been averred that the petitioner had exercised the option to remain in the existing (old) scale of pay.
A comparison of the increments provided in both the unrevised and revised scales of pay reveals that the value of increment in the revised pay scale is much less than the value of the increments in the old unrevised scales.
It has been stated by virtue of option exercised by the petitioner, his total emoluments in the unrevised scale of pay come to Rs. 5200/- while in the revised scale of pay he would have got only Rs. 4875/-.
Further, on promotion as Economic Adviser, his total emoluments are Rs. 5600/- by virtue of his having opted for the old scale of pay while in the new scale of pay he would have got Rs. 5150/- only.
On these promises, it has been stated that the petitioner has derived benefit by option for the old scale of pay and he cannot claim the benefits under the 1988 Rules.
8.
I have heard Mr. SS Nijjar for the petitioner and Ms. Charu Tuli, Assistant Advocate General for the respondent.
Mr. Nijjar contended that the purpose of granting ex gratia increment was to remove stagnation.
To achieve this object, every employee has a right to be treated equally and that the action of the Govt. was violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
Learned counsel submitted that the classification sought to be created by the Govt. did not satisfy the twin test of reasonableness and rational relationship of the classification with the object sought to be achieved.
Ms. Tuli on the other hand controverted the claim made on behalf of the petitioner and contended that the action was legal and valid.
9.
It is well settled that all persons who are similarly situate have a right to be treated equally.
However, the State is entitled to classify persons.
If the classification is reasonable and has a rational relationship with the object which is sought to be achieved then the action is not violative of Article 14 or Article 16 of the Constitution.
10.
In the present case, the Govt. initially promulgated the 1979 Rules.
Pay of an employee had to be fixed in accordance with the provisions of these Rules.
A person who was held up at the maximum of the scale was entitled to the grant of ex gratia biennial increment.
In the year 1988, the Govt. promulgated the 1988 Rules.
When these rules were originally notified, no provision for the grant of any ex gratia increment was made.
It was introduced for the first time vide notification dated June 6, 1989 which was enforced with effect from January 1, 1986.
By this amendment it was inter alia provided that an employee who is held up at the maximum of the scale shall be allowed exgratia annual increments.
Furthermore, a perusal of rule 14 of the 1988 Rules shows that the 1979 rules were not repeated except to the extent of the provision contained in the proviso to sub-rule (1) of rule 7.
Consequently, it is apparent that both sets of rules continue to remain in operation.
Still more, by virtue of rules 5 and 6 of the 1988 Rules, the employees were given an option.
One could opt to be governed either by the new rules or to continue to be governed by 1979 Rules.
It is thus apparent that the employees have been classified by virtue of the two legislative measures.
Those who opt for the old rules constitute one class while others who opt for the 1988 Rules constitute another class.
Within a particular class, all persons are treated equally.
Persons who opt to be governed by the 1979 Rules are treated equally.
There is no inter se discrimination.
In other words, all persons who opt for the 1979 Rules get only a biennial increment after reaching the maximum of the scale.
Similarly, all employees who opt for the new scales of pay get an annual increment.
There is thus no discrimination within the class.
11.
This classification brought about by statutory rules is not per se unreasonable.
The employee has been given a clear option.
He can choose the set of rules which he likes, Ms. Tuli had very fairly stated that even now the petitioner can opt for the 1988 Rules.
Mr. Nijjar categorically declined this offer.
In such a situation, when an employee has been given a right to opt for either the old or new rules, there seems to be no basis for the contention that the classification is unreasonable.
In fact if the Govt. had repealed the 1979 Rules and enforced the provisions of 1988 Rules qua all the employees, some of them may have had the grievance that they have suffered adversely in the matter of their emoluments etc. By giving the option the Govt. has offered a clear opportunity to the employees to choose whatever is more beneficial to them.
Having exercised that right and made the choice, the petitioner cannot complain that the classification is unreasonable.
Further, it is also clear from a perusal of the written statement filed by the respondent that the petitioner has got more money by option to be governed by the 1979 Rules in spite of the fact that he was given only biennial increments than he would have got if his pay had been fixed under the 1988 Rules after giving him annual increments in accordance with the provisions of the 1988 Rules.
In such a situation, the grievance of the petitioner that the classification is unreasonable or that the action of the respondent is arbitrary, cannot be sustained.
12.
Mr. Nijjar, however, contends that this classification has no rational relationship with the object of removing stagnation.
Even this contention appears to be wholly lacking in merit.
An employee is entitled to draw his emoluments in accordance with the scale and the rules provided for by the employer.
If he reaches the maximum of a scale of pay, he may not get any increment.
There is no indefeasible right in an employee to claim an increment after reaching the maximum provided by a rule or an order of the Govt.
In such a situation, if the Govt. decides to confer a concession on an employee and provides for the grant of a biennial or annual increment, the effort is to reduce the hardship and to remove the stagnation.
The decision in this behalf has to be taken by the appropriate authority on a consideration of a variety of circumstances.
It has to consider the availability of resources and funds, the extent of hardship involved and a variety of other factors.
If on consideration of various relevant matters, the Govt. feels that the employees who have opted for the old scales of pay get certain benefits which are not available to others who are governed by the revised rules of pay, it can pass appropriate orders in that behalf.
A perusal of the factual position in the present case shows that in spite of the grant of only a biennial increment in the old scale of pay, the petitioner has earned more than he could have earned if he had opted for the revised scale of pay after the grant of annual increments.
Consequently, the action of the Govt. in permitting only a biennial increment to all those who are held up at the maximum in the old scale of pay is not only reasonable and rational but is even related to the object of removal of hardship and evidence of stagnation.
In any event, the question is primarily of policy.
It was a matter for experts to decide.
The experts having taken the decision which is not unreasonable or irrational, this Court cannot substitute its own opinion for that of the experts.
13.
On a consideration of the totality of circumstances it appears that the petitioner wants to have the best of both the worlds.
He wants all the advantages admissible under the 1979 Rules.
In spite of the fact that he did not opt to be governed by the 1988 Rules, he wants to usurp the benefits even under those rules.
This surely is not permissible.
Such a course of action, if permitted, would be unfair to those who opted to be governed by 1988 Rules.
14.
The petitioner in his representation to the Govt. prayed for relaxation of the rules.
Relaxation cannot be claimed as a matter of right.
It is the discretion of the authority and it can be granted only if the conditions provided for in the rules are satisfied.
Relaxation can be granted if undue hardship is caused.
No such hardship can be said to have been caused in the present case.
Consequently, the claim for relaxation was rightly rejected by the Government.
15.
With the permission of the Court, the petitioner had filed an additional affidavit dated December 16, 1992.
In this affidavit it was inter alia averred that officers who had reached the basic pay of Rs. 2500/- in the pay scale on or before January 1, 1986 received the benefit of a jump and their pay was fixed in new scale after getting the benefit of 10 per cent rise.
These officers were granted annual increments in the revised scale of pay.
In this behalf, the petitioner referred to the case of Mr. Ajit Singh, Economic Adviser who had retired.
Learned counsel for the petitioner submitted that fixation of pay in the case of Mr. Ajit Singh showed that the petitioner had been discriminated against.
A reply to this affidavit was filed on behalf of the respondent.
It has been pointed out that Mr. Ajit Singh had reached the stage of Rs. 2500/- on January 1, 1987 and he had given his option for being governed by the 1988 Rules.
Consequently, it was pointed out that there was no parallel between the case of the petitioner and that of Mr. Ajit Singh.
16.
I think the respondent is right.
Persons who opted for the 1988 Rules constitute a separate class.
Their fixation of pay in accordance with the previsions of the rules cannot be said to have resulted in any discrimination.
The contention raised on behalf of the petitioner is wholly untenable.
It is consequently rejected.
There is no merit in this petition.
The complaint made by the petitioner is wholly unfounded.
Consequently, the petition is dismissed.
In the circumstances of the case, the parties are left to bear their own costs.
Petition dismissed.
