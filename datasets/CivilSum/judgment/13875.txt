Ashutosh Mohunta, J - The Punjab State Electricity Board, Patiala (for short `the Board') has filed this appeal to challenge the judgment and decree dated 2741995 passed by the Senior Sub-Judge, Kapurthala, decreeing the suit of the plaintiff-respondent in his favour declaring him entitled for promotion with effect from the date when his juniors were promoted as Assistant Lineman, as well as the judgment and decree dated 351999 passed by the Additional District Judge, Kapurthala, vide which the appeal filed by the Board was dismissed.
2.
Succinctly, the facts giving rise to this appeal by the Board are that in pursuance of a criminal case registered against the plaintiff-respondent, he was placed under suspension with effect from 1091980.
On his acquittal by the Sessions Court on 1081982, he was reinstated into service on 1411983.
The period of his suspension from 1091980 to 1411983 was considered as period spent on leave of the kind due.
In the interregnum of the period of suspension, the juniors of the plaintiff-respondent, namely Paramjit singh (defendant No. 5) and Arvind Kumar (defendant No. 6) were promoted as Assistant Lineman with effect from 1541981.
Earlier the plaintiff filed a Civil Suit wherein the order dated 1.7.1983 treating the period of suspension from 1091980 to 1411983 as the period spent on leave was challenged.
That suit was decreed in favour of the plaintiff vide judgment and decree dated 1611991 and the period of suspension from 1091980 to 1411983 was ordered to be considered as having been spent on duty.
Thereafter he was paid the arrears of pay etc. for the said period.
However, he was not granted promotion as Assistant Lineman with effect from the date 1541981 when his juniors Paramjit Singh (defendant No. 5) and Arvind Kumar (defendant No. 6) were promoted as such.
In order to challenge the action of the defendant-Board in not promoting him as Assistant Lineman with effect from 1541981, when his juniors were promoted as such, the plaintiff Ashok Kumar filed the present suit on 451993 for declaration to the effect that he was entitled for such promotion with effect from 1541981.
The suit was contested by the defendants, inter alia, on the grounds that the suit was hit by the principle of res judicata as well as on the ground of limitation.
However, after hearing the counsel for the parties and on going through the evidence adduced on record, the Senior Sub-Judge, Kapurthala, vide judgment and decree dated 2741995 decreed the suit in favour of the plaintiff.
The appeal filed by the Board was dismissed by the Additional District Judge, Kapurthala, vide judgment and decree dated 351999.
Now the Board has filed the present appeal in order to challenge the judgments and decrees passed by both the Courts below.
3.
It has been contended by Mr. PS Thiara, learned counsel for the appellant-Board that the suit filed by the plaintiff-respondent is hit by the principle of res judicata as he had filed an earlier suit against the Board wherein he had challenged the action of the Board in considering the period of suspension as having been spent on duty and the relief of promotion as Assistant Lineman could very well also be claimed in the said suit itself.
As the respondent has failed to claim the said relief in the earlier suit, the present suit is hit by the principle of res judicata.
Another argument raised by the learned counsel for the appellant is that the respondent had sought the promotion with effect from 1541981, whereas the present suit was filed by him on 451993, i.e., after the elapse of more than 12 years.
Thus, according to the learned counsel, the present suit is barred by the principle of limitation.
4.
The arguments raised by Mr. PS Thiara, learned counsel for the appellant, have vehemently been controverted by Mr. Gurcharan Singh, learned counsel for respondent No. 1.
5.
I have heard the learned counsel for the parties and gone through the evidence adduced on record.
6.
The following substantial questions of law have been framed by this Court in the present appeal vide order dated 5.8.2003 :-  1.
Whether the present suit of the plaintiff is barred by principle of Order 2 Rule 2 of the Code of Civil Procedure as he could claim the relief of further promotion in the earlier suit ?
2.
Whether the suit is barred by limitation ?
7.
So far as the first question is concerned, admittedly, after his acquittal on 1081982 by the Sessions Judge, Kapurthala, the plaintiff was reinstated into service on 1411983 and the suspension period from 1091980 to 1411983 was considered as the leave period vide order dated 306 dated 1.7.1983 passed by defendant No. 3.
It is also an admitted fact that after his reinstatement into service, the plaintiff was refused promotion retrospectively when his juniors, i.e., defendant Nos. 5 and 6 were promoted on the ground that the period of suspensions had not been considered as the period having been spent on duty.
Thus, he challenged the action of the defendants in treating the period of suspension as the period spent on leave.
The suit was decreed in his favour by the trial Court vide judgment and decree dated 1611991 when the Senior Sub-Judge, Kapurthala, declared that the plaintiff was entitled to all other benefits of pay, allowances and promotion.
In spite of this declaration made by the Senior Sub-Judge, Kapurthala, the authorities concerned refused to accept the request of the plaintiff on 2841993 to promote him with effect from 1541981, when his juniors were promoted.
This denial of the authorities had given the plaintiff a fresh cause of action and he was forced to file the present suit immediately thereafter on 451993.
Thus, I am of the considered view that the present suit is not barred by the principle of res judicata and it is held that it is not hit by Order 2 Rule 2, C.P.C.
The first question is answered accordingly.
8.
So far as the second question with regard to limitation is concerned, as already held above, a fresh cause of action had arisen to the plaintiff on 2841993 when the authorities concerned had refused to accept his claim to grant him promotion as Assistant Lineman retrospectively with effect from 1541981 when his juniors were promoted.
Immediately, thereafter on 451993, the plaintiff had filed the present suit.
Thus, by no stretch of imagination it can be said that the present suit is barred by limitation.
9.
In the light of the above discussion, I do not find any infirmity in the well-reasoned judgments and decrees passed by both the Courts below and I concur with the same.
10.
Consequently, there is no merit in this appeal.
It is, accordingly, dismissed.
Appeal dismissed.
