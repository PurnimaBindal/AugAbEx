Jawahar Lal Gupta, J - The petitioner, a Store Keeper in the Department of Civil Aviation, has approached this court for three reliefs.
He claims that he is entitled to be placed in the pay scale of Rs. 525-15-600-20-660/700-30- 830-EB-890-40-1050 instead of the scale of Rs. 400-10-490/540-15-600-EB-20-660 in which he has been working, since he was appointed in the year 1982.
Secondly the petitioner claims that he had rendered service in Air Force from November 6, 1965 to November 30, 1980.
He claims that he is entitled to the period from November 6, 1965 to January 10, 1968 to be counted towards his pay and seniority on his appointment as Store Keeper.
Thirdly, the petitioner claims regularisation against the post of Store Keeper which he has held since July 15, 1982.
2.
A brief reference to the factual position as emerging from the pleadings of the parties and the record produced before me is necessary.
In May 1982, the adviser Civil Aviation, Haryana sent a requisition to the Secretary, Rajya Sainik Board, Haryana for filling up the post of "Storekeeper in the scale of Rs. 400-10-490/540-15-600-EB-660.
A photostat copy of the original statement shown to me is placed on record as mark "A".
Another requisition was sent to Employment Exchange vide letter dated May 25, 1982.
Even in this requisition the post of Storekeeper was stated to be in the scale of Rs. 400-660, as mentioned above.
It appears that the name of the petitioner was sponsored by the Rajya Sainik Board.
The petitioner was considered and an offer was made to him vide letter dated August 17, 1982.
A copy of this letter is at Annexure P-1.
In this letter it was specifically stated that "you are hereby appointed as Store-keeper (Aviation) in the VIP Aircraft Establishment, Defence Aerodrome, Chandigarh of this department w.e.f.
15.7.1982 (F.N.)
in the scale of Rs. 400-10-490/540-15-600-EB-20-660, plus usual allowances admissible from time to time This appointment is against the post of a Storekeeper in the VIP Aircraft Establishment of this Department in the scale of Rs.
525-15-600-20-660/700-30-850-EB-890-40-1050."
The petitioner accepted this appointment.
However, later on he claimed that he had been orally assured and was entitled to the scale of Rs. 525-1050 instead of Rs. 400-660, in which he had been actually placed.
Through the present petition he claims that he is entitled to the scale of Rs. 525-1050 w.e.f.
the date of his appointment.
In any case it is averred that the petitioner had factually dscharged the duties of Storekeeper in the VIP Aircraft Establishment and as such he was entitled to the scale of pay meant for that post.
3.
It has been further averred in the petition that the petitioner had served the Indian Air Force from November 6, 1965 to November 30, 1980.
A part of this Service i.e. from November 6, 1965 to January 10, 1968 had been rendered by the petitioner during the period of Emergency proclaimed by President of India on account of Chinese aggression.
With reference to the provisions of the Punjab Government National Emergency (Concession) Rules 1965, the petitioner claims that even while working as a Storekeeper he was entitled to the period of military service to be taken into account for the purpose of determining his pay and seniority.
Reference has also been made to the letter of the Government of Haryana issued on June 29, 1979, by which the Government has decided or "extend the concession granted vide letter under reference to those ex-servicemen also who are reemployed in Civil Service on ad hoc basis."
Relying on this letter, the petitioner claims that he was entitled to the benefit of military service towards his pay and seniority.
Furthermore, the petitioner claims that vide letter of February 16, 1987, the Government had decided to regularise the services of all ad hoc employees, who had completed two years of service on November 1, 1986 and that in accordance with these instructions his services should have been regularised.
In the alternative, it has been averred that the action of the Government in excluding the post, which has not been taken out of the purview of the Subordinate Services Selection Board, was wholly arbitrary, discriminatory and violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
It is on this premises that the petitioner has claimed the benefit of higher scale of pay, military service benefit and regularisation.
4.
In the written statement filed on behalf of the respondents it has been inter alia averred that the petitioners had been appointed as Store-keeper in the scale of Rs. 400-660 against the post of Store-keeper, which carried a scale of Rs. 525-1050.
The claim of the petitioner regarding an oral assurance has been specifically denied.
In fact, it has been categorically asserted in paragraph 7 that no assurance was given to the petitioner with regard to the grant of higher pay scale of Rs. 525-1050.
With regard to the benefit of military service, it has been averred that the petitioner's case had been referred to the Chief Secretary, who vide his letter dated September 26, 1986, had rejected the petitioner's claim.
As for regularisation the respondents claim that the petitioner is not entitled to be regularised in pursuance to the instructions of February 16, 1987 as the post of Store-keeper held by him had not been taken out of the purview of the Subordinate Services Selection Board.
On the premises the claim of the petitioner has been as made out in the writ petition has controverted.
5.
The petitioner has today filed a supplementary affidavit.
With this affidavit he has produced a copy of Haryana Government Notification dated February 28, 1991.
In this notification, which has been issued under the proviso to Article 309 of the Constitution of India, it has been inter alia provided that all class III posts as have been held for a minimum period of two years on December 31, 1990 by class III employees on ad hoc basis be taken out of the purview of the Subordinate Services Selection Board, Haryana and their services shall be regularised.
It has been further provided that the services of the incumbents of such posts shall be regularised, if they have completed two years of service on December 31, 1990 and were in service on that date.
Various other conditions have also been laid down.
It appears that the petitioner fulfils all the conditions stipulated in this letter.
He had completed more than two years of service much prior to the date of the issue of this notification and also before the crucial date stipulated therein viz, December 31, 1990.
He was admittedly recruited after a requisition had been sent to the Employment Exchange.
Nothing has been pointed out against his work and conduct.
It has also not been suggested that he does not fulfil the prescribed qualifications.
In fact Mr. Rajan Gupta, Deputy Advocate General, appearing on behalf of the respondents, stated before me that the claim of the petitioner shall be duly considered.
In view of the fact that the petitioner fulfils all the qualifications, it is expected that the respondents shall regularise his services at the earliest possible.
6.
The claim of the petitioner even with regard to the grant of the benefit of military service appears to be well-merited.
The benefit of military service is available to a person on his appointment to a civil post.
The period of military service as defined in Rule 2 has to be counted for the purpose of fixation of pay/grant of increments as also for seniority.
Even by the Government notification, these benefits at least in the matter of pay were extended to ex-servicemen.
Even if the Government does not maintain the seniority of ad hoc employees, benefit has to be given to them after their services are regularised.
I am accordingly of the view that the petitioner's claim for granting him the benefit of military service appears to be tenable under the rules and the respondents should consider the matter within two months from the date of the receipt of a copy of this order.
7.
As for the claim of the petitioner with regard to the scale of pay of Rs. 525-1050, it appears that the department of Civil Aviation has three offices.
One is called a VIP Aircraft Establishment.
The second is the Central Stores at Pinjore and the third is the Certificate of Air Worthiness Engine Overall Workshop.
The first two have the posts of Store-keeper while third has the post of Storeman.
The post of Store-keeper in the VIP Aircraft Establishment carries the pay scale of Rs. 525-1050.
At the Central Stores, Pinjore, the post is not regular and the Storeman carries the pay scale of Rs. 400-660.
The third establishment, which is described as Certificate of Air Worthiness Engine Overall Workshop also has the post of Store-keeper in the pay scale of Rs. 400-660.
The requisition was sent by the Department for the post of Store-keeper in the Scale of Rs. 400-660.
An offer of appointment was made to the petitioner for the post of Store-keeper in the scale of Rs. 400-660.
The petitioner had accepted that offer without any objection.
After having joined the post and having worked thereon for a number of months, he submitted his first representation on March 14, 1983.
His repeated representations having failed, he made the claim before this Court through the present writ petition.
The claim is two-fold.
It is urged on behalf of the petitioner that he has factually discharged the duties of the post of Store-keeper from the year 1982 till he was transferred on September 19, 1984.
On this premises it is claimed that for the period from 1982 to 1984 when the petitioner had factually discharged the duties of Store-keeper in the VIP Aircraft Establishment he is entitled to the emoluments of the post.
8.
I am unable to accept the claim of the petitioner.
Besides the fact that the petitioner had been actually offered the post in the scale of Rs. 400-660 and he had accepted it, I cannot lose sight of the plea raised on behalf of the respondents that the writ petition for making this claim has been filed in the year 1987.
A petitioner for claiming arrears for the period from 1982 to 1984 was highly belated so far as this claim of the pettioner is concerned.
Secondly, the sequence of events viz, the requisition to the Employment Exchange and the Rajya Sainik Board, the offer of appointment and the acceptance thereof by the petitioner, militate against the petitioner's assertion that there was an oral promise to him with regard to his being granted the high er scale of Rs. 525-1050.
Two photostat copies of same office note dated 6.6.1983 and 661984 have also been produced alongwith affidavits filed by the petitioner.
There is a certain amount of cloud covering these documents and, therefore, the learned counsel for the petitioner has chosen not to press his claim on the basis of alleged oral assurance.
In any case, since there is a dispute of fact with regard to the oral assurance and there is no other document to support the petitioner's claim, I am not inclined to go into the matter in exercise of the extra-ordinary jurisdiction under Article 226 of the Constitution.
In this situation, the petitioner's claim for the grant of the scale of Rs. 525-1050 with effect from the date of his appointment in the year 1982 is declined.
9.
Mr. AK Chopra contends that the instructions issued by the Government in 1987 provided for the regularisation of such ad hoc employees, who have completed two years service on or before 1111986.
He contends that such persons as were holding posts, which were not taken out of the purview of the Subordinate Services Selection Board were treated as a separate category and illegally excluded from the benefit of regularisation.
He submits that in view of the law enunciated by the Division Bench of this Court in Piara Singh's case, 1988(4) SLR 783, the action is violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India.
In view, posts which are within the purview of Subordinate Services Selection Board, can be filled up only after they have been duly advertised through the Board.
The Government can fill up those posts only as an ad hoc measure till the persons selected by the Board become available.
In such a case, the Government could have reasonably felt that there would be no basis for regularising the services of a person, who has not been considered by the Board.
In case of posts which have been taken out of the purview of the Subordinate Services Selection Board, the various departments of the Government conducted selections at their own level and made ad hoc appointments.
In case of posts which did not fall within the purview of the Subordinate Services Selection Board, no selection by the Board was required.
The department itself was the selecting agency.
The decision of the Government to confine the benefit of regularisation only to the incumbents of the posts which had been taken out of the purview of the Board was based on valid classification and cannot be struck down as being violative of Article 14 of the Constitution.
Nor I am satisfied that in the facts and circumstances of the present case the ratio of the decision in Piara Singh's case is at tracted.
In my opinion, as far as the claim of the petitioner for regularisation is concerned, reference to Piara Singh's case does not appear to be of any material consequence.
This is all the more so, because the post of Store-keeper in the scale of Rs. 400-660 is a solitary post.
No difference in senority is likely to occur even if the petitioner's services are regularised w.e.f.
an earlier date.
10.
Accordingly the writ petition is allowed to the extent indicated above.
Rest of the claim is declined.
The authorities are expected to do the needful in pursuance of the above directions within two months from the date of the receipt of a copy of this order.
In the circumstances, the parties are, however, left to bear own costs.
Petition allowed.
