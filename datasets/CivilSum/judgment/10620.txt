Amar Dutt, J - This order will dispose of CWP Nos. 1842, 1976, 1984 and 1985 of 2001, which have been filed by Subedars Balwant Singh and Ganesh Dass and Naib Subedars Angrej Singh and Nachattar Singh respectively.
The petitioners after being initially inducted into the Signal Corps, Infantry in Dogra Regiment, Bengal Engineers and Armoured Corps on 15121977, 9101975, 711981 and 1881981 respectively were permanently transferred to Intelligence Corps with effect from February 1982, July 1980, January 1985 and January 1986 respectively.
The petitioners had filed the present petitions for quashing the impugned discharge order dated 362000 and discharge certificate dated 24112000 and issuing a writ of mandamus directing the respondents to reinstate them with all consequential benefits and give arrears along with interest till date.
The facts of each case are as under :  C.W.P.
No. 1842 of 2001  2.
Subedar Balwant Singh joined Signal Corps of the Indian Army on 15121977 after he was found to be medically fit.
He served in the said Corps until February 1982 (as per reply 3071982) when he was transferred to the Intelligence Corps.
Since then he has been serving in the said Corps and had been promoted as Naik in May 1983, Havildar in 1985, Naib Subedar on 10121990 and Subedar with effect from 1101995.
He was due to retire on 112006.
He was posted at Tangdhar, a field/ high altitude area.
On 6101998, he became unconscious probably on account of increase in level of sugar and was admitted in 168 Mary Hospital at Tangdhar and placed in medical category CEE (Temporary) for 6 months for disease NIDDN and CNS (INV) Seizures with effect from 9111998.
In May, 1999, a Review Medical Board was held and the medical category of the petitioner was upgraded to BEE (Permanent) with effect from 1951999 (as per reply 951999) for two years.
During operation Vijay (Kargil), the petitioner discharged his duties despite being placed in the lower medical category and was serving continuously at the line of control.
In March 2000, the petitioner was asked by the Records Office of the Intelligence Corps of his willingness to serve in Army having been placed in lower medical category B.E.E.
(Permanent).
He furnished his willingness certificate duly recommended by his Commanding Officer to continue in service.
On 362000 letter No. 1311/LMC/DO/CA was received in the Unit referring to Army Order 46 of 1980 approving the discharge of the petitioner from service being in the permanent low medical category.
According to the petitioner, it was mentioned therein that the individual is to be discharged under item I (iii) of the table annexed to Army Rule 13(3) on being placed in medical category lower than AYE for whom no alternative appointment could be found because manpower position of the Corps exceeds the sanctioned strength.
On receipt of the letter, despite the requirement that the unit should issue a show cause notice, no such notice was served upon the petitioner.
The petitioner sent his reply in which he requested that his case be reconsidered for premature discharge on medical grounds and his discharge on medical grounds is highly unfair in the facts and the circumstances of the case, especially in view of the excellent service record during the period in low medical category.

6.
The petitioners assail the orders whereby they were discharged from service as void on the ground that no statutory show cause notice was issued as is required under Rule 13 (3)(1)(iii) of the Army Rules.
It was also submitted that before passing the discharge order, the respondents did not comply with the provisions of the aforesaid Army Rules and instead have used the method of discharging them on the basis of the fact that the manpower position of the Intelligence Corps was exceeding its strength for which there was no provision under the above said Army Rules.
It was also contended by the petitioners that the order of discharge could not have been passed in their case without complying with the provisions of Army Order 46 of 1980 and offering them alternative employment and in their case where they had been transferred from the Signal Corps, Dogra Regiment, Bengal Engineers and Armoured Corps respectively to Intelligence Corps, excess of manpower would at best have obliged them to go back to their respective Regiment/Corps.
They also join issue on the correctness of their medical category and submitted that in any case, the disability being attributable to military service, they would be entitled to disability pension, which too has been denied to them,  7.
In the reply, the respondents have taken a preliminary objection to the effect that there was no non-compliance with any Rule, which might have created any legal and vested right in the petitioners to move the present writ petitions.
It was admitted that the petitioners had initially enrolled themselves in the Signal Corps, Dogra Regiment, Bengal Engineers and Armoured Corps respectively and had been transferred to the Intelligence Corps.
It was also submitted that due to their low medical category they were initially adjusted against sheltered appointment.
During the year 1999, according to the respondents, a number of field Security Sections in peace areas were disbanded as a result of NFF (Non Field Forces) Study Group recommendations as also reorganization of Army Photo Interpretation Sections in Infantry Divisions and Corps Headquarters and reduction in manpower in some of the liaison Units manpower of the Intelligence Corps had exceeded its sanctioned strength to the extent of 15% necessitating disposal of permanent low medical category JCOs/OR.
Accordingly, it was decided to withdraw sheltered employment in the case of JCOs/NCOs who had completed the aforesaid period of service in consultation with Army Headquarters and release orders of 30 JCOs and 28 NCOs along with the petitioners were approved by the OIC Records in accordance with the instructions contained in Army Headquarters letter No. B/10122/IMC/MP-3 (PBOR) dated 1532000.
It was submitted that the petitioners had been released from service with effect from 1122000 after they had been brought before duty constituted Release Medical Board.
It was also asserted that show cause notices were issued by the Commanding Officer of the Unit and service of show cause notice is a procedural formality and its acceptance or otherwise does not in any way alter the position with regard to contemplated discharge of a person.
It was further submitted that a similar case was filed by Ex-Naib Subedar Krishan Pal of the Intelligence Corps before the Delhi High Court which has passed the following orders on 24102000 :  "The petitioner is being invalided out of the Army with effect from 31st October, 2000 since he is in a low medical category.
It is not possible for me to sit in judgment over the views of the medical experts.
The Respondents have found that it is not possible to give a sheltered appointment to the petitioner.
Moreover, the manpower position of the Corps exceeds its sanctioned strength.
The petitioner has also filed a statutory complaint which is still pending.
I see no reason to interfere.
Dismissed."
8.
On merits, correctness of the most of the averments was denied.
It was submitted that on the basis of the policy laid down in Govt. of India Ministry of Defence letter No. A/32395/VIII/Org2 (MP)/713-S/A/D (AG) dated 1051977, permanent low medical category personnel can be retained in service subject to the following conditions :  (a) Availability of suitable sheltered employment commensurate with the disability, which can be justified in the public interest.
(b) Manpower position of the Corps does not exceed its sanctioned strength.
(c) Willingness of the individual.
It was submitted that since the petitioners, who were in the permanent low medical category had ceased to satisfy the above conditions for retention in service and irrespective of the fact that they were willing to continue in service, their release order had to be issued in accordance with the policy laid down in the above said govt. letter in order to reduce the surplus manpower along with other JCOs and NCOs.
It was submitted that the release of the petitioners was approved in pursuance, of the policy laid down vide Govt. of India Ministry of Defence letter No. A/32395/ VIII/Org2(MP)(C)/713-S/A/D (AG) dated 1051977 as amended vide Govt. of India, Ministry of Defence letter No. A/32395/X/Org.2(MP)(C)/ 7167/A/D/AG dated 26111979.
The petitioners were thereafter released from service under Army Rule 13(3), Item I (iii) read in conjunction with Army Rule 13(2A) after having been brought before a duly constituted Release Medical Board.
It was also submitted that permanent low medical category personnel, who are released because of non- availability of sheltered employment, suitable to their low medical category, are also deemed to have been invalided out of service.
Manpower to the different Regiments and Corps of Indian Army stands sanctioned.
It was also submitted that the petitioners were not eligible for transfer to other Regiment/Corps being low medical category as they do not fulfil one of the pre-requisites for transfer to other Regiment/Corps which stipulated that if so transferred to other Regiment/Corps one should be in medical category AYE.
It was also submitted that the petitioners were discharged not because they were unfit for any military duty but because they were placed in permanent low medical category and cannot be retained in service against sheltered employment beyond 15 years service when the manpower position of the Corps exceeds its sanctioned strength.
Regarding the entitlement to disability pension, it was submitted that the subject is always decided by the Medical Advisor (Pension) attached to the Principal Controller of Defence Accounts (Pension) Allahabad.
Asserting all this, the respondents submitted that the writ petitions are devoid of merits and should be dismissed.
9.
I have heard Mr. Rajneesh Bansal, learned counsel for the petitioners and Mr. Gurpreet Singh, Additional Central Government Standing Counsel and have perused the records that have been produced before me at the time of arguments.
10.
The facts regarding service profile of the petitioners are not in dispute.
They had initially been inducted in the Signal Corps, Dogra Regiment, Bengal Engineers and Armoured Corps respectively and were transferred to the Intelligence Corps.
Petitioner Balwant Singh was promoted in the Core and rose to the rank of Subedar and was serving as a Subedar with effect from 1101995 (as per reply 1101996).
He became unconscious probably on account of increase in the level of sugar and was admitted in the military hospital and placed in medical category CEE (temporary) for 6 months for disease NIDDN and CNS (INV) Seizures with effect from 9111998.
In May 1999, a Review Medical Board was held and the medical category of the petitioner was upgraded to BEE (Permanent) with effect from 1951999 (as per reply 951999) for two years.
Petitioner Ganesh Dass was promoted in the Core and rose to the rank of Subedar and was serving as such with effect from 121995.
On account of disability Solitary Seizure Partial Secondary Generalization, he was placed in low medical category BEE (Permanent).
Petitioner Angrej Singh was promoted to the rank of Naib Subedar with effect from 1101996.
On account of disability Mono Articular Rheumatoid Arthritis, he was placed in low medical category BEE (Temporary).
Petitioner Nachattar Singh was promoted to the rank of Naib Subedar with effect from 171998 and on account of Ventricular Premature Beats was placed in the low medical category BEE (Permanent).
Thereafter, the petitioners were put in the sheltered appointments.
At this point of time, it was found that the manpower of Intelligence Corps had exceeded its sanctioned strength to the extent of 15%.
In view of this, the respondents had decided to withdraw the appointments of 30 JCOs and 28 NCOs, who were working against the sheltered posts.
The petitioners were issued a show cause notice and appeared before the Release Medical Board, where they were released from service with effect from 1122000.
The Release Medical Board had found that petitioner Balwant Singh was suffering from NIDDM CNS (INV) SEIZURES, petitioner Ganesh Dass was suffering from Solitary SEIZURE PARTIAL SECONDARY GENERALISATION, petitioner Angrej Singh was suffering from MONO ARTICULAR RHEUMATOID ARTHRITIS and petitioner Nachattar Singh was suffering from VENTRICULAR PREMATURE BEATS.
The case of the petitioners was, in these circumstances, recommended for discharge under Rule 13(2A) of the Army Rules.
In view of these circumstances, the petitioners' discharge on account of lack of medical fitness, on account whereof they had been placed in Low Medical Category, was responsible for their release from service.
There is no infirmity in the order passed by the respondents' and consequently, there is no reason that this Court should interfere with the impugned order.
Dismissed.
Petitions dismissed.
