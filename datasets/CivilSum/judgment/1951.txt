8.
The learned Advocate for the petitioner argued that the Supreme Court in Union of India v. Shaik Ali, reported at 1990 Supp.
(2) SCC 717, held that para 620(ii) of the Railway Pension Manual is ultra vires of the Constitution.
The learned Advocate argued that since the orders under challenge were issued in purported exercise of power conferred by Rule 1803(a) of Indian Railway Establishment Code (6th Edition, 1987), Vol. II read with paras 620(ii) and 621(i) of Manual of Railway Pension Rules, 1950 and Schedule III of RPF Rules, 1987 and when paragraph 620(ii) of the Manual of Railway Pension Rules, 1950 has been declared to be ultra vires by the Supreme Court in Union of India v. Shaik Ali case (supra), then the exercise of the power by the Divisional Security Commissioner (RPF), Adra, was also unconstitutional and, therefore, the orders under challenge are unconstitutional, illegal and not valid.
9.
The argument of the learned Advocate for the petitioners require a close scrutiny.
In Union of India v. Shaik Ali case (supra), the Supreme Court, in paragraph 7 of the reported decision, observed as follows :  "Before we depart we may observe that the concerned authorities will do well to amend Rule 2(2) of the Pension Rules and para 620(ii) referred to above so as to incorporate therein the requirement of public interest, that is to say, the premature retirement on completion of qualifying service of thirty years can be ordered in public interest only."
Thus it is very clear from the paragraph of the reported decision in Union of India v. Shaik Ali case (supra), that Supreme Court observed that the concerned authorities would do well to amend para 620(ii) of the Pension Rules so as to incorporate therein the requirement of public interest.
10.
In Union of India v. R Narasimhan reported at 1988 Supp.
SCC 636, Supreme Court considered para 620 of Manual of Railway Pension Rules, 1950 and held that para 620 as valid and effective and supplementary to Rule 2046 of Railway Establishment Code.
The Supreme Court found that there was no inconsistency between Rule 2046 of Railway Establishment Code and para 620 of the Railway Pension Manual.
Supreme Court held that para 620 of the Railway Pension Manual Rules, 1950 should be treated as supplementary to Rule 2046 of Railway Establishment Code.
Supreme Court held that para 620 of the Railway Pension Rules had been framed by the Union Government in exercise of its executive power under Article 73 of the Constitution and should be given due effect since there is no statutory provision or a rule framed under the provision to Article 309 of the Constitution which is inconsistent with the Railway Pension Rules, 1950.
11.
Rule 2045 of Railway Establishment Code as referred to in Union of India v. R Narasimhan case (supra) reads as follows :  "2016 (F.R 56).
(a) Except of otherwise provided in this rule, every Railway servant shall retire on the day he attains the age of fifty-eight years.
xx xx xx  (h) Notwithstanding anything contained in this rule, the appointing authority shall, if it is of the opinion that it is in the public interest to do so, have the absolute right to retire any Railway servant giving him notice of not less than three months in writing or three months pay and allowances in lieu of such notice - (i) if he is in Class I or Class II service or post had entered Government service before attaining the age of thirty-five years, after he has attained the age of fifty years.
(ii) in any other case after he has attained the age of fifty-five years.
(i) Any Railway servant may by giving notice of not less than three months in writing to the appointing authority retire from service after he has attained the age of fifty years if he is in Class I or Class II service or post and had entered Government service before attaining the age of thirty-five years, and in all other cases after he has attained the age of fifty-five years.
* * * (b) Notwithstanding anything contained in clause (b), the appointing authority shall, if it is of the opinion that it is in the public interest to do so, have the absolute right to retire a railway servant in Class II service or post who is not governed by any Pension Rules after he has completed thirty years' service by giving him notice of not less than three months in writing or three months' pay and allowances in lieu of such notice."
12.
The relevant lines from paragraph 5 of the reported decision in Union of India v. R Narasimhan case (supra) are quoted hereinbelow :  "A reading of the relevant part of rule 2046 of the Rules and para 620 of the Railway Pension Manual shows that ordinarily every railway servant has to retire on the date he attains the age of 58 years.
Notwithstanding the said rule the appointing authority, if it is of the opinion that it is in the public interest do so, has the power to retire any railway servant giving him notice of not less than three months in writing or three months' pay and allowances in lieu of such notice, if he is in Class I or Class II service or post and had entered Government service before attaining the age of 35 years, after he has attained the age of 50 years and in any other case after he has attained the age of 55 years.
Similarly a railway servant may by giving notice of not less than three months in writing to the appointing authority retire from service if he is in Class I or Class II service or post and had entered Government service before attaining the age of 35 years, after he has attained the age of fifty years and in all other cases after he has attained the age of 55 years.
These two kinds of retirements are dealt with in clauses (h) and (i) of Rule 2046 of the Rules.
The governing factor in clauses (h) and (i) of Rule 2046 is the age of the employee concerned at the time when it is proposed to retire him from service or when he wishes to retire from service voluntarily as the case may be.
This rule applies no doubt to all employees irrespective of the fact whether they are governed by the Pensions Rules or not.
Clause (k) of Rule 2046 provides that notwithstanding anything contained in clause (b) the appointing authority shall, if it is of the opinion that it is in the public interest to do so, have the absolute right to retire a railway servant in Class III service or post who is not governed by any Pension Rules after he has completed thirty years' service by giving him notice of not less than three months in writing or three months' pay and allowances in lieu of such notice.
Clause (1) of Rule 2046 of the Rules provides that a railway servant in Clause III service or post who is not governed by any Pension Rules, may by giving notice of not less than three months in writing to the appointing authority, retire from service after he has completed thirty years' service.
It may be noted that in clauses (k) and (1) of Rule 2046 of the Rules the governing factor is not the age of the railway servant concerned but the length of the qualifying service rendered by him.
They are applicable only to a railway servant in Class III service who has completed 30 years of service in railway administration.
If a railway servant in Class III service has entered the service during his 21st year he can be retired by the Government on his completing 30 years of service in his 51st year or he may elect to retire from service in his 51st year after completing 30 years of service by following the prescribed formalities.
Clauses (k) and (l) of Rule 2046, as already stated above, do not apply to railway servants who are governed by Pension Rules.
Para 620 of the Railway Pension Manual, which is extracted above, contains provisions corresponding to clauses (k) and (l) of para 2046 of the Rules.
Under sub-para (i) of para 620 a railway servant governed by the Pension Rules may retire from service at any time after completing 30 years of qualifying service provided that he has given a notice in writing to the appointing authority three months before the date on which he wishes to retire.
Sub-para (ii) of para 620 of the Railway Pension Manual, which corresponds to clause (k) of Rule 2046 of the Rules, empowers the competent authority to remove a railway servant from service any time after he has completed 30 years of qualifying service provided that the authority has given in this behalf a notice in writing to the railway servant atleast three months before the date on which he is required to retire or three months' pay and allowances in lieu of such notice.
It may also be noted that while clauses (k) and (l) of Rule 2046 of the Rules apply only to a railway servant in Class III service or post not governed by any Pension Rules, para 620 of the Railway Pension Manual applies to all railway servants governed by the Pension Rules."
13.
Para 620(ii) of the Railway Pension Manual Rules, 1950 reads as follows :  "620 (i) ................... (ii) The authority competent to remove the Railway servant from service may also require him to retire any time after he has completed 30 years' qualifying service provided that the authority shall give in this behalf, a notice in writing to the Railway servant, at least three months before the date on which he is required to retire or three months' pay and allowances in lieu of such notice.
(iii) ....................." 14.
Rule 1803 of Indian Railway Establishment Code, Vol. II (6th Edition, 1987) reads as follows :  "1803.
(a) Notwithstanding anything contained in these rules, or any other rule or order for the time being in force, the appointing authority shall, if it is of the opinion that it is in public interest to do so, have the absolute right to retire a railway servant governed by any Pension Rules after he has completed thirty years' service qualifying for pension after giving a notice in writing in this behalf to the railway servant at least three months before the date on which he is required to retire, or three months' pay and allowances in lieu of such notice.
(b) A railway servant who is governed by any of the pension rules, may retire from service at any time after completion of 30 years of service qualifying for pension, after giving notice in writing to the appropriate authority, at least three months before the intended date of retirement : Provided that it shall be open to the appropriate authority to withhold permission to a railway servant under suspension, who seeks to retire under this clause".
15.
In Senior Superintendent of Post Offices v. Izhar Hussain reported in 1989(4) SCC 318 the Supreme Court was considering Liberalised Pension Rules, 1950 and held that Rule 2(2) of the Liberalised Pension Rules, 1950 as unconstitutional for giving unguided discretion in the matter of premature retirement of 30 years qualifying service.
Rule 2(2) of the Liberalised Pensions Rules, 1950 reads as follows :  "An Officer may retire from service any time after completing 30 years' qualifying service provided that he shall give in this behalf a notice in writing to the appropriate authority at least three months before the date on which he wishes to retire.
Government may also require an officer to retire, any time after he has completed 30 years' qualifying service provided that the appropriate authority shall give, in this behalf a notice in writing to the officer at least three months before the date on which he is required to retire, or three months' pay and allowances in lieu of such notice."
16.
In paragraph 5 of the reported decision in Senior Supdt.
of Post Offices v. Izhar Hussain (supra) Supreme Court observed as follows :-  "The object of Rule 2(2) of Pension Rules may also be to weed out those government servants who have outlived their utility but there is no guideline provided in the rule to this effect.
The rule gives unguided discretion to the government to retire a government servant at any time after he has completed 30 years of qualifying service though he has a right to continue till the age of superpannation which is 58 years.
Any government servant who has completed 30 years of qualifying service and has not attained the age of 55 years can be picked up for premature retirement under the rule.
Since no safeguards are provided in the rule, the discretion is absolute and is capable of being used arbitrarily and with an uneven hand.
We, therefore, agree with the Division Bench of the High Court and hold that Rule 2(2) of the Pension Rules is ultra vires Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India."
17.
In paragraph 9 of the reported decision in Senior Supdt.
of Post Offices v. Izhar Hussain (supra), the Supreme Court considered the decision in Union of India v. R Narasimhan, Paragraphs 9 and 10 of the reported decision in Senior Supdt.
of Post Offices v. Izhar Hussain case (supra) are quoted hereinbelow :  "9.
Relying on the decision of this Court in Union of India v. R Narasimhan, 1989 Lab IC 1259, Shri Anil Dev Singh contended that para 620 of the Railway Pension Manual, which is identical to Rule 2(2) of the Pension Rules, has been upheld by this Court.
In R Narasimhan case the scope of Rule 2046 of the Indian Railway Establishment Code and para 620 of the Railway Pension Manual was considered by this Court.
Rule 2046 is a statutory rule and is identical to fundamental Rule 56(j).
Para 620 is in the nature of executive instructions but is similarly worded as Rule 2(2) of Pension Rules.
A Division Bench of the Madras High Court came to the conclusion that Rule 2046 having been framed under Article 309 of the Constitution and being a compendious rule, the railway employees are only governed by the said rule and para 620 was void and inoperative.
This Court while setting aside the judgment of the High Court held as under (see p. 641, para 7) : "Thus the area of operation of para 620 of the Railway Pension Manual is different from that of clauses (h) and (k) of Rule 2046 of the Rules.
Para 620 of the Railway Pension Manual should be treated as supplementary to Rule 2046 of the rules.
The said para which has been framed by the Union Government in exercise of its executive power under Article 73 of the Constitution should be given due effect since there is no statutory provision or rule framed under the proviso to Aritlce 309 of the Constitution which is inconsistent with it."
"10.
R Narasimhan case has thus no relevancy to the controversy involved in this case.
There was no challenge to any of the rules or para 620 of the Manual of the ground that it gives unguided power to the Railway authorities to pick and choose railway employees for the purpose of premature retirement.
Para 620 of the Manual being executive instruction supplementing the statutory Rule 2046 has no parity with Rule 2(2) of Pension Rules which is a statutory rule.
In any case the point before us in the present case was neither involved nor raised in Narasimhan case and as such Shri Anil Dev Singh cannot derive any support form the said judgment."
18.
If para 620(ii) of Railway Pension Rules, 1950 is compared with Rule 2(2) of the Liberalised Pension Rules, 1950 then it will be seen that both the provisions are similar.
Under these circumstance it can be argued that para 620(ii) of the Railway Pension Rules, 1950 is ultra vires.
But I think it is not necessary to go into that question at all because in Union of India v. Narasimhan (supra), Supreme Court clearly laid down that para 620(ii) of the Railway Pension Rules, 1950 was framed by the Union Government in exercise of its executive power under Article 73 of the Constitution  19.
Rule 1803 of the Railway Establishment Code, Vol. II 6th Edition, 1987 clearly laid down that notwithstanding anything contained in those rules or any other rule or order for the time being in force the appointing authority shall, if it is of the opinion that it is in the public interest to do so, have the absolute right to retire a railway servant governed by any Pension Rules after he has completed 30 years service qualifying for pension after giving a notice in writing in this behalf to the railway servant at least three months before the date on which he is required to retire or three months' pay and allowances in lieu of such notice.
It is apparent on the face of the order under challenge that an opinion was formed by the authority that it was in the public interest to do so and, therefore, the petitioners were given notice in exercise of power conferred under Rule 1803(a) of Indian Railway Establishment Code (6th Edition, 1987) Vol. II that the petitioners had already completed qualifying service of 30 years and should retire from the railway service with effect from the date mentioned in the orders of retirement.
There was no violation of the conditions prescribed by Rule 1803(a) of Indian Railway Establishment Code (6th Edition, 1987) Vol. II.
The learned Advocate for the petitioners did not argue that the requirements mentioned in the said Rule 1803(a) have been violated or breached.
The said Rule 1803(a) being an non-obstante rule has its full application irrespective of what is contained in para 620(ii) of Railway Pension Rules, 1950.
The learned Advocate for the petitioner did not argue that the said Rule 1803(a) is also ultra vires of the Constitution.
20.
In Union of India v. Santi Kumar reported at AIR 1967 Cal.
129, a Division Bench of this High Court in paragraph 4 of the reported decision, inter alia, held that originally the Railway Establishment Code was made under Section 241(2) of the Government of India Act, 1935.
This had been continued in the subsequent Code and the Establishment Manuals under the authority of Article 309 of the Constitution.
It was held that under the authority of Article 309 the President has been given the right to delegate his power and by Rule 156 contained in the 1961 Code (corresponding to Rule 157 in the 1959 Code) the Railway Board had been given full power to make rules for general application to non-gazetted railway servants under their control.
Since the Railway Establishment Code was continued under the authority of Article 309 of the Constitution the rules contained in Railway Establishment Code have statutory force of law.
In view of the above decision of a Division Bench of Calcutta High Court I am of the view that Rule 1803 of the Railway Establishment Code (6th Edition, 1987) Vol. II have statutory force of law.
Therefore, even if para 620(ii) of the Railway Pension Rules, 1950 is made not applicable to the petitioners then also a competent authority in a bona fide exercise of power under Rule 1803 of the Railway Establishment Code (6th Edition, 1987) Vol. II can retire the writ petitioners and such exercise of power cannot be said to be illegal or invalid unless the conditions as mentioned in the said rule are violated or breached.
It is not the case of the writ petitioners that the conditions contained in Rule 1830 of Railway Establishment Code (6th Edition, 1987) Vol. II have been violated or breached.
Under these circumstances I am of the view that the orders of retirement passed in connection with the writ petitioners in exercise of power under the aforesaid Rule 1803 are legal and valid.
21.
In view of the discussions made hereinabove I am of the view that the orders of retirement are legal and valid and cannot be interfered with by this Court.
I am of the view that the writ petition has no merit.
The writ petition is dismissed.
Interim order, if any, is vacated.
There shall be no order as to costs.
Petition dismissed.
