Thus main judgment is prepared in the present case.
2.
The petitioners claim consideration and appointment as Lecturers/Masters/Mistresses on the ground that candidates lower in merit than the petitioners were selected and appointed as such.
They further claim quashing of the selection made by Departmental Selection Committee on the ground that procedure followed for the selection was illegal and arbitrary.
Writ of prohibition is also claimed prohibiting the respondents from filling up the remaining vacancies till appointment of the petitioner.
The educational qualifications possessed by the petitioners are narrated in para 2 of the writ petition along with percentage of marks obtained by them in their respective BA/BSc/M.Sc.and B.Ed.
courses.
Against remarks column qualifications of the petitioners i.e. belonging to Backward classes or freedom fighter category etc. and their achievements such like NSS certificates or experience certificate are mentioned.
Some Annexures have also been attached along with the writ petition regarding experience.
3.
The recruitment to the posts of Lecturers/Masters is governed by Punjab State Education Class III (School Cadre) Service Rules, 1978.
Rule 5 provides necessary qualifications and experience as laid down in Appendix `B' attached to Volume I of the Rules.
For the post of Math.
Masters the requisite qualifications are BA (Math.
`A' and `B' grades with B.Ed.)
and for the post of Science Masters/Mistresses the qualifications required are BSc.
and B.Ed.
Rule 8 provides appointment to such posts to be made by the Director, Public Instructions (Schools), Punjab, respondent No. 2.
The State of Punjab constituted Departmental Selection Committee with Director of Public Instructions (Schools) as its Chairman for making selections to the post of Masters as referred to above as well as Lecturers and Masters in other subjects.
Respondent No. 2 thus advertised several posts of Lecturers and Masters in different subjects.
Such advertisement was published in the newspaper dated August 19, 1992 - Annexure P 2.
The petitioners and several other persons applied for appointment to the posts of Masters and Mistresses in the subjects of Math.
and Science etc. All the candidates were issued roll numbers.
The roll number allotted to the petitioners are mentioned in para 5 of the writ petition along with the subjects for which they were allotted such roll numbers.
Some of the petitioners applied for Science as well as Math.
Masters posts.
Interview of the candidates were scheduled to be held on December 3, 1992 as per letters of interview issued on November 25, 1992, one of them being Annexure P 3.
For the posts of Social Studies Masters and Mistresses three different Departmental Selection Committees were constituted with different Chairman and Members with their headquarters at Patiala, Gurdaspur and Ferozepur.
Likewise different Selection Committees were constituted for other subjects.
After the interviews were held by such different Selection Committees, the result was complied in the office of respondent No. 2 by Dr. M L Sachdeva, Deputy Director of Public Instructions (Schools), Punjab.
4.
Challenge to the selection of the teachers is, inter alia, on the ground that the Director-respondent No. 2 was not competent to further constitute the Selection Committees with different Chairman and Members.
He had no power to delegate his own powers to such Departmental Selection Committees.
Instead of himself interviewing the candidates and applying his own independent mind to determine the merits of the candidates, he illegally delegated his powers to such Departmental Selection Committees.
Since different Departmental Selection Committees held meetings at different headquarters, inter se merits of the candidates could not be adjudged.
Such different departmental Selection Committees adopted different yard- sticks/criterion for making assessment of the candidates.
Thus, Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution were violated.
It is further asserted that at the time of interview no question, whatsoever, were asked and it was only a farce.
Interview marks were written in pencil and not in ink and they were subsequently manipulated and changed.
With respect to the criterion, it was stated that out of 100 marks 35 marks were awarded to all the candidates possessing the qualifications as prescribed i.e. BA/BSc.
and 35 marks were awarded to the candidates possessing B.Ed.
qualification.
After awarding 70% marks to all the candidates possessing such qualifications, as mentioned above, 15 marks were kept for other curricular activities and 15 marks for interview.
No weightage was given to candidates who were such meritorious in their academic career or had acquired higher marks in the requisite qualifications.
No merit list was published.
The names of the selected candidates were not displayed.
The appointment letters were being issued by following pick and choose methods.
No waiting list was notified or displayed.
The petitioners thus are not aware of their position.
This was done by the respondents to keep the petitioners and like others in dark that they could not challenge the selection in the Court of law.
Reference was also made to a statement made by the Minister in the Assembly that the selection would be exclusively on merits and that there will be no viva voce test.
In spite of such Statement selection was made on the basis of interview only.
Without giving weightage to the maximum marks obtained by the candidates in the educational qualifications.
Reference was also made to percentage of marks obtained in educational qualifications of some other candidates who were stated to have been selected to support the petition that the percentage of marks obtained by the petitioners was higher than them.
5.
On notice of motion written statement has been filed on behalf of the official respondents contesting the writ petition.
It is maintained that the selection and appointment of the Science and Math.
Masters and Mistresses as has been made on merit.
No person securing lesser marks than the petitioners has been selected and appointed.
Departmental Selection Committees were constituted by the State of Punjab vide notification dated November 3, 1992.

Respondent No. 2 was merely an Appointing Authority whereas Selections were made by the Chairman of the Departmental Selection Committees and they recommended candidates for appointment to respondent No. 2.
There was no question of respondent No. 2 shifting his responsibility to other persons for conduct of the interview.
The Government gave the criterion for the selection of candidates to all the Chairman of the Departmental Selection Committees which is uniform and mandatory.
All the assertions made in the writ petition in this respect were denied.
At the time of interview candidates were thoroughly questioned for formulating overall view of their performance.
It was denied that the assessment was made in pencil and not with ink.

7.
The foremost question for consideration is as to whether the criterion adopted in the present case for selection of Masters in different subjects is arbitrary and liable to be quashed.
As is clear from the written statement as well as records of selection the criterion adopted was not as has been alleged in the writ petition.
The break-up of 100 marks in the case of selection for Masters and Mistresses is :  (1) For academic qualifications; + (a) BA/BSc.
in the relevant subject 35%  (b) B.Ed.
35% (2) Supporting qualifications, details of which have already been given above 15 marks; and (3) For interview: 15 marks.
It may be observed that the stand of the petitioners that to every candidate 70 marks stood allotted as per criterion noted above is incorrect.
In fact 35% of marks obtained in the respective academic qualification Examinations as explained on behalf of the Departmental Selection Committee, is taken.
8.
For example, if in BA or BSc.
the candidate had secured 60 marks, applying the criterion aforesaid his marks would be calculated as 60 x 35/100 = 21.
Similarly 35% of B.Ed.
examination marks are to be taken out.
Thereafter weightage is to be allowed for other achievements as prescribed for experience/NCC certificates/Sports/Scouting/National and State Awards for teachers, maximum marks provided for such weightage being 15.
It is only 15% marks which are reserved for interview.
In this manner the persons obtaining higher marks in the academic qualifications, achievements and interview would stand selected, meaning thereby that meritorious candidates would get better chances of selection.
9.
Present is not a case where written test is prescribed followed by Viva Voce test for finalising the selection.
The Supreme Court in Anzar Ahmed v. State of Bihar and others, 1994(1) SCT 483(SC) : 1993(5) SLR 798, while making reference to the earlier decisions of the Supreme Court holding upto 15% marks for Viva Voce test where written test is also provided and explaining the same, observed in para 17 as under :-  ``We are unable to construe the said decision to mean that the principles which govern the allocation of marks for interview in a selection based on written and Viva Voce test would also apply to a selection where no written test is held and the selection is based on interview only.''
In the aforesaid case 50% marks were for academic performance and 50% for interview.
In para 18 of the judgment it was observed as under :-  ``It cannot be held that the said procedure adopted by the Commission suffers from the vice of arbitrariness.
By giving equal weight to academic performance the Commission has rather reduced the possibility of arbitrariness.''
Reference may also be made in this context to the decision of this Court in Sudha Yadava v. The State of Haryana and others, 1994(3) SCT 194(P&H) : 1994(3) Recent Services Judgments 201.
Relying upon the decision of the Apex Court in Anzar Ahmad's case, the criterion providing 50% marks for qualifications and achievements and the remaining 50% for interview was upheld.
The ratio of Anzar Ahmad's case, which related to selection for appointment was relied upon and applied in the case of selection for promotion by the Apex Court in AP State Financial Corporation v. CM Ashok Raju and others, 1994(4) SCT 585(SC) : JT 1994(5) SC 481.
Thus it is not considered necessary to refer to earlier decisions of the Apex Court where upto 15% marks for Viva Voce Test were hold to be proper when remaining marks were for the written test.
The criterion suggests that meritorious candidates are to be given preference/weightage, as is clear from the fact that in the academic qualifications their marks upto fraction are also to be taken into consideration while applying the percentage criterion.
The criterion adopted in the present case is valid and does not suffer from the vice of arbitrariness.
10.
Another ground of attack to the selection made was that marking was done initially in pencil by the different Selection Committees constituted.
It was at the time of compilation of the result that the pencil marks were altered and finalised in ink to suit the formation of the result to favour some.
This allegation has been denied in the written statement.
Even otherwise on perusal of the file we do not find support for this assertion.
Otherwise such disputed questions cannot be determined in these proceedings.
The contention is, therefore, repelled.
11.
Learned counsel for the petitioners has argued that merit list was not prepared and published as required.
In reply to this assertion it was asserted that different merit list district-wise were prepared and sent to the District Education Officers for display.
Ordinarily the consolidated result as finalised was required to be published to make it known to the public, but on that account per se the selection made cannot be quashed.
It was in this view of the matter that as a test case particulars of about 10 writ petitioners were obtained and supplied to the State Government and it was in response thereto that additional affidavit supported by Annexure A-1 has been produced.
Incidentally such of the writ petitioners already stand selected.
Non-publication of the entire result as such is not considered a ground particularly in the present case to quash the selection.
12.
One of the learned counsel appearing on behalf of the petitioners contended that for Scheduled Castes categories a separate merit list was required to be prepared considering their inter se merit.
This contention is again devoid of merit.
It has not been demonstrated by the petitioners that rights of any Scheduled Castes candidate has been jeopardised in this manner or that requisite number of Scheduled Caste, candidates has not been selected.
For want of material in this respect no further comment is called for.
It has also been argued that persons three times of the vacancies only should have been interviewed, to choose meritorious persons.
This contention in the facts of the present case cannot be accepted.
In view of the criterion referred to above the intention was vivid that meritorious persons were to be selected.
Non-observance of the principle of interviewing limited number of persons for limited number of vacancies as above is not considered enough to quash the selection.
13.
It has been rightly argued by learned counsel for the petitioners that selection could be made only upto the number of vacancies advertised and appointments made beyond such number of posts which were advertised be quashed.
The persons on the waiting list could only be considered for appointment if some persons already selected failed to resume duty.
Otherwise the waiting list is not to be treated as a source of recruitment.
It was so held in Gujarat State Dy.
Executive Engineers Association v. State of Gujarat, 1994(3) SCT 325(SC) : 1994(3) Recent Services Judgments 327.
The matter was considered by the Full Bench of this Court in Bijender Singh v. The State of Haryana, etc., 1994(4) SCT 382(P&H)(FB) : (1994-3) 108 P.L.R.
1, and relying upon several decisions of the Supreme Court, it was held that only number of posts advertised can be filled by selection and appointment against other posts which may have fallen vacant subsequently to the advertisement would be illegal.
The persons becoming eligible after advertisement was issued would be denied the right of consideration for appointment against such posts.
14.
It has been argued by learned counsel for the petitioner that respondent No. 2 was the competent authority to make appointments.
Constitution of different Committees by him for interviewing different sets of candidates is not contemplated under the law.
Such Committees followed different criterion for making selection.
This contention again is devoid of merit.
Respondent No. 2, the Director of Public Instructions (Schools), Punjab no doubt, is the Appointing Authority of the teachers, however, it is the State who had constituted different Selection Committees by issuing a separate notification dated November 3, 1992.
Such Committees have adopted uniform criterion for selection, which has been discussed above.
Such Committees were required to grant upto 15 marks in the interview.
Assuming, there being some element of variation in the method of judging the candidates during interview, that per se cannot be ground to quash the selection, more so, when there are no allegations of mala fides, against members of such Committees.
The contention is repelled.
15.
It was argued on behalf of the petitioners that some persons were appointed who do not possess even the minimum requisite academic qualifications.
In particular reference was made to the case of Manjit Singh, Roll No. 5266, a Science Master, who is not B.Ed.
It is argued that proper mind was not applied at the time of selection, which should be quashed.
Advocate-General has pointed out that on verification, necessary steps would be taken to cancel the appointment of such a candidate.
On this ground, according to him, entire selection is not to be quashed.
We are of the opinion that in such like cases, on verification of such petitioners, State should take necessary action.
16.
Learned counsel for the petitioners referred to the marks obtained by some of the petitioners and argued that if in the interview, they had secured 3 to 4 marks, they were bound to be selected.
This contention cannot be accepted.
As already discussed above, there may be several candidates above the petitioners securing higher marks (fraction by fraction).
17.
As would be clear from the advertisement Annexure A/2 subject-wise the posts of Lecturers/Masters/Mistresses/DPE etc. advertised were; Mathematics (116), Science (159) and Social Studies, Agriculture, Fine Arts, Music, Tailoring and Home Science (1000).
(Lecturers figure not given).
The mere fact that it was mentioned in the advertisement that number of vacancies may be increased or decreased, will not bestow power to make appointments beyond the number of vacancies advertised.
The State could not deny the right of consideration of such of the candidates becoming eligible for appointments after issuing of the advertisement aforesaid.
Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution stand infringed by appointment to the posts becoming available after issuing of the advertisement.
18.
Since the petitioners cannot be considered selected for the number of posts advertised, they cannot be granted any relief.
19.
For the reasons recorded above, these writ petitions are disposed of as under :-  (i) Selection and appointment of candidates upto the number of posts advertised is upheld; (ii) Directions are given to the respondents; (a) to publish the list of selected candidates subject-wise in the newspaper; (b) to take up the cases of such of the candidates, who did not possess the requisite qualifications and to pass appropriate orders according to law, as discussed above; (c) to take up the cases of persons appointed against posts which became available after issuing of the advertisement Annexure A/2 to pass appropriate orders according to law, as discussed above.
(iii) Petitioners cannot be granted any relief.
There will be no order as to costs.
