GS Singhvi, J - This petition is an example of the false hopes created in the mind of the public due to inadvertent or deliberate and misconceived concession made by public authorities in the proceedings pending before a Court.
The petitioners have heavily relied on order dated April 21, 1993 passed in C.W.P.
No. 10133 of 1992, which is based on a concession made by the District Primary Education Officer, Rohtak and, as would be seen hereinafter, this concession was thoroughly misconceived and this order cannot be made the basis for giving relief to the petitioners.
2.
Now a brief reference to the facts is necessary for deciding whether the claim made by the petitioner for grant of pension from the date of retirement of their husbands is legally tenable or not.
3.
The husbands of the petitioners were appointed as JBT Teachers in the erstwhile District Board Schools.
All of them were working as JBT Teachers on 1101957, the date on which the erstwhile District Board Schools were taken over by the Government and all the teachers were absorbed in the Government service.
They retired from service on the dates of attaining the age of superannuation and these dates were prior to 31.10.1966.
Late husbands of the petitioners represented for grant of pension and after considering the various aspects of the matter the Government of Haryana issued a policy circular dated 1471977 for grant of pension with effect from 18.12.1972 to those teachers who had served in the erstwhile District Board schools upto 1101957 and who retired from service between 1101957 to 31.10.1966.
On the basis of this circular the petitioners were given family pension.
4.
The petitioners have pleaded that in terms of circular dated 1471977 they are entitled to be paid pension/family pension from the dates of the retirement of their husbands and there is no justification for limiting the benefit of pension with effect from 18.12.1972.
The petitioners have further pleaded that the fixation of date for entitlement of pension/family pension as 18.12.1972 is wholly arbitrary and unconstitutional.
Another plea raised by the petitioners is that in view of the judgment of this Court dated 2141993 passed in C.W.P.
No. 10133 of 1992 (Smt. Kasturi Devi v. State of Haryana etc.) the Government issued circular dated 1311992 for grant of benefit of pension with effect from the date of retirement but confined this relief to those who are covered by the court order and in this manner other similarly situated persons, including the petitioners, have been discriminated.
5.
The respondents have opposed the writ petition on the ground that the benefit of pension/family pension has been extended to the employees of the erstwhile District Board schools by circular dated 1471977 and as a policy this benefit has been confined to the period after 18.12.1972 and the benefit of family pension is required to be regulated by Government letter No. 11/1 PR-FD-81 dated 19.3.1981.
Contention of the respondents is that fixation of the particular dates for grant of pension/family pension is neither arbitrary nor irrational.
According to the respondents, even if the Government had decided to grant benefit of pension with effect from 1471977 no exception could have been taken by the petitioners.
6.
Shri HS Gill relied on the order of the Division Bench passed in C.W.P.
No. 10133 of 1992 (Smt. Kasturi Devi v. State of Haryana) (supra) as well as the order dated 20.7.1989 passed by a learned Single Judge in C.W.P.
No. 10070 of 1988 (Bhim Singh and another v. The State of Haryana and another) and argued that when the Government has accepted the judgment of the Division Bench dated 2141993 and has issued cirular dated 3111992 granting benefit of pension to Smt. Kasturi Devi, in lieu of the service of her husband, with effect from the date of retirement of her husband, no discrimination could be made qua the petitioners.
According to Shri Gill the fixation of the date of grant of pension as 18.12.1972 is without any reason or rhyme and this date has no nexus or relation with the object of granting pension to those teachers who retired between 1101957 and 31.10.1966.
Learned Deputy Advocate General argued that the husbands of the petitioners were contributing to the Provident Fund and but for the policy decision taken by the Government in 1977 to extend the benefit of pension to such persons, they could not have claimed pension as a matter of right.
Learned counsel argued that while extending the benefit of pension to the retired employees, who were members of Contributory Provident Fund (CPF) the Government had the right to fix a particular date and, therefore, the grant of benefit of pension only with effect from 18.12.1972 cannot per se be treated as arbitrary or unreasonable.
Learned counsel argued that if the Government had fixed the date as 1471977 for grant of pension to the late husbands of the petitioners, then too no exception could have been taken to the fixation of such date.
Ordinarily in view of the decision of a co-ordinate Bench, we would have referred the matter to a larger Bench, but in view of the fact that the law has been settled by the Supreme Court, in our opinion, the order passed by the Division Bench on 2141993 in C.W.P.
No. 10133 of 1992 cannot be made the basis for grant of pensionary benefits to other erstwhile retirees or their family members from a date earlier than 18.12.1972.
A careful reading of the order dated 21.4.93 clearly shows that it was passed only on the concession made by the departmental authorities.
The Division Bench took notice of the letter written by the District Primary Education Officer, Rohtak, regarding the grant of benefits to Smt. Kasturi Devi with effect from the date of retirement of her husband.
Therefore, the direction given by the Division Bench cannot be treated as a declaration of law so as to be binding on a Bench of lesser number of Judges or a Co-ordinate Bench.
7.
A look at the order (Annexure R-1) shows that while extending the benefit of pension to the persons like the late husbands of the petitioners, the Government had made a further concession that they will not be required to refund to the Government share of CPF received by them from the Local Bodies/Government at the time of their retirement.
It clearly shows that the husbands of the petitioners were members of the CPF and they continued to be so till the date of retirement.
Had the Government not introduced the pension scheme vide order (Annexure R-1), the late husbands of the petitioners or for that reason the petitioners could not have claimed pensionary benefits.
8.
In Krishan Kumar v. Union of India and others, AIR 1990 SC 1782, a Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court considered a question almost similar to the one raised in the present case.
In that case the Railway employees who were covered by the Provident Scheme were given an option to switch over to the Pension Scheme with effect from a specified cut-off date.
This fixation was challenged and their Lordships of the Supreme Court held that those who are covered by the Provident Scheme constitute a different class than the pensioners and if the Government gives an adoption to such persons to opt for pension from a particular date, such decision of the Government cannot be declared as unconstitutional.
Similar view has been expressed by the Supreme Court in State of Rajasthan v. Pensioners' Smaj, (sic) 1991 SC 1743.
9.
In State of West Bengal etc. v. Ratan Behari Dey & others, 1994(1) SCT 96(SC) : JT 1993 (4) SC 501, a pension scheme was introduced by the Calcutta Municipal Corporation by framing the Municipal Corporation of Calcutta Employees (Death -cum-Retirement) Benefit Regulations, 1982.
Under these regulations the employees who retired on or after 1st April, 1977 were given option for switch over to the pension scheme.
While upholding the fixation of the date the Supreme Court relied on its earlier decision in Krishan Kumar v. Union of India (supra) and held that the Government cannot be said to have acted unreasonably in specifying the date as 1st April, 1977.
The Supreme Court held that the power to specify the date from which the terminal benefits are given cannot be declared as discriminatory.
In Union of India v. PN Menon and others, 1994(4) SCT 91 (SC) : 1994 (2) SLR 335, the fixation of the date for grant of pensionary benefits has been upheld by the Supreme Court.
In All India Reserve Bank Retired Officers Association and others v. Union of India and another, 1992 (3) SLR 35, their Lordships of the Supreme Court upheld the grant of benefit of pensionary scheme to the employees who retired on or after 1st January, 1986.
In view of these decisions we are of the opinion that it is not possible to give the benefit of the judgment of this Court dated 2141993 to the petitioners.
10.
We, therefore, hold that fixation of the date as 18.12.1972 for entitlement of pension to the persons like the late husbands of the petitioners does not suffer from any arbitrariness and the policy decision is not unconstitutional.
11.
We are also of the opinion that on the basis of the order passed by the learned Single Judge in C.W.P.
No. 10070 of 1988 (Bhim Singh and another v. The State of Haryana and another), dated 20.7.1989 and similar other decisions, no relief can be given to the petitioners.
The learned Single Judge also did not consider the judgments of the Supreme Court while granting relief to the writ petitioners in that case and, therefore, that order is of no help to the petitioners' case.
12.
For the reasons mentioned above, the writ petition is dismissed.
However, the parties are left to bear their own costs.
Petition dismissed.
