RS Mongia, J - Petitioners are working as Industrial Promotion Officers which is a class II post in the pay scale of Rs. 1640-2900/- (received w.e.f.
111986).
Some of the petitioners have been appointed by direct recruitment as Industrial Promotion Officers and others promoted as such.
The post of Block Level Extension Officer/Inspector of Industries is a feeder post to the post of Industrial Promotion Officer.
The time scale of the post of Block Level Extension Officer/Inspector of Industries is Rs. 1400/-2600/- and time scale of Industrial Promotion Officer is Rs. 1640-2900/- w.e.f.
111986.
By virtue of the present writ petition, they seek a writ of mandamus directing the respondents to grant higher scale to the petitioners in comparison to the feeder post mentioned above w.e.f.
111994.
The other prayer that respondents 1 and 2 should be directed to grant promotee quota to the petitioners while filling up the posts of Assistant Director and Deputy Director in respondents' department was not pressed.
Learned counsel for the petitioners is permitted to file a separate writ petition, if so advised with respect to the said relief.
Therefore, the scope of the present petition is confined to the first prayer seeking higher scale of pay to the petitioners in comparison to the feeder post from 111994.
2.
The precise grievance of the petitioners is that Government of Haryana issued policy decision, by virtue of which it was decided that Class III and Class IV employees of the Haryana Government, shall be granted first higher standard pay scale on completion of 10 years service and second higher standard pay scale on completion of 20 years service.
As a result of this policy decision (copies of which are Annexures P-1 and P-2), the employees/persons working in the feeder post of Block Level Extension Officers/Inspectors of Industries are having higher scale of pay than the higher post on which the petitioners are working.
The petitioners claim that as result of it they are being discriminated and consequently, they seek higher pay scale for their post as against those working on the feeder post.
3.
Needless to say that petition has been contested.
It is contended that it is in the inherent power of the State Government to prescribe and grant the additional increments to Group C and D employees on completion of certain years of service.
It was done after due consideration, keeping in view the various administrative factors.
It did not give the petitioners any right to claim higher pay scale.
There was no controversy raised about the decision of the Government (copies of which are Annexures P-1 and P-2) whereby the higher pay scale was given to Group C and D employees after completion of 10 years and 20 years of satisfactory service.
It was admitted that pay scale of Block Level Extension Officer/Inspector of Industries is Rs. 1400-2600/- while the time scale of Industrial Promotion Officer from 111986 is Rs. 1640-2900/-.
The higher standard pay scale on completion of 10 and 20 years regular satisfactory service was granted to remove the stagnation.
It was denied that there was any discrimination in this regard qua the petitioners.

From the aforesaid statutory Rules, it is clear that posts of Industries Promotion Officer/Development Officer are either filled up by way of direct recruitment or by promotion from Block Level Extension Officer/Inspector of Industries.
It is a common case of the parties that time scale of the post of Block Level Extension Officer/Inspector of Industries from 111986 is Rs. 1400-2600/- while the time scale of Industrial Promotion Officer is Rs. 1640-2900/-.
After 10 years of service, the Block Level Extension Officer/Inspector of Industries is granted the time scale of Rs. 1640-2900/- and after rendering 20 years of service, they are placed in the scale of Rs. 2000-3200/-.

It is on the strength of these facts that it is being alleged that the persons on the lower post of Block Level Extension Officer/Inspector of Industries after 20 years of service are placed in the higher scale of Rs. 2000-3200/- from 141995 while the petitioners who are working on the higher post and have put in more than 20 years of service, still are working in the lower scale of Rs. 1640-2900/-.
5.
It is true and there is considerable merit in the respondents' contention that writ of mandamus will not be issued to enact a particular rule.
It is entirely for the State to enact the relevant rules and decide the pay scales of a particular case.
When a State action is challenged, the function of the Court is to examine if the action is in accordance with law.
If it is contrary to law, the Court will strike it down.
While doing so, the Court must remain within its self imposed limits.
But at the same time when discrimination is writ large the face of it, then to set the illegal action in a proper mould an appropriate direction can always be issued.
6.
Our attention has been drawn towards the Division Bench decision of this Court in the case of Har Kishan and another v. State of Punjab and another, 1987(5) SLR 539.
In the cited case Har Kishan and another who were the petitioners, were working as Patwaris in the Irrigation Department and were promoted as Assistant Revenue Clerks.
The Patwaris were also known as Irrigation Booking Clerks.
The grades of Patwaris and Assistant Revenue Clerks were put at par by the Pay Commission in the year 1968.
The petitioners therein had put in a representation that since their posts were one of the promotion, it is irrational to equate their grades with Patwaris.
This Court directed the Government to refer the question of pay scales of Patwaris and Assistant Revenue Clerks to the Pay Commission holding that the situation created is anomalous and lower and higher posts should not be put in the same pay scale.
In paragraph 3 it was observed :-  ``From the provisions of Rule 10 of the Punjab Public Works Department (Irrigation Branch), Revenue Clerks' State Service Class III, Rules 1956, it is evident that the Patwaris on promotion are appointed as Assistant Revenue Clerks.
The anomaly is, therefore, writ large, because it would be highly irrational to place a junior post and a higher post in the same scale of pay.
We are, therefore, unable to understand as to why the Government rejected the representation of the petitioners and declined to remove the anomaly; in the pay scale of the Patwaris and the Assistant Revenue Clerks.''
Subsequently, in another decision of this Court in the case PL Goyal, Additional District and Sessions Judge v. State of Haryana and others, 1991(1) SCT 177 (P&H) : 1990(2) Recent Service Judgments 760 the same question came up for consideration.
The grievance of Shri PL Goyal who was a member of the Haryana Superior Judicial Service was that from 111986 on promotion from Subordinate Judicial Service to Superior Judicial Service, his pay scale has been reduced.
This Court held that it is violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution and in paragraph 7 the following findings were arrived at :-  ``There is obviously anomaly in this and it not only causes hardship to the petitioner, it does not stand the test of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India and the reduction in the pay scale will have to be quashed to bring the members of the Superior Judicial Service at least at par with Selection Grade of the Subordinate Judicial Service, of course, with necessary direction to the State Government to refix the pay scale of the members of the Superior Judicial Service so that the discrimination and hardship caused to them is removed.''
No different was the view of Delhi High Court in the case T.R.L.
Narayanan and others v. Union of India and others, 1986 Lab.IC 1671.
It was concluded that when the pay scale applicable to the lower or feeder post for promotion to the next higher post are increased, the pay scale of higher post should also be revised.
The findings recorded were in paragraph 9 to be :-  ``The situation created by the revision of scales of pay of the three feeder posts and of the scales of pay of Members of the Higher Judicial Service and Delhi Judicial Service, unless remedied by higher scales of pay for the three posts, would suffer from the vice of arbitrariness and unreasonableness and be liable to be stuck down as violating Article 14 of the Constitution of India Union of India has not been able to show any valid reason for withholding its approval to the recommendations or delaying its approval to them which leads one to an irresistible conclusion that the Union of India are acting unreasonably in dealing with the matter, particularly, where the Delhi Administration has concurred to the recommendations and this Court has already agreed to the suggestion that in the case the scales are revised, there would be no objection if special pays, now being drawn by the incumbents, are not attached with the revised scales of pay.
The petitioners would, therefore, be entitled to a writ of mandamus directing the Union of India to accord their approval to the proposed revision of the scales of pay.''
In other words when the feeder post by virtue of the instructions of the Government of Haryana carries higher scale after 20 years of service, it is anomalous that higher post is carrying lower scale.
Balance must be struck.
The principle of equality should not be forgotten.
A reasonable view must be taken which seemingly had been forgotten.
If after 20 years the feeder post employees were in the scale of Rs. 2000-3200/-, there is total discrimination when higher post is in the pay scale of Rs. 1640-2900/-.
Consequently, the writ petition necessarily must be allowed.
For these reasons, we allow the petition and direct that the State Government within 3 months from today will refix the pay scale of the post of the petitioners which should not be in any case lower than the pay scale of feeder post.
It will take into consideration, the scale of the feeder post after 10 or 20 years of service of the persons working thereon.
The parties are left to bear their own costs.
Petition allowed.
