GS Singhvi, J - Having failed to secure sufficiently high marks so as to become eligible to be called for viva voce for recruitment as Assistant District Attorney in the Prosecution Department, Haryana, the petitioners have invoked writ jurisdiction of this Court under Articles 226 of the Constitution for invalidation of the decision taken by the Haryana Public Service Commission (for short the Commission) to hold screening test.
2.
Perusal of the record shows that after having received large number of applications (according to the learned counsel, the number of applications received in pursuance of the advertisement, Annexure P1, was more than 1500), the Commission decided to hold screening test.
Intimation of this decision was given to the candidates by public advertisements.
The petitioners appeared in the screening test along with other eligible candidates with a view to qualify for being called for interview.
However, as the ill-luck have it, they have secured only 43 and 44 marks respectively and their roll numbers do not figure in the list of successful candidates (Annexure P5).
3.
Shri Mahavir Sandhu assailed the holding of screening test on the ground that no indication of such test had been given in the advertisement and the corrigendum issued by the Commission and also on the ground that syllabus of the examination had not been notified in advance so as to enable the candidates to prepare for the examination.
4.
In our opinion, there is no substance in the argument of the learned counsel and the petition deserves to be dismissed summarily.
5.
The record of the case shows that the petitioner appeared in the screening test without making any representation or lodging a protest on the ground of lack of information regarding the contents of the syllabus and the nature of the examination or the shortage of time for preparation.
Rather, they took a chance with full knowledge of the fact that their failure to secure high marks in the screening test, may leave them out of the zone of consideration for the Commission to hold screening test only after they have found that they are not among the candidates who have been called for viva voce test.
In our considered view, the petitioners are estopped from challenging the legality of the screening test in view of the law laid down by the Supreme Court in M/s Panna Lal Binjraj v. Union of India, AIR 1957 SC 397; Manak Lal, Advocate v. Dr. Prem Chand Singhvi and others, AIR 1957 SC 425 and Madan Lal v. State of Jammu and Kashmir, 1998(1) RSJ 566 : AIR 1955 SC 1088.
6.
As far as the jurisdiction of the Commission to hold screening test is concerned, we do not find any merit in the contention of the learned counsel because the recruiting agency, like the Commission, has the power to devise appropriate mode for shortlisting the candidates to be called for viva voce.
In this case, the number of applications received for recruitment against 74 posts was over 1500 and it would have been wholly impracticable for the Commission to interview each and every candidate.
It may have consumed many weeks or even months before the Commission could determine the comparative merit of the candidates by holding interviews.
Therefore, its action of holding screening test cannot be declared illegal.
This is also the view taken by the Apex Court and this Court in the following decisions :-  (1) Jayant Kumar Chavhan v. Public Service Commission, MP Indore and another, 1979(1) SLR 316;  (2) Ashok Kumar Yadav and others v. State of Haryana and others, AIR 1987 SC 454;  (3) VJ Thomas and others v. Union of India and others, AIR 1985 SC 1055;  (4) SB Mathur and others v. Hon'ble the Chief Justice of Delhi High Court and others, AIR 1988 SC 2073;  (5) The Government of Andhra Pradesh v. P Dilip Kumar and another, JT 1993(2) SC 138 : 1993(2) SCT 432 (SC);  (6) Madhya Pradesh Public Service Commission v. Navnit Kumar Potdar and another, 1995(1) RSJ 142 : AIR 1995 SC 77 : 1995(1) SCT 50 (SC);  (7) Union of India and another v. T Sundararaman and others, JT 1997(5) SC 48 : 1997(2) SCT 810 (SC);  (8) Dr. Lovekesh Kumar and others v. State of Punjab and others, 1998(1) RSJ 566 (P&H).
For the reasons mentioned above, the writ petition is dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
