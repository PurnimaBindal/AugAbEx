GS Singhvi, J - This petition is directed against the order dated 27.11.1993 issued by the Superintendent of Police, Ambala for premature retirement of the petitioner.
2.
The petitioner joined service on 631959 as Constable in the erstwhile State of Punjab.
He was promoted as Head Constable on 12.6.1964.
On the formation of the State of Haryana, he opted for the service of that State and thus he became an employee of the State of Haryana with effect from 1111966.
He was promoted as Assistant Inspector on 28101977 and then as Sub Inspector on 24.2.1983.
He was confirmed on the post of ASI on 591984.
On 2051983 the petitioner was reverted from the post of Sub Inspector in view of judgment of this Court which was given in a writ petition filed by the direct recruit Assistant Sub Inspectors of Police.
The petitioner was once again promoted as Sub Inspector of Police vide order dated 14121989 (Annexure P-5).
After about 10 months a fresh order of his reversion was passed on 16101990.
Representation submitted by the petitioner against his reversion came to be rejected by the Government vide letter dated 2461993.
Thereafter, the petitioner filed C.W.P.
No. 11894 of 1993 and while admitting the writ petition the Court passed an order on 1691993 staying the reversion of the petitioner.
The petitioner has pleaded that he has got good service record.
He has earned several commendation certificates and cash awards and that some adverse reports have been made by the concerned authorities due to personal prejudices and ill-will.
Petitioner's contention is that on the basis of his good record of service the competent authority could not have formed an opinion for his premature retirement.
Petitioner has characterised the action of the respondents as arbitrary, capricious and whimsical.
3.
Respondents have pleaded that the competent authority has retired the petitioner on the basis of an overall evaluation of the service record of the petitioner which is tainted with several adverse reports, including some remarks doubting his integrity.
Respondents have asserted that for the years 1984-85 and 1985-86 the petitioner earned adverse reports and even though he was given promotion as Sub Inspector, the adverse entries did not get washed off.
Respondents have further pleaded that the petitioner was not found fit to be continued in service beyond the age of 55 years and, therefore, the impugned order has been passed.
4.
The main argument of the learned counsel for the petitioner is that the competent authority has not applied its mind to the record of the petitioner in an objective manner and it has retired the petitioner solely on the basis of two adverse reports of the years 1984-85 and 1985-86 without taking into consideration the overall good performance of the petitioner and the various commendation certificates and cash awards given to him.
The learned counsel argued that the adverse entries made in the annual confidential reports of the petitioner stood washed off on his promotion as Sub Inspector vide orders dated 24.2.1983 and 14121989.
Learned Counsel placed reliance on the decision of the Supreme Court in Brij Mohan Singh Chopra v. State of Punjab, 1987(2) S.L.R.
1 and KK Vaid v. State of Haryana, 1990(1) S.L.R.
1.
The learned Assistant Advocate-General argued that the action taken by the competent authority is founded on the opinion formed by it that the petitioner was not fit for retention in service beyond the age of 55 years and that in the absence of any mala fides, the Court cannot substitute its own opinion in place of the opinion formed by the competent authority.
She argued that even though the petitioner may have been promoted as Sub Inspector, previous adverse record cannot be treated as completely obliterated.
The learned Assistant Advocate-General argued that the competent authority was possessed with an absolute right to retire the petitioner after attaining the age of 55 years and none of the legal or fundamental rights of the petitioner has been violated.
5.
Service record of the petitioner has been produced before the Court by the learned Assistant Advocate-General.
This record shows that for the purpose of considering the question of extension of the petitioner's service beyond 55 years a note was prepared by the office of the Deputy Inspector General of Police indicating that as per the Government instructions only those employees are to be retained in service who have 70% good or better than 'average' service record during the last 10 years.
In the note it came to be recorded that the petitioner has earned 'B' grading during the years 1983-84, 1984-85, 1987-88, 1988-89, 1989-90, 1990-91, 1991-92, 1992-93 and 1993-94.
For the years 1985-86 he is shown to have earned 'A' grading.
It has also been recorded in the note that as per the service rules, the petitioner has earned 43 'good' entries as against 4 bad entries.
On this note, the Deputy Inspector General of Police recorded the following order :  "Not allowed to serve beyond 55 years."
This he did apparently on the basis of some instructions of the Government that only those officials should be allowed to continue beyond 55 years whose service record shows that 70% record is good or better than average for the last 10 years and whose integrity has never been doubted during the last 10 years.
The service record of the petitioner also shows that on 12101993 the Deputy Inspector General of Police, Ambala Range, Ambala Cantt.
passed an order allowing the petitioner to cross efficiency bar with effect from 141992 in the pay scale of Rs. 1320-2040.

8.
Now it is to be seen as to whether the decision taken by the respondents retiring the petitioner on attaining the age of 55 years suffers from any legal infirmity so as to warrant interference by this Court.
There can be no manner of doubt that the competent authority has the discretion to retire an employee after he attains a particular age or completes a particular period of qualifying service.
This decision is open to challenge in a Court of law on the grounds of violation of law, mala fides or patent arbitrariness.
Law on the subject of compulsory retirement has more or less been crystallised by various judgments of the Supreme Court and the Courts are to be guided by the principles laid down by the Supreme Court while deciding cases involving challenge to the order of premature retirement.
In Sh. Baikuntha Nath Dass and another v. Chief District Medical Officer, Baripada and another, 1992(2) SCT 92 (SC) : 1992(2) S.L.R.
2, their Lordships of the Supreme Court have made reference to their own judgments in Sham Lal v. State of Uttar Pradesh, 1955(1) SCR 26; Union of India v. JN Sinha, 1971(1) SCR 791; Brij Bihari Lal Agarwal v. High Court of Madhya Pradesh, 1980(3) SLR 583; Baldev Raj Chadha v. Union of India, 1980(3) SLR 1; Brij Mohan Singh Chopra v. State of Punjab (supra) and other decisions and have then culled down some principles.
In para 32 of the decision, the Supreme Court enumerated the various principles governing the issue of premature retirement.
This paragraph is quoted below :  "The following principles emerges from the above discussion :  (i) An order of compulsory retirement is not a punishment.
It implies no stigma nor any suggestion of misbehaviour.
(ii) The order has to be passed by the government on forming the opinion that it is in the public interest to retire a government servant compulsorily.
The order is passed on the subjective satisfaction of the government.
(iii) Principles of natural justice have no place in the context of an order of compulsory retirement.
This does not mean that judicial scrutiny is excluded altogether.
While the High Court or this Court would not examine the matter as an appellate court, they may interfere if they are satisfied that the order is passed (a) mala fide or (b) that it is based on no evidence or (c) that it is arbitrary in the sense that no reasonable person would form the requisite opinion on the given material; in short, if it is found to be a per verse order.
(iv) The Government (or the Review Committee, as the case may be) shall have to consider the entire record of service before taking a decision in the matter of course attaching more importance to record of and performance during the later years.
The record to be so considered would naturally include the entries in the confidential records/character rolls, both favourable and adverse.
If a government servant is promoted to a higher post notwithstanding the adverse remarks, such remarks lose their sting, more so, if the promotion is based upon merit (selection) and not upon seniority.
(v) An order of compulsory retirement is not liable to be quashed by a Court merely on the showing that while passing it uncommunicated adverse remarks were also taken into consideration.
That circumstance by itself cannot be a basis for interference."
The same view has been reiterated by a Full Bench of this Court in Daya Nand v. State of Haryana and another, 1995(1) SLR 57.
The Full Bench has over-ruled the decision of the Division Bench in KK Vaid's case (supra) in so far as it relates to the proposition that the circular issued by the Government of Haryana laying down the percentage of 70% Food reports as a condition percentage to the continuation of an employee in service beyond the age of 55 years is ultra vires to the provisions contained in rule 326(a) and (d) of the Punjab Civil Services Rules Volume I The Full Bench has held that the instructions issued by the Government are not inconsistent with the rules.
9.
Promotions to various cadres in the Police Service of the Government of Haryana are regulated by the provisions contained in the Punjab Police Rules, which have been adopted by the Government of Haryana.
Chapter XIII of the Punjab Police Rules deals with promotions.
Para 131 (1) of this Chapter reads as under :  "131 Promotion from one rank to another  (1) Promotion from one rank to another, and one grade to another in the same rank shall be made by selection tempered by seniority.
Efficiency and honesty shall be the main factors governing selection.
Specific qualifications, whether in the nature of training courses passed or practical experience, shall be carefully considered in each case.
When the qualifications of two officers are otherwise equal, the senior shall be promoted.
This rule does not affect increment within a time scale."
From the above, it is clear that promotions to the rank of Sub Inspector can be made only on the basis of merit-cum-seniority.
Efficiency and honesty of an employee are required to be taken into consideration before he is promoted to the higher rank.
Rule 918 of the Punjab Police Rules empowers the appointing authority to retire an employee after attaining the age of 55 years.
The note appearing below this rule shows that the power to effect retirement on attaining the age of 55 years is to be exercised only in public interest.
10.
In the light of the principles laid down by the Supreme Court and the rules referred to hereinabove, it is to be seen as to whether the competent authority applied its mind to the relevant considerations and then decided to retire the petitioner after attaining the age of 55 years.
Although the petitioner has not alleged mala fides against any particular officer of the department and, therefore, it is not required to be decided by this Court as to whether the impugned order suffers from malice in fact, the proceedings recorded by the office of the Deputy Inspector General of Police as well as the order passed by the said Officer show that the decision to retire the petitioner has been taken mechanically.
The competent officer thought that he was not required to make an evaluation of the service record of the petitioner in order to come to the conclusion as to whether his retention in service beyond 55 years of age would be in public interest or not.
One of the important features of the record of the petitioner's service which has not been taken into consideration by the competent authority is that after having earned adverse reports for the years 1984-85 and 1985-86 the petitioner was promoted as Sub Inspector because his name was approved by the Deputy Inspector General of Police and there is a prima facie presumption that this was done by the said authority after due application of mind to the record of the petitioner.
Indeed, in terms of rule 131 of the Punjab Police Rules, the petitioner could have been recommended for promotion and could have been promoted only after due consideration of his merit and seniority as well as efficiency and honesty.
The Deputy Inspector General of Police has also not taken note of the law laid down by the Supreme Court in Sh. Baikunth Nath Dass's case (supra) that if promotion is based on merit much of the sting of adverse reports is lost with such promotion.
The Deputy Inspector General of Police has also not taken into consideration the fact that the petitioner was allowed to cross efficiency bar with effect from 141992 vide order dated 12101993 and also the fact that after 1986 the petitioner has consistently earned good reports except the one in which his performance was described as 'average' by the Deputy Inspector General of Police (the Superintendent of Police had rated his performance as 'good').
Non-consideration of these important features of the service record of the petitioner as well as the principles of law laid down by the Supreme Court leads to a conclusion that the decision taken by the competent authority is not founded on an opinion formed after objective consideration of the record of the petitioner and it is well settled that an order of retirement passed without application of mind to the relevant record is legally unsustainable.
Consequently, the writ petition is allowed.
Order dated 27.11.1993 is declared illegal and is hereby quashed.
However, it is left open to the competent authority to make a fresh evaluation of the record of the petitioner and take a fresh decision on the issue of retention of the service after 55 years of age.
Parties are left to bear their own costs.
Petition allowed.
