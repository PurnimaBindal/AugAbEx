RS Mongia, J - This judgment will dispose of L.P.A. No. 1354 of 1988 and L.P.A. No. 1355 of 1988.
L.P.A. No. 1354 of 1988 is against the judgment in C.W.P.
No. 6133 of 1988, whereas L.P.A. 1355 of 1988 is against the judgment in C.W.P.
No. 6705 of 1980.
Both these writ petitions were disposed of by observing "same order as in C.W.P.
No. 5876 of 1988".
2.
The main judgment was written in C.W.P.
No. 5876 of 1988.
Though three writ petitions were decided against the State on the same point by the learned Single Judge, yet for the reasons best known to it, no appeal has been filed against the judgment in C.W.P.
No. 5876 of 1988.
3.
The writ petitioners (now respondents) in the present appeal had passed their BDS Course from the Dental College, Medical College and Hospital, Rohtak.
According to them, they were admitted to the BDS course of duration of four years in the year 1984.
There were in all 21 students who were admitted.
It is provided in the Dental Council of India Regulations framed by the Central Government under the Dentists Act, 1948, that after completion of four years of BDS Course, a paid house job training would be given to 50% candidates of the total admissions made in the batch of a particular year session.
The relevant regulation is reproduced below :-  PAID HOUSE SURGEONS  (i) Not less then 50% of the number of admissions and each of minimum 6 months' duration.
4.
The grievance of the petitioners was that out of the 21 students admitted to the BDS Course in the year 1984, only six candidates had been given the junior paid house job, which is less than 50% quota as per the Regulation, quoted above.
They prayed that they should be deputed for the said paid course.
5.
In the year 1981, 36 candidates were admitted to the BDS Course and only 12 candidates were given the junior paid house job, which was less than 50% of the admissions.
Some of the candidates who were within the range of 50% quota approached this Court by means of C.W.P.
No. 3575, with a prayer that they should be deputed for the junior paid house job.
This writ petition was allowed on 23rd September, 1985.
6.
In the writ petition, against which the present Letters Patent Appeal has been filed by the State no written statement had been filed on its behalf.
The learned Single Judge allowed the writ petition by observing that the case is squarely covered by a decision of this Court in C.W.P.
No. 3575 of 1985 (Dr. Gurmeet Singh and others v. The State of Haryana and others), decided on 23rd September, 1985, in which it was held as under :-  "Thus, on the true interpretation of Regulation No. III, it is incumbent upon the respondents to give the paid House Job training to at least 17 of the students admitted in 1981.
It is, therefore, clear that the petitioners have a legal right to get the House Job training as claimed by them and this right has been illegally withheld from them.
For these reasons, the respondents are directed to give the paid House Job training to the petitioners, during the course being held from July 1, 1985 to December 31, 1985.
Accordingly, a direction was given by the learned Single Judge that the writ petitioners (now respondents) should be imparted the paid House Job training which they had already stated undergoing till 31st December, 1988 and they be paid their arrears of stipends admissible to them.
7.
The learned counsel appearing for the appellants has not been able to persuade us to take a different view that what the learned Single Judge in C.W.P.
No. 3575 of 1985.
We find that the learned Single Judge was correct in interpreting the Regulation, quoted above, and giving the necessary directions in favour of the writ petitioners for being imparted the requisite paid House Job training.
8.
For the reasons recorded above, we find no merit in this appeal, which is hereby dismissed.
However, the parties are left to bear their own costs.
Appeal dismissed.
