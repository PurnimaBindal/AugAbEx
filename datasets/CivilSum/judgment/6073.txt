MM Kumar, J (Oral) - This order shall dispose of Civil Revision Nos. 1043 of 1987, 1265 of 1987 and 1874 of 1987 as common question of facts and law are involved.
However, the facts are taken from Civil Revision No. 1043 of 1987.
2.
This petition filed under Article 227 of the Constitution prays for setting aside order dated 20.8.1986, passed by the learned Additional District Judge, Jalandhar, while dismissing appeal of the petitioner Department by upholding the order dated 261986, passed by the Authority constituted under the Payment of Wages Act, 1936.
According to the order dated 261986, passed by the Authority under the Payment of Wages Act, 1936, the order imposing punishment of stoppage of one annual grade increment with cumulative effect, passed by the petitioner Department, dated 1391978 and 9111981 were held to be illegal as those orders were non-speaking and cryptic.
The order of the Authority has been upheld on appeal by the learned Additional District Judge, by observing as under :-  "9.
I have gone through the orders dated 1391978 and 9111981 contained in the enquiry files produced on the record of this case.
The perusal of the same would show that these orders do not show as to what were the allegations against the applicant, what was his reply to the same and on what grounds the punishing Authority arrived at a conclusion that the reply submitted by the applicant to the show cause notices, was not satisfactory.
In the absence of this material, these orders cannot be termed as speaking ones.
To my mind the learned Authority was right to place its reliance on the Authority reported as 1968 SLR 792 Short Note and the learned Govt. pleader has not been able to cite any authority to the contrary.
He also could not show me as to how the application is barred by limitation qua such an order which has no existence in the eye of law.
The findings of the learned Authority on issue No. 1, are kept intact."
3.
Mr. Satish Bhanot, learned State counsel has argued that the Authority under Section 15(2) of the Payment of Wages Act, 1936, has no jurisdiction to set aside the orders passed by the punishing authority in pursuance to initiation of either disciplinary proceedings or show cause notice.
According to the learned counsel, such orders could be challenged before the Civil Court alone or the jurisdiction of this Court under Article 226 of the Constitution could be invoked.
In support of his submission, learned counsel has placed reliance on an order passed by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in Civil Appeal No. 5950 of 1994, decided on 1101997 (State of Punjab and others v. Baldev Singh), holding that the Authority constituted under the Payment of Wages Act, 1936, has no jurisdiction to interfere with the order passed by the disciplinary authority against the employee concerned and such orders cannot be set aside by the Authority.
4.
I have thoughtfully considered the submissions made by the learned counsel and am of the view that this petition is liable to be dismissed because the order No. 5663, dated 1391978 and order No. 2742, dated 9111981, stopping one increment of the workman-respondent with cumulative effect cannot be sustained in the eyes of law because such like order cannot be passed without holding a regular departmental inquiry.
It is not disputed that the aforementioned two orders were passed by the punishing authority after issuing show cause notice to the workman-respondent and without holding any regular departmental enquiry.
In the case of Kulwant Singh Gill v. State of Punjab, 1991(2) S.C.T.
30 : 1991 Supp (1) SCC 504, it has been categorically held by the Hon'ble Supreme Court that stoppage of increment(s) with cumulative effect is a major penalty and it would call for holding of a regular departmental inquiry.
Even otherwise order dated 5663/PMA, dated 1391978 is a cryptic and non-speaking order and the same is extracted below for facility of reference :-  "Shri Dildar Singh Dr. No. 262 was served with a show cause Notice No. 13835/WC1, dated 681978 causing the damage of Bus No. PUX-5703.
The reply submitted by the Official was considered and found unsatisfactory.
His one increment with C/effect is hereby stopped."
The order dated 9111981, which is available at page 53 of the record is almost identical to the above extracted order and these are non-speaking orders.
The law laid down by this Court in the case of Ram Dass Chaudhary v. State of Punjab, 1968 SLR 792, can safely be relied upon in the present case.
The aforementioned principle has been repeatedly applied by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in numerous judgments including Bhagat Ram Patanga v. State of Punjab, AIR 1972 SC 1571; Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India, AIR 1978 SC 597 and Mohinder Singh Gill v. Chief Election Commissioner, AIR 1978 SC 851.
5.
It is true that the Authority constituted under the Payment of Wages Act, 1936, is not competent to interfere with the orders passed by the punishing authorities and the orders impugned in this case could have been set aside on that score alone as has been held by their Lordships in the case of Baldev Singh (supra).
However, if the orders are not sustainable on merit and do not stand judicial scrutiny then such order are liable to be set aside even otherwise.
It is well settled that the basic purpose of exercising jurisdiction under Article 227 of the Constitution is to advance justice, not to thwart it and, therefore, the jurisdiction even under Article 227 could be exercised for setting aside the orders dated 1391978 and 9111981.
For the aforementioned proposition, reliance could be placed on judgments of the Hon'ble Supreme Court in the case of Roshan Deen v. Preeti Lal, 2002(2) SCT 106 (SC) : 2002(1) SCC 100 and Surya Dev Rai v. Ram Chander Rai and others, 2004(1) RCR(Civil) 147 (SC) : 2003(6) SCC 675.
While upholding the objection of the petitioner department with regard to lack of jurisdiction of the Authority constituted under the Payment of Wages Act, 1936, I find that the order No. 5663, dated 1391978 and order No. 2742, dated 9111981 are unsustainable in the eyes of law.
Therefore, the orders are set aside in exercise of supervisory jurisdiction of this Court under Article 227 of the Constitution.
In view of the above, the net result remains the same.
Accordingly, the orders dated 1391978 and 9111981 are set aside by holding that the Authority constituted under the Payment of Wages Act, 1936, had no jurisdiction to entertain a petition under Section 15(2) of the Payment of Wages Act, 1936.
This petition stand disposed of in the above terms.
Order accordingly.
