Tapen Sen, J. (Oral) - Heard the learned counsel for the Parties.
2.
The Petitioner was initially appointed on the post of a Clerk on 581976 but on account of his experience, as also being a graduate in the subject of Economics, he was promoted on the post of investigator by the Chief Executive officer, District Rural Development Agency, Ambala (for short, "the DRDA") on 23.5.1989 in the pay-scale of Rs. 1400-2600/-.
3.
On 591990, by Annexure P-3, the Additional Deputy Commissioner-cum-Chief Executive Officer of the DRDA issued on office order by reason whereof the petitioner was granted revised pay-scale of Rs. 1600-2660 with effect from 151990 as per the Haryana Government Notification No. 6/23/3PR (ID)-88 dated 2381990.
That notification is Annexure P-12 appended to the instant writ petition and begins at running page 41.
The revised scales have been marked as Annexure-A and the same is appended to the said Notification as contained in Annexure P-12.
From a perusal thereof, it is seen that it reads thus :  Sr. No. Name of the Deptt.

Nothing was done in respect of the post of an Investigator.
4.
According to the petitioner, the aforementioned scale of Rs. 1600-2600 was subsequently revised and the substituted scale became Rs. 5450-8000 and his pay was fixed at Rs. 5900.
This was done vide Annexure P-2.
The aforementioned pay-scale was made effective from 111996.
According to the petitioner, the respondents should have, therefore, given him the said pay-scale of Rs. 5450- 8000 and not Rs. 5000-7850.
It appears that subsequently on 2312004 the Respondents refused to grant him the scale of Rs. 5450-8000 and made an observation to the effect that the amount that has been paid to him was erroneous and the Department should be informed after making recovery of the higher pay-scale granted to the petitioner.
It is based on the aforementioned Order dated 2312004 (Annexure P-1) that the petitioner has filed this writ petition praying that the same be quashed and that a writ of mandamus be issued directing the respondents to restore his grade already granted to him.
Learned counsel makes this submission stating that since the petitioner, vide Annexure P-3, had been granted the scale of Rs. 1600-2600, the grade, therefore, which was lawfully payable would be Rs. 5450-8000/-.
5.
It is noticed that the impugned Order, as contained in Annexure P-1, has been passed on the basis of the Rules of 2001.
However, what is relevant to be taken note of is that at the time when the relevant promotion was given, the 2001 Rules were not in force.
At that time the 1991 Rules held the field.
Mr. Yaswinder Singh, learned counsel appearing for respondent No. 3 has produced a copy of the 1991 Rules for the perusal of this Court.
Let it be kept on record.
It is seen that the Schedule A thereto contains a specific distinct post of Investigator and also a specific distinct post of a Statistical Assistant.
Both carried the same pay-scale of Rs. 1400-2600.
However, it was only in the 2001 Rules that the posts of Statistical Assistant and Investigator were made equivalent by Schedule `A' appended to Annexure P-7.
Upon perusal of the same it is evident that for the first time in the year 2001, the posts of Statistical Assistant/Investigator who fulfilled the prescribed qualification as per Schedule `B' therein were entitled to be paid the scale of Rs. 5450-8000.
The petitioner evidently qualified as he was a graduate in Economics and the qualification creiteria has also been brought on record vide Annexure P-8 (relevant at running page 32).
Thus, it is evident that the scale of Rs. 5450-8000 came to be paid both to the Statistical Assistant as also to an Investigator only with effect from 2001 but prior to the same, the scale of an Investigator was not Rs. 1600-2660 (substituted scale being Rs. 5450-8000) but it was Rs. 1400-2600 (the substituted scale of which was Rs. 5000-7850).
6.
In this context, it is relevant to notice that on 2381990 (Annexure P- 12), the State of Haryana had actually revised only the pay-scale of Statistical Assistants and not of the Investigators.
Evidently, therefore, by applying the Notification dated 2381990 in Annexure P-3, the respondents acted incorrectly because the said scale could, at the relevant time, only be given to a Statistical Assistant.
This would be evident from reading Annexure `A' appended to the letter dated 2381990 which has been brought on record by the petitioner vide Annexure P-12 and the relevant portion is at running page 45 thereto.
7.
At this juncture this Court takes notice of another letter dated 1231999 which has been referred to in Annexure P-1.
The said letter dated 1231999 is Annexure P-4.
This letter, written by the Financial Commissioner & Secretary to the Government of Haryana, is addressed to all Additional Deputy Commissioners-cum-Executive Officers, DRDA in the Haryana State and upon perusal thereof, it is evident that it deals with the revision of pay-scales for the employees directly recruited in the DRDA in the State of Haryana.
It is evident that the Government, after careful consideration, decided to revise the pay-scale of the employees of the DRDA and accordingly, the details of the revision of the pay-scales of the employees of the DRDA were mentioned in Annexures `A' and `B' appended thereto.
Strangely, however, these Annexures `A' and `B' are not appended to Annexure P-4 and instead only Annexure-C has been brought on record.
Mr. MS Sindhu, learned D.A.G, Haryana has produced for perusal of this Court the original letter dated 1231999 which contains Annexures `A' and `B'.
Upon perusal of the same, it is evident that the pay-scale of Investigators was revised from Rs. 1400-2600 to Rs. 5000-7850 and not Rs. 5450-8000 as has been claimed by the petitioner.
Mr. Sindhu is directed to file a photo copy of the aforementioned document/letter dated 1231999 in its original along with the Annexures thereto shall be kept in the records of this case.
8.
Thus, for all practical purposes, it is seen that in fact, the basis of the claim of the petitioner being Annexure P-3 was, in its turn, based on the Notification dated 2381990 which, as already taken note of, could not have been applied to the petitioner who, at that stage was an Investigator and not a Statistical Assistant.
The Notification dated 2381990 was certainly a revision of the pay-scales from Rs. 1400-2600 to Rs. 1600-2660 but this was confined only to Statistical Assistants and not to Investigators.
Thus, the respondents, by passing the impugned order cannot be said to have acted illegally or to have acted in an irregular manner in so far as the petitioner is concerned notwithstanding a statement having been made in the Replication to the effect that other persons working in Gurgaon are being paid Rs. 5450- 8000.
This Court will not make any comments on the aforementioned statement made by the petitioner in the Replication, save and except to record that if something has been wrongly done by the respondents in an illegal and/or irregular manner, the same should not receive the approval of this Court.
This Court leaves the matter entirely upon the State of Haryana to deal with this issue and if such Investigators are actually being paid a scale which was not supposed to have been given to them, it is expected that the authorities concerned will do the needful strictly in accordance with law after giving adequate opportunity of hearing to all such persons.
9.
However, this Court frowns upon the proposed action threatened to be taken against the petitioner and which is writ large in the impugned order itself.
It is the threat of recovery.
In this context, it would be relevant to notice what the Joint Director has specifically stated in Annexure P-1.
He clearly says that the " `DRDA' has violated the service rules by upholding wrongly the post of Investigator equivalent to Statistical Assistant, Economics and Statistical Department, Haryana and by granting pay scale of Rs. 1600-2600 SISC without approval and acceptance, instead of granting the pay-scale of Rs. 1400-2600 of Investigators of the DRDA" [SIC.
Emphasis however added]  10.
From the entire facts and circumstances that have been taken into consideration and from the facts and records pleaded and brought on record, neither the Respondents have been able to establish nor does it appear to this Court that any point of time, it was the petitioner who was at fault in getting the higher/upgraded scale.
If the upgraded pay-scale has been given to him by the DRDA in violation of Rules as stated by the Joint Director, it cannot be said that there was any mis-representation or fraud played by the petitioner in getting the said upgraded pay-scale.
On the other hand, Annexure P-1 is clear pointer to the fact that it was the DRDA itself which gave the higher pay-scale to the petitioner.
Following the ratio of the law laid by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in Sahib Ram v. State of Haryana, 1995(1) SCT 668 (SC), this Court holds that in view of the fact that there was neither any mis-representation nor any fraud played by the petitioner, any amount paid to him being in the nature of higher pay/up-graded pay-scale, should not be allowed to be recovered from the petitioner by the respondents.
It is another matter that the respondents may make their own enquiry at their own level to find out the person/persons who was/were responsible in granting the said higher pay-scale to the petitioner than his entitlement and fix responsibility in accordance with law but for the mistake or negligence of the officer or authority or any employee, the petitioner, by no stretch of imagination, should be subjected to the status of recovery of the amount already paid to him on account of his having received a higher/up-graded pay-scale.
This would be all the more appropriate taking into consideration that even though the two posts were distinct in 1991, the Government did ultimately decide to make the two equivalent as a result of which, in the year 2001 the post of Statistical Assistant became at par with the post of an Investigator.
It was thus a mere paper segregation which ultimately culminated in the combination of the two posts as being equivalent.
Such are the affairs of the Government but for such paper irregularities and/or rules which were in existence at a particular point of time having a different colour and hue and which takes a different shape at a different point of time, the same should not be allowed to come in the way of the petitioner seeking equity before a Court of law under Article 226 of the Constitution.
11.
For the foregoing reasons, this writ petition is partly allowed and the impugned order in so far as it gives out a threat of recovery is quashed.
Liberty, however, is given to the State-respondents to initiate enquiry if they so desire at their own level to find out and fix responsibility upon the person/persons who was/were negligent and responsible for giving higher/up- graded pay-scale to the petitioner, but the said enquiry should not be at the peril and prejudice of the petitioner.
Lastly, it goes without saying that on or from the coming into force of the 2001 rules, the same should now be taken into consideration while fixing the pay-scale of the petitioner.
Since this Court has already held that the petitioner's pay-scale at the relevant time should have been Rs. 5000-7850 and taking that into consideration that since the respondents had fixed his pay at Rs. 5,900/- with effect 111996 together with increments etc., it is not known as to how and under what circumstances the respondent authorities, even after passing of an interim Order by this Court, have reduced his pay scale to Rs. 5,300/- as stated by the learned counsel during the course of arguments.
As a consequence of this judgment, the respondents are directed to immediately and forthwith look into this aspect also and do the needful in accordance with law.
Writ petition disposed of.
No order as to costs.
Petition partly allowed.
