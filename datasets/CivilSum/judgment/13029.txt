Tapen Sen, J - In this writ petition, the petitioner has prayed for quashing the Order dated 592003 (Annexure P-11), purporting to have been issued under the seal and signature of the Commissioner-cum-Secretary, Government of Haryana, Department of Education, Chandigarh whereby and whereunder the petitioner was informed that she will be deemed to have been retired from government service w.e.f.
3062003 on attaining 58 years of age.
The petitioner also prays for quashing the Order dated 23122003 (Annexure P-21) purporting to be under the seal and signature of the Director, Secondary Education, Government of Haryana, Chandigarh, whereby and whereunder he came to conclusion that the petitioner, being the head of the Institution, had concealed facts relating to results and therefore, she was not entitled to receive the State Award which was liable to be withdrawn and therefore, the action that was proposed to have been taken in the earlier show-cause notice dated 2452002 was confirmed.
The petitioner also prays for an appropriate direction upon the respondents to allow her to retain the Award and the consequential benefits enuring to her credit under the Award.
It is not necessary to go into the service details of the petitioner, save and except to record that the petitioner was appointed as a Head Mistress as a direct recruit on 631989 after having been selected by the Haryana Public Service Commission.
In paragraph 3, the petitioner has stated that her work was highly appreciated by her superiors as also by the society at large.
She was conferred several honours by the authorities as also by several social and cultural organizations connected with the field of education.
She has also stated that she has more than 100 commendation letters and certificates which she has received from time to time over the years.
The petitioner has brought on record these certificates and letters vide Annexures P-1/1 to P-1/17.
The statements made in paragraph 3 have been replied by the respondents in their counter affidavit as being matters of record.
In other words, the respondents have not denied the fact that the petitioner was highly appreciated by her superiors as also by the society at large and that she was conferred several honours by higher authorities as also by several social and cultural organizations connected with the field of education and that she has more than 100 such commendation letters and certificates received from time to time over the years.
2.
In paragraph 4, the writ petitioner has stated that she has an excellent service record and was considered for conferment of the most prestigious honour in the field of education which is a rare achievement for any teacher.
She has also stated that on the basis of her exemplary and meritorious performance and records, her name was recommended for State Award for the year 1999-2000.
This paragraph has also been answered in the written statement as being matters of record.
In other words, there is no denial of what the petitioner has stated.
3.
In paragraph 5 of the writ petition, the petitioner has also stated that on 1682000, she was informed by Annexure P-2 that she had been selected for the State Teachers Award.
This information came to the petitioner vide letter dated 1682000 of the Director, Secondary Education, Haryana.
It was through this letter that the petitioner was further informed that she would be presented the State Award on 592000 by His Excellency, the Governor of Haryana at the Governor's House in Chandigarh and therefore, she was to report to the Assistant Director, Public Relations at 200 pm on 492000.
After the aforementioned letter had been received, the petitioner was then congratulated by the Director, Secondary Education by a personal letter dated 2282000 as contained in Annexure P-3 congratulating her for the distinction conferred on her for her meritorious service.
Pursuant to the aforementioned developments, the petitioner received the Award on 592000 and the benefits were as follows :-  (a) Cash Award of Rs. 5,000/-; (b) Silver Medal - One in number; (c) Certificate; (d) Two annual increments for 5 years; (e) Extension in service by two years; and (f) One Shawl.
4.
In support of the aforementioned contentions, the petitioner has relied on Annexures P-5 and P-6 which are extracts of the Service Book of the petitioner.
Annexure P-5 appears to be a declaration by the Financial Commissioner issued on 592000 itself declaring that the petitioner was honoured with the State Teachers' Award for admirable services in the field of the Development of Study and Education.
5.
As per the Award, the petitioner got an extension of two years in service and therefore, against her normal date of superannuation on 3062003, she should have continued till 3062005 but instead of allowing her the benefit of the aforementioned extended period of service, the Director, Secondary Education issued a letter on 2452002 (Annexure P-7) by which he made an allegation to the effect that in order to get the aforementioned Award, the petitioner had given incorrect details relating to examinations held in the year 1996-97 which were found to be 1737 (minus) and therefore by showing 191 (+), the petitioner had concealed true facts.
Thus, the Director, Secondary Education alleged that she had cheated the Department and had obtained the Award in an inappropriate manner and therefore, before the benefits were withdrawn from her, an opportunity was given to explain as to why these benefits should be not taken away.
She was given 25 days to file an explanation.
The petitioner has stated in paragraph 12 that the letter dated 2452002 was actually initiated on the basis of a complaint that was sent against her containing false allegations in relation to the petitioner.
This statement, made in Paragraph 12, has been replied by the respondents also in paragraph 12 of written statement wherein they have stated that a complaint was received against the petitioner and after investigating the matter, it was proved that the petitioner has produced false results and therefore, the Award was withdrawn after giving opportunity to the petitioner.
6.
It is evident from the pleadings that as soon as the petitioner received the aforementioned letter dated 2452002 as contained in Annexure P-7, she sent a reply on 1072002 requesting the Director that a photo copy of the complaint should be sent to her so that she could give a detailed reply.
This letter sent by the petitioner on 1072002 is Annexure P-8.
The petitioner has stated that the complaint was never given to her whereafter she made a request on 25102002 vide Annexure P-10 and instead of furnishing the complaint to the petitioner, the respondents straightaway passed the impugned order on 592003 vide Annexure P-11 whereby and whereunder she was made to superannuate w.e.f.
3062003.
7.
The petitioner appears to have challenged the aforementioned letter dated 592003 before this Court vide C.W.P.
No. 16993 of 2003.
By an Order dated 682004 a Division Bench dismissed the Writ Petition as withdrawn making it clear that the respondents would not recover the amount of salary and allowances paid to the petitioner in lieu of her having worked upto 592003 i.e. the date on which the impugned Order was passed.
The aforementioned prayer for withdrawal of the Writ Petition appears to have been made on account of the fact that in the meantime, on 23122003, the respondents had passed another Order (Annexure P-21) whereby and whereunder, the action proposed to be taken by the aforementioned show-cause notice dated 2452002 was confirmed.
In other words, by Order dated 23122003, the benefits given to the petitioner under the Award were withdrawn.
The respondents have themselves stated in paragraph 1 of the written statement that the Writ Petition was withdrawn on account of the statement of the counsel for the petitioner that the petitioner may be allowed to withdraw the Writ Petition with liberty to challenge the order dated 23122003.
8.
It is in that context, that the present Writ Petition has now been filed challenging both the orders.
The learned counsel appearing for the State drew attention to the portion appearing just after the Index against Column No. 4 where an earlier Writ Petition namely, C.W.P.
No. 16993 of 2003 has been referred to.
Upon a perusal of the said portion, it appeared that only a part of the order passed in the earlier Writ Petition had been quoted and it appeared that in that Writ Petition, the petitioner had challenged the Order dated 592003.
Prima facie, therefore, it appeared to this Court that this second Writ Petition qua the Order dated 592003 is barred under the principles of res judicata.
Consequently, records of C.W.P.
No. 16993 of 2003 were called for and from the Order dated 682004, it is evident that the other portion thereof reads as follows :-  "Ms. Sanjivni Gupta, learned counsel for the petitioner says that her client may be permitted to withdraw the writ petition with liberty to file fresh one so as to enable her to challenge order dated 23122003 in addition to order dated 592003.
Ms. Ritu Bahri, learned Deputy Advocate General says that she does not have any objection.
In view of the above, the writ petition is dismissed as withdrawn with liberty in terms of the prayer made.
While disposing of the writ petition in the manner indicated above, we make it clear that the respondents shall not recover the amount of salary and allowances paid to the petitioner in lieu of her having worked up to 592003."
Thus, in view of the observations made by the earlier Division Bench, it cannot be said that this Writ Petition is barred under the principles of res judicata in so far as it relates to the Order dated 592003.
9.
Now coming back to the merits of the case, it is evident that after the aforementioned Order had been passed on 592003, the petitioner was made to superannuate on having reached the age of 58 years on 3062003 but because of the interim order granted in the earlier Writ Petition, the respondents have not recovered the salary or allowances paid to her for the period she was worked upto 592003.
However, by letter dated 892003 i.e. three days after passing of the impugned Order dated 592003, District Primary Education Officer, Hisar wrote a letter to the petitioner informing her that as per telephonic message received from the Personal Assistant to the Director, Primary Education, Haryana, the petitioner should appear in the said Office for personal hearing on 1092003.
The petitioner has stated that in the meantime after having received the letter dated 592003, she was so "traumatised" that she had to be hospitalised on 792003 because she could not tolerate the false insinuations being imputed against her.
It was thereafter that the respondents issued the letter dated 892003 which she received while she was interned in the hospital.
On 1092003, the petitioner sent a letter dated 1192003 (Annexure P-15) by fax requesting that since she was in hospital, 15 days' time be given to her to appear.
Thereafter, on 2292003, the petitioner sent a detailed reply wherein she categorically explained, in extenso, that the allegations against her were absolutely baseless and the complaint on the basis of which she had been insulted and harmed, were at the instance of some mischievous elements.
She also denied having given wrong evaluations and explained the different possibilities of evaluation.
This letter is dated 2292003 and on the same day, the enquiry officer handed over to her the pseudonymous complaint when she appeared before the enquiry officer.
However, a copy of this complaint was never given to the petitioner earlier as has been stated in paragraph 24 of the writ petition.
The written statement is silent on the statements made by the petitioner in Paragraph 24 and the only reply that they have given is in Paragraph 24 of the written statement and it reads thus :-  "That in reply to para 25 of the petition it is submitted that as and the petitioner asked for the copy of the complaint that was made available to her........" (sic) 10.
In fact in paragraph 24, the petitioner has stated that there was no enquiry in the eyes of law because she was asked to appear on 2292003 vide letter dated 16.9.2003 without supplying the copy of the complaint which was supplied to her only when she appeared on 2292003.
She has also stated that in the enquiry proceedings, the petitioner was not confronted with any document or witnesses of the Department and that she was not given any opportunity to cross-examine any witnesses.
It is evident that after the aforementioned reply had been submitted by the petitioner on 2292003 vide Annexure P-17, the respondents did not do much in the matter thereafter save and except that on 23122003, they passed the second impugned order (Annexure P-21) by which the Director, Secondary Education confirmed the action that was proposed to be taken in the show-cause notice - meaning thereby the withdrawal of facilities under the Award.
Surprisingly, the Director, Secondary Education has not even referred to the reply of the petitioner filed on 2292003 vide Annexure P-17.
That apart, there is also another explanation that appears to have been given by the petitioner before the enquiry officer on 26.9.2003 which has been marked Annexure P-19.
In this letter, the petitioner gave para-wise reply to the allegations made against her but strangely the Director, Secondary Education has not even bothered to touch the explanations given by the petitioner.
That apart, it is evident that the respondents passed the order superannuating the petitioner on 592003 by Annexure P-11 and it was thereafter that they issued a letter on 892003 asking the petitioner to be present for personal hearing on 1092003.
The entire exercise therefore is an eye wash and suffers from complete non-application of mind.
11.
For the foregoing reasons, this Court is also of the view that there has been gross violation of the principles of natural justice in holding the petitioner guilty merely on the basis of a complaint without there being a finding of guilt in a properly constituted enquiry set up strictly in accordance with law.
Moreover, when the petitioner came out with explanations which appear to be such that they ought to have been considered, the respondents had no right to have ignored these explanations without so much as even bothering to refer to them in the final Order of punishment.
For the foregoing reasons, this Court is of the view that the impugned Orders dated 592003 as well as 23122003 ought to be set aside.
Accordingly, these Orders are quashed and the matter is remanded to the appropriate authority to pass a fresh Order after initiating the matter de novo strictly in accordance with law.
The Writ Petition is accordingly allowed.
Petition allowed.
