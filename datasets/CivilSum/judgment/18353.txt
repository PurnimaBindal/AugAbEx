Ashutosh Mohunta, J. (Oral) - The plaintiff-respondent was promoted from the post of Sub-Divisional Clerk to Accounts Clerk on 13.12.1979.
Accordingly, his pay was fixed by the Accounts Officer, Irrigation Department, Chandigarh, at Rs. 620/- in the pay-scale of Rs. 525-1050 on 141980.
The plaintiff continued to draw his salary in the pay-scale of Rs. 525-1050.
However, in the year 1997 ie after a gap of 17 years, the State suddenly realised that the pay of the plaintiff/respondent has wrongly been fixed and actually the pay of the plaintiff should have been fixed at Rs. 600/- in the same pay-scale.
As according to the State the pay had wrongly been fixed, therefore, recovery proceedings were initiated.
The plaintiff filed a suit for declaration that the order dated 3131997 passed by the defendants/appellant-State is wholly illegal as no show-cause notice was given to him before effecting recovery.
It was further contended that the pay of the appellant was correctly fixed on 141980.
2.
The suit of the plaintiff was decreed vide judgment dated 13.12.2001 passed by the Civil Judge (Jr.
Divn.
), Hisar, and it was held that the order dated 3131997 for recovery from the plaintiff is illegal.
The State filed an appeal before the Additional District Judge, Hisar, who vide his judgment dated 6.9.2002 dismissed the appeal.
3.
Mr. Hooda, learned counsel for the respondent has contended that as no show-cause notice was given to the plaintiff/respondent before effecting the recovery from the plaintiff/respondent yet once it comes to the notice of the State that the pay of an employee has been wrongly fixed then the State is empowered to effect recovery.
4.
On the other hand, counsel for the respondent has contended that as no show cause notice was given to the plaintiff/respondent before effecting any recovery, hence, the orders of recovery have rightly been held to be bad.
Learned counsel relies on the judgment in Bhagwan Shukla v Union of India and others, 1994(4) SCT 406 (SC) : JT 1994(5) SC 253, wherein it has been held that reduction in pay on an employee without giving him an opportunity of being heard has to be set aside as such an order is in violation of the principles of natural justice.
Learned counsel further relies on Union of India v. M Bhaskar and others, 1996(4) SCT 57 (SC), wherein it has been held that "if an employee was wrongly given a pay-scale by the authorities but later on he was held to be not entitled to the same, amount already paid on account of pay-scale given wrongly should not be recovered because it would cause hardship to the concerned employee".
In the present case DW-1 Saya Parkash SDO has admitted in his cross-examination that before recovery of the impugned amount from the respondent no show-cause notice was given to him.
5.
In view of the above, the Courts below have rightly set aside the order dated 3131997 by which recovery was sought to the effected from the respondent.
I find no merit in the present appeal and the same is dismissed.
Appeal dismissed.
