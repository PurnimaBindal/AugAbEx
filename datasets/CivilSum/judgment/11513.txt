Amar Dutt, J - This defendants' appeal seeks to challenge the judgment and decree dated 20th October, 1983 passed by the Additional District Judge, Patiala, reversing the judgment and decree of the Senior Subordinate Judge, Patiala dated 331982.
2.
Sarup Singh, who working as an officiating C Class Tehsildar, had filed a suit for declaration challenging the validity of an order passed on 991975 by the Financial Commissioner, Revenue, Punjab, Chandigarh, (hereinafter referred to as "the FCR") effecting his premature retirement from 30th of September, 1975, as illegal, null, void, arbitrary, malafide and without jurisdiction.
According to him, he had joined the Revenue department as a Naib Tehsildar candidate in the year 1947 and on completion of training was confirmed against the said post on 991948.
After considering his service as satisfactory, he was promoted as a Tehsildar, Municipal Committee, Jalandhar by the Commissioner, Jalandhar Division on 991969.
He worked satisfactorily as Tehsildar at different stations until 991975, when the impugned order was passed by the FCR ordering his premature retirement in public interest in pursuance of Rule 3(1)(a) of the Punjab Civil Services (Pre-mature Retirement) Rules, 1975 (hereinafter referred to as "the Rules").
The representation submitted by the plaintiff on 26.9.1975 was rejected vide order dated 26111976.
This order was conveyed to him vide letter dated 2451977.
Thereupon, he made another representation dated 571977 to the Revenue Minister and sent a reminder on 2231978.
The plaintiff asserted that he had worked at various stations in the revenue department and there was no complaint against his integrity.
A false case, however, was registered against him vide FIR No. 545 dated 20th of April, 1971 under Section 5(2) of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1947 (hereinafter referred to as "the 1947 Act"), in Police Station, Jalandhar at the instance of local politicians.
He was tried and acquitted by the Special Judge, Jalandhar, on 2181972.
He was then reinstated and posted as Tehsildar.
He had filed a suit for recovery of Rs. 30,000/-, as damages against the Chief Secretary and others for malicious prosecution, which was decreed on 20.8.1979 against Gulzar Singh complainant with costs.
Consequently, he assailed the order of his pre-mature retirement as politically motivated, illegal and based on false prestige.
3.
The State of Punjab and District Collector, Patiala contested the suit.
In the written statement, it was admitted that the petitioner joined as a Tehsildar, as alleged and that the impugned order had been passed after thorough consideration with the result that the plaintiff was retired in public interest on the basis of his over all unsatisfactory record under the provisions of the Rules.
In support of this averment, the adverse remarks awarded to the plaintiff during the years 1957-58 to 1970-71 and four minor punishments awarded in the years 1960, 1961, 1966 and 1969 were appended as Ex.DA with the written statement.
4.
From the pleadings of the parties, the following issues were framed :-  (1) Whether the impugned order dated September 9, 1975 vide which Sarup Singh plaintiff was given pre-mature retirement from service is illegal, null and void, without jurisdiction etc. as is alleged in the plaint ?
If so, its effect ?
IPP.
(2) Whether the instant suit is within limitation ?
OPP (3) Whether the subject matter of the suit is not justiceable ?
OPD (4) Whether notice under S. 80 CPC is invalid or illegal ?
OPD (5) Whether the plaintiff had no cause of action qua the instant suit ?
OPD (6) Relief.
5.
The trial Court returned findings on issue Nos. 1 and 2 against the plaintiff.
Issue Nos. 3, 4 and 5 were decided against the defendants.
The result was that the suit was dismissed.
In appeal the lower Appellate Court reversed the findings in relation to issue Nos. 1 and 2 with the result that the suit was decreed.
The State of Punjab had come up in appeal.
6.
I have heard the learned State counsel and have gone through the record of the case.
7.
State counsel has made a valiant effort to assail the judgment of the lower appellate Court inspite of the fact that the finding returned by the Court, in view of the circumstances brought on the record, is unassailable.
From the record, it is apparent that the respondent had joined the service of the Punjab State as a Naib Tehsildar in the year 1947 and had admittedly been promoted as a Tehsildar, Municipal Committee, Jalandhar by the Commissioner, Jalandhar Division on 991969.
He continued working as Tehsildar at different stations until 991975 when the impugned order under the Rules was passed.
While there is no doubt that the respondent had been tried under Section 5(2) of the 1947 Act and acquitted by the Special Judge, Jalandhar on 2181972 and had been reinstated, he had also filed a suit for recovery of Rs 30,000/- as damages, which was decreed against one Gulzar Singh complainant.
These are some of the circumstances, which had been taken into consideration by the lower appellate Court for coming to the conclusion that the order of pre-mature retirement that was passed by the appellant could not be sustained.
Apart from the fact that there were some adverse entries in his ACRs for the period prior to 1969, which were ruled out of consideration on the basis of judicial precedent available in Lalji Lal Srivastava v. Distt.
Judge, Lucknow and others, 1982(3) S.L.R.
607 to the effect that the old and obsolete A.C.R.
record of an official of five-six years prior to his promotion could not be considered for basing the order of pre-mature retirement as also the judgment in The Regional Manager and another v. Pawan Kumar Dubey, AIR 1976 SC 1766, the State counsel has not been able to indicate how the findings in relation to issue No. 1 are against the record and, therefore, have to be reversed.
These findings together with the observations regarding interpolation in some of the ACRs have consequently to be affirmed.
8.
The second issue relating to limitation too had been decided in favour of the respondent and the State counsel was not in a position to assail it as a incorrect conclusion on the basis whereof the lower appellate Court had come to the conclusion that "now we have to examine if the plaintiff could be given benefit of principle of merger in respect of limitation for filing the suit.
Cause of action firstly accrued to the plaintiff on 30975 when he was prematurely retired.
He filed the representation Ex.P7 and the same was rejected vide letter Ex.P10 dated 261176.
Plaintiff admitted that he had come to know about this order on 261176.
This again gave him a cause of action to assail the order of his retirement within statutory period of three years plus 60 days allowed in lieu of notice admittedly served by him under S. 80 C.P.C. as provided under Section 15 of the Limitation Act."
For the reasons recorded above, this appeal fails and the same is dismissed.
Appeal dismissed.
