Swatanter Kumar, J (Oral) - Khushbir Singh, petitioner No. 1 working as Private Secretary and Raj Kumar Sahore, petitioner No. 2 working as Judgment- Writer in the Registry of this High Court had approached this Court under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India praying that Annexure P4 Notification dated 15.6.1990 be quashed in so far as it abolishes the category of Senior Scale Stenographer Grade-I retrospectively.
Further, they prayed that the expression "in between 111986 to 31121987" be also quashed and finally the petitioners further prays for issuance of writ of mandamus to the respondents, directing them to grant Rs. 2000/- being the minimum of the revised scale of Rs. 2000-3500 corresponding to the unrevised scale of Rs. 800-1400 as an initial start on the present scale of Rs. 1800-3200 and also pay additional pay of Rs. 120/- per month to them from the dates they were placed in the unrevised pay scale of Rs. 800-1400.
2.
While claiming the above relief, counsel appearing for the petitioners contended that the said notification is arbitrary and cannot be given effect retrospectively and there is a patent discrimination between the Secretary working in the Secretariat as well as the employees who were granted the above minimum pay scale of Rs. 2000-3500.
According to the counsel, these grounds would entitle the petitioners for relief claimed in the writ petition.
In order to appreciate the contention, reference to the basic facts would be necessary.
3.
There is a cadre of Senior Scale Stenographer Grade-I in the Registry of this Court as well as in the Secretariat of the Punjab Government, which were divided into two categories in the ratio of 50:50, namely, Senior Scale Stenographer in the unrevised pay scale of Rs. 600-1120 and Senior Scale Stenographer Grade-I in the unrevised pay scale of Rs. 800-1400.
The petitioners were granted the pay scale of Senior Scale Stenographer with the effect from 131988.
Punjab Civil Services (Revised Pay) Rules, 1988 were notified on 991988 and first schedule appended to the said Rules describing the revised scales of pay of different classes.
On refixation of pay scale of Senior Scale Stenographer, the petitioners were granted the said higher revised pay scale with effect from 111986.
Upon notification of the Rules of 1990 and the expressions were introduced in the notification, which according to the petitioners, are affecting their basic relief to receive higher pay scale.
It is averred by the petitioners that the Senior Assistant and Senior Scale Stenographer of the Punjab Civil Secretariat have been granted by scale of Rs. 1920/- in the revised pay scale of Rs. 1800-3200 with effect from 111986 to 31121987.
However, this benefit was denied to the petitioner.
Thus giving rise to this writ petition.
4.
The respondents contested the claim of the petitioners.
According to them, this is a policy decision and the notification or any part thereof does not suffer from arbitrariness muchless illegal or perversity.
According to the respondents, no discrimination has resulted from this action.
5.
As far as the main relief claimed by the petitioner is concerned, it is not necessary for us to discuss the same in any greater detail.
Suffice it to note that the question in relation to minimum pay scale, being challenged on the ground of discrimination, which is squarely answered by a Division Bench of this court in the case Tej Kaur v. Chief Secretary to Government of Punjab, 1996(2) SCT 120, where their Lordships while dealing with identical situation, held as under :-  "The ratio decidendi of the said decision was that in service matters merit or experience could be a proper basis for classification.
Same question as to whether there could be clarification on basis of experience and qualification was considered by the Supreme Court in the case of Shyam Babu Verma and others v. Union of India and others, 1994(2) SCT 296 (SC) : JT 1994(1) SC 574.
A similar argument of `equal pay for equal work' pertaining to employees performing similar duties was advanced.
The Supreme Court held that classification made by expert body should not be disturbed except on strong reason and there could be classification on the basis of experience or academic qualifications.
The said principle that there can be classification on basis of seniority even was accepted by the Supreme Court in the case of State of West Bengal and others v. Sri Deb Kumar Mukherjee and others, 1995(2) SCT 844 (SC) : JT 1995(3) SC 333.
In fact the Supreme Court against in the case of Secretary Finance Department and others v. The West Bengal Registration Services Association and others, 1992(2) SCT 301 (SC) : 1992(2) SLR 82 deprecated the courts to enter on the test of job evaluation.
The courts have only to interfere if the action of the State was arbitrary.
In paragraph 12 it observed : "We do not consider it necessary to traverse the case law on which reliance has been placed by counsel for the appellants as it is well-settled that equation of posts and determination of pay-scales is the primary function of the executive and not the judiciary and, therefore, ordinary courts will not enter upon the task of job evaluation which is generally left to expert bodies like the Pay Commissions etc.
But that is not to say that the Court has no jurisdiction and the aggrieved employees have no remedy if they are unjustly treated by arbitrary State action or inaction.
Courts must, however, realise that job evaluation is both a difficult and time consuming task which even expert bodies having the assistance of staff with requisite expertise have found difficult to undertake sometimes on account of want of relevant data and scales for evaluating performance of different group of employees."
What then is the position in the present case ?
The gist of the same is the earlier there were two scales namely Rs. 600-1120/- and Rs. 800-1400/-.
From 111986 after certain decisions already referred to above, they were brought into one scale of Rs. 1800-3200/-.
The respondents-State of Punjab subsequently decided that those Senior Assistants/Senior Scales Stenographers who were in the scale Rs. 800-1400/- before 111986 would be given the initial start of Rs. 1920/-.
Subsequently, the benefit of initial start of Rs. 1920/- was given to those employees who were in the pre-revised scale of Rs. 800-1400/- from 111986 to 31121987.
It is obvious from aforesaid that the decision in the case of Krishan Lal Sehgal (supra) and PS Savita (supra) will not help the petitioners because therein there were two different pay scales.
In the present case the scale remained the same.
All the Senior Assistants and Senior Scale Stenographers remained in the scale of Rs. 800-1400/-.
There was only some difference of pay but in the same pay scale.
Certain Senior Scale Stenographers and Senior Assistants who had earlier been in the pre-revised pay scale of Rs. 800-1400/- before 31121987 by virtue of their seniority were allowed to have initial start of Rs. 1920/-.
The scale was not changed.
They were a class apart on the cut-off date of 31121987.
Seniority and increments are all parts of the same service and cadre.
Certain senior persons at times are given mere salary and if initial start of Rs. 1920/- was given to certain Senior Assistants and Senior Scale Stenographers but in the same pay scale, it will not amount to arbitrariness or hostile discrimination as is being alleged.
It is a valid classification.
The object to be achieved is little too hazy but one thing is obvious that some credit is given to the persons who have more experience by virtue of their seniority.
If the plea of the petitioners is to be accepted, this would be re-writting and converting the scale of Rs. 1800-3200/- to Rs. 1920-3200/- because every person on being appointed as Senior Assistant or Senior Scale Stenographer would claim the said scale in terms of start of the salary.
The Court would refrain in doing so because impugned action does not tantamount to discrimination in terms of Articles 14 and 39(d) of the Constitution."
6.
With respect we adopt the reasoning given and consequently decline the relief prayed for by the petitioners in relation to directing the respondent to grant Rs. 2000/- being the minimum of the revised scale of Rs. 2000-3500 and for grant of special pay scale.
7.
The only contention, which remains to be considered by this Court is the effect the Annexure P4 being violative of rights of the petitioners, as it cannot be given effect retrospectively.
This contention again is of no merit.
The bare reading of Notification Annexure P4 shows that mentioning of the date is of cut off date and does not cause any prejudice to the rights of the petitioners retrospectively.
In the proviso to the notification, it has been noticed that wherever an employee has been granted selection grade on or before the 31121987, he may be allowed the revised scale corresponding to the selection grade as a measure personal to him.
We fail to appreciate the contention of counsel for the petitioners that this notification gives the retrospective effect particularly when it is conceded before us by both the sides that no reduction in the salary of the petitioner had been made during that period.
The date is primarily intended to substitute to an earlier date, which was with effect from 111986.
Vide letter dated 8.10.1992 of the Punjab Government, it was directed that initial start of Rs. 1920/- in the present pay scales of Rs. 1800-3200 with effect from 111986 be given to those Senior Assistants and Senior Scale Stenographers of Secretariat Offices who were in the unrevised pay scale of Rs. 800-1400/- on 31121985.
Subsequently, this benefit instead of 111986 has also been extended for the period intervening 111986 to 31121987.
We see no arbitrariness in this action.
It is a mere cut off date relatable to the notification.
Dismissed, leaving the parties to bear their own costs.
Petition dismissed.
