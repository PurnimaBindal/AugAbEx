RK Nehra, J - The petitioner has assailed the validity of the order dated January 16, 1990 issued by respondent No. 2 in terms of Rule 3.26(d) of the Punjab Civil Services Rules, Vol.
I, Part I, as applicable to the State of Haryana, giving him three month's notice to retire from service in this petition under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India.
2.
The petitioner was appointed as a Patwari on December 24, 1953.
On his attaining the age of 55 years, the appointing authority issued him a notice in terms of Rule 3.26(d) of the Punjab Civil Services Rules, Volume I, Part I, as applicable to the State of Haryana (for short, the Rules) saying that in public interest he will be deemed to have retired from service after the expiry of three months' notice from the date of its receipt.
3.
In the written statement filed on behalf of the respondents, it is averred that the order of compulsory retirement is not a punishment.
It implies no stigma or any suggestion of misbehaviour.
The appointing authority takes into consideration the entire record of service before taking a decision in the matter, of course, attaching more importance to record of performance during the later years.
The record to be considered would naturally include the entries in the confidential record/character rolls, both favourable and adverse.

In para 3 of the petition, the petitioner has stated that for the year 1988-89, the report eared by him was 'Average' and for the year 1989-90, the appointing authority did not think it necessary to record his annual confidential report.
The correctness of the averment made in para 3 of the writ petition was not controverted by the respondents in their written statement.
4.
Annual confidential report for the year 1988-89 is recorded as 'average'.
The report for the year 1989-90 was not recorded.
If the annual confidential reports for the years 1988-89 and 1989-90 are considered to be 'average', these cannot be treated as adverse reports.
The appointing authority vide order dated March 14, 1986 ordered stoppage of two increments with cumulative effect on account of wrong reports made by the petitioner regarding the income of Chander Bhan s/o Bishna and Fateh, s/o Raja in order to help them to secure undue benefit.
The petitioner challenged this order in appeal before the Commissioner, Ambala Division, Ambala, who reduced the punishment from two increments to one increment with cumulative effect vide order dated December 19, 1986.
An entry to this effect had been made in the service record of the petitioner.
A perusal of the summary of the annual confidential reports of the petitioner reveals that during the last 10 years, he earned 4 'average' reports and the remaining as 'good'.
He was inflicted punishment of stoppage of two increments with cumulative effect in the year 1985-86, although on appeal the punishment was reduced to stoppage of one increment with cumulative effect.
The appointing authority, after taking into consideration his favourable and unfavourable entries in the annual confidential reports, came to the conclusion that it would be in public interest to retire him from service.
We are not inclined to interfere with the impugned order of the appointing authority in this petition as we find that the petitioner has an equally efficacious remedy by way of appeal against the order of compulsory retirement in terms of Rule 9 of the Haryana Civil Services (Punishment and Appeal) Rules, 1987 (for short, the Punishment Rules).
Rule 9 of the punishment rules says that a government servant aggrieved against an order of compulsory retirement can assail the same under the Punishment Rules.
The petitioner ought to have availed the alternative remedy of appeal under the Punishment Rules.
We accordingly relegate him to the alternative remedy of appeal under the Punishment Rules.
If the petitioner prefers an appeal within three months, the competent authority will entertain the same and dispose it of on merits.
The respondents will not raise the plea of limitation before the appellant authority.
5.
With these observations, the writ petition is disposed of, but with no order as to costs.
Ordered accordingly.
