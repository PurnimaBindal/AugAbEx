MN Rao, J - The petitioners herein are employees of the Society for Employment and Training in Twin Cities ('Setwin' for short) a society registered under the Societies Registration Act, but funded completely by the State Government.
The organisation Setwin was formed in November, 1978 by GOMs.
No. 814 dated 25th October, 1978.
Originally the Chairman of the Setwin was the Director of Industries and all the members were Government Officials.
The employees working in different sections of the Industries Department of the State Government were transfered to Setwin and they constituted the nucleus.
Subsequently the Setwin recruited several other employees on monthly consolidated pay.
SO far as the employees drawn from the Industries Department are concerned, their pay, allowances and other conditions of service were protected.
The differences in the remuneration and other conditions of service between the two classes of employees continued till 111983.
The State Government by GO Rt.
No. 89, appointed a committee consisting of senior IAS, officers for the purpose of examining the work load and making recommendations regarding revision of pay scales.
The Government felt that "any revision of pay should be based on internal workload, justification and the skills needed."
The committee which was headed by Sri.
SR Govindarajan, a senior IAS officer examined the entire set up of the Setwin and made certain recommendations.
The Committee recorded the following as the factual position.
"The Employees of Setwin who were originally given consolidated pay, were given regular scale of pay on par with Government servants with effect from 111983."
The Committee recommended that -  "As Setwin is an autonomous body and not a department of Government the revision of pay scales of Setwin employees on par with Government employees, on the basis of Pay Revision Commission recommendations, has not been accepted by Government.
However, in view of the increase in the cost of living index and the necessity to raise the pay scales of staff, commensurate with the internal workload the following recommendations are made."
(emphasis added).

No extra provisions will be made for this purpose."
2.
Challenging the said GO the present writ petition was filed.
The relief the petitioners are seeking is that whatever remuneration is paid to employees of Setwin drawn from Industries Department, the same should be paid to them also.
3.
Sri Syed Sharee Ahmefad, the learned counsel for the petitioners contends that whatever be the difference prior to 111983 between the employees drawn from Industries Department and employees appointed by the Setwin, with the application of Government scales of pay and allowances to both the classes of employees, the difference was obliterated and thereafter the uniformity that was achieved should not be disturbed.
Controverting this contention the learned Government Pleader contends that the petitioners cannot compare themselves with persons drawn from the Industries Department, the source of recruitment being different, the conditions of service being different, there cannot be parity between the two classes.
Sri Ramanujachary, the learned counsel for the Setwin says that the financial position of the Setwin does not permit payment of enhanced remuneration to the petitioners.
4.
From the report of the committee of officers (relevant-portion extracted supra) it is clear that with effect from 111983 Setwin employees were given scales of pay on par with the Government Servants.
This single circumstance had brought about uniformity in the pay scales of the employees of Setwin irrespective of the sources from which they were originally recruited.
When once the employees were brought into a uniform pay structure there cannot be any discrimination based upon the original source of recruitment especially when persons drawn from the Industries Department and persons appointed by the Setwin perform similar duties and discharge similar functions.
But in the matter of remuneration a discrimination has been brought about based upon the recommendations of the committee of officers.
The Committee in its recommendation had clearly stated that the remuneration should be "commensurate with the work load".
If so, there cannot be any legitimate differences in the pay scales between the two classes of employees.
This is a clear violation of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India.
There is no rationale in the State action in bringing about the discrimination between the two classes of officers after uniformity was achieved with effect from 111983.
In V Marrendeya v. State of AP, AIR 1989, SC page 1308 the Supreme Court ruled at page 1315 in para 13 -  "In view of the above discussion we are of the opinion that where two classes of employees perform indentical or similar duties and carrying out the same functions with the same measure of responsibility having same academic qualifications, they would be entitled to equal pay.
If the State denies them equality in pay, its action would be violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution and the Court will strike down the discrimination and grant relief to the aggrieved employees.
But before such relief is granted the Court must consider and analyse the rationale behind the state action in prescribing two different scales of pay."
The discrimination is very patent.
The only justification in support of this discrimination put forth by the learned Government pleader and the counsel for the Setwin is that the source of recruitment of the two classes of employees was different.
This contention is totally unacceptable.
The source of recruitment had lost its relevance with effect from 111983. when uniformity was achieved.
If the organisation is not in a position to pay higher remuneration it is always open to it to structure the staff in such manner as to be within the limits of the grants received from the Government.
But that cannot be a ground for bringing into being two classes of employees for the purpose of payment of remuneration when both are doing the same type of work.
5.
For these reasons the writ petition is allowed.
No costs.
Advocate's fee Rs. 350/-.
6.
The respondents are directed to fix the pay scales of the petitioners on par with the employees drawn from Industries Department but working now in Setwin.
Petition allowed.
