VS Aggarwal, J - Ms Venus Sengam has filed the present petition under Article 226 of the Constitution of India for directing the respondents to grant her admission in MTech.
(Electrical) or Instrumentation because the candidates lower in merit than her have been admitted to this course.
2.
The relevant facts are that petitioner possesses the qualification of BTech.
in Electrical Engineering from Dr. Ram Manohar Lohia Avadh University, Faizabad.
She had secured 70% marks.
The Regional Engineering College, Kurukshetra issued a prospectus for MTech.
Degree Course 1999-2000 and Part B of the prospectus relates to admission to MTech.
Degree course in Electrical Engineering.
The eligibility for admission was that a candidate should possess the qualification of B.Sc.
(Engineering) or BTech./B.E.
degree from an affiliated University or equivalent examination.
The candidate should have secured more than 60% marks.
The petitioner had more than 70% marks and applied for MTech.
Electrical Engineering and Physics (Instrumentation).
She received a call letter dated 17.8.1999 to attend the counselling scheduled for 3081999.
On 3081999 the petitioner appeared before respondent No. 2 (Chairman, Admission Committee, Regional Engineering College, Kurukshetra).
She figured at serial No. 5 as per merit in Non-GATE candidates.
On 3081999 respondent No. 2 intimated that list of GATE candidates is not complete.
They were orally told that counselling had been postponed to 491999.
The petitioner belonged to the State of Manipur.
Instead of going back, she stayed with her local guardian at Kurukshetra and visited respondent 1 and 2 on next 3 days to enquire about the process of admission.
She was told that counselling will be held on 491999.
3.
As per petitioner in Non-GATE category, there were 3 candidates namely petitioner, Dharminder Singh and Arbind Kumar Gupta.
Dharminder Singh was from Srinagar and Arbind Kumar Gupta from Banaras.
On 491999 the petitioner went to the college and was told that counselling took place on 391999 although it was a gazetted holiday on account of Janamasthmi.
The grievance of the petitioner is that respondent 1 and 2 in an arbitrary and mala fide manner kept the petitioner in dark about the date of the counselling and instead gave admission to Jasmer Singh and Ramesh Chand in Non GATE category though they were having less marks than the petitioner.
The said action of the respondents is challenged to be illegal, further asserting that some of the candidates were related to the local Professors in the University.
4.
In the written statement filed by respondent 1 and 2, it has been pointed that the date of counselling was.
391999 when the petitioner did not appear.
She had applied for MTech.
degree course in Electrical Engineering.
There are two courses namely Control System and Power System.
Both the courses have 10 seats.
Eight seats are for general category candidates and two seats are meant for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes candidates.
In addition to above seats, maximum of 5 sponsored/part-time candidates can be admitted.
The petitioner had not cleared GATE examination.
She could only be considered as a Non-GATE qualified candidate.
In all 32 applications were received.
In sponsored category only 4 applications were received.
It was decided to call all the GATE candidates.
Inadvertently out of 32 candidates who had applied as GATE candidates, interview letters could not be issued for 3081999 to all.
On 3081999 out of 21 GATE candidates to whom interview letters were sent, only 12 appeared.
Seven persons including the petitioner appeared against general category seats.
Since mistake was realised, the counselling for general category candidates had to be postponed.
They were called for interview on 391999.
All such candidates including the petitioner were sent intimation about the counselling to be held on 391999.
Telegrams were also given at the address supplied.
The petitioner did not appear before the Admission Committee on 391999, therefore, the other candidates who appeared were admitted.
It is denied that petitioner or other candidates were told that counselling shall take place on 491999.
5.
Perusal of these facts clearly indicate that admittedly, the petitioner appeared for counselling on 3081999.
However, there is a controversy if on 3081999 the petitioner had been told that she should come for counselling on 491999.
6.
It is admitted on behalf of the respondents that firstly the persons who had qualified in GATE test had to be considered.
Some of them had not been issued the requisite notices.
This mistake was detected only on 3081999 itself.
The notices were issued to other GATE qualified persons for 391999 and notices were also issued to general category candidates including the petitioner for 391999.
7.
In order to verify this fact the respondents were called upon produce the original record which reveal that notice had been sent to the petitioner on 31.8.1999 to appear on 391999.
The petitioner's claim is that she had stayed at Kurukshetra.
However, she had given her address of 2, Sardar Patel Marg, Chanakyapuri, New Delhi.
Therefore, notice is stated to have been sent on that address.
Necessarily, the respondents had to send the same at that place which was the given address.
At this stage, it cannot, therefore, be termed or decided that petitioner was told to come again on 491999.
This factual controversy cannot be agitated in the present writ proceedings.
But for purposes of the present writ, suffice to say that it cannot be held definitely that 491999 was given the date to the petitioner for counselling or that she had not been informed that counselling was on 391999.
In these circumstances, there is no ground to interfere.
The petitioner if so advised may challenge the said controversy in civil suit.
Subject to aforesaid, the writ petition is dismissed.
There will be no order as to costs.
Petition dismissed.
