Swatanter Kumar, J - Both these writ petitions are being disposed of by this common judgment as the facts and law involved in both the cases are similar.
However, facts are taken from CWP No. 12237 of 2002.
2.
In this writ petition, the petitioners are praying for issuance of a writ, order or direction in the nature of certiorari quashing the order dated 1892001 whereby punishment of recovery has been ordered and the order dated 13.5.2002 Annexure P/5 to the writ petition, vide which the appeals filed by the petitioners have been dismissed.
The petitioners further claim for the refund of money with interest at the rate of 18% per annum.
3.
Upon notice, the respondents have filed reply wherein it has been stated that under the Food Corporation of India Staff Regulations, 1971, statutory remedy of review is available and the petitioners are obliged to seek such remedy approaching this Court under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India.
4.
We find merit in the contention of the respondents.
In any case various contentions raised in other connected matter, where much severer punishments had been imposed or departmental enquiries would be conducted against the persons and plea of delay was taken, as taken in the present case, the Court directed the petitioner to seek recourse to alternative remedy.
Reference can be made to Bal Krishan v. Food Corporation of India and others, 2002(4) S.C.T.
1020, CWP No. 4714 of 2002 decided on 1392002.
5.
For the reasons afore-stated, these writ petitions are disposed of with liberty to the petitioners to seek review of the impugned orders and if such a review petition is filed, the same shall not be dismissed on the plea of limitation, as the petitioners are bonafidely pursuing the present petitions before this Court under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India.
However, the parties are left to bear their own costs.
Order accordingly.
