THB Chalapathi, J - The petitioners who are working as teachers in various Government schools in the State of Haryana, filed this writ petition for issuance of a writ directing the respondents to grant them the higher pay scale from the date of their acquiring the qualification of BA or equivalent examination, according to the orders of the Government.
2.
The erstwhile State of Punjab issued a Circular dated 23.7.1957, revising the pay scales of the employees of the Government including the teachers working in Education Department for possessing higher qualifications.
When the relief of higher pay (sic) approached this Court through different writ petitions.
These writ petitions were allowed.
The matter was also decided by a Full Bench of this Court in CWP No. 484 of 1986 (Om Parkash Arya v. State of Haryana ).
The Supreme Court also in Civil Appeal No. 3799 of 1983 (Avtar Singh v. Manmohan Singh ) and Chaman Lal v. State of Haryana, 1987(4) SLR 4.
This Court in view of the orders of the Supreme Court held that the circular is applicable to the teachers working in the State of Haryana.
In CWP No. 10365 of 1994 (Haryana Rajkiya Adhyapak Sangh v. State of Haryana), 1995(1) SCT 748(P&H)(DB) decided on 3111995, a Division Bench of this Court held that teachers are entitled to the relief in view of various decisions of the Supreme Court and a large number of other decisions of this Court.
After considering various pronouncements of this Court as well as of the Supreme Court a Division Bench of this Court in CWP No. 15630 of 1993 (Rattan Singh v. Haryana State, 1995(1) S.C.T.
711.
), decided on 1491994, directed the respondents to give relief to the petitioners by fixing their pay in the higher scale from the date they acquired the requisite qualifications.
Thus it has been consistently held that the Circular dated 23.7.1957 is applicable to all the teachers in the Education Department.
3.
The Supreme Court in Mamleshwar Prasad v. Kanahaiya, AIR 1975 SC 907 observed as followd :-  "Certainty of the law, consistency of rulings and comity of courts - all flowering from the same principle-converge to the conclusion that a decision once rendered must later bind like cases.
We do not intend to direct from the rule that, in exceptional instances, where by obvious inadvertence or oversight a judgment fails to notice a plain statutory provision or obligatory authority running counter to the reasoning and result reached, it may not have the say of binding precedents.
It should be a glaring case, an obtrusive omission.
No such situation presents itself here and we do not embark on the principle of judgment per incuriam."
Thus it is clear that the petitioners are entitled to the relief of higher pay scale in terms of the decisions of the Supreme Court and this Court as well.
The writ petition is accordingly allowed and the petitioners shall be given the relief of higher pay scale from the date of acquiring higher qualification on the same terms as given to the petitioners in C.W.P.
No. 15630 of 1993 (Rattan Singh and others v. State of Haryana etc.), decided by a Division Bench of this Court on 1491994.
However, there will be no order as to costs.
