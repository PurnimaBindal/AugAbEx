RS Mongia, J - Petitioner, Puran Chand Sharma, a Clerk in the Rural Employment Exchange, Morni, District Ambala, (Haryana), impugns the order of Respondent No. 1, dated 2/351991 (Annexure P-1 to the writ petition), by which he was ordered to be prematurely retired on attaining the age of 55 years.
2.
Briefly the facts of the case are that the petitioner before joining the Haryana Government service as a Clerk under the Director, Employment Exchange, Haryana, on 5th January, 1972, had served in the Army from 14th June, 1955 to 2nd November, 1971 as a Signal Man.
The petitioner is to attain the age of 58 years, i.e. the age of superannuation in June, 1994.
As observed above, the petitioner was ordered to be prematurely retired vide impugned order on attaining the age of 55 years.
3.
The grievance made by the petitioner is that in spite of his good record of service, he was prematurely retired by judging the suitability for retention in service beyond the age of 55 years in accordance with the Haryana Government Instructions, dated 16th August, 1983, which have been annexed by the respondent as Annexure R-II with the written statement.
According to these instructions, only such Government employees were to be retained in service after the age of 55 years if they had earned 70% good or better reports in the last 10 years.
4.
The learned counsel for the petitioner submitted that these instructions have been struck down by a Division Bench of this Court in KK Vaid v. State of Haryana, 1990(1) S.L.R.
1.
He further submitted that according to the judgment in KK Vaid's case (supra) the average report cannot be considered as 'adverse' to an employee.
In fact, 'average' means not so good and not so bad.
According to the learned counsel, under the Premature Retirement Rules, only those persons are to be retired who have really become dead would and such a wood is to be chopped off.
Those persons who may be just 'average' cannot be made to retire.
5.
In the written statement filed on behalf of the respondents, it has been admitted that action of premature retirement against the petitioner has been taken by judging the suitability for his retention under the instructions, dated 16th August, 1983.
The record of the petitioner for the last 10 years has been mentioned in Annexure R-1 to the written statement.
The record from 1980-81 onwards is as under :-  "Summary Record of Shri Puran Chand Sharma, Clerk.

6.
The learned counsel for the respondents has not disputed that the instructions of the Government, dated 16th August, 1983, have been struck down by the Division Bench of this Court in KK Vaid's case (supra).
It is not understood as to why the respondents are applying these instructions, which already stand struck down by this Court.
These instructions just provided mathematical calculations.
If a person had more than 70% good or better reports, he would be retained in service.
The overall record was not kept in view and in case a person had six good or above reports and four average or below reports, he would be retired from service under the instructions.
No mind has been applied to the overall record of the petitioner.
Take for example in a given case, the last six reports of a person are good or very good, but previous to that he had earned only average reports, according to the instructions, such a person would be prematurely retired simply because he had earned four average reports in the first four years of the last ten years.
According to me such a person need not be prematurely retired.
A person who had average reports initially and has improved work and earned good or above reports, must not be retired as by good work he showed improvement and earned good reports.
It would be arbitrary to retire a person who has shown good work in the recent past.
In my view immediate past record is more relevant.
In this case, I find that from 1987-88 onwards, petitioner had earned good reports and even the remarks of 1986-87 were expunged.
In 1990-91, the petitioner had earned average report.
According to the reports reproduced above, the petitioner cannot be said to be a dead wood or a person who cannot be allowed to be retained in service on the rank he was serving before the impugned order was passed.
7.
For the foregoing reasons this writ petition is allowed and the impugned order, Annexure P-1, dated 2/351991, is hereby quashed.
Resultantly, the petitioner would be deemed to be in service from the date of his premature retirement with all consequential benefits.
There will be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
