Jawahar Lal Gupta, J. (Oral) - The petitioner who has since retired from service, prays for the issue of an appropriate writ or direction quashing the orders dated April 28, 1980, June 2, 1980, and September 20, 1980, copies of which have been attached as Annexures P-1, P-3 and P-5 with the writ petition.
By these orders, various persons who were junior to the petitioner, were promoted to the posts of Assistant District Industries Officer (ad hoc) and Assistant Director on officiating basis.
The ground for challenge is that the petitioner's claim was not considered when respondent No. 3 who was junior to him was promoted as Assistant District Industries Officer w.e.f.
June 2, 1976, vide order dated April 28, 1980.
It has also been stated that the petitioner's claim for promotion was not considered when respondent Nos. 4 to 6 were promoted as Assistant District Industries Officers vide orders dated September 20, 1980, with effect from January 2, 1979.
According to the petitioner, his claim was also not considered for promotion to the post of Assistant Director when respondent No. 3 was promoted as such w.e.f.
June 2, 1980.
2.
In the written statement filed on behalf of the respondents, it has been stated that the petitioner's case was considered for promotion in the year 1971.
It was found that his two increments had been stopped with cumulative effect "from February 1970 for wilful absence from duty..." and his conduct was also under enquiry.
Consequently, he was overlooked for promotion.
The other officials including respondent No. 3 who were found suitable were promoted on ad hoc basis vide order dated June 28, 1971.
The promotion of respondent No. 3 had actually become effective from July 19, 1971.
Similarly, respondents No. 4 and 5 were promoted on ad hoc basis in the cadre of Assistant District Industries Officer/Development Officer/Industrial Promotion Officers for a period of four months.
Subsequently, vide order dated September 20, 1980, their promotions were regularised w.e.f.
January 2, 1979.
Respondent No. 6 was promoted vide order dated September 20, 1980.
Vide order dated May 10, 1983, the petitioner was promoted as officiating Industrial Promotion Officer w.e.f.
April 1, 1979.
Thereafter, vide order dated January 7, 1984, the petitioner was promoted as Assistant District Industries Officer on ad hoc basis w.e.f.
July 19, 1971.
Copies of these two orders are on record as Annexures RIII and RII respectively with the written statement.
By another order dated January 7, 1984 (copy at Annexure R.4 with the written statement), the petitioner was promoted as Assistant Director w.e.f.
April 1, 1983.
On these averments, it is stated that there was no violation of the provisions of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
3.
The short question that arises for consideration in this case is as to whether the petitioner's claim for promotion was considered w.e.f.
the date the persons junior to him were actually prompted ?
4.
In order to verify the factual position, the record of the case was sent for.
On October 11, 1996, a summary of the petitioner's record was produced by Mr. Jaswant Singh.
However, the file in which the petitioner's claim for promotion was considered, was not produced.
Today, Mr. Jaswant Singh has produced the file.
He has pointed out that initially the petitioner was not found suitable for promotion as Assistant District Industries Officer when respondent No. 3 was promoted in the year 1971 on ad hoc basis.
However, after the receipt of the notice of the writ petition, the matter was examined by the Government.
A note dated January 4, 1984 was put up.
The petitioner's claim for promotion was examined with reference to each of the dates with effect from which a person junior to him was promoted.
It was found that the petitioner was eligible for the grant of promotion as Assistant District Industries Officer (ad hoc) with effect from July 19, 1971.
Accordingly, the orders of his promotion were issued.
With regard to the grant of regular promotion as Assistant District Industries Officer w.e.f.
June 2, 1976, when respondent No. 3 had been granted regular promotion, it was found that out of the ten reports, the petitioner had earned three good reports while the remaining seven were `average'.
Thus, he was not considered suitable for promotion with effect from June 2, 1976.
It was further observed that persons junior to the petitioner had been promoted on regular basis in the year 1979.
So far as the petitioner is concerned, he was declared suitable for promotion w.e.f.
April 1, 1979 after taking into consideration his report for the year 1978-79.
Still further, it was observed that persons junior to the petitioner like respondent Nos. 4 to 6 having been promoted w.e.f.
January 2, 1979 and he having been promoted w.e.f.
April 1, 1979 as Assistant District Industries Officer, he had become junior to them.
Since respondent No. 3 had been promoted on regular basis w.e.f.
June 2, 1976, the petitioner was junior to him also.
Consequently, he had no right to claim promotion to the post of Assistant Director with effect from the date respondent No. 3 had been promoted.
This proposal was approved by the Government.
Accordingly, the learned counsel submits that the petitioner's claim stands duly considered and no further relief can be granted to him.
5.
Mr. Amrit Pal, counsel for the petitioner submits that he had a right to be considered for promotion in the year 1980 when respondent No. 3 was promoted on regular basis.
The petitioner's claim having not been considered on the dates when his juniors were promoted, the impugned orders deserve to be set aside and the respondents should be directed to consider his claim afresh.
6.
This plea cannot accepted.
An employee has only a right to be considered for promotion.
Even if it is assumed that the petitioner's claim for promotion was not considred on April 28, 1980, when respondent No. 3 was promoted as Assistant District Industries Officer on regular basis w.e.f.
June 2, 1976 or in September 1980 when respondent Nos. 4 to 6 were promoted w.e.f.
January 2, 1979, the fact remains that in the year 1984 the petitioner's claim for promotion with effect from the dates the persons junior to him had been promoted had been duly considered.
On a consideration of the record, it was found that he was suitable for regular promotion as Assistant District Industries Officer w.e.f.
April 1, 1979.
The other claims as made out by the petitioner were rejected.
This position is clearly borne out from the record.
Mr. Jaswant Singh has also produced a photo copy of the relevant portion (pages 96 to 103) of the file.
It is taken on record as Mark `A'.
It is directed that it shall be kept in a sealed cover.
Even a perusal of the summary of confidential reports of the petitioner as produced by Mr. Jaswant Singh from the year 1962-63 to 1972-73 shows that he was graded as `Average'.
For the years 1968-69 and 1969-70, the petitioner's reports are not available.
From the year 1973-74 to 1975-76, the petitioner had remained under suspension.
For the subsequent period, the petitioner was graded as a `good' `to very good' officer.
In view thereof, he was actually promoted as Assistant District Industries Officer w.e.f.
April 1, 1983.
Keeping in view the petitioner's record of service, action of the respondent appears to be just and fair.
Consequently, it calls for no interference under Article 226 of the Constitution.
No other point has been urged.
In view of the above, there is no merit in this writ petition.
It is, consequently, dismissed.
However, since the petitioner has already retired from service, there will be no order as to costs.
