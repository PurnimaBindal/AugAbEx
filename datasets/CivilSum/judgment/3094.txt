GS Singhvi, J - Whether the petitioners, who attained the age of majority after 4 years and 5 months of the death of his father Shri Shardha Nand is entitled to be appointed on compassionate grounds is the question that arises for consideration and adjudication in this petition filed by Piyush Harit for quashing the order Annexure P.7 dated 2181997 issued by the Director, Secondary Education, Haryana.
2.
Admittedly, the petitioner was less than 14 years of age (his date of birth being June 30, 1979) on 30.1.1993, the date of the demise of his father.
However, just after six days of the demise of her husband, Smt. Pushpa Devi (mother of the petitioner) submitted an application to the respondent No. 2 for petitioner's appointment on compassionate grounds.
Vide Annexure P.3 dated 13.10.1993, the respondent No. 2 requested the Chief Secretary, Government of Haryana to give a decision on the request of Smt. Pushpa Devi that her son be appointed on attaining the age of majority.
After about 3 years and 10 months of the submission of application by her, the respondent No. 2 wrote letter Annexure P4 dated 11121996 to Smt. Pushpa Devi that instructions regarding reservation of posts for minors have been withdrawn.
After passage of another 8 months, he issued the impugned order rejecting the petitioner's claim for compassionate appointment on the ground that he did not apply within 3 years of the death of his father.
3.
Shri Randhir Sharma argued that the instructions issued by the government vide circular dated 851995 cannot be applied to the case of the petitioner because his mother had submitted application for compassionate appointment much before the issuance of those instructions.
Learned counsel further argued that the petitioner, who was minor at the time of death of his father, could not be appointed to the service till attaining majority and, therefore, the respondents No. 2 is not right in rejecting his application on the ground of bar of limitation.
Learned counsel relied on the judgment of this Court in Sunder Pal v. State of Haryana and others, 1998(3) RSJ 65 to support the petitioner's claim for compassionate appointment.
The learned Deputy Advocate General submitted that the instructions issued by the government vide circular letters dated 851995 and 3181995 have been rightly applied to the case of the petitioner.
In support of this argument, he relied on the judgment of this Court in Bijender Singh v. State of Haryana and others, 1997(3) RSJ 669 : 1997(1) SCT 756 (P&H).
He also submitted that there is no provision in the policy of compassionate appointment for reservation of post in favour of minor dependents and, therefore, the petitioner, who was less than 14 years of age at the time of death of his father, cannot claim appointment on compassionate grounds as of right.
4.
We have thoughtfully considered the respective submissions and have perused the judgments relied upon by the learned counsel for the parties.

6.
In Jagdish Prasad's case (supra), the Supreme Court upheld the judgment of the Patna High Court declining relief to the writ petitioner on the ground that compassionate appointment cannot be claimed after lapse of long period.
Their Lordships held :-  "The very object of appointment of a dependent of the deceased employee who die in harness is to relieve unexpected immediate hardship and distress caused to the family by sudden demise of the earning member of the family.
Since the death occurred way back in 1971, in which year the appellant was four years old, it cannot be said that he is entitled to be appointted after he attained majority long thereafter.
In other words, if that contention is accepted, it amounts to another mode of recruitment of the dependent of a deceased government servant which cannot be encouraged de hors the recruitment rules."
7.
In Naresh Tanwar's case (supra) and Hakim Singh's case (supra), the Apex Court reversed the judgments of this High Court granting relief to the applicants even though they were minor at the time of the demise of their parent(s).
In Naresh Tanwar's case (supra), the Supreme Court observed as under :-  "It has been indicated in the decision of Umesh Kumar Nagpal (supra) that compassionate appointment cannot be granted after a long lapse of reasonable period and the very purpose of compassionate appointment, as an exception to the general rule of open recruitment is intended to meet the immediate financial problem being suffered by the members of the family of the deceased employee.
In the other decision of this Court in Jagdish Prasad's case, it has been also indicated that the very object of appointment of dependent of deceased-employee who died in harness is to relieve immediate hardship and distress caused to the family by sudden demise of the earning member of the family and such consideration cannot be kept binding for years.
It appears to us that the principle of compassionate appointment as indicated in the aforesaid decisions of this court, is not only reasonable but consistent with the principle of employment in government and public sector.
The impugned decisions of the High Court therefore cannot be sustained."
8.
In Hakim Singh's case (supra) the Supreme Court reiterated the principles laid down in Jagdish Prasad's case (supra); Naresh Tanwar's case (supra) and held that the appellant Board has rightly rejected the application filed by the petitioner's mother for his employment on compassionate grounds.
9.
In Gurdarshan Singh's case (supra), a Division Bench of this Court reviewed case law on the subject and culled out the following propositions :-  "(a) Appointment to the public service should be made strictly on the basis of open invitation of applications and merit.
(b) The public employer is duty bound to advertise the posts through newspapers, radio, television, news bulletins etc. and also send requisition to the employment exchange in order to enable all the eligible persons to compete for recruitment.
(c) However, as an exception to the general rule appointment on compassionate grounds can be made without competitive selection in case such appointment is permissible under the rules or policy decision taken by the government/public employer.
(d) The sole object of compassionate appointment is to relieve the family of the deceased employee of the unexpected immediate hardship and distress caused due to the loss of the bread winner.
In other words the purpose of compassionate appointment is to enable the family of the deceased to overcome the sudden crisis caused due to the death of the employee.
(e) Compassionate appointment can be given only against Class III and IV posts.
(f) Before making appointment on compassionate grounds the government/public employer is duty bound to examine the financial condition of the family with a view to find out whether there is any source of substenance and whether it is necessary to rescue the family from the state of financial destitution.
(g) The dependent member of the family does not have the right to seek appointment on the same very post which was held by the deceased.
(h) If the sole dependent of the deceased is a minor at the time of death then his case may be considered for compassionate appointment on his/her attaining majority provided there exists a rule or policy framed by the employer to provide employment in such a situation.
(i) In case the family of the deceased has more than one dependent then the government is not obliged to reserve one post for the minor dependent and appoint him on his attaining majority.
Only in the rarest of the rare cases, the Government may consider giving appointment to a minor on his attaining majority if no other family member is eligible for employment immediately after the death of the employee and there exists any rule or policy to consider such cases for compassionate appointment.
(j) If any period of limitation is prescribed in the rules or the policy instructions relating to compassionate employment then the government is not obliged to consider an application submitted after the expiry of the period of limitation.
In any case appointment on compassionate grounds cannot be claimed after long lapse of time calculated from the date of the death of the employee."
Of the various propositions extracted above, proposition Nos. (h), (i) and (j) have been applied in various other cases decided by this Court while rejecting the claim of minor dependents to be appointed on compassionate grounds.
10.
In view of the above stated legal position, we cannot entertain the claim of the petitioner for appointment on compassionate grounds because he attained majority after 4 years and 5 months of the demise of his father and in terms of the prevalent instructions he was not entitled to be appointed on compassionate grounds.
If he was to make an application on 2961997 at the age of 18 years, the same was bound to be rejected in view of the instructions contained in the circular dated 851995 and 3181995 specifying the limitation of 3 years for submission of application.
Therefore, the mere fact that his mother had applied 4 years and 5 months prior to his attaining majority cannot be a ground for entertaining the claim of the petitioner.
11.
The judgment of Surinder Pal's case (supra) on which reliance has been placed by Shri Randhir Sharma appears to support the contention urged by him but a careful analysis of that decision leaves no room for doubt that while issuing direction for reconsideration of petitioner's case, the Court did not examine the issue in the light of the principle that a minor dependent cannot seek ex gratia employment after a gap of many years.
To that extent judgment in Surinder Pal's case (supra) will have to be read subject to the judgments of Apex Court and proposition Nos. (h), (i) and (j) laid down in Gurdarshan Singh's case (supra).
12.
The argument of Shri Sharma that the instructions issued subsequent to the submission of application by his mother could not be applied to the case of the petitioner merits rejection because :-  (i) the application filed by Smt. Pushpa Devi 4 years prior to the petitioner's attaining the majority was simply not maintainable because at that time he was not eligible to be appointed in the government service for which the minimum age is 18 years, and (ii) in Bijender Singh v. State of Haryana (supra), an identical submission has been rejected by this Court in the following words :- "Having given thoughtful consideration to the observations of the learned Single Judge, we are inclined to agree with him that the mere submission of the application for compassionate appointment does not create a vested right in favour of the applicant and the instructions which are in force at the time of consideration of the case for compassionate appointment will have to be applied for deciding the claim made by the dependent of the deceased employee for compassionate appointment.
A careful reading of the judgment in Surinder Singh v. State of Haryana and others, 1996(2) SLR 230 shows that the peculiar facts of that particular case influenced the Court's observations that the instructions contained in the circular dated 851995 cannot be applied retrospectively so as to prejudicially affect the right of the petitioner to be considered for appointment on compassionate ground.
In that case, the Court had noted that the petitioner's mother had applied for compassionate appointment of her son on 9.5.1994 and a vacant post of Chowkidar-cum-Mali was available since 941994.
However, the consideration of the application of the petitioner's mother was delayed for almost one year and after coming into force of the circular dated 851995, the application was rejected on the basis of those instructions.
Moreover, in view of the observations made in Umesh Kumar Nagpal's case (supra); HSEB v. Naresh Tanwar (supra) and also the law laid down in LIC v. Asha Ram Chander Ambedkar, 1994(2) SCC 718; State of Bihar v. Samsuz Zoha, 1996(4) SCC 546 : 1996(4) SCT 51 (SC) and Hindustan Aeronautics Ltd v. A Radhika Thirumalai, JT 1996(9) SC 197 : 1997(1) SCT 329 (SC), it must be held that the policy of compassionate appointment does not confer a right upon the dependent of the deceased employee to be appointed to the service and the judgment in Surinder Singh v. State of Haryana and others, 1996(2) SLR 230 will have to be read as confined to the peculiar facts of that case and it cannot be treated as a precedent for claiming a right to be appointed de hors the instructions which are in force at the time of consideration of the application.
We may add that paragraph 1 of the instructions issued by the government vide circular letter dated 3181995 makes it clear that all pending cases are to be decided in the light of the new policy irrespective of the date of death of the deceased employee.
In view of the latest instructions, no right can be claimed by the dependent of the employee that his case should be considered under the old instructions."
On the basis of the above discussion, we hold that rejection of the petitioner's claim for compassionate appointment cannot be declared illegal.
13.
However, there is considerable force in the submission of the learned counsel that due to delayed communication of the decision, the petitioner's mother has also been deprived of the opportunity to seek her own appointment on compassionate grounds.
Admittedly, the application submitted by petitioner's mother for latter's appointment was kept pending for a period of four years and 7 months.
If she was made aware of the decision of the competent authority immediately after the submission of application for compassionate appointment of the petitioner, Smt. Pushpa Devi could legitimately apply for her own appointment.
However, the delayed communication of decision has prevented her from making application within 3 years of the demise of her husband.
In these circumstances, we feel that ends of justice will be met if Smt. Pushpa Devi is given an opportunity to seek appointment of compassionate grounds.
14.
For the reasons mentioned above, the writ petition is dismissed subject to the direction that if within 3 months of the receipt of certified copy of this order Smt. Pushpa Devi makes an application for her own appointment on compassionate grounds, then the competent authority shall consider and decide the same on merits within next 4 months ignoring the fact that it has been filed more than 3 years after the demise of her husband.
15.
A copy of this order be given to the learned Advocate General, Haryana in order to enable the government to examine the desirability of issuing instructions to the Heads of Departments and the Heads of Offices requiring them to communicate the family of the deceased government servants about the entitlement of one of the eligible dependents to be appointed on compassionate grounds.
This will go a long way to obviate the hardships caused to the family of the deceased employee due to lack of knowledge about the relevant policy decisions of the government.
Petition dismissed.
