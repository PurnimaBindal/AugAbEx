M.H.S.
Ansari J - The case of the petitioner is that he served the Indian Army since March, 1963 for fifteen (15) years and seven (7) months and retired in August, 1978.
Thereafter, the petitioner was recruited by the Director General, CISF as a Security Guard.
The pay of the petitioner in the revised scale in terms of 4th Pay Commission was fixed in the scale of 825-1100 and determined at Rs. 920/- per month.
The 4th Pay Commission also recommended the revision of Army Pension which came to be fixed at Rs. 375/- per month in the case of the petitioner and he has been drawing the said pension without any Dearness relief on the Army Pension.
The 2nd Respondent by his Letter No. F-20099/Misc./SHAR/Acts/92/4791, dated 28.8.1992 observed that in the revised scale, the pay of the petitioner should have been fixed at Rs. 855/- instead of Rs. 920/- for the reason that the petitioner's pension from Army has been enhanced.
Recovery of over payment at Rs. 264/- per month was started from June, 1992.
Consequently upon re-fixation of pay by the said order dated 28.8.1992, it was directed that for the remaining period of service of the petitioner i.e., 54 months, a total sum of Rs. 20,231/- should be recovered and the 1st instalment was fixed at Rs. 393/- and the remaining 53 instalments at Rs. 376/- with effect from, September, 1992.
This order has been questioned in the above writ petition.
2.
In the counter, it is stated that the petitioner had submitted an application with a request to relax the time for giving option as per Ministry of Finance OM dated 13121983 so that the petitioner could come under the purview of OM as he was not asked to give option earlier.
The said request regarding extension of time limit for giving fresh option was rejected as it was belated.
Insofar as re-fixation of pay is concerned, it is stated that the pay fixation case of the petitioner was taken up and as approved by Force Headquarters, New Delhi, the pay was re-fixed in the following manner :-  ``His pay has been re-fixed at Rs. 920/- less 263-91 i.e., 65609''.
The respondents rely upon the instructions of Government of India letter dated 1191987 Air Force Quarters, New Delhi letter dated 2741989 wherein it has been instructed that if the revised pension is not taken into consideration while fixing the pay of Ex-Army pensioner with effect from 1186, certain unintended benefits are likely to accrue to re-employed pensioners as they will draw the revised amount of pension which would invariably be higher than the earlier amount of pension, in addition to pay already fixed on the basis of pension granted to them earlier.
3.
It is further stated that a decision was taken by Government that the pay of the pensioners who were in re-employment on 1186 be re-fixed with effect from 111986 by taking into account revised pension and the over payment already made be recovered/adjusted as deemed necessary.
The pay in respect of the petitioner was re-fixed in the revised scale at Rs. 920/- as per the recommendation of the 4th Pay Commission and his Army pension was already enhanced to Rs. 375/- per month with effect from, 111986.
The over payment arising on account of enhanced pension with effect from 111986 was calculated and recovery of over payment has been effected at the rate of Rs. 393/- as the first instalment and at the rate of Rs. 376/- per month for the remaining 53 instalments from the salary of the petitioner with effect from September, 1992.
According to the respondents, the pay of the petitioner has been re-fixed at Rs. 920/- less Rs. 26391 ps i.e. Rs. 65609 ps.
as per rules and instruction of Government of India in its Memo dated 1191987.
4.
The case on hand is directly covered by a judgment of Supreme Court in Union of India and others v. G Vasudevan Pillay and others, 1995(2) SCC 32 : 1995(2) SCT 842 (SC).
The Supreme Court in that case was dealing with a conglomeration of appeals wherein three questions were required to be decided.
The third question in that case which needed decision is as follows :  ``(3) Reduction of pay equivalent to enhanced pension of those ex- serviceman who were holding civil posts on 111986, following their re- employment, is permissible or not.''
The Supreme Court considered three questions seriatim.
We are concerned in the instant case with question No. 3, extracted above, which was considered by the Supreme Court as under.
5.
The Supreme Court considered the Office Memorandum dated 1191987 which is mainly relied upon by the respondents in the instant case also.
According to the Memorandum dated 1191987, the pay of ex-servicemen who were in employment in civil post as on 111986 following their re-employment is required to be reduced by an amount equivalent to the enhanced pension made available pursuant to the report of the 4th Pay Commission.
The ground of attack in that case was that the aforesaid decision violates Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution inasmuch as there is no rational basis for classifying the employees for the aforesaid purpose on the basis of their being in employment on 111986.
The Apex Court noted the submission that because the reduction of the aforesaid nature has not been made in respect of those who have been in employment since 111986.
It was noted that the pensioners who are re-employed after 111986 enjoy the benefit of revised pay and also revised pension with effect from 111986.
The Supreme Court held as under :  ``14...., we find no logic and basis for classifying the re-employed persons on the basis of their being in employment on 111986.
Indeed, no justification has been canvassed before us.
The decision which held that field before the impugned memorandum in not taking note of pension while fixing pay of the ex-serviceman on re-employment, which was based on good reasons, had no good reason for its reversal, as enhanced pension was not confined to those who were in employment on 111986.
The impugned decision is, therefore, arbitrary and is hit by Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
We, therefore, declare the same as void.''
(emphasis supplied)  6.
The Government Memorandum dated 1191987 has been declared void in the judgment supra cited.
The position as obtaining prior to the said Memorandum dated 1191987 has been held to be good and has therefore to be given effect to.
7.
In the light of the above the writ petition is accordingly allowed and the action of the respondents in re-fixing the salary of the petitioner by deducting the enhanced army pension drawn by him is illegal and is accordingly declared void.
8.
A consequential direction shall accordingly issue to the respondents to pay the petitioner, the salary without taking note of the enhanced pension drawn by the petitioner from the Army and to release all deductions made from the salary of the petitioner with effect from September, 1992.
Time for the said purpose is granted upto six months from the date hereof within which period, the petitioner's salary shall be re-fixed and deductions made shall be released within the stipulated period.
9.
With the directions as above, the writ petition is accordingly allowed.
But, in the circumstances without costs.
Petition allowed.
