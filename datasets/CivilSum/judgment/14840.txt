RL Anand, J - Shri Deepak Aggarwal, Ex. Development Officer, C.B.O.-III of the Oriental Insurance Company has given challenge to the Enquiry Report dated 2471997 (Annexure P-18), order dated 2691997 (Annexure P-24) vide which he was removed from service; order dated 13.2.1998 (Annexure P-28) rejecting his appeal Annexure P-15, filed by the petitioner and the order dated 461999 (Annexure P-33) passed by respondent No. 2 treating them as illegal, arbitrary and unjustified.
2.
The petitioner was charge-sheeted and the gist of the charge-sheet runs as follows :  1.
He with mala fide intention issued Covernote No. 657541 on 2431991 covering Bus No. CHW 8865 by making the coverage of risk effective from 2231991 in back date in order to bring the loss caused to the said vehicle and its passengers by terrorists in the evening of 2231991 within the purview of the said covernote and to camouflage the misdemeanour, he tampered/altered the dates on the previous covernotes to adjusts the covernote No. 657541 thereby exposed the Company to an unwarranted litigation/liability.
2.
He issued Covernote No. 657541 covering Bus No. CHW-8865 for comprehensive risk without a) physical inspection of vehicle and/or b) obtaining the duly completed proposal form when there was a break in insurance thereby flouted well established under-writing norms to the company in this regard.
He by this above dishonest act failed to maintain absolute integrity, devotion to duty, exhibited conduct unbecoming of Public Servant and acted in a manner prejudicial to the interest of the Company thereby violated Rule 3(1), Rule 4(1), (5) of General Insurance (CDA) Rules, 1975."
3.
The enquiry report was given and Shri Deepak Aggarwal was found guilty of issuing Covernote No. 657541 on 2431991 covering Bus No. CHW 8865 by making the coverage of risk effective from 2231991 in back date.
He was also found guilty of tampering/altering the dates on the previous Covernote Nos. 657536, 37 and 38 to adjust the Covernote No. 657541.
He issued the Covernote No. 657541 without physical inspection of the vehicle.
However, the charge of not obtaining a completed proposal form was not proved.
It was also recommended by the Enquiry Officer that the company may consider the viability to proceed against Shri G.B.S.
Kathuria and Shri Dalip Singh.
The Enquiry Report was supplied to the petitioner with a show cause notice.
He gave the reply to the show cause notice and finally the order of removal was passed.
Appeal was filed which was dismissed.
After losing the legal battle in the domestic proceedings, the present writ petition has been filed mainly on the ground that the impugned orders were passed without basis of any evidence and it is not proved in the departmental proceedings remotely that the covernote was antedated.
The allegations of the petitioner were denied by the Company which tried to justify the finding of the Enquiry Officer as well as the Appellate Authority.
4.
I have heard Shri Anuj Raura, Advocate, on behalf of the petitioner and Shri SC Pathela, Advocate, on behalf of the respondents and with their assistance, have gone through the record of the case.
5.
We will know that in the departmental proceedings the writ jurisdiction in the High Court is confined to prove as to whether there is any illegality in conducting the inquiry and on account of the alleged illegality, whether the prejudice has been caused to the delinquent official so as to set aside the findings of the Enquiry Officer as well as the Punishing Authority/Appellate Authority/Revisional Authority.

Again Covernote No. 657537 bears interpolation.
It was issued on 23rd March, 1991 but its date was again changed to 22nd March, 1991 so as to give genuinity to Covernote No. 657541.
Further, Covernote No. 657538 was changed and initially the date was 23rd March, 1991 and later on it was changed to 22nd March, 1991.
Covernote No. 657539 was again interpolated by changing the date of its validity.
It was subsequently cancelled.
Covernote No. 657540 pertains to Bus No. CHW 6065.
The petitioner tried to become cleaver.
This Covernote, in fact, pertains to the same insurer.
Just to show that everything was in order, the disputed Covernote No. 657541 was issued.
It is the case of the parties that Bus No. CHW 8865 caught fire on 22nd March, 1991.
6.
The frontal argument of the learned counsel for the petitioner is that the premium was also deposited by the petitioner on 22nd March, 1991 in the office and, therefore, there is no question of any interpolation.
The submission of the learned counsel for the petitioner cannot be accepted.
The person to whom the amount was allegedly paid has stated that the endoursment on the cover- note does not bear his signature though the other official tried to show that the entry "received late, initials, 22/3" is in the hand of Shri G.B.S.
Kathuria, Manager.
The petitioner has tried to become over smart and he tried to give a dodge to the Insurance Company in order to favour of the insurer.
In fact, the covernote was issued on 24th March, 1991 as is evident from the various other covernotes issued by the petitioner.
It was antedated 22nd March, 1991 in order to cause loss to the Insurance Company.
In these circumstance, it cannot be said that it was a case of no evidence.
Moreover, the petitioner has also confessed at one point of time during the proceedings about his alleged involvement.
Be that as it may, I am of the opinion that it cannot be said that it is a case of no evidence, rather it was a case of evidence.
7.
Faced with this difficulty, learned counsel for the petitioner has relied upon Ministry of Finance and another v. SB Ramesh, 1998(1) SCT 738 (SC) : 1998(1) R.S.J.
526 in which it was held that the departmental enquiry cannot be proved when the findings of the Enquiry Officer were rendered on surmises and presumptions and when the documents were not properly proved or accepted.
The judgment is distinguishable on facts.
I have already tried to show that the due procedure has been complied with by the departmental inquiry.
The Enquiry Officer has taken into consideration the other covernotes (bearing Nos. 657536 to 657541) issued by the petitioner.
This is a clear case of interpolation.
The counsel for the petition has also relied upon Messrs Fabril Gasosa v. Labour Commissioner and others, JT 1997(2) SC 171.
This judgment is not applicable to the facts in hand.
Reliance was also placed upon Shiv Charan v. Superintendent of Police Gurgaon, 1998(3) SCT 255 (P&H) : 1998(3) R.S.J.
151.
In this case, it was held that if the finding recorded by the inquiring authority held to be based on a total misreading of the evidence and perverse, such finding could not form basis for imposing the extreme penalty of dismissal.
This judgment is again not applicable to the facts in hand in view of the discussions made above.
Resultantly, there is no merit in this writ petition and the same is hereby dismissed with no order as to costs.
Petition dismissed.
