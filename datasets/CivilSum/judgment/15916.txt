DK Trivedi, J - By means of the present petition the petitioner has challenged the order of removal from service dated 17.12.1993 received on 22.12.1993 contained in Annexure No. 31 to the writ petition.
The petitioner further prays for a writ of Mandamus commanding the opposite parties to allow the petitioner to continue in service.
Apart from this the petitioner has also prayed for a writ of quo warranto against opposite party No. 4 challenging the authority and questioning the holding of post as Managing Director of UP Hill Electronics Corporation.
At the time of argument relief No. (a) writ of quo warranto was not pressed and, therefore, we are not considering the said relief while disposing of this writ petition.
2.
The brief facts of the case are that the petitioner was appointed as Steno Typist in UP Electronics Corporation Ltd (UPTRON) on 871982.
Initially he was kept on probation for one year but he was confirmed within six months.
The petitioner thereafter was confirmed on the post of Stenographer in July, 1985.
It is further pointed out that the petitioner again was promoted to the post of Junior Personal Secretary in July, 1986.
It is not disputed that the Government in order to promote the development of electronic units in hill region formed new corporation namely UP Hill Electronics Corporation (HILTRON).
The petitioner's services were transferred to the HILTRON on 26101986 and he had also been sanctioned a sum of Rs. 150/- per month in addition to his salary as special allowance.
The petitioner thereafter was promoted to the post of Private Secretary to the Chief Executive in March, 1987.
It is not disputed that the petitioner's services were permanently absorbed in the UP Hill Electronics Corporation (HILTRON) vide order dated 8.5.1990.
It is said that in 1993 one Nauneet Mathur was transferred to Ram Nagar but he filed a writ petition and obtained a stay order.
In these circumstances, the petitioner was transferred temporarily to see the functions of Ram Nagar in February, 1993.
It is also alleged that during his temporary posting, the petitioner was directed to report to Shri Bharat Sinha, Deputy Manager at the Headquarters Lucknow.
The petitioner joined the post at Ram Nagar on 15.2.1993.
It is said that on 18.3.1993 the petitioner fell ill and, therefore, he moved leave application for 15 days.
He again applied for extension of the leave from 341993 to 9.4.1993.
The petitioner was not well; hence he again moved an application for extension of medical leave from 1041993 to 17.4.1993 and finally from 1841993 to 2051993 vide letters dated 20.4.1993 and 2341993.
It is not disputed that the petitioner was granted leave up to 9.4.1993.
The application of the petitioner for leave from 1041993 to 17.4.1993 is alleged to have been rejected by the authorities concerned on 1541993.
It is stated by the petitioner that letter dated 1541993 rejecting the application for medical leave of the petitioner was received by the petitioner on 461993.
It is not disputed that the applications for leave by which the leave was prayed for from 1841993 to 2051993 vide applications dated 20.4.1993 and 2341993 have not been disposed of and no orders have been passed on the said applications.
It is also not disputed that the petitioner has not been informed that his application for leave from 1841993 to 2051993 cannot be entertained.
The petitioner was admittedly suspended on 151993.
As the petitioner was not paid salary for the months of March and April, 1993, therefore, he moved an application and on the said application orders have been passed that petitioner's pay for March and April, 1993 be sent to Ram Nagar vide letter dated 1061993 contained in Annexure No. 25.
As pointed out above, the petitioner's leave was granted up to 9.4.1993, therefore, he was charge-sheeted for over-staying from 1041993 to 30.4.1993 and was finally removed from service on the basis of over-staying as mentioned above.
The above mentioned facts are not disputed except the counsel for the opposite parties contended that the letter rejecting the medical leave was sent through registered post to the petitioner on 1641993 but the opposite parties have no information about the service of the said letter on the petitioner.
On the other hand, the petitioner has categorically stated that he had received the said letter on 461993.
On the basis of the above mentioned facts, the counsel for the petitioner contended that even accepting the fact that the petitioner has over-stayed 20 days, the order of removal from service is a harsh one and the opposite parties committed an error in awarding such harsh punishment.
According to the petitioner, he could be punished by awarding some minor punishment.
Apart from this, it is also contended by the petitioner's counsel that no enquiry has been conducted in this case nor the petitioner was informed of any date or place of the enquiry.
It is also contended by the learned counsel for the petitioner that the impugned order of removal from service contained in Annexure No. 31, is, on the face of it, illegal as the authority concerned has considered extraneous matters also.
He pointed out that after receipt of the enquiry report and the reply, the authority concerned made enquiry from Bharat Sinha and thereafter passed an order on the basis of the said enquiry.
According to him this enquiry from Bharat Sinha and taking into account the report of Bharat Sinha, is nothing except to show that the order was passed on consideration of extraneous matters.
3.
We have heard the learned counsel for the petitioner as well as the Standing Counsel at great length.
The parties counsel agree that the present writ petition can be disposed of on the question of punishment alone without entering the merits of the case.
4.
The question involved in this case is as to whether even accepting the case of the State that the petitioner over-stayed for 20 days is itself sufficient to award punishment of removal from service or not specially when the petitioner has already applied for extension of medical leave.
As pointed above, the petitioner has already moved applications one after the other for leave.
It is also not disputed that the authorities concerned have also accepted the said applications and granted leave including medical leave up to 9.4.1993.
It is stated that after 9.4.1993, leave cannot be granted to the petitioner.
The application for leave from 1041993 to 17.4.1993 was alleged to have been rejected by the order dated 1541993 but the said letter was received by the petitioner on 461993.
The fact that he received the said letter on 461993 is not specifically controverted.
The opposite parties have also not said that the petitioner received a letter before 461993.
At the time of arguments learned counsel for the opposite parties tried to say that registered letter was sent to the petitioner on 1641993 and the same was tendered before the petitioner on 2141993 and 22.4.1993 by the Postman.
The petitioner's counsel further tried to say that the Postman made an endorsement on the said envelope that the petitioner was not available nor he has come out from his house.
There is nothing on record to show that the Postman has made any such entry and secondly there is no explanation as to how the opposite parties were in possession of the envelope which has already been handed over to the petitioner on 461993.
In any case no such entries are on record hence the same cannot be considered.
In these circumstances, it is proved that the petitioner was ill and his application for grant of leave was accepted by the authorities up to 9.4.1993.
It is also proved that the petitioner thereafter moved an application for extension of his leave from 1041993 to 17.4.1993 and thereafter further extension of leave from 1841994 to 2051993 vide applications dated 20.4.1993 and 2341993.
As pointed out above the petitioner did not appear to be aware of the fact that his application for leave from 1041993 to 17.4.`993 was refused and admittedly no orders have been passed on the applications dated 20.4.1993 and 2341993 by which the petitioner sought medical leave from 1841993 to 2051993.
In these circumstances it cannot be said that the petitioner had willfully and intentionally flouted the orders of the opposite parties.
There is nothing on record to show that the petitioner was aware of the order of rejection and intentionally flouted the orders.
On the other hand by letter dated 1061993 the petitioner was informed that his pay of March and April 1993 has already been sent to Ram Nagar.
There is nothing on record to show that full pay for the month of April, 1993 was not sent to Ram Nagar.
In these circumstances it is not possible for us to hold that the petitioner was fully aware of the orders of rejection of leave application by the opposite parties.
The petitioner is a confirmed employee and as stated above, his service record was also found good.
Keeping these facts in mind, in our opinion, over- staying for 20 days by the petitioner cannot be said to be intentional and therefore, the punishment of removal from service, in our opinion is harsh one.
In these circumstances, in our opinion, the major penalty of removal from service was not called for and minor punishment will be adequate punishment.
The present view as enumerated by us finds full support from the case of Union of India v. Giriraj Sharma, 1994 Supplement (3) SCC 755 : AIR 1994 SC 215, in which the apex Court took a view that by awarding punishment of dismissal from service on the charge of overstaying for 12 days as harsh one.
In the case of Ashok Kumar v. Union of India, 1988(2) SLR 209 the Hon'ble Supreme Court has specifically observed that punishment of termination of service imposed by the authorities is grossly disproportionate to the charge which was to the effect that the employee had absented himself from duty for 3 days without leave.
5.
Apart from this, it is not disputed that the punishing authority has considered the extraneous matters also at the time of passing of the impugned order.
Therefore, the impugned order, on the face of it, is illegal.
The mentioning of fact that the petitioner met with Bharat Sinha and gave an undertaking to Bharat Sinha for joining the post at Ram Nagar within a week itself shows that the authorities concerned have considered the extraneous matters.
6.
In the result, the writ petition is allowed and a writ of Certiorari is issued quashing the impugned order dated 17.12.1993 contained in Annexure No. 31 and further a writ of Mandamus is issued directing the reinstatement of the petitioner in service with monetary benefits.
However, it will be open to the authorities concerned if it so desires to pass fresh orders in the light of the observations made above and in accordance with law.
The opposite parties are further directed to reinstate the petitioner forthwith and pay him arrears within two months from the date of receipt of a copy of this order.
There will be no orders as to the costs.
Petition allowed.
