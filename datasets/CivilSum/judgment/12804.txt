GS Singhvi, J - This petition has been filed to quash the order (Annexure P-18) dated 2041992 and to direct the respondent to grant the selection grade to the petitioners with effect from 111987.
2.
In order to decide the claim of the petitioners, it would be useful to take cognizance of some facts.
Petitioner No. 1, Darshan Singh Sandhu, was accepted as Naib Tehsildar candidate w.e.f.
151955.
He was promoted as Tehsildar on 29.6.1962.
He was appointed to Punjab Civil Service (Executive Branch) from Register A-1 wef.
2.7.1979 against a vacancy which occurred in the year 1974.
Petitioner No. 2 Baj Singh Bhullar, was appointed as Naib Tehsildar on 851955.
He was promoted as Tehsildar on 22.9.1961.
He was appointed to Punjab Civil Service (Executive Branch) on 2.7.1979 against the vacancy of the year 1974.
Petitioner No. 3, Baldev Singh, was appointed as Naib Tehsildar on 1661970.
He was promoted as Tehsildar on 31.5.1979 and then to Punjab Civil Service (Executive Branch) on 2.7.1979 against the vacancy of 1974.
Petitioner No. 4, Hardip Singh Sandhu, was appointed as Naib Tehsildar on 1.10.1955.
He was promoted as Tehsildar on 5.5.1972 and then to Punjab Civil Service (Executive Branch) on 2.7.1979 against the vacancy of 1974.
Petitioner No. 5 Om Prakash, was appointed as Naib Singh on 10101960.
He was promoted as Tehsildar in May, 1976 and then to Punjab Civil Service (Executive Branch) on 2.7.1979 against the vacancy of 1975.
Petitioner No. 6 Harbhajan Singh Sodhi, was appointed as Naib Tehsildar in the year 1960.
He was promoted as Tehsildar on 29.4.1975 and then to Punjab Civil Service (Executive Branch) against the vacancy of the year 1975.
3.
For grant of selection grade in terms of provisions contained in Rule 24 of the Punjab Civil Service (Executive Branch) (Class 1) Rules, 1976 (hereinafter referred to as the `1976 Rules'), the petitioners filed Civil Writ Petition No. 15531 of 1989.
The learned Single Judge disposed of the Writ Petition on 17.2.1992 by directing the respondent to decide the matter within two months.
In compliance of this direction, the Chief Secretary, Government of Punjab, passed the impugned order (Annexure P-18) and rejected the claim made by the petitioner for grant of Selection Grade w.e.f.
111987.
4.
The petitioners have pleaded that in terms of the 1976 Rules as well as the instructions issued by the Government of Punjab vide Memo No. 8/30/75- FR(5)/1845 dated 331980, they have acquired a legal right to be given Selection Grade and the respondent has acted illegally in not giving the said benefit to the petitioners.
5.
Claim of the petitioner has been contested by the State of Punjab on the ground that the Selection Grade to the officers of Punjab Civil Service (Executive Branch) is admissible on completion of 12 years service from the first day of the January of the year of allotment and as the petitioners have been allotted the year 1979, they have no legal right to the grant of the Selection Grade w.e.f.
1987.
It has been stated in the reply that the petitioners are not entitled to count their service from the year of vacancy.
The respondent has pleaded that persons from Register B were appointed in the year 1979 and, therefore, the year allotted to the petitioner is 1979 as per the provisions of Rule 24(5) of the 1976 Rules, and those who have completed 12 years service calculated with reference to the year 1979 have been granted the benefit of Selection Grade.
The respondent has pleaded that rejection of the claim made by the petitioners does not suffer from any legal infirmity because none of the petitioners fulfils the conditions of eligibility laid down in the Rules or the Circular dated 331980.
6.
Before proceeding further, we deem it proper to mention that as on the date of filing of this writ petition, petitioners Nos. 1 and 2 stood retired from service.
Remaining petitioners have been given Selection Grade w.e.f.
111991.
Rule 3 of the 1976 Rules lays down that the service shall consist of such number of posts as the Government may from time to time determine.
The proviso to this Rule empowers the Government to increase or reduce the number of posts.
Rule 7 provides for appointment from amongst accepted candidates whose names are entered in the Register of accepted candidates.
Rule 8 specifies the different Registers of accepted candidates which are required to be maintained by the Chief Secretary.
Register A-1 contains the names of Naib Tehsildar who are accepted as candidates.
Register B contains the name of persons accepted as candidates as a result of competitive examination.
Rule 9 lays down the procedure for selection of the candidates for Register A-1.
Rules 12 to 14 contain the procedure for selection of candidates for Register B Rule 24, as it stood prior to the amendment made vide notification dated 29.8.1988 provided for grant of Selection Grade and Senior Selection Grade.
Sub-rule (4) of Rule 24 contained a provision that the number of appointments in the Selection Grade shall not exceed 20% of the total number of appointments on the sanctioned cadre.
It also laid down that the number of appointments on the sanctioned Grade shall not be less than 45 and the number of appointments in the senior selection grade shall not be more than 45.
With effect from 111978 the Selection Grade and the Senior Selection Grade were merged, but the number of Selection Grade posts continued to be 90.
For the purpose of this writ petition, it would be useful to reproduce below the extract of Rule 24, as it stood prior to the amendment of 1988:-  ``24(1) xx xx xx xx xx (2) Members of the service shall be eligible for promotion, permanently or provisionally or officiatingly, in the following selection grade or senior selection grade, as the case may be:- (a) Selection Grade: Rs. 1300-50-1500 (b) Senior Selection Grade : 1500-50-1700 Provided that the pay of a member of the service who, on the date of his promotion to the Senior Selection Grade, was drawing pay at the maximum of the ordinary Selection Grade, shall be regularised as under:- (i) Pay shall be fixed at the next stage in the Senior Selection Grade where pay at the maximum of the ordinary Selection Grade has been drawn for more than one year; (ii) Period of service amounting to less than one year rendered at the maximum of the ordinary selection grade shall count for purposes of increment in the Senior Selection Grade; and (iii) the next increment in the Senior Selection Grade would accrue after rendering the requisite qualifying service in the grade.
Note : The pay scale mentioned in sub-rule (2) for the Selection Grade has been given with effect from 1st February, 1968.
(3) xx xx xx xx xx  (4) the number of appointments in the Selection Grade shall not exceed twenty per cent of the total number of appointments on the sanctioned cadre; Provided that: (a) the number of appointments in the Selection Grade shall not be less than 45, and the number of appointment in the Senior Selection Grade shall not be more than forty-five; and (b) no member of the service shall be eligible for appointment: (i) to the Selection Grade unless he has entered the thirteenth year of service; and (ii) to the Senior Selection Grade unless he has entered the seventeenth year of service; calculated from the first day of January of the year of allotment assigned to him under sub-rule (5).
``(5) The year of allotment of an officer appointed to the service from Register B shall be the year in which he is appointed and the year of allotment of an officer appointed from other Register shall be the same as that of the officers appointed to the Service from Register II against their corresponding quota of vacancies.''
Amendment Rule 24(2), (4) and (5) are also reproduced below:- ``24(2) The members of the Service shall be eligible for promotion, permanently, provisionally or on officiating basis to such selection Grade, as may be determined by the Government from time to time.
(3) xx xx xx xx xx (4) The number of appointments in the selection grade shall be such percentage of posts, as may be fixed by the Government from time to time.
(5) The criteria of eligibility for appointment in the Selection Grade shall be such as may be determined by the Government from time to time.''
7.
After coming into force the 1976 Rules, the Government issued instructions vide Circular dated 331980 laying down the criteria for grant of Selection Grade in the cadre of various services.
This Circular reads as under:-  ``No. 8/30/75-FR(5)/1845 Government of Punjab Department of Finance (Finance Regulations) Chandigarh, the 3rd March, 1980.
To  All Heads of Departments; Commissioners of Divisions; Registrar, High Court of Punjab and Haryana;  District and Sessions Judges, and All Deputy Commissioners in the State.
Subject : Criteria for the provisions of Selection Grade in the Cadre of the various services - Implementation of the recommendations of Second Punjab Pay Commission.
Sir, I am directed to invite a reference to the subject cited above and to any time the State Government have considered the recommendations of Second Punjab Pay Commission contained in para 7-A and 914 of its report relating to this matter and in supersession of all previous instructions on this subject have decided to lay down the following guidelines for the provisions of selection grade in the cadre of the various services:- (i) Selection grade will be provided in cases where the number of higher posts (all events taken together) to which employees in a particular case can seek promotion in less time half the strength of that cadre, or where there is no promotion post at all available to a cadre or service; (ii) The number of selection grade posts at each level should not exceed 20% of the strength of the basic scale for which, the selection grade posts are to be sanctioned for the purpose of calculating the number of selection grade posts including temporary posts, which have been in existence for three years, should be taken into account; and (iii) `Selection Grade' will not be admissible to an officer unless he has completed 15 years' service.
The service should either be in the basic scale where appointment to the basic scale is from the point of fresh entry into service or to the sum total of the employees' service in the basic scale and in the one scale next below.
2.
It is, therefore, requested that the above guidelines should be kept in view while sending proposal to the Department of Finance for providing selection grade post(s) in a particular cadre and while making appointments of employees in the selection grade as well.
The proposals sent to the Department of Finance should be completed giving full details on the above mentioned points.
Yours faithfully, Sd/- (Jagjit Singh) Deputy Secretary to Government Punjab.''
8.
Reference also deserves to be made to the Circular dated 1851987 and letter dated 511988 written by the Department of Personnel and Administrative Reforms, Government of Punjab, to the Advocate General, Punjab, with reference to Civil Writ Petition No. 7395 of 1987 Harjinder Singh and others v. State of Punjab.
In the Circular dated 1851987 it has been mentioned that a junior employee may be allowed Selection Grade earlier than the senior employee but a post in the Selection Grade be kept reserved for the senior employee and the grant of Selection Grade to the junior employee will not affect the seniority of the senior employee.
Vide letter dated 511988, the Government clarified in that in computing 15 years of service, the service, if any, rendered prior to entry in PCS, has also to be taken into account.
In Para 2 of the this letter, it has been reiterated that the number of Selection Grade posts should be 20% of the substantive posts in the cadre, i.e. 34, with effect from 131987 and prior to this date the excess number of posts have been allowed by the Finance Department to cover all those officers who had already been allowed Selection Grade but could not be adjusted against the sanctioned number of posts i.e. 34.
9.
From the averments made in the writ petition, reply and the various documents placed on records, it is clear that prior to 111978 Selection Grade as well as Senior Selection Grade were admissible to the officers of the Punjab Civil Service (Executive Branch).
In all 45 posts were available for Selection Grade and another 45 posts for Senior Selection Grade.
With Effect from 111978 the two Grades were merged by the total number of posts continued to be 90.
This fact is fortified from the admission made by the respondent in para 7 of the reply filed by them to Civil Writ Petition No. 15531 of 1989 Darshan Singh and others v. State of Punjab.
In that reply, it has been admitted that the concept of 90 posts in Selection Grade remained in operation upto 2881988, i.e. the date prior to the amendment of the 1976 Rules.
This is also established from the contents of Annexure P-6.
10.
In the background of the above, it has to be examined whether the petitioners are entitled to the grant of Selection Grade w.e.f.
111987.
11.
Shri RS Dass, learned counsel for the petitioners, invited my attention to the fact that although in terms of Rule 24, as it stood upto the year 1988, 12 years of service in Punjab Civil Service (Executive Branch) was required for the purpose of grant of Selection Grade but the Government acted upon the instructions contained in Circular 331980 and gave Selection Grade by the counting 15 years of service including the service rendered in the cadre next below the Punjab Civil Service (Executive Branch).
Shri Dass pointed out that if the service rendered by the petitioners as Naib Tehsildar/Tehsildar is added, they would be deemed to have completed 15 years as on 151970, 851970, 1661985, 1101970, 10.10.1975 and 2221975, respectively and, therefore, each of the petitioners had become entitled to the grant of Selection Grade as on 111987, and when the Government has followed the instructions contained in Circular dated 331980 in the cases of to others officers, there could be no rhyme or reason to deny similar benefits to the petitioners.
Shri Dass further argued that the respondent- Government gave benefit of Selection Grade to Shri M.M.
Chaudhary, who stood at Serial No. 78, and the petitioners stood at Serial Nos. 86 to 91 and if the number of posts available for grant of Selection Grade had been correctly calculated, the respondent could not have withheld the Selection Grade admissible to the petitioners on 111987.
12.
Shri Dass further argued that total number of Selection Grade posts were 90 as on 111987 and but for the inclusion of 31 officers, who stood promoted as IAS, in Annexure P-9 which is a list of Selection Grade officers, the petitioners would have been granted the Selection Grade.
Shri Dass pointed out that names of these 31 officers have been excluded from the subsequent gradation list and if this had been done earlier, the petitioners could not have been deprived of their right to be granted the Selection Grade.
Shri Dass submitted that the Selection Grade has been denied to the petitioners w.e.f.
111987 on a wholly erroneous assumption that the number of posts in the Selection Grade was less than 90.
He argued that the respondent-Government cannot go back from the admissions made by it in the earlier litigation and the letter Annexure P-6.
The learned Deputy Advocate General argued that after the amendment of Rule 24, the total strength of the cadre was 170 and only 34 officers would be granted the Selection Grade and the petitioners who have been assigned the year 1979 in the PCS (Executive Branch) cannot be treated eligible for grant of Selection Grade.
Learned Deputy Advocate General submitted that the rejection of the claim made by the petitioners does not suffer from any illegality because the Chief Secretary has rightly observed that as per the existing rules, the Selection Grade is admissible only against 20% of the total cadre strength and only those who completed 12 years service in the PCS (Executive Branch) are eligible.
13.
A look at the extract of the reply filed by the respondent State in CWP No. 15531 of 1989, which has been referred hereinabove, shows that the Government had admitted the availability of 90 Selection Grade posts upto 2881988.
Even in Annexure P-6, which the Government wrote to the Advocate General with reference to Civil Writ Petition No. 7395 of 1987, it was admitted that excess number of selection grade posts have been allowed prior to 131987.
No doubt there is an apparent contradiction made in para 7 of the reply to CWP No. 15531 of 1989 and the letter Annexure P-6 and the reply being subsequent in point of time will carry greater weight but in any case the number of Selection Grade posts was 90 as on 2021987.
Contents of Annexure P-6 and the facts mentioned in paragraph 13 of the writ petition show that the Government had acted upon the criteria of 15 years service enumerated in the Circular dated 331980.
A number of officials, who did not complete 12 years service in the PCS (Executive Branch) have been allowed Selection Grade by computing 15 years of service in PCS (Executive Branch) and the next below grade.
There is no dispute that that the petitioners had also completed 15 years of such service as on 111987.
Therefore, it is evident that the petitioners have been arbitrarily denied the benefit of Selection Grade on the basis of an erroneous assumption regarding the applicability of Rule 24 and the Circular dated 331980.
In my opinion, the Government cannot adopt different yard-sticks for judging the eligibility of the candidates, who had become entitled to the grant of Selection Grade upto 28.2.1987 and if the names of 31 officers who are promoted as IAS are excluded from the category of those, who were granted Selection Grade, sufficient number of vacancies would be deemed to be available for grant of the Selection Grade to the petitioners w.e.f.
111987.
14.
On the basis of the above discussion, the writ petition is allowed.
Annexure P-18 is quashed with a direction that the respondents should grant the benefit of Selection Grade to the petitioners by considering their case for grant of that benefit as on 111987.
The Government shall undertake this exercise by taking the number of Selection Grade posts as 90 as on 111987.
The number of persons who have got promotion to IAS should be excluded from the consideration.
It is also made clear that if any person senior to the petitioners had also become entitled to the grant of Selection Grade his case shall be considered for such benefit irrespective of the fact that such candidates has not moved this Court.
This direction is necessary in order to obviate further litigation by senior persons.
15.
Parties are left to bear their own costs.
Petition allowed.
