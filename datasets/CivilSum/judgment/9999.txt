BN Kirpal, J (Oral) - The petitioners, who are Stenographers working in the Subordinate Courts in Delhi in the pay scale of Rs. 1640-2900, are seeking, by virtue of this writ petition, that they should be placed in the higher grade of Rs. 2000-3200.
2.
Briefly stated, the facts are that the petitioners were employed as Stenographers in the Subordinate Courts and prior to 6th January, 1977 they were in the scale of Rs. 330-560.
On 6th January, 1977 the Government of India issued a circular to the effect that pay scale of Rs. 425-700 should be allotted to the officers who were drawing pay in the scale of Rs. 1500-2000 and above.
This was followed by another circular dated 4th July, 1978 whereby pay scale of Rs. 550-900 was allotted to the post of Stenographers attached to the Heads of Department drawing a pay of Rs. 2250-2500.
Subsequently, a circulars dated 19th November, 1979 was issued by the Government to the effect that the post of Stenographers may be upgraded to Rs. 550-900 irrespective of the fact whether they have been attached to the Heads of Department or not.
3.
Those Stenographers who were working in the pay scale of Rs. 330-560 and had not been granted the benefit of the aforesaid circular dated 6th January, 1977 filed a writ petition in this Court being Civil Writ Petition No. 49 of 1984.
Vide judgment dated 10th October, 1984 it was noted that the Delhi Administration had given a revised pay scale of Rs. 425-700 to those Stenographers who were attached to officers drawing the pay scale of Rs. 1200-2000.
The Court also took note of the fact that those Stenographers in the Administration who were attached to officers in the pay scale of Rs. 2250-2500 were granted the scale of Rs. 550-900 in terms of the circular dated 4th July, 1978.
This Court accordingly directed that the petitioners therein should be given the benefit of both the circular dated 6th January, 1977 and 4th July, 1978 and they should be placed in the revised pay scale of Rs. 425-700 and Rs. 550-900.
4.
The Union of India filed an appeal against the said order.
The Supreme Court, vide order dated 29th March, 1985, remanded the case to this Court, inter alia, in order to see whether officers of the Delhi Administration in the scale of Rs. 1200-2000 did have Stenographers in the pay scale of Rs. 425-700 or not.
An affidavit was filed by the Delhi Administrative in which it was admitted that there were 39 posts of Stenographers in the pay scale of Rs. 425-700 with effect from 1st January, 1973.
This Court concluded that since 1965 Stenographers attached to the officers in the grade of Rs. 1200-2000 in Delhi Administration were put in the scale of Rs. 425-700.
The Court reiterated its earlier order and directed the payment in the scale of Rs. 425-700 pursuant to the circular dated 6th January, 1977 and also payment in the scale of Rs. 550-900 in view of the latter circular of 1978.
The aforesaid judgment of this Court, after remand, has become final.
It is clear therefrom that those of the Stenographers who were attached to the officers in the pre-revised scale of Rs. 1200-2000 were granted higher pay scale.
On the same principle, this Court also directed payment of higher scale to the Stenographers working in the Subordinate Courts.
5.
After the Fourth Pay Commission Report, fresh scales of pay were fixed in respect of the Delhi Higher Judicial Service.
As of today, there are 42 posts in the scale of Rs. 4500-5700, 11 posts in the selection grade of Rs. 5900-6700 and one post of super time scale of Rs. 5900-6700.
This means that in the grade of Rs. 5900-6700 there are 12 officers in the Delhi Higher Judicial Service who are in the said selection grade.
It is not in dispute that the Stenographers in the Delhi Administration who are attached to the officers drawing the salary in the pay scale of Rs. 5900-6700 have been placed in the pay scale of Rs. 2000-3200.
The only reason which has been stated by the Delhi Administration and the Union of India in not granting to the petitioners the higher pay scale of Rs. 2000-3200 is that 11 out of the 12 officers of the Delhi Higher Judicial Service who are in the selection grade are not Heads of the Department.
In our opinion, this is a wholly irrelevant consideration.
As has already noticed, vide a circular dated 19th November, 1979 it was clarified that the posts of Stenographers attached with officers in the pay scale of Rs. 2250-2500 should be upgraded to the pre-revised scale of Rs. 550-900 irrespective of the fact whether they were attached to the Heads of Department or not.
What held good in 1979 must, necessarily, hold good today.
It is not as if those Stenographers who were attached to persons who were in the selection grade were not given the scale of Rs. 550-900.
This scale was given to them in the Administration, and it is for this reason that the same scale was given to the Stenographers in the Subordinate Courts.
At that time there were 5 posts but the number of posts now in 12.
Denial of the higher pay scale on this irrelevant consideration cannot be upheld.
All the Judicial Officers do similar kind of work.
There is no reason as to why the higher scale should be given only to the Stenographer who is attached to the District Judge.
The officers of the Delhi Higher Judicial Service who are in the selection grade of Rs. 5900-6700 are entitled to have attached with them Stenographers who are in the grade of Rs. 2000-3200.
It is not in dispute that in the Delhi Administration also the Stenographers attached to the officers who are in the scale of Rs. 5900-6700 are in the pay scale of Rs. 2000-3200.
We may also refer to the circular dated 6th January, 1989 issued by the Government of India, Ministry of Personnel, in which it has been stated that the officers in the scale of Rs. 5900-6700 and above are entitled to have Stenographic assistance of persons who are in the pay scale of Rs. 2000-3200.
There is no requirement in this circular to the effect that the officer concerned must be the Head of the Department.
This circular issued by the Government of India has been accepted by the Delhi Administration and has been circulated amongst different departments vide its own circular dated 28th April, 1989.
It is evident therefrom that all officers in the pay scale of Rs. 5900-6700 are entitled to have Stenographers in the pay scale of Rs. 2000-3200 irrespective of the fact whether those officers are Heads of the Department or not.
The contention raised on behalf of respondents 1 and 2 that because the Additional District Judges are not the Heads of Department, therefore, the scale of Rs. 2000-3200 could not be sanctioned is not well founded.
In our opinion, the petitioner are entitled to the relied prayed for.
6.
The petition has been filed by the Association as well as two members thereof.
As the petition has been filed in the representative capacity, therefore, the relief which is to be granted cannot be restricted only to petitioners 2 and 3.
7.
We are informed that including the District & Sessions Judge, there were 8 officers in the pay scale of Rs. 5900-6700 as on 111986.
One more post in this scale was created w.e.f.
25.11.88 and three more were created w.e.f.
27689.
These posts which were created on 25.11.88 and 27689 were of the Additional District Judges in the selection grade.
The effect of this would be that 8 Stenographers would be entitled to get the higher scale of Rs. 2000-3200 w.e.f.
25.11.88 and 3 more w.e.f.
27689.
These posts of Stenographers should be designated as Senior Stenographers and the said Senior Stenographers have to be attached to those Additional District Judges who are in the pay scale of Rs. 5900-6700 besides the District & Sessions Judge.
The grant of the scale of Rs. 2000-3200 should be in accordance with the seniority of the Stenographers.
The pay of the Stenographers should be fixed in the said scale within six weeks from today and the arrears should be paid by the respondents within two months thereafter.
There will be no order as to costs.
