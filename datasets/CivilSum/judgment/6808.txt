DN Baruah, J - The question involved for determination in this Civil Rule is the date when the petitioner attained the age of superannuation i.e. 60 years.
2.
The petitioner had been working as Zonal Manager, Northern Zone, Tezpur in the Assam Co-operative Apex Bank Ltd - respondent No. 2.
He passed Matriculation examination in the year 1952 and joined the Bank on 9354.
As per the Matriculation certificate he was 18 years on 1352.
Therefore, according to the petitioner he reached the age of superannuation i.e. 60 years on 1394.
However, the petitioner received Annexure-C letter dated 3.12.93 from the 3rd respondent whereby he was informed that he would attain the age of superannuation on 28294 as per Rule 21(a) of the Staff Rule of the Bank.
On receipt of the said Annexure-C letter, the petitioner preferred an appeal before the 3rd respondent contending, inter alia, that his date of superannuation should be accepted as 1394 as per the Matriculation certificate.
The Manager (Administration) of the Bank in reply to the petitioner's appeal (vide Annexure-D), by Annexure-E letter dated 6.1.94 intimated the petitioner that the petitioner would attain the age of superannuation on 28294 and not on 1394.
On receipt of the Annexure-E letter, the petitioner submitted Annexure-F representation dated 29194 before the 3rd respondent.
In the said representation also the petitioner contended that his age of superannuation ought to be 1394.
In the representation there had been a typographical mistake regarding the date of birth.
The date of birth was wrongly shown as 28.2.34 instead of 2334 which was later on corrected, therefore, the petitioner received Annexure-G letter.
By the said letter the petitioner was informed by the 3rd respondent that after carefully going through the records it was found that the petitioner's date of superannuation would be 28294 as intimated earlier.
The petitioner, thereafter, submitted yet another representation intimating the 3rd respondent that as per the decision of the High Court his date of superannuation should fall on 1394.
However, this was not agreed to.
Hence, the petitioner has approached this Court for issuance of appropriate writ or direction for quashing and setting aside the Annexures-C and E letters and for further direction to allow the petitioner to continue till 31394.
3.
Heard both sides.
4.
Mr. C Choudhary, learned counsel appearing on behalf of the petitioner submitted that as per the Matriculation certificate the date of birth of the petitioner was 1.3.34 and, therefore, he would reach the age of superannuation only on 1394.
As the petitioner's date of birth as per the said Matriculation certificate was 1.3.34, his date of superannuation would not be 28294.
5.
Mr. HN Sarma, learned counsel appearing on behalf of respondents 2 to 4, on the other hand, submitted that the age of superannuation was correctly determined as on 28th February, 1994.
6.
In the affidavit-in-opposition filed by respondent Nos. 2, 3 and 4 it is stated that as per the Matriculation certificate of the petitioner the petitioner was 18 years on 3152.
Accordingly, the petitioner attained 60 years on 28294 and, therefore, he was notified by the Managing Director - 3rd respondent about his date of superannuation by letter dated 3.12.93.
It was further stated by the said respondents that the date of birth of the petitioner being the 1st day of March, 1934, he completed the age of 60 years on the mid night on 28294 and of 1394 he would be one day more than 60 years.
In this connection my attention was drawn to decisions of the Supreme Court in Prabhu Dayal Sesma v. State of Rajasthan, reported in AIR 1986 SC 1948 and in Secretary and Commissioner Home Department v. R Kirubakaran, reported in 1993(4) SCT 803 (SC) : AIR 1993 SC 2647.
7.
It is well settled that in computing a period of time, at any rate, when counted in years or months, no regard is generally paid to fractions of a day, in the sense that the period is regarded as complete although it is shop to the extent of a fraction of a day.
In calculating a person's age, the day of his birth should be counted as a whole day, and he attains the specified age on the day next before the anniversary of his birth day.
8.
In Prabhu Dayal (supra) the Supreme Court observed thus -  ``..........As already stated, a legal day commences at 12 O'clock midnight and continues until the same hour the following night.
It would therefore appear that the appellant having been born on January 2, 1956, he had not only attained the age of 28 years but also completed the same at 12 O'clock on the midnight of January 1,1994.
On the next day i.e. on January 2, 1994, the appellant would be one day more than 28 years..........'' 9.
In the instant case the petitioner's date of birth as per the Matriculation certificate is the 1st of March, 1934.
The decision of the Supreme Court in Secretary and Commissioner Home Department (AIR 1993 SC 2647) (supra) is, however, not applicable in the present facts and circumstances of the case.
Therefore, following the decision of the Supreme Court in Prabhu Dayal (supra) the petitioner attained the date of superannuation i.e. 60 years on the 28th day of February, 1994.
10.
In view of the above, I do not find any infirmity in the impugned Annexures-C, E and G letters.
The petition is accordingly dismissed.
However, in the facts and circumstances of the case I make no order as to costs.
Petition dismissed.
