RS Mongia, J - This judgment will dispose of Civil Writ Petition Nos. 11511 and 12785 of 1993 as common questions of law and fact are involved in both these cases.
2.
Petitioners in both these cases are working as Sectional Officers (Soil Conservation) in the Department of Agriculture, Punjab (Soil and Water Conservation and Wasteland Development).
They have challenged the following notifications/orders :-  (1) Notification dated 2731991 (Annexure P.5 in CWP No. 11511/93 and P7 in CWP No. 12785/93) issued by the State Government, whereby the pay scale of Soil Conservation Inspectors/Agricultural Inspectors/Soil Survey Assistants/Research Assistants was revised from Rs. 700-1200 to Rs. 2000-3500, but no revision in the pay scale was made so far as Sectional Officers (Soil Conservation) were concerned.
Notification dated 2471991 (Annexure P. 6 in CWP No. 11511 of 1993 and Annexure P.8 in CWP 12785 of 1993), whereby the pay scale of Sectional Officers (Soil Conservation) was revised from Rs. 700-1200 to Rs. 1800-3200 and not giving them the same pay scale as given to the other categories, mentioned in notification dated 273.
1991.
(2) Order dated 11.3.1993 (Annexure P 8 in CWP 11511 of 1993 and P. 10 in CWP 12785 of 1993) passed by the Government rejecting their representation and declining the grant of parity of pay scales with the Agricultural Inspectors/Soil Conservation Inspectors/Research Assistants etc.  (3) Notification dated 3061993 issued by the Government (Annexure P. 9 in CWP 11511 of 1993 and P 11 in CWP 12785 of 1993) whereby the disparity in the pay scale between the petitioners (Sectional Officers) and Agricultural Inspectors/Soil Conservation Inspectors etc. has been continued and they have been made ineligible for promotion to Class-II posts and 5% posts have been reserved for this category for being promoted as Agricultural Inspectors/Soil Conservation Inspectors etc. which were being designated as Soil Conservation Officers.
3.
So far as the challenge to the notification dated 3061993 is concerned, learned counsel for the petitioners has referred to Rule 8 of the Punjab Soil Conservation and Engineering Service (Class II) Rules, 1974 (hereinafter referred to as 'the Rules'), which reads as under :-  "Rule 8 : (1) Recruitment to the posts in the Service shall be made in the following manner :-  (a) Fifty per cent of the posts by direct recruitment; (b) Fifty per cent of the posts by promotion from amongst Agricultural Inspectors/Soil Conservation Inspectors, Agricultural Inspectors (Soil Survey), Soil Survey Assistants, Research Assistants and Sectional Officers (Overseers) from the subordinate service who have an experience of working on any one or more of these posts for a minimum period of five years; (c) By transfer of an officer already in Class II Service of the Government of India or of a State Government; Provided that any person appointed by transfer will be accounted for in the quota of direct recruitments; Provided further that the first vacancy shall be filled in by direct recruitment and the next by promotion and so on.
(2) Persons appointed in the Service shall be required to undergo six months' training in Soil Conservation works at the Soil Conservation and Research Institute, Dehra Dun.
(3) No person shall be appointed to a post in the Service by direct recruitment unless he possesses the qualifications specified in Appendix 'B' to these rules for that post.
(4) All appointments to the posts in Service by promotion shall be made on the basis of seniority-cum-merit and no person shall have any right of promotion merely on the basis of seniority.
(5) For the purposes of making appointment by promotion, there shall be a joint seniority list of Agricultural Inspectors, Agricultural Inspectors (Soil Survey), Soil Conservation Inspectors, Soil Survey Assistants, Research Assistants, Sectional Officers (Overseers) which shall be prepared on the basis of the continuous length of service, as such officials irrespective of the scales of pay."

5.
Learned counsel for the petitioners argued that by the impugned notification reproduced above, the following things were sought to be done :-  (1) The designation of the post of Sectional Officer was being changed to Junior Engineer.
(2) The Agriculture Inspectors/Soil Conservation Inspectors/Agriculture Inspectors (Soil Survey) / Research Assistants (Soil Survey) all were to be designated as Soil Conservation Officers.
(3) The qualifications for appointment/promotion to the posts of Soil Conservation Officers were being laid down as B.Sc.
Agril./B.Sc.
Agricultural Engineering/B.Sc.
Civil Engg.
(4) 5% of the posts of Soil Conservation Officers were to be filled by promotion from amongst the Junior Engineers in the pay scale of Rs. 1800-3200.
(5) The pay scale of the posts, which were being designated as Soil Conservation Officers, was being revised from Rs. 2000-3200 to Rs. 2200-4000 (Rs. 3000-4500 after eight years of service and Rs. 3700-5300 after 18 years of service).
There was no revision of pay scale of the Sectional Officers being designated as Junior Engineers and their pay scale remained at Rs. 1800-3200.
6.
Learned counsel for the petitioners argued that as per Rule 8(1) of the Rules, 50% of the posts in Class-II were to be filled by promotion from amongst the Agricultural Inspectors, Soil Conservation Inspectors, Agricultural Inspectors (Soil Survey), Soil Survey Assistants, Research Assistants and Sectional Officers (Overseers), who had a minimum working experience on any one or more these posts for a minimum period of five years.
Further under Rule 8(4) of the Rules, the appointments by promotion were to be made on the basis of seniority-cum-merit and under Rule 8(5) of the Rules, a joint seniority list had to be maintained of all the categories of the posts, including the Sectional Officers, who were to be considered for promotion to Class-II.
This rule was sought to be amended by a mere notification dated 3061993, which cannot be done, the argument being that the statutory rules framed under Article 309 of the Constitution cannot be amended by a mere executive order or the instructions.
By the impugned notification dated 3061993 what was sought to be done was that firstly the Sectional Officers (which were being designated as Junior Engineers) were first to seek promotion to the rank of Agricultural Inspectors, Agricultural Inspectors, (Soil Survey) etc. (which were being designated as Soil Conservation Officers), before they could be considered for promotion to Class-II Service and secondly 5% posts were being earmarked for the Junior Engineers for consideration for promotion to the posts of Soil Conservation Officers.
In other words, Sectional Officers were being made ineligible for promotion to Class-II posts and an intermediatory step was being created that Sectional Officers must get first promotion to the posts of Agricultural Inspectors etc. (Soil Conservation Officers).
It is admitted in paragraph 12 of the written statement (in CWP No. 11511 of 1993) that the department is in the process to amend the service rules in accordance with the aforesaid notification.
In the other writ petition, it has been mentioned in reply by the State Government that the impugned notification had been issued recently and the action to amend the rules is being taken.
This written statement is dated 221994.
Learned counsel for the respondents has not brought anything on record or even stated that the Class-II Rules stand amended.
7.
It is by now well settled that the statutory rules framed under Article 309 of the Constitution cannot be amended or modified by a mere executive order or instructions.
As noticed above, the impugned notification does run counter or Rules 8 of the Rules inasmuch as the class to which the petitioners belong has been ousted from consideration for promotion to Class-II posts and further they are required now first to get promoted to the rank of Agricultural Inspectors etc. (now known as Soil Conservation Officers) before they can be considered for promotion to the Class-II service.
As observed above, the State Government itself has stated in the written statement that the rules are in the process of being amended to bring them in conformity with the impugned notification.
Consequently, I am of the view that the impugned notification is liable to be struck down as being contrary to Rule 8 of the Rules.
8.
Now coming to the disparity regarding the pay scales of the posts of Sectional Officers on one side (now designated as Junior Engineers) and the other posts of Agricultural Inspectors, Soil Conservation Inspectors, Soil Conservation Assistants etc. on the other side (now known as Soil Conservation Officers).
The pay scales of Soil Conservation Inspectors/Agricultural Inspectors/Soil Survey Assistants/Research Assistants was revised vide notification dated March 27, 1991 from Rs. 700-1200 to 1800-3200, but no revision was made so far as the Sectional Officers were concerned.
Later on vide notification dated 2471991, the pay scale of Sectional Officers was revised from 700-1200 to Rs. 1800-3200 and not to Rs. 2000-3500 as was revised qua the aforesaid four categories.
It has been averred in Paragraph 3 of the Writ Petition No. 11511 of 1993 that the posts of Sectional Officers of Soil Conservation are borne on a common cadre with other posts of Agricultural Inspectors, Soil Conservation Inspectors, Soil Survey Assistants and Research Assistants.
All these five posts have a common seniority and form a joint cadre.
All incumbents of these posts are Section Incharge(s) and according to the posting a member of the cadre is designated either as Sectional Officer (Soil Conservation) or Agricultural Inspector or any of the designation of the posts, mentioned above.
Recruitment to these posts, which have different qualifications, is made at the same time and the appointments are made in a consolidated form and the inter-se seniority of the direct recruits is determined on the basis of common merit at the time of selection.
The merit position is irrespective of the posts to which they are to be appointed.
A final seniority list of the combined cadre of the above posts dated 3091980 has been attached as Annexure P2.
It has further been averred that all the five posts are interchangeable and incumbents thereof perform identical duties and have identical responsibilities.
Some orders have been attached to illustrate that the incumbents were being transferred from one post to another.
For example, in order dated 29.5.1992 (Annexure P.3) Shri Daman Davinder Singh, Sectional Officer was being transferred against Sh. Rajinder Singh Sharma (sic), Agricultural Inspectors and vice versa.
Similarly, Sh. Om Parkash, Sectional Officer was being transferred against Sh. Lal Singh, Agricultural Inspector.
It was further averred that out of the total cadre strength of 233 posts, no posts have been separately earmarked or sanctioned for any of the five categories, mentioned above.
The pay scales of all the posts had been identical i.e. Rs. 700-1200 and with effect from January 1, 1986, on the basis of the recommendations of the IIIrd Pay Commission, all these five posts were placed in the pay scale of Rs. 1640-2925.
However, vide notification dated 2731991 (Annexure P.5 in CWP No. 11511 of 1993) the four categories of posts, other than the Sectional Officers were placed in the pay scale of Rs. 2000-3500, whereas vide notification dated 2471991 (Annexure P. 6), the Sectional Officers were placed in the pay scale of Rs. 1800-3200.
Petitioners filed Civil Writ Petition No. 13611 of 1992 claiming direction to the respondent-State to grant pay scale of Rs. 2000-3500 to the Sectional Officers as had been granted to the other similarly situated employees of the four categories in their cadre.
That writ petition was disposed of on 11101992 with a direction to the State to decide the representation of the petitioners within a specified time.
The State Government vide order dated 11.3.1993 decided the representation, a copy of which has been appended as Annexure P 8 in CWP 11511 of 1993, declining the claim of the petitioners.
As already noticed above, by later notification dated 3061993, the four categories of the posts other than the Sectional Officers were granted pay scale Rs. 2200-4000 in place of Rs. 2000-3500 whereas there was revision of pay scale of the petitioners which remained at Rs. 1800-3200.
9.
In reply to the aforesaid averment, the State has admitted that a common seniority list is there for all the posts of five categories and the selections to these posts are made by a single Departmental Selection Committee.
However, it has been stated that the soil conservation is a multi-disciplinary subject and its works are got executed through two sets of personnel, one is constituted by Agricultural Inspectors, Agricultural Inspectors (Soil Survey), Soil Conservation Inspectors, Soil Survey Assistants and Research Assistants and under the other group come Sectional Officers.
The work of civil engineering nature are got executed through the Sectional Officers and the rest of the departmental works are got executed through other group.
The basic qualifications for these posts are also separate.
The Sectional Officers Soil Conservation have been equated for the purpose of pay scale with the Sectional Officers in the Engineering Departmental of the State Government.
It has not been denied that the posts are inter-changeable or that there is no specific earmarking of the posts in the different categories in the total cadre strength of 233 posts (according to the Government the posts are 243).
It has further not been denied that whenever simultaneous recruitment is done to different categories of posts, a common merit list is prepared which also determines the seniority of the direct recruits.
10.
Learned counsel for the petitioners submitted that under Rule 8(5) of the Rules, for purpose of promotion, a joint seniority of these five categories' posts, which are of the feeder cadre, is to be maintained and factually also it is the admitted position that such a joint seniority list is being maintained.
The posts are interchangeable which would show that any one of the incumbents can perform the duties of the other category of posts.
The merit list at the time of selection for the different categories of posts is a joint merit list which determines seniority for the direct recruits.
All the categories of the posts are eligible for promotion to Class-II as per Rule 8(1)(b) of the Rules on the basis of seniority-cum-merit, as per Rule 8(4) of the Rules.
The Government itself had kept the pay scale of the category of the petitioners equal to that of all the other four categories.
Further, on the basis of the recommendations of the IIIrd Pay Commission, the pay scale of all the five categories was revised to Rs. 1640-2925 with effect from January 1, 1986.
Simply because the qualifications for appointment to the different categories of posts are different is no ground to deny the parity of the pay scales to the petitioners with their counterparts i.e. the other four categories which, as observed above, had been equal prior to the issuance of the notifications dated 2731991 and 2471991 (Ann.
P.5 and P. 6 in Civil Writ Petition No. 11511 of 1993).
In support of the contention learned counsel relied on a judgment of this Court in Ramphal Thakur v. State of Haryana, 1990(2) SLR 707, wherein it was held that Coaches and District Sports Officers, who formed one cadre, had joint seniority, posts being interchangeable, was sufficient to hold that all of them were entitled to the same pay scale.
11.
Learned counsel for the respondents, on the other hand, submitted that not only that at the time of entry into service their qualifications were different, but their nature of duties are also different.
However, it cannot be spelled out either from the written statement or otherwise as to what is that in the nature of duties which is different.
It has only been stated, as observed above, that works of civil engineering nature are got executed through the Sectional Officers and the rest of the departmental works are got done through the Agricultural Inspectors etc. As observed above, the posts are inter-changeable, meaning thereby that incumbent of one post can perform the duties of the other post.
Factually there have been inter- transfers from one category to the other.
All of them are eligible for consideration for promotion to Class-II as per Rule 8(1)(b) of the Rules.
A joint seniority list of all the categories is maintained for purpose of consideration for promotion to Class-II.
That being so, there cannot be any discrimination regarding their pay scales, especially when the Government itself had been giving the same pay scales and even on the recommendations of the IIIrd Pay commission, all categories were given the same pay scales.
12.
The discrimination is writ large and is violative of equality clause as enshrined under Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India.
13.
For the foregoing reasons, these writ petitions are allowed and the notification dated July 24, 1991 (Annexure P.6 in CWP 11511 of 1993 and Annexure P 8 in CWP 12785 of 1993) whereby the pay scale of the Sectional Officers (Soil Conservation) was revised from Rs. 700-1200 to Rs. 1800-3200 is hereby quashed.
It is further held that the category of Sectional Offers (Soil Conservation) (now known as Junior Engineers) are entitled to the same pay scales with effect from the same dates as were given to the Soil Conservation Inspectors/Agricultural Inspectors/Soil Survey Assistant/Research Assistants vide notification dated March 27, 1991 and also to grant them the same pay scale as has been given to the Soil Conservation Officers vide notification dated June 30, 1993.
The arrears so calculated be released within a period of four months of the receipt of a copy of this order either from this Court or a certified copy thereof from the petitioners.
Order dated 11.3.1993 also stands quashed.
The notification dated 3061993 (Annexure P. 9 in CWP No. 11511 of 1993 and P 11 in CWP No. 12785 of 1993) is quashed to the extent it lays down that 5% of the posts of soil Conservation Officers shall be filled by promotion from amongst the Junior Engineers and also to the extent it lays down the entry qualification for the posts of Soil Conservation Officers.
It is further held that the Junior Engineers (earlier known as Sectional Officers) would be entitled to consideration for promotion to the Class-II posts in accordance with Class-II Rules along with the other categories of posts which are now known as Soil Conservation Officers.
There will be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
