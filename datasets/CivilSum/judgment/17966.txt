NK Sodhi, J - By an order dated March 28, 1989, the Director of Secondary Education, Haryana, Chandigarh, pre-maturely retired the petitioner from Government service in public interest on his attaining the age of 55 years and this was done in terms of Rule 326 of the Punjab Civil Service Rules, Vol.
I, Part 1, read with Rule 5.32A of the Punjab Civil Service Rules, Vol. II, as applicable to the State of Haryana.
It is this order which has been impugned by the petitioner in this petition under Article 226 of the Constitution of India.
2.

It is the firm stand of the petitioner that after he was allowed to cross the efficiency bar he did no earn any adverse report and on the other hand, his Annual confidential Reports w.e.f.
the year 1981-82 onwards have been mostly 'good' though somewhere 'average'.
No return has been filed on behalf of the respondents and when the matter came up for regular hearing before me on July 8, 1991, the respondents through their counsel were directed to produce the records pertaining to the petitioner in regard to his Annual Confidential Reports.
The record has been produced today and I have perused the same.

A perusal of the record indicates that the service record of the petitioner has been rather good there was no occasion for the respondents to retire him pre-maturely.
In the preceding ten years from the date when he was prematurely retired the petitioner had earned six 'good' reports whereas the other four were 'average'.
The instructions issued by the State Government whereby an employee could be retained in service after the age of 55 years only if he had at least 70% good reports in the last ten years has already been quashed by a Division Bench of this Court in KK Vaid v. State of Haryana, 1990(1) S.L.R.
1.
In view of the Annual Confidential Reports earned by the petitioner, it could not be said that he had become a dead-wood or a drone or a do-nothing sort of employee.
The impugned order of pre-mature retirement has obviously been passed without application of mind and to.
say the least, is arbitrary.
4.
For the reasons stated above, the writ petition is allowed and the impugned order pre-maturely retiring the petitioner from service is quashed and the respondents are directed to reinstate the petitioner in service within three months from today.
All consequential benefits like salary etc. to which the petitioner may be found entitled, would also be given within a period of three months.
The petitioner shall have his costs which are assessed at Rs. 500/-.
Petition allowed.
