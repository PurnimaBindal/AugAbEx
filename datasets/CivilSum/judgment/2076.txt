GR Majithia, J - This judgment disposes of Civil Writ Petition Nos. 295 and 1666 of 1987.
On May 11, 1987 the motion Bench directed thus :  "Admitted D.B.
To be heard with C.A.
(C) No. 19) of 1986."
It is how these petitions have been placed before us.
C.A.
(C) No. 19 of 1986 has been disposed of by us by a separate order of even date.
2.
In these writ petitions the petitioners have sought a mandate to the respondents not to deduct the excess payment from their salaries in view of the judgment rendered by the Supreme Court in Civil Appeals No. 31 and 32 of 1985.
3.
Reference to relevant facts has been made from Civil Writ Petition No. 295 of 1987 :-  The petitioner was appointed as a Science Master in the Masters cadre after his selection by the Punjab Subordinate Services Selection Board in the Punjab Education Department.
He joined the service on January 25, 1967.
He was initially paid a fixed salary of Rs. 110/- per mensem on the ground that he did not posses the B.Ed.
qualification and was an untrained Science Master.
The pay scale of the Masters was revised to Rs. 220-500 with effect from November 1, 1966.
On revision of the pay scale, the petitioner was allowed to draw fixed pay of Rs. 220/- in the scale of Rs. 220-500, which was allowed to the Masters.
On recommendations of the Second Pay Commission, pay scales of Punjab Government employees were revised with effect from January 1, 1978.
The State Government by its letter dated January 22, 1980 granted the pay scale of Rs. 620-1200 to Musters with effect from January 1, 1978.
The State Government again revised the pay scale of Masters from Rs. 620-1200 to Rs. 660-1200 by its letter dated February, 1983.
The revision was to come into effect from January 1, 1978.
The petitioner and other similarly situated untrained Masters were clamoring that they should be granted the same pay scale as was allowed to the regular trained Masters.
Such untrained Masters filed Civil Writ Petition No. 656 of 1977, which was allowed by a learned Single Judge of this Court by Judgment dated May 18, 1977.
This judgment was, however, reversed by a Division Bench of this Court in Civil Writ Petition No. 3674 of 1977, decided on September 12, 1979.
The respondents instead of allowing the regular running grade of Masters to the petitioner and other similarly situated untrained masters with effect from the date of their appointment decided to reduce the pay of the petitioner and other untrained teachers from Rs. 660/- fixed to Rs. 200/-.
Feeling aggrieved, the petitioner filed Civil Writ Petition No. 4159 of 1983 for a direction to the respondents to grant regular running grade of Masters to him with effect from the date of his appointment and not to reduce his salary from Rs. 660/- (fixed) to Rs. 200/-per month (fixed).
The writ petition was, however, dismissed by this Court on February 14, 1984.
Feeling aggrieved against the judgment of this Court, the petitioner moved Special Leave Petition No. 8597 of 1984 in the apex Court.
The Special Leave petition was allowed by the apex Court and treated as Civil Appeal No. 32 of 1984.
The apex court by its judgment dated December 17, 1984 allowed Civil Appeal Nos. 31 and 32 of 1985 and set aside the judgment rendered by the Division Bench of this Court in Civil Writ Petition No. 3674 of 1977.
It directed the State Government to grant running pay scale of Masters to the petitioners from the date of their appointment.
4.
Written statement has been filed on behalf of the respondents.
It was stated therein that the minimum prescribed qualifications under the statutory rules regulating the conditions for appointment of Science Masters are B.Sc.
plus B.T./B.Ed.
The petitioner did not possess the requisite qualifications.
He was appointed as B.Sc.
Science Master on a fixed pay of Rs. 110/- pm till he acquired the teaching qualification of B.T./B.Ed.
The petitioner was to get fixed salary till he acquired the professional qualification of B.T./B.Ed.
It was further stated that the claim of the petitioner for allowing him the same pay scale as was allowed to the regular grade masters was turned down by this Court.
Civil Appeal Nos. 31 and 32 of 1985 has affirmed the judgment of this court rendered in Civil Writ Petition No. 656 of 1977.
According to the Judgment, arrears of pay were to be paid under the Limitation Act.
The Department had made excess payment inadvertently and the same has to be recovered from the petitioner in accordance with law.
It was further stated that the petitioner was appointed on January 25, 1967 as untrained Science Master and till today he has not acquired the professional teaching qualification of B.Ed.
5.
The precise question which arises for determination is whether the respondents are justified in effecting recoveries from the salary of the petitioners on account of the excess payment made to them.
6.
In Civil Writ Petition No. 656 of 1977 (Tilak Raj v. The State of Punjab and another) decided on May 18, 1977, the petitioners were untrained teachers.
They moved this Court for the grant of regular running grade of Masters from the date of their original entry into service.
They supported their claim on the basis of letter No. 2524-IEB-Il-77, dated March 10, 1977, issued by the Secretary to Government, Punjab, Education Department, Chandigarh to the Director Public Instruction (Schools), Punjab.
The relevant portion of that letter is in the following terms :-  "2.
On reconsideration of the matter, in partial modification of Punjab Govt.
Memo No. 169-IED-11-77/3922, dated 2711977, the Governor of Punjab is pleased to order that all the Matric untrained teachers who were appointed on fixed pay in the post be allowed running grade of pay of JBT teachers from the date of their appointment and the arrears be paid to them under the Limitation Act."
The learned Single Judge, relying upon this letter issued directions to the State Government to grant the running grade of JBT teachers to the untrained in the light of the letter dated March 10, 1977.
The judgment rendered in Civil Writ Petition No. 656 of 1977 was disapproved by the Division Bench of this Court in Civil Writ Petition No. 3674 of 1977, decided on September 12, 1979.
The matter was taken to the apex Court through Special Leave Petitions, which were registered as Civil appeals No. 31 and 32 of 1985.
The apex Court by its judgment dated December 17, 1984 rendered in Civil Appeal Nos. 31 and 32 of 1985 held thus :-  "We find from the judgment of the Division Bench of the High Court in Writ Petition No. 3674/77 Shervinder Kaur v. State of Punjab and another, decided on 12th September, 1979 that pursuant to the decision of a Single Judge of the High Court in the case of Tilak Raj v. State of Punjab and according to the respondents, by way of concession, the State Government gave the running pay scale of master funtrained masters from the date of their appointment.
But having regard to the judgment in Shervinder Kaur's case where the decision in Tilak Raj's case was not approved by the Divisions Bench, the State Government refused to give the running pay scale of masters to the remaining untrained masters.
We do not think that the State Government was right in giving the running pay scale of masters to some of the untrained masters and denying it to others.
All the untrained masters were similarly situate and it was not open to the State Government to make the distinction between them for the purpose of giving the running pay scale.
Indeed, there was no justification at all for the State Government to make such a distinction.
If the State Government was of the view that the decision in Tilak Raj's case was wrong, the State Government should have appealed from it but once it granted the running pay scale of master to some of the untrained masters either as a result of concession or pursuant to the decision in Tilak Raj's case, it was not permissible to the State Government to deny the same running pay scale to the other untrained masters.
That was clearly violative of Article 14 of the Constitution.
We, therefore, allow the appeals set aside the order of the High Court and direct that the State Government shall give the running pay scale of masters to all untrained masters from the date of their appointment in the same manner as they did in regard to the first group of untrained masters as and by way of concession or pursuant to the decision in Tilak Raj's case.
The arrears of salary calculated on this basis shall be paid by the respondents to the untrained masters within 4 months from today.
There will be no order as to costs."
The apex Court laid down that the State Government was bound by the decision rendered in Tilak Raj and another v. The State of Punjab & another, (Civil Writ Petition No. 656 of 1977) and that it could not grant the running pay scale of Musters to some of the untrained masters and refuse to grant the same to the others.
The apex Court ordained that the State Government was obliged to grant the running pay scale of Masters to all the untrained Masters.
In Tilak Raj's case (supra), the learned Single Judge had allowed the running pay scale of JBT teachers to all the untrained teachers in the light of the letter No. 2524-IEB-II-77, dated March 10, 1977 issued by the Secretary to Government, Punjab Education Department, Chandigarh to the Director, Public Instruction (Schools), Punjab.
In that letter it was specified that arrears of pay would be paid to them under the Limitation Act, meaning thereby that the arrears of pay were to be paid for 38 months and not more.
The apex Court did not say that if any excess payment had been made inadvertently, the State Government could not withdraw the same from the employees who were made payments under a mistake.
7.
In the instant case, the respondents had made payments to the petitioners inadvertently in ignorance of the decision rendered by this Court in Tilak Raj's case (supra), which was ultimately approved by the apex Court in Civil Appeal Nos. 31 and 32 of 1985.
They could retrieve the excess payments made to them.
8.
For the reasons stated above, the writ petitions are devoid of any merit.
The same are dismissed, but with no order as to costs.
Petition dismissed.
