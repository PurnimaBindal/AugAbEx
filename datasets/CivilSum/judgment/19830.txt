RS Mongia, J - Petitioner Nos. 1 to 3 are working as Clerks with the Markfed in its Rice Mills at Rajpura whereas Petitioner No. 4 is working as Clerk in Food Technologies India Limited, Patiala.
Petitioner No. 5 is working as Clerk in the Head Office at Chandigarh.
Prior to June, 1983, all the Clerks in the Markfed, including the petitioners, were in the pay scale of Rs. 400-600.
Vide decision of the Administrative Committee dated 21st June, 1983, the pay scale of Clerks was sought to be raised from Rs. 400-600 to Rs. 510-800.
The relevant decision of the Administrative Committee dated 21st June, 1983 is reproduced as under :  "Item No. 7  To consider to grant the pay approved scale of Rs. 510-800 to all the Clerks/Typists, Asstt.
Time Keepers and Steno-Typists, who are presently working in the Markfed Plants/Units on the pay scale of Rs.
400-600."
2.
It is the case of the petitioners that in pursuance of the above-quoted decision, the Clerks working in the Oil Mills at Khanna, Kapurthala and Gidderbaha were given revised scale with effect from 21st June, 1983.
In fact, it is the case of the petitioners that the clerks working at Khanna Plant were given the revised pay scale from 17th December, 1982.
However, the petitioners and the other similarly situated Clerks were not granted the revised pay scales.
The petitioners had represented that there was no justification to deprive them of the revised pay scales.
Vide order dated 26th July, 1988 (Copy Annexure P.9) it was informed by the respondents that the matter had been considered and the prayer of the petitioners was being rejected as they had already been placed in the higher pay scale of Rs. 510-800 with effect from 29th January, 1985 by the Board of Directors of the Markfed.
3.
The case of the petitioners is that prior to the revision of the pay scale in June, 1983, all the Clerks in the Markfed were getting pay-scale of Rs. 400-600, including the Clerks in the Oil Mills.
Later on also the petitioners were again given the revised pay scale with effect from 29th January, 1985.
But only during the interregnum period from 21st June, 1983 to 28th January, 1985, they were given step-motherly treatment.
The further case of the petitioners is that they were performing the same duties now which they were performing prior to June, 1983, and, therefore, denying to them the revised pay scale as ordered by the Administrative Committee vide its decision dated 21st June, 1983 was arbitrary and discriminatory and violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India.
4.
On the other hand, Mr. Arun Nehra, learned counsel for respondent No. 3 submitted that while revising the pay scale, the Authorities were deciding only regarding the revision of pay scale of the Clerks of the Oil Mills at Khanna, Kapurthala and Gidderbaha.
For this submission he sought to derive support from the Agenda note of the meeting of Administrative Committee, on the basis of which, decision dated 21st June, 1983 was taken.
He further submitted that the duties of the Clerks in the Oil Mills, their separate seniority, profit and loss in the Oil Mills, have been taken into consideration for revising the pay scale only of the Clerks of the Oil Mills.
5.
After hearing the learned counsel for the parties at some length, I am of the view that this petitioner deserves to be allowed.
There is no denying the fact that prior to June, 1983, all the Clerks working with the Markfed, wherever working, were in the pay scale of Rs. 400-600.
During the period 21st June, 1983 to 28th January, 1985, the petitioners were not granted revised pay scale of Rs. 510-800, but with effect from 29th January, 1985 they have been given the same revised pay scale.
In other words, the reason for not granting the revised pay scale to the petitioners with effect from 21st June, 1983 was found to be no more there with effect from 29th January, 1985.
However, the fact remains that during the interregnum period also they were performing the same duties and functions as prior to June, 1983 and after 29th January, 1985.
So there was no reason to deprive the petitioners of the revised pay scale of Rs. 510-800.
Apart from that, the decision of the Administrative Committee dated 21st June, 1983, quoted above, does not confine only to the clerks, of the Oil Mills rather it talks of all the Clerks/Typists, Asstt.
Time Keepers and Steno-typists.
That being so, there was no reason to keep the petitioners as a class apart.
6.
For the foregoing reasons, I allow this petition, quash the order Annexure P-9 to the extent it deprives the petitioners of the revised pay scales of Rs. 510-800 with effect from 21st June, 1983 to 29th January, 1985.
The respondent No. 3 is directed to calculate the arrears of pay and release the same to the petitioner within three months from the date of the receipt of this order.
However, there will be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
