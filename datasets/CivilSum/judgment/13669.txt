RS Mongia, J (Oral) - Instead of passing any interim order in the Civil Miscellaneous Application, I am disposing of the main case with the consent of the learned counsel for the parties.
Petitioner joined the Department of Public Relations in the State of Haryana as Teleprinter Operator on 2nd April, 1990.
He was deemed to have been appointed as such w.e.f.
8th December, 1977, after giving him benefit of military service, which he had rendered during the period of emergency.
On 1st September, 1988, four persons, including one Smt. Inderjeet Kaur, were promoted as Assistant-cum-Accountant.
Petitioner made a grievance against the promotion of Smt. Inderjeet Kaur on the ground that though she was junior to the petitioner yet in supersession of the name of the petitioner, she had been promoted.
This representation was turned down on the ground that the petitioner did not have 70% good reports for the last ten years and in accordance with the Haryana Government instructions, he could not be promoted.
On February 16, 1990, five more persons, who were junior to the petitioner, were promoted as Assistant-cum-Accountant.
These are respondent Nos. 3 to 7.
Respondent No. 8, Sarwan Singh, who was also junior to the petitioner, was promoted as Assistant-cum-Accountant on 13th March, 1990.
Petitioner was again not promoted when respondent Nos. 3 to 8 were promoted.
This led the petitioner to file the present writ petition.
2.
In the writ petition, it was averred that in spite of the petitioner's record being good for the entire period of ten years prior to the dates of promotion of respondent Nos. 3 to 8, yet he was arbitrarily ignored.
In the written statement, it has been averred that petitioner did not have 70% good reports for the last ten years prior to the dates of promotion of respondent Nos. 3 to 8 and in accordance with the Haryana Government instructions, he could not be promoted on that ground.
It has been further stated in the written statement that the petitioner's record was as under :-  Year Overall Assessment 1980-81 Average 1981-82 Average 1982-83 Average 1983-84 Good 1984-85 Average 1995-86 Good 1986-87 Good 1987-88 Good 1988-89 Good 3.
The learned counsel for the petitioner submitted that the average reports for the years 1980-81 and 1981-82 were never conveyed to the petitioner, but only average reports for the years 1982-83 and 1984-85 were conveyed.
According to the learned counsel, the average reports for the years 1980-81 and 1981-82 could not be taken into consideration for any purpose detrimental to the petitioner as these had not been conveyed.
For this reliance was placed on the Supreme Court judgment in Brij Mohan Chopra v. State of Punjab, 1987(2) SLR 54, and on a Division Bench judgment of this Court in KK Vaid v. State of Haryana, 1990(1) S.L.R.
1.
It was also argued by the learned counsel that his record upto the date of promotion should have been considered and the respondents should have not stepped short up to the year 1988-89.
According to the allegation of the petitioner, his report for the year 1989-90 was good.
4.
In reply to the writ petition, it has been admitted that average reports for the years 1980-81 and 1981-82 were not conveyed and further that for the purpose of promotion in February, 1990, and March, 1990, the report for the year 1989-90 had no relevance.
It has been argued by the learned counsel for the respondents that average report cannot be considered adverse and consequently there was no necessity to convey the same.
It has been further argued that the average reports for the years 1982-83 and 1984-85 were conveyed as per Haryana Government instructions, which were issued in the year 1983.
5.
The validity of the Haryana Government instructions that a person must have 70% good reports for the last ten years before he can be promoted, have not been challenged before me.
However, there is no dispute that the average reports, which are being taken adverse for the purpose of promotion, were not conveyed to the petitioner.
In KK Void's case (supra) a Division Bench of this Court took a view that the average reports, which were not conveyed to an employee, could not be taken into consideration as adverse to the petitioner.
Moreover, in Brij Mohan Singh Chopra's case (supra), the Supreme Court opined that the adverse reports, which are not conveyed to an official, cannot be taken into consideration for any purpose.
Taking support from the abovementioned two authorities, the learned counsel for the petitioner rightly argued that the average reports for the years 1980-81 and 1981-82 could not be taken into account for any purpose against the petitioner.
That being so, taking the Haryana Government instructions as valid, the petitioner has 70% good reports.
Moreover, the record upto the date of promotion should have been considered and it has not been denied that for the year 1989-90, the report of the petitioner was not good.
6.
For the foregoing reasons, I hold that the petitioner could not be ignored for promotion on the ground that he did not have 70% good reports, when respondent Nos. 3 to 7 were promoted on 16th February, 1990.
If that was the only ground for not promoting the petitioner, he is entitled to the promotion from that date.
7.
Consequently, this writ petition is allowed and it is held that the petitioner could not be ignored for promotion to the rank of Assistant-cum-Accountant, when his juniors (respondent Nos. 3 to 7) were promoted on 16th February, 1990, on the ground that he did not have 70% good reports for the last ten years.
No other ground has been suggested in the written statement as to why the petitioner was not promoted.
Therefore, the petitioner would be deemed to have been promoted w.e.f.
16th February, 1990, as Assistant-cum-Accountant and will be entitled to all consequential benefits.
There will be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
