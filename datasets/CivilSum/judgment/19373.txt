The petitioners have pleaded that the interruptions in their service were not due to their absence or other similar grounds but due to the arrangement of the Government in the Education Department.
4.
On 111980, the Government of Haryana issued notification (Annexure P-1) for regularisation of the services of Class III employees, who had worked for a period of two years as on 31121979 on ad hoc basis.
This was, however, subject to the condition that break in service rendered on ad hoc basis upto a period of one month may be condoned but break occurring due to voluntary act of the employee was not to be condoned.
In pursuance of this notification, the petitioners applied for regularisation of their services, but benefit of regularisation was not given to them because as per the Department, total break in the service of the petitioner was more than 30 days.
According to the petitioners, the action of the respondents in not regularising them in service has resulted in hostile discrimination because other persons having lesser length of service have been regularised and the petitioners have been deprived of the benefit of regularisation even though the interruptions in their services were not due to any voluntary act on their part.
5.
In reply, the respondents have pleaded that though the petitioner No. 1 had completed two years of service as on 31121979, there were total break of 59 days and, therefore, he was not eligible to be considered for regularisation.
Regarding petitioner No. 2, it has been stated that he has total break of 87 days and, therefore, even though he has completed two years of service on 31121979, he was not entitled to be regularised.
The respondents have further pleaded that petitioner No. 1 was relieved due to the joining of a regular hand (Shri Lal Chand Chhabra, Stenographer).
6.
The issue which calls for determination is - whether the respondents have acted illegally in denying regularisation of service to the petitioners.
The notification (Annexure P-1) shows that ad hoc Class-III employees who had completed two years' service on 31121979 were to be regularised in service subject to the condition that there should not be a break in service of period of more than one month.
Break in service upto one month could be condoned.
But if such break was due to the concerned employee leaving the service on his own volition or where appointment was against leave vacancy or short term vacancy was not to be condoned.
In the case of petitioner No. 1, all the breaks in service is less than one month whereas in the case of petitioner No. 2, the break in service is of almost three months.
Therefore, so far as petitioner No. 2 is concerned, it is not possible to accept the submission of Mr. Malik that the said petitioner is entitled to be regularised in service only because break in his service was not voluntary.
7.
However, in the case of the petitioner No. 1, I find justification in Mr. Malik's contention that he has been erroneously denied the benefit of regularisation in terms of notification Annexure P-1.
The notification (Annexure P-1) empowers the competent authority to condone the break in service upto a period of one month.
This shows that if the service of ad hoc employee is interrupted for a period of one month, the competent authority is empowered to consider the case for the purpose of regularisation of service.
This discretion vested in the competent authority has ordinarily to be exercised in favour of the employees and only in exceptional case, the competent authority can decline to condone the break in service.
If notification (Annexure P-1) is to be interpreted as conferring unguided power upon the competent authority to regularise or not to regularise the services of ad hoc employees by condoning the break upto one month, the provision itself may become target of being condemned as unconstitutional.
Thus, it is held while petitioner No. 1 has a right to be considered for regularisation of service, the petitioner No. 2 cannot be considered entitled to be regularised in service in view of break in service for almost three months (86 days).
For the reasons mentioned above, the writ petition is partly allowed.
The respondents are directed to consider the case of the petitioner No. 1 for regularisation of service on the post of Clerk by condoning the break in service.
If the petitioner No. 1 is found otherwise suitable, he should be regularised in service w.e.f.
31121979.
He shall get benefit of notional seniority and notional fixation of pay, but he shall become entitled to actual salary only from the date of this order.
Claim of the petitioner No. 2 for regularisation of service is rejected.
