NK Sodhi, J - Petitioner was appointed as a Clerk on January 27, 1977 on ad hoc basis in the Department of Agriculture, Haryana.
He was made regular on January 1, 1980 in the pay scale of Rs. 400-600/- and was granted the selection grade on December 28, 1987 with effect from July 31, 1981.
In the selection grade, he was drawing a basic pay of Rs. 525/- on July 1, 1984.
On his passing a departmental test, he was posted as a Stenotypist on October 17, 1984 in the pay scale of Rs. 400-600/- with a special pay of Rs. 50/-.
As Stenotypist his basic pay was fixed at Rs. 470/- by an order dated January 13, 1988.
There is no gainsaying that the petitioner was not heard when the order fixing his basic pay as a Steno-typist was passed.
He filed a representation to the Director of Agriculture, Haryana on February 1, 1988 claiming that on being posted as a Steno-typist his basic pay could not be reduced to Rs. 470/- as he was earlier drawing a basic pay of Rs. 525/- as a clerk in the selection grade.
This representation was declined by the Financial Commissioner and Secretary to Government, Haryana, Agriculture Department, who communicated his decision to the Director of Agriculture as per communication dated September 5, 1988.
It was then that the present petition was filed under Article 226 of the Constitution challenging the order dated January 19, 1988 and also the rejection of his representation.
2.
It was strenuously urged by learned counsel for the petitioner that since the order fixing the basic pay of the petitioner at Rs. 470/- was passed without affording an opportunity of hearing to him it was violative of the rules of natural justice and, therefore, liable to be set aside.
It was also urged that the impugned order violated the provisions of rule 44 of the Punjab Civil Services Rules, Volume I Part I as well.
According to the counsel, the basic pay of the petitioner which he was drawing as a clerk had to be protected under this Rule.
3.
Having heard counsel for the parties, I am of the view that the petition deserves to succeed.
The order reducing the basic pay of the petitioner in the scale of Rs. 400-600/- as Steno-typist was certainly prejudicial to him and since it had been passed without affording him an opportunity of being heard it offended against the rules of natural justice and must, therefore, be quashed.
Consequently, the writ petition is allowed and the order dated January 19, 1988 (Annexure P2 with the writ petition) is quashed and the case is sent back to the Department with a direction that the competent authority will refix the basic pay of the petitioner as Steno-typist after hearing him and considering the effect, if any, of Rule 44 of the aforesaid Rules.
As the matter pertains to the year 1988, the competent authority is directed to dispose of the same expeditiously preferably within a period of three months from the date of receipt of this order.
Parties are left to bear their own costs.
Petition allowed.
