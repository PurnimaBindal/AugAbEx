Swatanter Kumar, J - The petitioners were appointed as Beldars and have been continuously working since 111990.
The State Government issued a policy of regularisation of daily wagers on 731996, which was further amended vide policy dated 18th March, 1996.
In terms of the policy, the persons who had completed 3 years as on 31st January, 1996 and were in service and had completed 240 days in each year of service, were entitled to regularisation.
The petitioners made a representation on 20th August, 2001.
They prayed for regularisation and for grant of equal pay for equal work i.e. for the salary which their counter-parts of the regular cadre were being granted.
The petitioners relied upon different judgments of this court.
2.
Upon notice, the respondents have filed written statement.
The fact that the petitioners were working on daily wagers since 111990 has not been disputed.
According to them, they were being paid wages as prescribed by Labour Department, Haryana from time to time and as such, they were not entitled to minimum of the pay scale.
Further, according to the respondents, the petitioners were also not entitled to regularisation, as they have not worked continuously for 240 days in 3 or 5 years immediately preceding the cut off date.
3.
We have already notice that there is hardly any controversy on the facts of the present case.
The respondents themselves have annexed the attendance chart in relation to attendance of the petitioners.
Petitioners No. 1, 4 and 6 have admittedly completed more than 240 days in three years, of course not preceding to the cut off date i.e. 111996.
While petitioners No. 2, 3 and 5 have not worked for 240 days in any of the three years, as such these three petitioners are not entitled to claim for regularisation in terms of the policy of the Government.
Thus, in the light of the Division Bench judgment of Kavita Gandhi v. State of Haryana and others reported as 1997(4) R.S.J.
76 and Full Bench judgment in he case of Tek Chand v. State of Haryana, 2002(1) SCT 308 (P&H) (DB) : reported as 2001(3) R.S.J.
604, the respondents are bound to liable to be directed to consider the cases of the petitioners No. 1, 4 and 6.
Admittedly, the petitioners have worked continuously from 111990 on daily wages for different number of days.
They are discharging the same functions and duties and for the same hours as their counter-parts in the regular cadre.
Thus, in light of the Full Bench judgment of this Court in the case of Vijay Kumar and others v. State of Punjab and others, 2001(2) SCT 732 (P&H) (DB) : 2002(1) S.L.R.
694, they are entitled to get minimum of the pay scale with dearness allowance, as directed by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in other similar matters.
4.
For the reasons stated above, the writ petition is allowed limited to the extent indicated above.
The respondents are directed to consider the case of the petitioners and pass appropriate order within a period of three months from the date a certified copy of this judgment is brought to their notice.
Pitition allowed.
