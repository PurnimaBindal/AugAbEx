VK Jhanji, J - This shall dispose of Civil Writ Petitions No. 5737, 5744, 5745, 6623 of 1991, 7358, 8785, 7376, 7602, 13836 of 1992 and 361 of 1993.
2.
In all these writ petitions, petitioners were duly selected as Head Constables in the Haryana Police.
Subsequently, they were promoted as Head Constables on officiating/ad hoc basis on various dates as mentioned in their respective writ petitions.
In the writ petitions, petitioners are challenging the orders whereby they have been sought to be reverted to the post of Constables.
3.
In Civil Writ Petition No. 5737 of 1991, petitioner was promoted as Head Constable on 23.1.1983 and sought to be reverted to the post of Constable on 2431991.
In Civil Writ Petition No. 5745 of 1991, petitioner was promoted as Head Constable on 2111982 and sought to be reverted on 821990.
In Civil Writ Petition No. 5744 of 1991, petitioner was promoted on 28121989 and reverted on 2431991.
In Civil Writ Petition No. 6623 of 1991, petitioner was promoted on 28121991 and sought to be reverted on 2431991.
In Civil Writ Petition No. 8785 of 1992, petitioner was promoted on 3041984 and sought to be reverted on 261992.
In Civil Writ Petition No. 7358 of 1992, petitioner was promoted on 16.1.1990 and sought to be reverted on 2751992.
In Civil Writ Petition No. 7376 of 1992, petitioner was promoted on 10101978 and sought to be reverted on 2751992.
In Civil Writ Petition No. 13836 of 1992, petitioner was promoted on 571988 and sought to be reverted on 261992.
In Civil Writ Petition No. 361 of 1993, petitioner was promoted on 22.9.1988 and sought to be reverted on 2751992.
Likewise, in Civil Writ Petition No. 7602 of 1992, petitioners 1, 2, 3 and 4 were promoted on officiating/ad hoc basis on 1121983, 22101986, 4101985 and 23101986 respectively and they have been sought to be reverted as Constables by order dated 261992.
In all these petitions, apart from others the main grievance of the petitioners is that they could not be reverted without having been afforded an opportunity of hearing.
4.
Respondents in their written statements have stated that the petitioners cannot make any grievance with regard to their reversion because their promotion was not in terms of rule 138 of the Punjab Police Rules, 1934 and this being so they cannot claim that they were entitled to show-cause notice before orders of reversion were passed against them.
5.
At the time of motion hearing, in some writ petitions reversion of the petitioners was stayed.
6.
Counsel heard.
7.
The case of the petitioners is that their promotions were approved by the Deputy Inspector General of Police and were on the basis of achievement in sports and they having worked on the posts in question for considerably long time could not have been reverted without being afforded an opportunity of hearing.
However, the counsel has fairly conceded that there is only provision for making out of turn promotion to the post of Head Constable as contained in sub-rule (2) of rule 138 of the 1934 Rules providing that the constables who are otherwise considered suitable may with the approval of the Deputy Inspector General of Police be promoted to the posts of Head Constables upto a maximum of 10% of vacancies.
Apart from this rule, there is no other rule providing for out of turn promotion to the persons having distinction in sports.
The contention of counsel for the petitioners that promotions of the petitioners were with the approval of the Deputy Inspector General of Police and were within 10% of vacancies is being seriously disputed by learned State counsel.
Be that as it may.
I am of the view that in the facts and circumstances of this case, the petitioners are required to be given an opportunity of hearing before they are ordered to be reverted.
In this regard, judgment of this Court in Hardev Singh v. State of Haryana and others, 1991(2) SCT 393(P&H) : 1991(7) S.L.R.
141 may be noticed wherein the impugned order was quashed because no opportunity of hearing was afforded to the petitioner therein.
8.
Accordingly, the writ petitions are allowed and impugned orders quashed, leaving it open to the respondents to pass fresh orders within three months from today after affording an opportunity of hearing to the petitioners.
Orders whereby reversion of some of the petitioners was stayed shall continue till fresh orders are passed.
No costs.
