Adarsh Kumar Goel, J - The appellant filed suit for declaration to the effect that order of termination of his services dated 22121993 was void.
Case of the plaintiff is that he was working as constable since April 14, 1992 and on 9.4.1993, he suddenly fell ill and proceeded to his village after informing the MHC.
He returned back on 27.7.1993 but was again taken ill on 3081993 and went to his village after informing he MHC.
He applied for leave and was never conveyed its rejection.
He was abruptly discharged from service under Rule 1221 of the Punjab Police Rules.
Since the order of discharge was punitive, the same was illegal as no enquiry was held.
He made a representation to the DIG, Jalandhar but the same was rejected on 1.6.1994.
His further representation was rejected by the DGP on 1611995.
2.
Suit was contested by the defendants stating that plaintiff was absent from duty from 611993 to 1431993 and from 9.4.1993 to 26.7.1993 and on considering his service record, it was found that he was not likely to become a good police officer and he was discharged from service under Rule 1221 for which no enquiry was required.
The plaintiff was given an opportunity of personal hearing before passing the order.
3.
The trial court decreed the suit holding that the order of discharge was based on misconduct of absence from duty and, therefore, in absence of opportunity being given, the order was void.
4.
On appeal, finding of the trial court was reversed in view of a Full Bench judgment of this Court in Sher Singh v. State of Haryana and others, 1995(1) SCT 679 (P&H) : 1994(2) SLR 100 wherein following propositions were laid down :-  "(1) A constable can be discharged from service under Rule 1221 at any time within three years of his enrolment in spite of the fact that there is a specific allegation which may even amount to misconduct against him; (2) A superintendent of Police can form his opinion regarding the likelihood or otherwise of a constable making a good police officer not only on the basis of periodic reports contemplated under Rule 195 but also on the basis of any other relevant material; and  (3) The provisions of Rule 1624 and Article 311 shall be attracted only when the punishing authority decides to punish the constable."
Order of discharge from service is as under :-  "Constable Naresh Kumar No. 3624 of this district is, hereby, discharged under PPR 12.21 w.e.f.
22121993 as he is not likely to become a good police officer."
Rule 1221 is as under :-  "Discharge or Inefficient : A constable who is found unlikely to prove an efficient police officer may be discharged by the Superintendent at any time within three years of enrolment.
There shall be no appeal against an order of discharge under this rule."
5.
Learned counsel for the appellant submitted that order of discharge though innocuous in form, was punitive in substance being founded on allegation of misconduct of absence from duty.
He submitted that Ex.D.4 was a note dated 2871993 by SP (Headquarters) recommending discharge of the appellant from service.
He relied on judgment of the Apex Court in Prithipal Singh v. State of Punjab and others, 2001(1) SCT 459 (SC) : 2000(4) SLR 754 wherein it was held that even where order of discharge appears to be innocuous, the same will be treated as stigmatic if allegation of misconduct existed which was the foundation of the passing of the order.
6.
Learned counsel for the State supported the judgment of the lower appellate court to the effect that order could not be treated as punitive merely by looking at the reason for discharge which is the motive for passing the order.
7.
After hearing learned counsel for the parties, I do not find any merit in this appeal.
The order is simpliciter order of discharge which is permissible under the rules.
In Pavanendra Narayan Verma v. Sanjay Gandhi PGI of Medical Sciences and another, 2002(2) SCT 358 (SC) : AIR 2002 SC 23, after reviewing the earlier case-law, the Apex Court observed "courts continue to struggle with semantically indistinguishable concepts like `motive' and `foundation' and termination founded on a probationer's misconduct have been held to be illegal while terminations motivated by the probationer's misconduct have been upheld.
The decisions are legion and it is an impossible task to find a clear path through the jungle of precedents."
It was then concluded that to hold an order to be punitive, three factors should be present :- (a) a full scale formal enquiry (b) into allegations involving moral turpitude or misconduct and (c) which culminated in a finding of guilt.
If all the three factors are present, the termination has to be held to be punitive.
Conversely if any one of the three factors is missing, the termination has to be upheld.
The said decision has been followed in a recent decision in Mathew P Thomas v. Kerala State Civil Supply Corporation Ltd and others, 2003(1) SCT 1051 (SC) : (2003) 3 SCC 263.
On the interpretation of the same rule i.e. Rule 1221 of the Punjab Police Rules, 1934, the matter came up before the Apex Court in State of Punjab and others v. Bhagwan Singh, 2002(1) SCT 253 (SC) : (2002) 9 SCC 636.
Allowing the appeal of the State of Punjab holding an order of termination to be punitive, it was held that when a probationer is discharged during probation and for that purpose, assessment of his work is done, the same cannot amount to stigma.
In the judgment relied on by the counsel for the appellant, there is no reference to decisions of the Apex Court in Bhagwan Singh and other cases.
For the above reasons, I do not find any merit in this appeal and the same is dismissed.
Appeal dismissed.
