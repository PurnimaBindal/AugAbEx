VK Bali, J (Oral) - Concededly, the petitioner had to his credit service from 571973 to 12.9.1983.
The disputed period in between was from 28.2.1974 to 2061974.
Concededly as well if the petitioner might have put in ten years of service, he will be entitled to all his retiral benefits.
Pension has not been given to the petitioner solely on the ground that he has not put in ten years of service.
2.
Mr. RK Malik, learned Counsel for the petitioner, however, contends that services of the petitioner were terminated on 28.2.1974 and he was taken back in service on 2061974, while taking back in service the period from 28.2.1974 to 2061974 was regularised.
Only pay was not to be paid to the petitioner.
As per the contention raised by Mr, Malik, the period from 28.2.1974 to 2061974 has necessarily to be counted for pension.
His second contention is that petitioner was in any case given marching orders on 10.9.1983 Annexure P-4 and the said order is an order of compulsory retirement.
While asking the petitioner to quit the job, he was neither given three months notice nor three months pay as is enunciated in rule 326(d) of the Punjab Civil Services Volume I, Part I. Mr. Malik on the basis of rule 326 contends that if three months pay of the petitioner was not given, then he should be treated to be in service upto the period 12.12.1983 i.e. three months period after 12.9.1983.
If these three months are counted, the petitioner again concededly has 10 years of service to his credit.
3.
All that could be said in opposition to the contention raised by Mr. Malik, by the State counsel is that once the petitioner's services were terminated from 28.2.1974 to 2061974 it cannot be said that he was in service for the said period as also that during the said period, petitioner was necessarily to be deemed to be on extra-ordinary leave.
4.
After hearing learned counsel for the parties, I am of the view that the period between 28.2.1974 to 2061974 has to count for the purpose of calculating qualifying service of the petitioner for grant of pension.
Rule 47 of the Punjab Civil Services Rules, Volume II reads as follows :-  "In respect of Government employees who retire or die on or after the Ist April, 1963, the time passed on leave of all kinds except extraordinary leave shall count as service for pension.
Period of overstay of leave does not count for pension."
5.
It is not a case where the petitioner might have asked for extra-ordinary leave.
As mentioned above, on the contrary, period between 28.2.1974 to 2061974 was regularised.
That apart, as has been noted above, while the petitioner was asked to quit before the date of superannuation by virtue of Rule 326(d) PCR Vol.
I, Part I, he was neither given the pay for three months nor was given notice of three months, as is necessary precondition envisaged under the rule itself.
Having not been given either pay or notice for three months, it has to be taken that the petitioner was in service during the said period of three months and concededly, if that period of three months is considered, the petitioner is entitled to pension on completion of ten years' service.
For the reasons recorded above, this petition is allowed and a direction is issued to the respondents to calculate the pension of the petitioner commensurate to service rendered by him and make over the arrears thereof within three months from today.
Parties are, however, left to bear their own costs.
Petition allowed.
