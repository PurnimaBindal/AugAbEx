GS Singhvi, J - All these petitions involve determination of an identical issue relating to the interpretation of the circular dated 2171978 and, therefore, these petitions are being decided by a common order.
2.
Petitioner-Gurnam Singh joined service on appointment as clerk in the Consolidation Department, Punjab on 6111958.
This appointment was given to him on the basis of selection made by the Subordinate Services Selection Board.
He was confirmed as clerk in the Consolidation Department on 14.4.1969.
As a result of the policy decision taken by the Government of Punjab to wind up the Consolidation Department, the employees of that Department including petitioner-Gurnam Singh were rendered surplus.
He was absorbed in the office of the Commissioner, Jalandhar Division, Jalandhar with effect from 23.12.1971.
After about two years, the respondent No. 2 took a decision to send the petitioner back to the Consolidation Department but on 29.8.1973 the Government finally decided that the petitioner should be absorbed in the Commissioner's office in terms of the policy decision of the Government.
Subsequent to his absorption in the Commissioner's office a circular dated 391973 was issued by the Commissioner, Jalandhar Division and objections were invited from the aggrieved persons against the placement of petitioner at Sr. No. 29 in the seniority list.
The petitioner submitted his representation dated 27.9.1973.
While the issue of seniority was still in fluid, the Government issued instructions dated 2171978 (Annexure P.4) and issued further directions on 1591978 (Annexure P5) for determination of the seniority in accordance with para (iii) of instructions Annexure P4.
A dispute was raised regarding applicability of the instructions in the face of provisions contained in service rules but vide letter dated 321979 (Annexure P8), the Government clarified that the instructions were supplemental to the service rules and not inconsistent therewith.
Consequently, the seniority of the petitioner was changed from Sr. No. 29 to S.No.
6 and he was given promotion to the post of Assistant on 20.7.1979.
Respondent No. 3 filed an appeal before the Financial Commissioner against the assignment of seniority to the petitioner by pleading that the petitioner cannot be assigned seniority from the date earlier to his absorption in the Commissioner's office in view of the mandatory provisions contained in Punjab Commissioners Subordinate Service Rules, 1941 which were superseded by the Punjab Commissioners' Offices (State Service Class III) Rules, 1976.
The petitioner contested the appeal filed by respondent No. 3 and after hearing the parties, the Financial Commissioner (Revenue), Punjab passed the impugned order Annexure P.9 dated 19.5.1980 and set aside the order assigning seniority to the petitioner at Sr. No. 6.
The petitioner has challenged this order on the ground that the Financial Commissioner (Revenue) had no jurisdiction to entertain and decide the appeal against the order passed by the Commissioner, Jalandhar Division, Jalandhar assigning him seniority as per the instructions issued by the Government of Punjab on 2171978.
The petitioner has contended that the impugned order passed by the Financial Commissioner is without jurisdiction apart from being arbitrary and unreasonable.
3.
In their reply, respondents 1 and 2 have pleaded that vide government memo dated 6101969 a decision was taken not to fill any vacancy in the clerical cadre from Clerk to Superintendent but against one temporary post lying vacant the petitioner was allowed to join with effect from 23.12.1971.
Subsequently the petitioner was ordered to be sent to the Consolidation Department but on the instructions of the Government he was retained in Commissioner's office.
Regarding the seniority list, the respondents have stated that the order passed by the Commissioner assigning seniority No.6 to the petitioner was reversed by the Financial Commissioner (Revenue) who was competent to do so being a higher authority.
According to the respondents, the order of the Financial Commissioner is fully justified in view of the provisions contained in the Rules of 1976 and the instructions issued by the Government on 2171978 could not operate in the field which was already occupied by the statutory rules.
A similar reply has been filed by respondent No. 3 who has pleaded that the very absorption of the petitioner in the Commissioner's office was illegal.
According to respondent No. 3, the instructions issued on 341961 for absorption of the surplus staff of the Consolidation Department had been quashed by the High Court in C.W.P.
Nos. 1778 and 1963 of 1966 and in view of the judgment of the High Court, the instructions issued by the Government on 2171978 could not be relied upon by the petitioner for the purpose of assigning seniority to him.
Reliance has also been placed on the order passed by this court in C.W.P.
No. 5054 of 1975 (Ujagar Singh v. Punjab State etc.) in which the Commissioner, Jalandhar Division had justified the seniority list issued earlier by him and in which the petitioner had been placed at Sr. No. 29.
In substance, the case of respondent No. 3 is that the petitioner could at the best be considered to be an employee of the Commissioner's office with effect from 6101969 the date on which the new vacancy had become available.
According to respondent No. 3, the Government suo motu issued circular dated 6101969 withdrawing instructions dated 341961 regarding absorption of the employees and therefore, the petitioner is not entitled to claim benefit of total length of service for the purpose of determination of seniority.
4.
In CWPNo.
2554 of 1987, Tarlochan Singh and Chaman Lal have prayed for quashing of the order dated 2841987 issued by the Government regarding seniority of confirmed clerks as on 111980/111982.
According to the petitioners, they were appointed as clerks on 1551957 and 2261960 in the Consolidation Department on the basis of the recommendations made by the Subordinate Service Selection Board and on winding up of the Consolidation Department, they were absorbed in the office of Deputy Commissioner, Jalandhar on 6121961 and 511l969.
These petitioners have also claimed assignment of the seniority on the basis of instructions dated 2171978.
According to them, the action of the respondents in issuing order dated April 28, 1987 which resulted in depriving them of the seniority based on the length of service apart from being arbitrary is violative of the principles of natural justice.
5.
The reply filed by the two sets of the respondents is similar to the reply filed to the C.W.P.
No. 1991 of 1980.
6.
C.W.P.
No. 4401 of 1987 has been filed by Ram Parkash challenging the promotion accorded to respondent No. 3 on the post of Superintendent.
The petitioner has claimed that he is a member of the Scheduled Caste whereas respondent No. 3 is a member of Backward Class and although respondent No. 3 was appointed in the office of the Commissioner, Jalandhar Division much after his appointment, his (petitioner's) claim for promotion to the post of Superintendent has been ignored while promoting respondent no. 3.
According to the petitioner, he is second senior most scheduled caste candidate, the first being Daulat Ram and as per the seniority list issued by respondent No. 2, he has a right to be promoted against 6th and 11th vacancies meant for members of the Scheduled Castes and as per the roster, only 16th vacancy could go to a candidate of Backward Class and, therefore, respondent No. 3 could not have been promoted in preference to him.
7.
This writ petition has been opposed by respondents No. 1 and 2 who have disputed the claim of the petitioner for promotion against reserved vacancy.
These respondents have admitted that the petitioner had joined service earlier to respondent No. 3 in the office of the Commissioner, Jalandhar Division but according to them, respondent No. 3 was substantively appointed as a clerk on 6111958 on the recommendation of the Subordinate Services Selection Board and he was subsequently absorbed in the office of the Commissioner, Jalandhar Division in the year 1971 and claimed benefit of total length of service for the purpose of determination of seniority.
Further case of the respondents 1 and 2 is that according to the instructions, the vacancies at point 1, 6, 11 were reserved for the members of the Scheduled Castes, but, as per the instructions contained in memo No. 3192-DEI-63/1877 dated 1561963, the minimum experience required for promotion to the post of Assistant Superintendent from the post of Assistant is 5 years and the petitioner did not fulfil the requirement of five years' experience so as to become entitled to be promoted.
According to the respondents, the post falling at point No. 1 and 6 (years 1967 and 1970) elapsed in view of para 4 of the Punjab Government (Scheduled Caste and Backward Classes Department) circular dated 2381966 because in terms of the aforesaid circular, the reserved points at No. 1 and 6 could be carried forward only for two years.
Thereafter, Shri Daulat Ram was promoted as Assistant Superintendent against the reserved point no. 11 in the year 1974 and the vacancy at Sr. No. 16 which was reserved for a member of Backward Class was given to respondent No. 3 who was senior-most Backward Class candidate available in the Department.
Respondents 1 and 2 have also pleaded that the petitioner has no right to be promoted against the vacancy reserved for Backward Classes.
8.
Circular dated 2171978 has been interpreted by a Full Bench of this Court in Kartar Singh v. State of Punjab, 1989(4) SLR 430 and it has been held that proviso to Rule 15 of the Punjab Revenue Patwaris Class III Service Rules, 1966 will not come into operation because the said rule does not deal with the absorption and the appointment by way of absorption cannot be treated to be covered by the methods of recruitment prescribed in the Rules.
The Full Bench further held that the circular dated 2171978 issued by the Government for fixation of the seniority of the staff of Consolidation Department on absorption in the Renevue Department does not suffer from any legal or constitutional infirmity.
9.
A similar view has been expressed by a learned single Judge of this Court in State of Punjab v. Shiv Karan, 1995 (3) S.C.T.
690 and it has been held that after absorption in the Department of Printing and Stationery the respondent who was a clerk in the Consolidation Department was entitled to have seniority fixed on the basis of continuous length of service i.e. date of his appointment in the Consolidation Department.
10.
Aforementioned judgments of the Full Bench and the learned single Judge have obviated the necessity of fresh discussion on the subject.
Suffice it to say that the petitioner Gurnam Singh and two petitioners in C.W.P.
No. 2554 of 1987 have a right to be assigned seniority in terms of para (iii) of the circular dated 2171978 i.e. on the basis of total length of service rendered by them after appointment in the Consolidation Department pursuant to the selection made by the Subordinate Services Selection Board and they could not have been treated as fresh entrants in service on their absorption in the other departments.
11.
The claim made by petitioner-Ram Parkash in C.W.P.
No. 4401 of 1987 cannot be accepted for the simple reason that the assertion made by the respondents about the lease of roster points No. 1 (1967) and No. 6 (1970) in view of the existing instructions of the Government of Punjab have remained unrebutted.
It is an admitted position that Daulat Ram was senior to the petitioner-Ram Parkash amongst the Scheduled Castes candidates and he was promoted in the year 1974 as Assistant Superintendent against the roster point No.11.
This being the factual matrix the 16th point which was reserved for the Backward Class candidate had to be given to respondent No. 3 who happened to be the senior-most Backward Class candidate eligible for promotion as Assistant Superintendent.
Thus, no illegality has been committed by respondents No. 1 and 2 in promoting respondent No. 3 on the post of Assistant superintendent.
12.
In the result, writ petitions No. 1991 of 1980 and 2554 of 1987 are allowed.
Order Annexure P.9 passed by the Financial Commissioner (Revenue), Punjab which has been challenged in C.W.P.
No. 1991 of 1980 is quashed.
Similarly, order dated 2841987 (Annexure P.7) which has been impugned in C.W.P.
No. 2554 of 1987 is quashed with a direction that the petitioners in both these writ petitions should be assigned seniority on the basis of total length of service rendered by them in the cadre of clerks counted from the date of their initial appointment in the Consolidation Department and they shall be given all consequential benefits with future promotion from the date their juniors were promoted.
They shall get all consequential monetary and other benefits as a result of their retrospective promotion.
Necessary orders should be passed by the respondents in this regard within four months of the submission of a certified copy of this order.
C.W.P.
No. 4401 of 1987 involving a challenge to the promotion of respondent No. 3 is dismissed.
Parties are left to bear their own costs.
