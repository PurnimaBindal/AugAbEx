Jawahar Lal Gupra, J (Oral) - This order will dispose of Civil Writ Petitions Nos. 13645 of 1991, 2608 and 3125 of 1994.
2.
The dispute in all these cases relates to inter se seniority.
The short question that arises is - could the seniority be changed without notice to the persons who were likely to be affected.
Learned counsel for the parties have referred to the facts as averred in Civil Writ Petition No. 2608 of 1994.
These may be briefly noticed.
3.
The petitioner was recruited as a clerk on January 30, 1979.
This appointment was on purely temporary basis.
On January 21, 1980, his services were regularised.
On June 22, 1984, the petitioner was promoted to the post of Assistant.
Respondent No. 2 was admittedly recruited as a Store-keeper on February 13, 1981 On July 10, 1985, she was transferred as an Assistant.
Respondent No. 3 was recruited as a Liability Assistant in the office of General Manager, Cooperative Consumer Store, Ambala on May 12, 1977 on a fixed salary of Rs. 225/-.
He continued working as such till February 1, 1979, when his salary was raised from Rs. 225/- to Rs. 250/-.
He was placed in the regular pay scale of Rs. 110-210 meant for the post of clerk-cum-typist w.e.f September 1, 1979.
He was actually promoted as an Assistant w.e.f.
August 20, 1991.
Similarly, respondent No. 4 was recruited as a cashier in the office of General Manager, Cooperative Consumer Store, Ambala on September 6, 1977.
He was granted a consolidated pay of Rs. 250/-.
He was placed in the regular scale of Rs. 110-210 sanctioned for the post of Cashier w.e.f.
April 23, 1979.
He was actually promoted as an Assistant on March 29, 1993.
4.
A tentative seniority list of Assistants was issued on November 15, 1989.
A copy of this list has been produced as Annexure P-9 with the writ petition.
In this list, the petitioner's name appeared at Sr. No. 11 while that of respondent No. 2 was shown at Sr. No. 12.
The names of respondents 3 and 4 did not find mention in this list.
Since the petitioner was assigned her rightful seniority, she did not represent against the position assigned to her in this list.
However, on June 18,1991 the Managing Director of the Haryana State Federation of Consumers Cooperative Wholesales Stores Limited, Chandigarh (hereinafter referred to as the Federation) passed an order on the representations submitted by different officials, in response to the tentative seniority list of Assistants, Respondent No. 2 claimed seniority as an Assistant w.e.f.
February 13, 1981 when she had actually joined service as a Store-keeper.
This request was accepted on the ground that the pay scale of the post of Store-keeper was identical to that of an Assistant and that in fact, she had been discharging the duties of an Assistant.
Similarly, with regard to respondent No. 3, it was observed that "......reasons for non- inclusion of his name in the initial seniority list circulated on 22.5.1979 is folly, committed by the office.
His service for the period 1341977 to 2279 on daily wages was not taken into consideration.
The benefit of seniority for the service has to be allowed to him.
Thus, Sh. Sanwal Ram is allowed the seniority from 1341977 as clerk and consequently placed in the seniority list of Assistants w.e.f.
2261984 the date since his juniors i.e. Sh. Shish Pal, Shri Onkar Singh and Smt. Vijay Arora were promoted."
With regard to respondent No4, an order dated March 29, 1993, a copy of which has been produced as Annexure P-16, was passed.
By this order, it was directed that "his deemed date of promotion as Assistant will be 2261984 i.e. the date on which his juniors S/Shri Shish Pal, Onkar Singh and Smt. Vijay Arora were promoted ..." On the basis of these orders, the seniority list was issued on January 24, 1994, a copy of which has been produced as Annexure P-17, with the writ petition.
The result is that the petitioner's name has been changed from Sr. No. 11 to Sr. No. 13.
As against this, respondent No. 2 who was at Sr. No. 12 has been catapulted to Sr. No. 2 and respondents Nos. 3 and 4 who were not in the list at all, have been placed at Sr. Nos. 9 and 10.
Similar is the position in the two connected petitions.
5.
The short grievance made on behalf of the petitioners is that the seniority has been upset without the grant of any opportunity whatsoever.
On behalf of the respondents, it has been urged that the action taken by the Managing Director, is legal and fair.
6.
Learned counsel for the parties have been heard at some length.
However, in view of the ultimate directions that I propose to issue, it does not appear to be proper to express any opinion on merits.
Suffice it to say, as has been held in various judicial pronouncements that the action of the respondents in upsetting the seniority of the petitioners vis-a-vis the other members of the service without the grant of any opportunity, was violative of the principles of natural justice.
It is not merely a technical objection.
In fact, if an opportunity had been given, the petitioners could have shown that the action taken by the respondents was not permissible under the rules; that the service rendered on daily wages by respondent No. 3 could not have been taken into consideration for the purpose of determination of seniority and that persons who were recruited as Store-keepers, cashiers, steno-typists and Accounts Clerks could not be granted seniority with the clerks.
The failure to grant opportunity has resulted in material mis-carriage of justice.
7.
Accordingly, these writ petitions are allowed.
The impugned orders are set aside and the respondents are directed to re-decide the matter after hearing the petitioners as well as the respondents.
It will be open to the Federation to even grant hearing to others who may be adversely affected by the final decision.
The respondents may do the needful as expeditiously as possible preferably within six months.
In the circumstances of these cases, there will be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
