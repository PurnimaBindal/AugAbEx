GS Singhvi, J - This petition has been filed for issuance of a writ in the nature of mandamus directing the respondents to make the payment of General Provident Fund (for short `GPF') along with interest @ 18% per annum and also to pay him the balance of the subsistence allowance for August 1972.
The petitioner has also prayed that the respondents be directed to allow him to resume duty as Peon.
2.
The facts on which there is no dispute between the parties are that while he was working as Peon in the office of the Director of Industries (Sericulture), Punjab, the petitioner was arrested in connection with FIR No. 117 dated 18.10.1971 in which it was alleged that he has committed offences under various provisions of Indian Penal Code including Section 302 read with Section 149 I.P.C.
He was convicted by the trial Court and was sentenced to ten years rigorous imprisonment and fine of Rs. 2,000/- or in default to undergo further rigorous imprisonment for one year.
In the appeal filed by him against the verdict of trial Court, the High Court ordered the suspension of his sentence.
That appeal was ultimately dismissed some time in December 1972.
Consequently, the petitioner was re-arrested on 24121972 and sent to Jail.
He was released from Jail on 22.8.1975.
3.
Immediately after his arrest by the Police, the petitioner was placed under suspension and in view of his conviction by the trial court, the petitioner was dismissed from service.
When he was released from the Jail, the petitioner reported for duty.
Then he was informed about the factum of his dismissal from service.
Thereafter, the petitioner repeatedly requested for release of the amount lying in his GPF.
He also served a notice of demand for justice but could not get a favourable reply and this appears to be the primary reason why he has invoked writ jurisdiction of this Court under Article 226 of the Constitution of India.
4.

They have pleaded that the petitioner cannot claim reinstatement in the service in the wake of his conviction by the Court of competent jurisdiction.

6.
We have heard learned counsel for the parties.
I our opinion, there is no legal justification to withhold the amount of GPF payable to the petitioner because what he is claiming is his own property which was kept in trust with the government.
That apart, the unequivocal admission made by the respondents about the preparation of documents on the issue of payment of GPF, there does not remain any justification for non-payment of the amount due to the petitioner which must be treated as his property in view of the various decisions of the Supreme Court including the following :-  (i) Deokinandan Prasad v. State of Bihar, A.I.R. 1971 SC 1409; (ii) State of Punjab v. K.R.
Erry and Sobhag Rai Mehta, A.I.R. 1973 SC 834; and (iii) State of Kerala v. M Padmanabhan Nair, A.I.R. 1985 SC 356.
7.
However, other reliefs sought by him cannot be granted to the petitioner in view of his conviction by the trial Court and dismissal of appeal by this Court.
8.
For the reasons mentioned above, the writ petition is partly allowed.
The respondents are directed to pay the amount of GPF due to the petitioner along with interest @ 18% within a period of one month of the submission of certified copy of this order, failing which the respondent shall have to pay interest @ 24% annum.
Petition partly allowed.
