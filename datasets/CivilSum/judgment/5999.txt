GS Singhvi, J - This is the second round of litigation foisted upon the petitioner on account of patently illegal and arbitrary order dated 2761987 (Annexure P7) and 1671986/1771986/451987 (Annexure P9) passed by Chief Engineer, Irrigation Works, Punjab (respondent No. 2) vide which the dates of his promotion to the posts of Superintendent Grade-I (SG-I) and Superintendent Grade-III (SG-III) respectively, were revised to his prejudice.
2.
The facts of the case lie in a narrow compass.
The petitioner is a member of Scheduled Caste.
He was initially appointed as Clerk in the office of respondent No. 2.
In the course of service, he was promoted as Assistant.
In 1976, the petitioner filed C.W.P.
No. 1889 of 1976 claiming refixation of his seniority above Shri Dev Raj Birdi by contending that the latter had wrongly been placed above him in the seniority list dated 2931976.
During the pendency of that petition, the department refixed the petitioner's seniority above Dev Raj Birdi and by taking into consideration that fact, a learned Single Judge allowed the writ petition with the direction to respondent No. 2 to consider his claim for promotion w.e.f.
the date his junior, namely, Dev Raj Birdi was promoted.
The relevant extract of the order passed by the learned Single Judge is reproduced below :-  "Learned counsel for the petitioner now states that since the filing of this petition the petitioner has been granted his due seniority and has been placed above respondent No. 2 vide order dated 25.11.1981 (Now placed on record).
In the light of this, learned counsel only seeks a direction to the competent authority to consider the case of the petitioner for promotion w.e.f.
the date respondent No. 2 was promoted.
Learned counsel for the respondent-authorities frankly concedes that this has to be done in view of the order dated 25.11.81.
In the light of this, I allow this petition and direct the respondent- authorities to consider the claim of the petitioner for promotion w.e.f.
the date respondent No. 2 was promoted and to finalise this process of consideration within a period of 3 months from the date of receipt of this order.
No costs."
Letters Patent Appeal No. 370 of 1983 filed by the State of Punjab against the order of the learned Single Judge was summarily dismissed by the Division Bench vide its order dated 1141983.
SLP No. 8571 of 1983 filed against the order of the Division Bench was dismissed by the Supreme Court on 4121989. Review application No. 48 of 1983 filed by Shri Dev Raj Birdi was also dismissed by the learned Single Judge vide order dated 541983 (Annexure P3).
Thereafter, Chief Engineer, AHPC, Irrigation Works, Punjab issued orders dated 461983 (Annexure P4) and 1281983 (Annexure P5) provisionally promoting the petitioner as officiating SG-III w.e.f.
2931976 and as officiating SG-I w.e.f.
4.12.1981 respectively subject to the conditions enumerated in those orders including the one relating to finalisation of the seniority list of Assistants in pursuance of order dated 931983 passed in C.W.P.
No. 4536 of 1976-Teja Singh and others v. State of Punjab and others and other pending writ petitions.
Sarv Shri Dev Dutt Salwan and Yashpal Sharma challenged orders Annexures P4 and P5 in C.W.P.
No. 139 of 1984 which was dismissed by a Division Bench of this court on 351984 (Annexure P6).
After about four years of his retrospective promotion as officiating SG-III and officiating SG-I respectively, respondent No. 2 passed the impugned orders revising the date of promotion of the petitioner as SG-III from 2931976 to 30.10.1982 and as officiating SG-I from 4.12.1981 to 1881983.
The petitioner protested against the revision of the dates of his promotion and also served a notice upon the State Government but failed to elicit response from the concerned authorities.
In the background of the aforementioned facts, the petitioner has challenged the impugned order on various grounds including the one that the adverse revision of dates of his promotion is vitiated due to the violation of the rules of natural justice.
He has averred that before adversely affecting his position in the cadre of officiating SG-III and officiating SG-I, respondent No. 2/competent authority did not give him action-oriented notice and opportunity of hearing.
He has further averred that order dated 931983 passed by the learned Single Judge in Teja Singh's case (supra) did not have any bearing on his promotion, in-as-much as, the orders impugned in that petition were quashed only qua the parties and he was not a party respondent in that petition.
According to him, the official respondents could not have revised his promotion on the basis of the direction given by the learned Single Judge in Teja Singh's case (supra) because order dated 131983 passed in C.W.P.
No. 1889 of 1976 filed by him had become final with the dismissal of the Special Leave Petition filed by the State of Punjab against order dated 1141983 passed by the Division Bench of the High Court in L.P.A. No. 370 of 1983.
3.
In the written statement filed on behalf of respondent Nos. 1 and 2, it has been averred that the petitioner's challenge to order Annexures P7 and P9 should be negatived because he has not challenged the seniority of Assistants circulated vide order dated 891983 (Annexure R2).
They have further averred that on account of revision of the seniority of Assistants the petitioner's position had been down-graded necessitating revision of the dates of his officiating promotion as SG-III and SG-I which were provisional in character and were subject to the implementation of order dated 931983 passed in C.W.P.
No. 4536 of 1976.
However, respondent Nos. 1 and 2 have not controverted the petitioner's assertion that L.P.A. No. 370 of 1983 filed against order dated 131983 passed by the learned Single Judge in C.W.P.
No. 1889 of 1976 filed by him had been dismissed by the Division Bench and SLP No. 8571 of 1983 filed against the order of the Division Bench was dismissed by the Supreme Court on 4121989.
They have also not disputed that before passing the impugned orders, no notice or opportunity of hearing was given to the petitioner.
4.
I have heard learned counsel for the parties and carefully perused the record.
In my opinion, the impugned orders are liable to be quashed on the ground of violation of the rule of audi alteram partem because it is an admitted position that before revising the date of the petitioner's promotions as officiating SG-III and SG-I respectively, the competent authority did not give him any notice and opportunity of hearing.
It is true that the petitioner's promotions as officiating SG-III w.e.f.
2931976 and as SG-I w.e.f.
1281981 were subject to the order passed by the High Court in Teja Singh's case (supra), but that order could not be made basis for revising his promotions to his prejudice because while allowing that petition, the learned Single Judge had specifically confined the nullification of Annexure P15 and P26 impugned therein only qua the parties.
This is clearly borne out from the following extracts of order dated 931983 :-  "Thus the fact remains that at no stage the Chief Engineer has passed any speaking order dealing with the merits of the case of the parties and in the light of that the impugned seniority lists Annexure P15 and P26 so far as these relate to the parties, cannot possible be sustained.
In the face of this, the respondents or reverting the petitioners also cannot possible be sustained.
Thus while quashing Annexure P.15 and P26 and the orders based thereon, I direct respondent No. 2, that is, the Chief Engineer, to redetermine the seniority of the petitioners after affording a hearing to them by passing a speaking order.
In case the Chief Engineer comes to the conclusion that while fixing the seniority of the petitioners, the seniority of any of the respondents is likely to be disturbed, he would also afford a hearing to that respondent before the passing of the speaking order.
Since the case appears to have already been delayed, the said respondent is further directed to finalise the whole process and determine the seniority within a period of six months from today.
Thus this petition stands disposed of as indicated above with no order as to costs."
5.
It appears that the authorities of the department did not apply their mind to the content of the order passed by the learned Single Judge in the case of Teja Singh and others and mechanically revised the petitioner's promotions by assuming that the Court had adversely commented upon his promotions.
However the fact of the matter is that the learned Single Judge had consciously adjudicated on the legality of Annexures P15 and P26 only qua the parties meaning thereby that those like the petitioner, who were not parties to C.W.P.
No. 4536 of 1976 were not to be adversely affected by revision of seniority of Assistants.
That apart, even in terms of the direction given by the learned Single Judge in that petition, revision of seniority could have been effected only after giving notice and opportunity of hearing to the affected persons and no material has been placed on record to show that before passing the order adversely affecting the petitioner's position in the cadre of Assistants, respondent No. 2 or any other competent authority had given him notice and opportunity of hearing.
6.
De hors the aforementioned conclusion, I am convinced that the officiating promotions accorded to the petitioner on the posts of SG-III and SG-I could not have been revised because the order passed in the petition filed by him became final with the dismissal of the SLP filed by the State Government and the review application filed by the contesting respondent.
No other point has been argued.
In the result, the writ petition is allowed.
Orders Annexures P7 and P9 are declared illegal and quashed.
The petitioner shall get all consequential benefit including revised fixation of pay and revised retiral benefits.
The needful be done within a period of four months from the date of presentation/receipt of a copy of this order.
Petition allowed.
