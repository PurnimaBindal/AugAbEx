THB Chalapathi, J - This judgment will dispose of two Regular Second Appeals, i.e. RSA No. 3110 of 1997 filed by the Chairman, PSEB and others; and the other RSA No. 1924 of 1997, filed by Narinder Paul.
Both the second appeals are directed against the decree and judgment of the learned Additional District Judge, Amritsar, in Civil Appeal No. 13 of 1995, dated April 22, 1997.
2.
Regular Second Appeal No. 1924 of 1997 is filed by the plaintiff Narinder Paul against restricting the benefit of the second emergency from 2441972 to 12.8.1974 only.
According to the plaintiff-appellant, the second emergency was imposed from 2441972 to 2631977 and he remained in the Army upto 2421978.
Therefore, he claimed the benefit of Military service from 2441972 to 2631977.
3.
The respondent in RSA No. 3110 of 1997 filed the suit claiming that the benefit of the Military service rendered by him from 2441972 to 10.2.1978, should be given to him.
According to him, he served the Indian Army during that period and after discharge from the Army he was employed as Lower Division Clerk in the Electricity Board on 10.3.1980.
Though initially the benefit of Military service was granted to him on 5111990, the same was withdrawn on 17121990.
Therefore, he filed the suit for mandatory injunction directing the defendants to implement the orders dated 5111990 and to restrain them from implementing the withdrawal order dated 17121990.
4.
According to the defendants, the plaintiff, who served the Military during second emergency, is not entitled to the benefit of the same.
Therefore, the suit is liable to be dismissed, and that the benefit of Military service was wrongly given to him on 5111990 and, therefore, the same has been withdrawn by the subsequent order dated 17121990.
5.
On the basis of the pleadings, the trial Court framed appropriate issues and on consideration of the material placed before him, dismissed the suit.
According to the trial Judge, the benefit of Military service i.e. from 2441972 to 10.2.1978 is to be extended to those people who served the Army during the first emergency and the benefit of Military service during the second emergency has not been extended under the Rules.
Therefore, he dismissed the suit.
Aggrieved by the said decree and judgment of the trial Court, the plaintiff filed the appeal before the learned Additional District Judge, Amritsar.
6.
Relying on a decision of a Division Bench of this Court in Narinder Nath Sharma v. State of Punjab and another, 1993(2) RSJ 173 : 1993(1) SCT 759 (P&H), the learned Additional District Judge, decreed the suit.
But, it has to be seen that there is an earlier decision of the Full Bench of this Court in Rajendar Kumar S.I.
v. State of Haryana, 1992(2) P.L.R.
754, wherein it has been held that the employees are not entitled to the benefit of the second emergency declared by notification dated December 3, 1971.
A Division Bench of this Court in Pritam Singh v.
P.S.E.B., ILR 1998(1) Punjab and Haryana 322 : 1997(4) SCT 471 (P&H), following the Full Bench decision in Rajendar Kumar's case (supra), held that the benefit of Military service rendered by the employees from March 12, 1969 to August 1, 1984, is not admissible under the Rules.
Again another Full Bench decision of this Court in Jang Singh and others v. State of Punjab, reported in 1997(3) R.S.J.
464 : 1997(4) : 1997(4) SCT 471 (P&H), held that the law laid down in Narinder Nath Sharma's case (supra) was not correct law in so far as it directed that the petitioners in that case were entitled to the benefit of entire period of Military service and not limiting the claim of the petitioners as aforesaid, as indicated by their Lordships.
Thus, the decision rendered in Narinder Nath Sharma's case (supra) does not lay down the correct law.
In this view of the matter, the appeal is to be allowed.
7.
The learned counsel for the respondent contended that the Government of Punjab issued a notification dated 2361998 in Memo No. 15-33/97-4DW/1354 declaring the period of second emergency (3.12.1971 to 2731977) as Military service for the purpose of rule 2 of the Punjab Government National Emergency (Concession) Rules, 1965.
In paragraph 2 it has been stated in the said notification as follows :-  "It is observed by the Hon'ble Punjab and Haryana High Court in Narinder Nath Sharma v. State of Punjab and another, 1992(7) SLR 345, that the service rendered by the Armed Forces personnel during the period of Emergency shall have to be reckoned as military service ipso facto for the purposes of these Rules.
It is further observed that the definition of the expression "military service" is so broad based and comprehensive, that an enabling provision has been made for all times to come under which the State Governments can always exercise its power only by making a simple declaration.
This means that although the period of first Emergency has been specified in rule 2 of the Punjab Government National Emergency (Concession) Rules, 1965, but the second period of Emergency has not been specified therein."
8.
Thus, it is clear that the basis for the issuance of the notification dated 2361998 is the decision of this Court in Narinder Nath Sharma's case (supra).
The said decision has not been upheld in the decision of Pritam Singh's case (supra) decided on 2081997, as it is contrary to the earlier Full Bench of this Court in Rajender Kumar's case (supra) and the same was also specifically over-ruled by another Full Bench decision of this Court in Jang Singh's case (supra), decided on August 18, 1997.
It is, thus, obvious that the notification of the State of Punjab dated 2361998 was issued in ignorance of the binding decision of this Court and also on a decision which has been specifically held to be laying not correct law.
It is unfortunate that the State Government has not been properly advised in this regard by its Legal Advisers.
I trust and hope that the Chief Secretary will look into the matter.
It is also pertinent to note that 1965 Rules have been specifically repealed by the Punjab Government by framing Punjab Recruitment of Ex- Servicemen Rules, 1982.
Therefore, there is no question of amending Rule 2 of the Punjab Government National Emergency (Concession) Rules, 1965 by notification issued in June 1998.
There is no question of amending the repealed Rules since they ceased to exist and have no operation after their repeal in 1982.
Therefore, the plaintiff-respondent cannot take advantage of the Government notification issued on 2361998.
9.
Apart from that, the Punjab State Electricity Board is an autonomous body.
It is governed by its own rules.
It is, no doubt, true that the Electricity Board may adopt the Rules framed by the Government but there must be a separate resolution by the Board adopting the Rules framed by the Government.
Since the Electricity Board has not adopted the amendment issued on 2361998 to the 1965 Rules, there is no question of applying the Government notification dated 2361998 to the case of the plaintiff.
10.
In view of my foregoing discussion, I am of the opinion that the suit filed by the plaintiff claiming benefit of the Military service rendered by him during the period from 2441972 to 10.2.1978 is liable to be dismissed.
The appeal, is, therefore, allowed and the decree and judgment of the lower appellate Court are hereby set aside and that of the trial court are restored.
11.
A copy of this judgment is directed to be sent to the Chief Secretary, State of Punjab, for taking appropriate action in the matter against those persons who are responsible for not bringing to the notice of the Government the correct legal position.
Appeal allowed.
