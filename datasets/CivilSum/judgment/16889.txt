RL Anand, J - Professor Bhim S Dahiya has filed the present writ petition under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India and it has been prayed that a writ of mandamus be issued against the respondents directing them to fix his pension by counting the following period of service as qualifying service for the purpose of pensonary benefits :-  a) From 2111965 to 30111965  b) From September 1972 to October 1974  c) From 881996 to 3131998  It was also prayed by the petitioner that his pension should be calculated on the basis of last pay drawn.
2.
The case set up by the petitioner in short can be described in the following terms :-  The petitioner was born on 741938.
He joined the service of the University as a Lecturer in the Department of English on 2111965.
Subsequently, he was selected as Reader in the same Department.
In the month of September, 1972 he was sanctioned study leave up to 1974 for doing PhD from the University of Cincinnati USA.
He successfully completed PhD and was twice awarded the prestigious Taft Fellowship.
In the year 1985 he was selected and joined as Professor of English.
He was appointed Pro-Vide Chancellor on 711992 for a period of three years.
Yet he was appointed Vice Chancellor of the University for a period of three years on 30.6.1993 and he worked up to 3061996 on a monthly pay of Rs. 7,600/- besides allowances.
On 171996 he joined back as Professor in the Department of English.
On 681996, he was awarded fellowship in the Indian Institute of Advanced Study, Shimla to work on a research project on the University Autonomy in India.
On 781996, he applied to the University for leave without pay but without any loss of service benefits.
The leave without pay was sought by him to comply with the necessary requirement of joining the Institute at Shimla.
As per the instructions of the Central Government dated 2821991, no fellow joining the institute shall be put to any loss of service or superannuatory benefit on account of leave without pay.
The petitioner was, however, treated on extra- ordinary leave without pay, which meant loss of service and superannuatory benefits to him.
This was objected to by the petitioner and he requested the University to treat this period as deputation period.
The University agreed to the request of the petitioner provided the borrowing institute agreed to pay his provident fund and other contribution due from the employer.
It is alleged by the petitioner that the University intimated the Institution the rate of matching contribution required from it towards petitioner's provident fund so as to treat his period of leave as deputation with the Institute.
Accordingly the Institute sent a draft of Rs. 8,068/- as the matching contribution to the University.
But the University returned the same stating that it was not acceptable to it.
Subsequently, the petitioner on 26111997 requested the University to treat the period of service with the Institute as deputation period, but the University did not accept his request in spite of a reminder.
The grievance of the petitioner is that the period of study leave and he period when he joined the Institute at Shimla from 881996 to 3131998 should be considered as a qualifying service and after counting this period as qualifying service his pension case should have been prepared by the University.
Since the University has not counted the aforesaid period as qualifying service, hence this writ petition.
3.
Notice of the writ petition was given to the respondents and the reply of the respondent-University can be reproduced in the following manner :-  "As per the Pension Rules/Scheme (Annexure R-1/1) the period from September, 1972 to October, 1974 and the period from 08.08.1996 to 31.03.1998 cannot be counted towards qualifying service for pension of the petitioner.
The contribution from 05091972 to 28.10.1974 has also not been contributed by the respondent University because the petitioner remained on Study Leave (without pay) during this period, also the contribution from the respondent University for the period from 08.08.1996 to 31.03.1998 has also not been contributed by the respondent University because the petitioner was on extra- ordinary leave without pay for this period.
As per the University Pension Scheme/Rules (Annexure R-1/1), the University Provident Fund contribution is necessary for the period which is to be counted as qualifying service.
As regards counting of period of 29 days ie from 02111965 to 30111965 towards qualifying service for pension, it is submitted that it was not counted inadvertently in the pension actually calculated.
However, it would be worthwhile to state here that its inclusion in the pension does not affect the quantum of the pension of the petitioner already fixed as the same has been fixed on six monthly basis.
However, this period is being added appropriately for up-dating the office record.
In so far as the calculation of emoluments is concerned, it is submitted that these have been calculated correctly on the basis of the petitioner's pay/emoluments as Professor in the Department of English in the respondent University as this post was the petitioner's substantive post.
Therefore, the present writ petition filed by the petitioner is liable to be dismissed."
4.
I have heard Mr. PS Dhaliwal, Advocate on behalf of the petitioner, Mr. Sumeet Goel, Advocate on behalf of the respondent-University and with their assistance have gone through the record of the case.
5.
During the course of submissions learned counsel for the petitioner submitted that no dispute is left between the petitioner and the respondent- University for the period from 2111965 to 30111965 i.e. a period of 29 days and the University has agreed to treat this period as qualifying service.
The learned counsel for the respondent-University also admits the contention raised by the learned counsel for the petitioner with the explanation that inadvertently this period could not be considered as a qualifying service and the University has no objection if the petitioner is given the benefit of this period of 29 days.
6.
The dispute is with regard to two terms.
Firstly, from September, 1972 to October, 1974, when the petitioner remained on study leave with the permission of the University.
The learned counsel appearing on behalf of the respondent-University submitted that since the petitioner did not discharge the duties or perform the functions, therefore, this period will not be considered as a qualifying service.
The submission of the learned counsel for the respondent-University is not acceptable to the Court because the petitioner remained on study leave which was granted by the University.
In these circumstances when the petitioner had sought the prior permission of the University for advance studies, in such a situation, it will be considered a qualifying service and this period has to be counted for the purpose of pension.
7.
The second disputed term is from 881996 to 3131998.
The learned counsel for the petitioner submitted that during this period the petitioner was sent on deputation by the University and, therefore, it should be considered as a qualifying service, especially when the University refused to accept the contribution which was sent to it by the Shimla Institute.
On the contrary, the learned counsel for the respondent-University submitted that the petitioner proceeded on deputation on his own request and this period was considered extraordinary leave without pay.
In these circumstances, the petitioner is not entitled to the benefit of the period from 881996 to 3131998.
There is a merit in the contention raised by the learned counsel for the respondents.
Resultantly, this period cannot be considered as a qualifying service for the purpose of counting pension.
8.
Consequently, the writ petition is partly allowed and a writ of mandamus is issued against the respondent-University directing it to count the following period as qualified service for the benefit of the petitioner in order to calculate the pensionary benefits :-  i) From 2111965 to 30111965  ii) From September, 1972 to October, 1974  9.
A declaration is also given that after counting the aforesaid period, the respondents shall re-assess the pension case of the petitioner within three months from the receipt of the copy of the order according to rules and it will be open to the petitioner to convince the authorities at what rate the pension should be calculated.
The case of the petitioner for calculating the revised pension shall be considered as per rules.
No order as to costs.
Petition partly allowed.
