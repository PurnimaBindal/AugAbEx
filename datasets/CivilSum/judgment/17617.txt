GS Singhvi, J - Petitioner, Som Nath, who is working as Head Constable in the Haryana Police, has filed this petitions for issue of a mandamus to promote him as Assistant Sub Inspector of Police (hereinafter referred to as 'ASI') and to quash the promotion of respondent Nos. 5 to 11.
2.
The petitioner joined service in the Police Department as Constable on 15121970.
After having passed the Lower School Course, he was promoted as Head Constable w.e.f.
411983.
He was confirmed on this post w.e.f.
31.1.1986.
According to the petitioner, respondent Nos. 5 to 11 are junior to him in the cadre of Head Constables but without considering his case, respondent Nos. 5 to 11 have been promoted as ASIs.
and this action of the respondents is arbitrary and unconstitutional.
The petitioner says that his work and conduct has been very good throughout his career and he has earned more than 70 per cent good entries.
According to the petitioner, there is no bad entry in his service record and there is no reason on the basis of which, the respondents could deny him promotion.
3.
In the reply, the respondents have stated that name of the petitioner was considered by respondent No. 3 for promotion to the post of ASI but he was found unsuitable.
According to the respondents, the petitioner has earned less than 70 per cent good entries and, therefore, he has no right to be promoted to the post of ASI.
4.
In this replication, the petitioner has reiterated that his Annual Confidential Reports are good and there is no record showing adverse remarks and in any case after passing the Intermediate School Course, the petitioner has a right to be promoted as ASI by being brought on List 'D'.
5.
A reply to this replication has been filed by the respondent Nos. 1 to 4.
The respondents have also reiterated their stand that the petitioner's record does not warrant his promotion to the post of ASI.
Reference has been made to the remarks made by the Superintendent of Police on 941992 casting aspersion on the integrity of the petitioner.
6.
Argument of Shri I.S.
Balhara, learned counsel for the petitioner, is that in terms of the Rule 139 of the Punjab Police Rules as they are applicable to the State of Haryana, the petitioner has a legal as well as Constitutional right to be promoted as ASI in preference to his junior and the respondents have seriously erred in overlooking his candidature.
Learned counsel argued that there does not exist such adverse remarks on the basis of which the official-respondents could treat the petitioner unsuitable for the post of ASI.
Shri Balhara argued that the so-called adverse remarks relating to the intergrity of the petitioner are inconsequential because the very officer who gave the adverse remarks against the petitioner had commended the service of the petitioner.
On the other hand, Shri Raina, learned counsel for respondent Nos. 1 to 4, argued that in the face of adverse entries made in the confidential record of the petitioner, the respondent No. 3 was fully justified in holding that the petitioner was not fit to be promoted by the post of ASI.
7.
Shri Raina produced before us the record containing the Annual Confidential Reports of the petitioner from 141983.
This record shows that between 141988 and 3131989, the petitioner has been described as a good officer.
Similar remarks have been made between 141989 and 3131990, and between 1.4.1990 and 3131991.
In the last report for the period 141991 to 14.2.1992 also, the petitioner has been described as a good officer.
The record also contains a printed Form No. 13-9(1) containing notes made by the Superintendent of Police and the Deputy Inspector General of Police.
This Form contains several entries.
On 941992, the following note has been recorded :-  "Clever Head Constable.
Require constant watch."
These remarks have been conveyed to the petitioner vide Memo No. 196/Steno dated 2621993.
Then there appears following remarks entered on 31.12.1992 :-  "Poor health, poor knowledge of law and procedure.
Require immediate improvement."
The remarks have also been conveyed to the petitioner vide Memo No. 197/Steno dated 2621993.
On 841993, the following remarks have been recorded for the period between 1.1.1993 and 3151993 :-  "Satisfactory.
Should be watched."
8.
On 16.5.1993, the remarks made in the Form are "Doing well".
For the period from 141993 to 1591993, the Superintendent of Police, Ambala, has given remarks showing that the petitioner's integrity is beyond doubt.
In the column of General Remarks, the Officer has recorded.
"Satisfactory performance".
For the period between 2261994 and 6.12.1994, the Superintendent of Police, Ambala, has again given the remarks "Satisfactory performance".
In the column of integrity, it has been indicated that there is no complaint against the petitioner.
For the period between 1591993 and 2261994, the petitioner is shown to be undergoing Intermediate School Course at Police Training Centre, Madhuban.
9.
A close scrutiny of this record shows that only one officer has made three remarks which can be described as adverse.
The same very officer has described the integrity of the petitioner 'beyond doubt' for the period from 141993 to 1591993.
The remarks which have been made for the period 1.4.1992 to 31.12.1992 have been signed in the first instance with the date of April, 1992, but then with a different ink, it has been made as 1.12.92.
Both these remarks are shown to have been conveyed on 2621993.
The remarks made on 841993 are also shown to have been conveyed but surprisingly no date has been given about the communication of these remarks.
Another surprising feature is that for the period between 1.1.1993 and 3151993, the remarks have been made on 841993.
The respondents have not explained as to how the officer could make remarks on 841993 for the period ending on 3151993.
The respondents have not indicated from the record as to whether the communications sent to the petitioner on 2621993 and 841993 were served upon the petitioner or not.
In the absence of any proof that these adverse remarks were conveyed to the petitioner coupled with the fact that the petitioner has been categoric in his assertion that the remarks were to conveyed to him, I am of the opinion that in the peculiar facts of this case, it would be just and proper to direct the competent authorities to review the case of the petitioner for promotion to the post of ASI.
I am quite conscious of the fact that promotion cannot be accorded to a person whose integrity is doubtful but the facts of this case show that the petitioner has not been treated fairly.
The casual approach with which the remarks have been made by the concerned officer who himself described the integrity of the petitioner 'beyond doubt' for the period between 141993 and 1591993, gives an indication that the petitioner's case has not been properly evaluated by the competent authority while considering his case for promotion and while superseding him.
10.
I, therefore, partly allow this writ petition and declare that the petitioner's case for promotion to the post of Assistant Sub Inspector of Police has not been fairly considered.
The official-respondents are directed to reconsider the case of the petitioner for his promotion as ASI in accordance with the existing policy of the Government which was operative at the time of promotion of his juniors.
The competent authority shall independently and objectively evaluate the remarks made in the Annual Confidential Reports and the Form 13-9(1) and decide - whether the remarks made by the officer on 941992, April, 1992/31121992 and 841993 are justified on not.
After such reassessment of the suitability of the petitioner, an order be passed by the competent authority declaring whether the petitioner has been found fit for promotion or not.
If the petitioner is considered suitable for promotion to the post of ASI, he shall be given such promotion from the date his juniors have been promoted.
In that event, he shall get all consequential benefits.
Consideration of the petitioner's case shall be made within a period of three months of the submission of the certified copy of this order before the Deputy Inspector General of Police, Ambala Range, Ambala.
Parties are left to bear their own costs.
