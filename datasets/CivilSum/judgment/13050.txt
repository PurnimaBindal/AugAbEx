KS Garewal, J - JP Hooda and 16 Accountants of Haryana State Cooperative Bank Limited had filed this civil writ petition under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India and had been joined by 84 other employees of the Bank who were Junior Accountants, Stenographers, Record Keepers, Peons, Gunmen, Sweepers and Gardeners etc.
However, barring JP Hooda and 16 Accountants all other petitioners have received the benefits which they were claiming and are no longer interested in pursuing this petition.
The petition survives only in respect of petitioners 1 to 17.
2.
According to the petitioners, Haryana State Cooperative Bank Limited is a cooperative society registered under the relevant law.
The Bank is an instrumentality of the State.
The State of Haryana and Registrar, Cooperative Societies, Haryana Chandigarh have a complete hold on the functioning and day-to-day management of the respondent bank.
The service conditions of the bank including those of the petitioners are governed by the Haryana State Cooperative Staff Service (Common Cadre) Rules, 1976.
The pay sales of various employees are also governed by the said Rules of 1976 which empower the Bank to revise the pay scales from time to time.
This is done by the Board of Directors with the concurrence of the Registrar.
3.
The Board of Directors had resolved to approve pay scales of the petitioners on the pattern of the State Government vide resolution dated June 19, 1987.
This resolution noted that the existing pay scale in force w.e.f.
141979 in respect of Senior Accountant was Rs. 750-1250, and revised pay scale with effect from January 1, 1986 in respect of Senior Accountant to Rs. 2000-3200.
The copy of the resolution is Annexure P-3.
4.
However, Registrar, Cooperative Societies vide order dated August 13, 1987 exercising powers conferred under Section 37 of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act, 1984 and in consultation with the Bank amended the Common Cadre Rules and revised pay scale for Accountants, with effect from January 1, 1986, to Rs. 1640-2900.
According to the learned counsel this was contrary to the recommendations of the Board of Directors of the Bank.
5.
According to the petitioners all employees of the Bank were drawing the same pay scales as were being drawn by their counterparts employed at the Haryana State Land Development Bank.
Indeed vide resolution dated June 19, 1987 the revision of pay was the same as the revised scale of Rs. 800-1600 given to the Accountants of HSLDB.
The petitioners also pleaded that Common Cadre Rules had been further amended with effect from May 5, 1988 and the revised pay scale to the petitioners which had been recommended by the Bank on June 19, 1987 was granted but this was only with effect from May 5, 1988 and not from January 1, 1986.
6.
The sole question in this petition is whether the revised pay scales should be made applicable with effect from January 1, 1986 or not ?
7.
On the basis of the petitioners' request this court had directed the respondents to consider the petitioners' case for revision of pay scale with effect from January, 1986.
In pursuance to this direction the whole matter was reconsidered by Registrar, Cooperative who passed order dated September 30, 1998 annexed herewith as Annexure P-8 and concurred with the decision of the Standing Committee of Bureau of Public Enterprises which observed as under :-  "Grant of pay scale of Rs. 2000-3200 to the Accountants of Harcobank and Senior Accountants of Central Coop.
Banks w.e.f.
111986 instead of 5588 and 10.5.88" was examined in detail and it was observed by the Committee that the pay scale of Accountant (Harcobank) and Senior Accountant Central Coop.
Banks before 1186 were Rs. 750-1250, the replacement of which after revision comes to Rs. 1600-2600 however, they have been placed in the pay scale of Rs. 1640-2900 w.e.f.
14.8.87 and Rs. 2000-3200 w.e.f.
5588 & 10.5.88 even without the approval of Finance Department.
Whereas the pay scale of the Managers/Accountant of Land Development Bank as on 4.6.86 were Rs. 800-1600, the replacement of which comes to Rs. 2000-3200 after revision w.e.f.
111986.
After 4.6.86 the posts of Accountants & Managers were merged into Managers in the pay scale of Rs. 2000-3200 against which the pay scales of Managers of Harco Bank is Rs.
2200-4000."
Relying upon the above observations the petitioners were recommended pay scale of Rs. 2000-3200 with effect from May 5, 1988 and May 10, 1988 respectively for Accountants and Senior Accountants.
According to the learned counsel for the petitioners they were entitled to same pay as Accountants of Haryana State Land Development Bank (HSLDB).
This was the revised scale given to the petitioners with effect from January 1, 1986 by the Directors of the bank in resolution Annexure P-3.
Indeed the resolution mentioned that the scale of Rs. 2000-3200 was the revised scale of Rs. 800-1600 given to the accountants of HSLDB.
This parity should have continued and not disturbed by the Registrar by accepting the recommendation of the Standing Committee of the Bureau of Public Enterprises.
8.
Learned counsel for the petitioners has referred to Ajmer Singh v. State of Haryana and others, 1995(1) RSJ 838 in which scale granted to Junior Engineers of the Corporation was with effect from January 1, 1993 while other Junior Engineers working in various Departments of the State Government had been granted the same scale of pay with effect from January 1, 1992.
This court held that this action was discriminatory and arbitrary and similar treatment should be given unless good reasons were shown.
It was held that the employer does not have an inherent right to fix different dates regarding the grant of revised scales of pay.
The Bench had drawn a distinction between removal of anomalies and revision of pay while referring to Division Bench of this court in SK Bishnoi v. State of Haryana, 1993(4) SCT 127 (P&H) : 1993(3) RSJ 444.
Learned counsel also referred to Gurmail Singh and others v. State of Haryana and others, 1991(1) RSJ 388, Haryana State Biologists Association v. The State of Haryana, 1994(3) SCT 198 (P&H) : 1994(4) RSJ 444, Rattan Singh v. State of Haryana, 1995(1) SCT 711 (P&H) : 1994(4) RSJ 422 and Gurmail Singh and others v. State of Haryana and others, 1999(1) RSJ 388 (supra).
9.
Opposing the petition, learned counsel for the respondents placed heavy reliance on SK Bishnoi and others v. State of Haryana (supra) and on the principle that the court could not take upon itself the responsibility of equating two posts.
This was the exclusive prerogative of the executive wing of the Government, which too had to be assisted by expert bodies before it comes to the conclusion of equating various posts for the purpose of fixation of pay scales.
According to the learned counsel for the respondents, in nut- shell, if it was a case of removal of an anomaly then upon removal of the anomaly the revision of pay scale would be payable from January 1, 1986 but if it was a case of revision of pay then the revised pay would become payable from the date of the revision, which was May 5/10, 1988 in the present case.
10.
The petitioners could still succeed if they are able to show that Accountants of the Bank were not drawing less pay than Accountants in other banks on the basis of the principle enunciated in Ajmer Singh's (case) and other cases cited above.
However, if we examine the pre-revised pay of accountants of the Bank and the accountants of HSLDB it would become apparent that the accountants of the Bank such as the petitioners, were drawing Rs. 750-1250 earlier while the accountants of HSLDB were drawing Rs. 800-1600.
Therefore, there appears to be no parity as such between the accountants.
In fact the accountants of HSLDB were merged with cadre of Managers and their revised pay with effect from January 1, 1986 was Rs. 2000-3200.
The Standing Committee came to the conclusion that the revised pay scale of the accountants of the bank came to Rs. 1600-2660 on the basis of the revision of the pre- revised scale of Rs. 750-1250.
They were placed in the pay scale of Rs. 1640- 2900 with effect from August 14, 1987 and Rs. 2000-3200 with effect from May 5/10, 1988.
11.
Vide resolution of the bank Annexure P-3 dated June 3, 1987, the Board of Directors had resolved to revise the pay scales and granted the Accountants the pay scale of Rs. 2000-3200 with effect from January 1, 1986.
This being revised pay scale given to the Accountants of HSLDB.
Does the action of the respondents smack of discrimination or is it perfectly sound if viewed from the administrative angle.
12.
It is a fact that petitioners were drawing less pay than the accountants of the Haryana State Land Development Bank (HSLDB).
Therefore, their respective revised pay would naturally be less.
The petitioners' pay, however, has been revised and is equivalent to the pay being paid to accountants of HSLDB but the only difference is that revision in their case became effective 2-1/2 years later.
The present is the case of revision of pay and not a case of removal of anomaly.
Therefore, the revision has to become applicable from the date the pay has been revised.
The petitioners claim that there was parity between them and the accountants of HSLDB is not a valid claim because the later drew higher pre-revised pay and also worked as Managers.
Consequently this being a case of pay revision and not being case of pay anomaly the revision cannot be directed to be made retrospective.
The petitioners case is not covered by the authorities Ajmer Singh and others (supra) but by SK Bishnoi (supra).
There is no merit in this petition.
The same is hereby dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
