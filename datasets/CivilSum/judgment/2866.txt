GS Singhvi, J - This petition is directed against order dated 3061997 (Annexure P1) passed by Chandigarh Bench of the Central Administrative Tribunal (for short, `the Tribunal') vide which it allowed OA No. 494-CH of 1997 filed by respondent Nos. 1 to 13 - Smt. Sudesh Rathore and others against the withdrawal of the benefit of fixation of pay in the Central Pay Scale with effect from 111973.
2.
Respondent Nos. 1 to 13 were initially appointed as Instructors in the National Discipline Scheme (hereinafter described as `NDS Instructors') framed by the Central Government under the programme titled "National Fitness Corps".
In the year 1965, the Central Government decided to de-centralize the National Fitness Corps Programme and to transfer NDS Instructors under the control of the governments of the States/Union Territories.
However, that decision could not be effectively implemented till 1972.
3.
In the meanwhile, respondent Nos. 1 to 13 gave option for absorption in the service of Union Territory, Chandigarh.
They were finally absorbed in the service of Union Territory, Chandigarh as Physical Training Instructor in October, 1972.
Their pay was fixed in the pay scale prescribed for that post by the Government of Punjab because the administration of Union Territory, Chandigarh had adopted Punjab Pay Scales for its employees.
4.
In the year 1980, some of the employees of National Fitness Corps Programme filed Writ Petition No. 5450 of 1980 in Karnataka High Court for grant of benefit of the Central Pay Scale till their absorption in the State services.
The High Court allowed the writ petition and directed the grant of Central Pay Scale to the employees till their absorption in the State service.
Special Leave Petition No. 12335 of 1985 filed by the Union of India was dismissed by the Supreme Court.
Thereafter, the Government of India decided that the benefit of the revised Central Pay Scales be extended to the NDS Instructors till their absorption.
That decision was circulated vide letter No. F.21-3/86-YS-IV dated 1951986 (Annexure P4"B") issued by the Ministry of Human Resource Development, Department of Youth Affairs and Sports, paragraphs 2 and 3 of which are reproduced below :-  "2.
The question of grant of revised scale as allowed to teaching staff in schools run by Central Government or by the Union Territory administrations from 111967 onwards to NDS Instructors of the erstwhile National Discipline Scheme now absorbed in the State service has been reconsidered by the Central Government in the light of judgment dated the 11th April, 1985 of the Karnataka High Court in writ petition No. 5450/80 and judgment dated the 20th December, 1985 of the Supreme Court in SLP No. 12335/85 and it has been decided that benefit of the following revised pay scales be extended to the NDS Instructors who have been absorbed in the State service:- Sr. No. Old Scale of pay Revised pay scale Date from which benefit is to be given.
(i) Rs. 110-200 Rs. 113-225 1.1.67 (ii) Rs. 110-225 Rs. 118-270 21.12.67 (iii) Rs. 118-270 Rs. 125-330 27.5.70 (iv) Rs. 125-330 Rs. 165-330 1.9.72 (v) Rs. 165-330 Rs. 330-560 1173 3.
The benefit of these scales to be given only till the date of their absorption in the State service.
From the date of absorption their pay scale will be fixed in the State scale."
5.
Although respondent Nos. 1 to 13 were not entitled to the benefit of the above reproduced decision of the Central Government because they stood absorbed in the service of Union Territory, Chandigarh in October, 1972, but by mis-interpreting circular dated 1951986, District Education Officer, Chandigarh (petitioner No. 4) fixed their pay in the Central Pal Scale w.e.f.
111973 and also paid the arrears.
6.
In 1994, the Accountant General, Union Territory, Chandigarh pointed out that grant of Central Pay Scale to NDS Instructors, who were absorbed in the service of the Union Territory, Chandigarh in the year 1972 was irregular.
Thereupon, Director, Public Instructions (Schools), Union Territory, Chandigarh (petitioner No. 3) vide memo dated 1291994 (Annexure P5) directed petitioner No. 4 to refix the pay of respondent Nos. 1 to 13 and other NDS Instructors after withdrawing the benefit of the Central Pay Scale and work out the recoveries to be made from their pay.
7.
Respondent Nos. 1 to 13 and others challenged the direction contained in memo dated 1291994 by filing an application under Section 19 of the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985 which was registered as OA No. 923/CH/1994.
They claimed that their pay had been correctly fixed in the Central Pay Scale w.e.f.
111973 and petitioner No. 3 did not have the jurisdiction, power or authority to withdraw that benefit simply on the basis of the audit objection.
The petitioners contested that application by asserting that the pay of only those NDS Instructors was to be fixed in the Central Pay Scale, who had not been absorbed in the State services till 111973 and as the applicants stood absorbed in the service of Union Territory, Chandigarh in October, 1972, they were not entitled to fixation of pay in the Central Pay Scale.
8.
After an indepth examination of the issues raised by the parties, the Tribunal dismissed the application of respondents Nos. 1 to 13 vide its order dated 841996 (Annexure P2).
Some of the conclusions which can be culled out from the detailed reasons recorded by the Tribunal are as under :-  (i) The applicants were not entitled to fixation of their pay in the Central Pay Scale w.e.f.
111973 because they had been absorbed in the service of the Union Territory, Chandigarh in October, 1972.
(ii) From the date of absorption, the applicants were entitled to get pay in the scale applicable to the Union Territory, Chandigarh on Punjab Pattern.
(iii) Only those NDS Instructors were entitled to fixation of pay in the Central Pay Scale who were absorbed on or after 111973.
(iv) The direction contained in memo dated 1291994 did not suffer from any legal infirmity.
(v) The arrears paid to the applicants cannot be recovered without giving them reasonable opportunity to make representations in the matter.
9.
Thereafter, petitioner No. 3 issued notices dated 15101996 to the affected NDS Instructors proposing to refix their pay w.e.f.
111973 on the premise that they were not entitled to fixation of pay fixed in the Central Pay Scale with effect from that date.
Respondent Nos. 1 to 13 filed replies to contest the notices.
They claimed that they had not made any misrepresentation or played fraud for fixation of their pay in the Central Pay Scale and, therefore, there was no justification to withdraw the benefit given to them.
However, the concerned officer did not agree with them and passed order dated 7/1421997 (Annexure P5/A) for refixation of their pay without giving them the benefit of the Central Pay Scale w.e.f.
111973.
He also ordered recovery of the excess amount paid to them.
10.
Respondent Nos. 1 to 13 challenged order Annexure P5/A in OA No. 494-CH of 1997.
They claimed that in view of order dated 791995 passed in OA No. 331-CH of 1994 - Nathi Ram v. Union of India; order dated 18.3.1997 passed in OA No. 1274-CH of 1995 - Smt. Indra Vij v. Union of India; the order passed in OA No. 211-CH of 1995 - Smt. Dropti Chawla v. Union of India order dated 26.1.1996 passed by the Supreme Court in SLP No. 96 CC 672/96 - Union Territory, Chandigarh v. Nathi Ram, the direction given by the petitioner No. 3 for refixation of their pay after withdrawing the benefit of the Central Pay Scale may be declared as nullity.
11.
In their reply, the petitioners relied on order dated 841996 passed by the Tribunal in OA No. 923/CH/1994 and pleaded that the applicants are estopped from questioning the refixation of their pay.
However, without dealing with the objection raised on behalf of the petitioners and by placing reliance on the orders passed in the cases of Nathi Ram, Smt. Dropti Chawla and Smt. Indra Vij and the order passed by the Supreme Court in the SLP filed by Union Territory, Chandigarh in the case of Nathi Ram, the Tribunal allowed the application and quashed order Annexure P5/A with the direction that the applicants will continue to be paid in the Central Pay Scale and no recovery be made from their salary.
12.
Mrs. Lisa Gill, learned counsel for the petitioners argued that the impugned order is liable to be declared as vitiated by an error of law and quashed because it runs contrary to order dated 841996 passed in OA No. 923/CH/1994.
She emphasized that while dismissing the previous application filed by respondent Nos. 1 to 13 and others, the Tribunal had categorically held that they were not entitled to fixation of pay in the Central Pay Scale w.e.f.
111973 and the direction given by petitioner No. 3 for refixation of their pay after withdrawing the benefit of the Central Pay Scale was legally correct and submitted that in the face of that finding, the Tribunal did not have the jurisdiction to entertain the prayer made in the second application for allowing respondent Nos. 1 to 13 to retain the benefit of the Central Pay Scale.
She further argued that the orders passed by the Tribunal in the cases of Nathi Ram, Dropti Chawla and Indra Vij turned on the peculiar facts of their cases and the same could not have been made basis for granting relief to respondent Nos. 1 to 13 in the face of order dated 841996 passed by it in OA No. 923/CH/1994.
Mrs. Gill referred to the contents of circular dated 1951986 to show that the benefit of the Central Pay Scale was to be given only to those NDS Instructors who had not been absorbed in the State services till 111973 and argued that respondent Nos. 1 to 13 who had been absorbed in the service of Union Territory, Chandigarh in October, 1972 were not entitled to the benefit of the Central Pay Scale and they cannot retain the benefit of wrong fixation made by the petitioner No. 4.
13.
Shri AK Chopra, learned Senior Advocate appearing for respondent Nos. 1 to 10, 12 and 13 argued that the impugned order cannot be termed as erroneous because it is essentially based on orders passed by the Tribunal in Nathi Ram's case (supra) which was confirmed by the Supreme Court and two other orders passed in the cases of Indra Vij and Dropti Chawla.
He then argued that the object of policy contained in circular dated 1951986 was to extend the benefit of the Central Pay Scale to the erstwhile NDS Instructors and no illegality was committed by petitioner No. 4 by fixing the pay of respondent Nos. 1 to 13 in the Central Pay Scale w.e.f.
111973.
Shri Chopra submitted that his clients could not have been singled out for being denied the benefit of fixation of pay in the Central Pay Scale because all other NDS Instructors who were absorbed in the State Services were given the benefit of that pay scale.
He laid emphasis on the fact that the Central Government had agreed to bear the burden of salary etc. payable to the erstwhile NDS Instructors and argued that respondent Nos. 1 to 13 should not be penalised by denying them the benefit of fixation of pay in the Central Pay Scale merely because they had dutifully exercised option for absorption in the service of Union Territory, Chandigarh.
He then argued that respondent Nos. 1 to 13 cannot be treated to have been absorbed in the service of Union Territory, Chandigarh till 111973 because they had been appointed in October, 1972 against temporary posts.
In the end, he argued that even if, this Court comes to the conclusion that respondent Nos. 1 to 13 were not entitled to the benefit of the Central Pay Scale w.e.f.
111973, the direction given for recovery of the arrears may be nullified because they had not made any contribution in the wrong fixation of the pay in the Central Pay Scale.
Shri Chopra pointed out that there is no allegation against respondent Nos. 1 to 13 that they had made misrepresentation or had played fraud for securing fixation of pay in the Central Pay Scale and argued that they may not be saddled with the liability to refund the excess amount because most of them have retired from service.
14.
We have given serious thought to the respective arguments of the learned counsel.
15.
A careful reading of order dated 841996 shows that while dismissing OA No. 923/CH/1994, the Tribunal had recorded a categorical finding that the direction given by petitioner No. 3 vide letter dated 1291994 for refixing the pay of respondent Nos. 1 to 13 and others after withdrawing the benefit of the Central Pay Scale does not suffer from any legal infirmity.
This is clearly borne out from paragraphs 10 and 12 of the order of the Tribunal which are reproduced below :-  "10.
From a reading of the Govt. of India's letter dated 19586 (Ann.
A/10), it is clear beyond any doubt that the benefit of the revised scales was to be given only till the date of the absorption of the NDS Instructors in the State/UT Administration service and from the date of such absorption their pay was to be fixed in the State scales.
It is further clear from this letter that the States were requested to get the arrears from grant of revised pay scales calculated upto the date of their absorption in State Service, verified from the Accountant General concerned as also to get the total amount of arrears intimated to the department of the Govt. of India to enable the Central Govt. to allocate additional funds to the State Governments.
It is also stated in this letter that, "it may also be made clear in the order of refixation of pay that any excess payment, if any made erroneously, would be recoverable from them."
In para 6(ii) of this letter again, it is stated that, "it may kindly be ensured that the NDS Instructors get the revised any scale in the State scale from the date of absorption only after refixation has been approved by the Central Govt. No document has been placed on record to show that such a verification was got done from the AG, UT, Chandigarh i.e. Respondent No. 7 before the pay of the individual applicants was refixed on the basis of the letter dated 19596.
Respondents No. 4 and 5 had only asked Respondent No. 6 to take necessary action on the basis of the UO advice of the Finance Deptt.
In that advice, as already mentioned earlier, it is clearly mentioned that the amount of the arrears was to be calculated only upto the date of absorption of the NDS Instructors.
Thus, the applicants, cannot be held entitled to pay in the scale of Rs. 330- 560 w.e.f.
111973 as they had been absorbed in the UT Chandigarh Administration in October, 1972 and from such absorption they were to get pay in the scales applicable to them under the UT Chandigarh Administration.
It may be mentioned here that at the time of Punjab pattern of scale of pay was applicable to the employees of the UT, Chandigarh Administration and these had been earlier revised in 1968 and were again subsequently revised in 1978 and in 1986.
It also cannot be ignored that in some States/Union Territories, the actual date of absorption of NDS Instructors may have been different and in cases where the absorption took place on or after 1173, they would be entitled to the benefit of the revised Central Pay Scales w.e.f.
111973 till the date of their actual absorption.
This fact in itself would not substantiate any plea of discrimination, for the simple reason, that the scales of pay and the formula for payment of dearness allowance varies from State to State and this difference is such which cannot be ignored in the case of the UT, Chandigarh Administration for the reason, that UT, Chandigarh Administration has been following right from the date of its coming into existence from 111966, the Punjab pattern of scales of pay except for a brief period of 111986 till 3131991 during which the employees were given an option to cover on over to the Central Pay Scales of pay which were adopted by the orders issued by the Central Govt. in April, 1988.
However, later on w.e.f.
1491, the Punjab pattern of scales of pay was re-introduced and that too w.e.f.
111986 notionally and for actual payment from 141991.
The pay scales under the Central pattern have from time to time been linked up with a different stage of price index as compared to the stage of price index adopted in the Punjab pattern of scales of pay.
The order of Respondent No. 6 fixing the pay of the applicants in the scale of Rs. 330-560 w.e.f.
111973 and again in the UT scales of pay with effect from the same date must result in an anomalous situation and confusion, particularly, with regard to the admissibility of dearness allowance etc. 12.
From all what has been said and discussed above, we have no hesitation in concluding that there is neither any legal or administrative justification for quashing the impugned letter dated 1291994 as at Annexure A/1.
We further hold that as respondent No. 7 has raised objection in the discharge of his constitutional responsibilities, he cannot be faulted for raising an objection in regard to expenditure met out of the State Funds.
This is a right given to him under the Constitution of India.
It is for the competent executive authority to give to him his point of view and if the Accountant General accepts the point of view of the executive authority, well and good, otherwise respondent No. 7 is free to include the same in his Annual Report to be presented to the Parliament or the State Legislature, as the case may be."
(Emphasis added).
16.
The Tribunal also distinguished the order passed in Nathi Ram's case (supra) in the following words :-  "Learned counsel for the applicants also placed reliance on a judgment of a Division Bench of the Chandigarh Bench of the Central Administrative Tribunal, dated 791995 rendered in OA No. 331-CH of 1994.
One of us, namely, Shri Jasbir S Dhaliwal, Judicial Member, was a party to that judgment.
We have carefully considered that judgment and find that in that judgment the issue of giving the revised scale of Rs. 330-560 to the NDS Instructors w.e.f.
111973 has neither been considered nor decided and, therefore, this judgment is of no avail to the applicants."
17.
Respondents Nos. 1 to 13 did not challenge the aforementioned order of the Tribunal by filing appeal in the Supreme Court.
Therefore, the finding recorded by the Tribunal on the legality of the direction contained in letter dated 1291994 issued by petitioner No. 3 will be deemed to have become final and neither respondent Nos. 1 to 13 could challenge the refixation of their pay after withdrawing the benefit of the Central Pay Scale nor could the Tribunal accept their plea that they should be allowed to retain the benefit of fixation of pay in the Central Pay Scale.
However, while deciding OA No. 494-CH of 1997, the successor Tribunal completely ignored the effect and implication of the findings recorded in order dated 841996 on the legality of the instructions contained in memo dated 1291994, the binding nature of that order and granted undeserved relief to respondent Nos. 1 to 13.
Therefore, we have no hesitation to hold that order Annexure P1 is vitiated by an error of law apparent on the face of the record.
18.
The argument of Shri Chopra that respondent Nos. 1 to 13 were entitled to the fixation of pay under the Central Pay Scale because they had not been finally absorbed in 1972 and will be deemed to be holding the posts of NDS Instructors on 111973 is based on a wholly misconceived assumption about their status.
In our considered view, respondent Nos. 1 to 13 had become employees of Union Territory, Chandigarh in October, 1972 for all intents and purposes and severed their relations with the Central Government.
They were entitled to the protection of the benefit earned on the basis of the previous service, but could not claim that they were continuing as NDS Instructors on 111973.
Therefore, the fixation of their pay in the Central Pay Scale w.e.f.
111973 was clearly erroneous and petitioner No. 3 did not commit any illegality by directing refixation of their pay and withdrawal of the benefit earned by them an account of wrong fixation of pay.
19.
The orders passed by the Tribunal in the cases of Nathi Ram, Dropti Chawla and Indra Vij and the one passed by the Supreme Court in the SLP filed by Union Territory, Chandigarh in the case of Nathi Ram do not, in any manner, help the cause of respondent Nos. 1 to 13 because in those cases, relief was granted to the applicants without examining the legality of the instructions contained in letter dated 1291994 and the entitlement of the employees to fixation of pay in the Central Pay Scale.
The Tribunal was of the view that the benefit granted to the applicants by fixing their pay in the Central Pay Scale cannot be withdrawn without giving them notice and opportunity of hearing.
Their Lordships of the Supreme Court refused to interfere with the order passed by the Tribunal in Nathi Ram's case apparently because he had retired in the meanwhile.
However, at the same time, the Supreme Court clearly disapproved the observations made by the Tribunal qua the audit objection.
Therefore, those orders cannot be made basis for granting relief to respondent No. 1 to 13.
Moreover, in so far as these respondents are concerned, order dated 841996 passed by the Tribunal in OA No. 923/CH/1994 was binding on the parties and the second application filed by them was liable to be dismissed as barred by res judicata.
20.
However, we find merit in the submission of Shri Chopra that the petitioners should not be allowed to recover the arrears paid to respondent Nos. 1 to 13.
Indeed, it is not the petitioners' case that respondent Nos. 1 to 13 had obtained the benefit of fixation of pay by making misrepresentation or playing fraud upon the authorities concerned.
Therefore, keeping in view the fact that most of them have retired, we feel that it would be too harsh to direct them to refund the amount paid in pursuance of the fixation of the pay in the Central Pay Scale.
This is also the view expressed by the Supreme Court in Sahib Ram v. State of Haryana, 1995(1) SCT 668; Union of India v. M Bhaskar, 1996(4) SCT 56 and by this Court in HR Dhanjal and others v. State of Haryana and others, 2002(2) SLR 150 : 2002(1) SCT 503 (P&H).
21.
In the result, the writ petition is allowed.
Order dated 3061997 passed by the Tribunal in OA No. 494-CH of 1997 is quashed.
The petitioners shall be free to refix the pay of respondent Nos. 1 to 13 after withdrawing the benefit of the Central Pay Scale w.e.f.
111973.
They shall also be entitled to accordingly revise the pension of those NDS Instructors, who have retired from service.
However, the petitioners shall not recover the arrears paid to respondent Nos. 1 to 13 in pursuance of the wrong fixation of the pay made by petitioner No. 4.
Petition allowed.
