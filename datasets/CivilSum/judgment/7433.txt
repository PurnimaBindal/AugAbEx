Tapen Sen, J. (Oral) - Heard the learned counsel for the parties.
2.
The writ Petitioner is aggrieved by the Order dated 23.9.1988 (Annexure P- 8) by which the respondent No. 3 (Shri Harsharan Singh) was promoted as an Establishment Officer thereby superseding the claims of the writ Petitioner.
The Petitioner prays for the issuance of a Writ of Mandamus commanding upon the State Respondents to consider and promote him to the post of an Establishment Officer with effect from the date the Respondent No. 3 was so promoted and thereafter, grant all consequential benefits to him such as fixation of pay, seniority, arrears etc.  3.
The facts which are necessary to be taken note of are that on 1661969, the Petitioner was promoted as an Assistant, whereafter on 18.8.1979, he was promoted to the post of a Deputy Superintendent which subsequently came to be termed as Superintendent Grade-II.
In 1981, a final seniority list of such Superintendents Grade-III was circulated and in that list, the Petitioner was placed at Serial No. 7 while the Respondent No. 3 was placed below him at Serial No. 10.
4.
On 281982 (i.e. after about three years from the date of promotion of the Petitioner), the Respondent No. 3 was similarly promoted on the post of Superintendent Grade-III.
On 1611985, the Petitioner was again promoted as Superintendent Grade-I and almost more than 1-1/2 years thereafter, the Respondent No. 3 was promoted as an Assistant Superintendent Grade-I Subsequently, on 1481986, the Petitioner was confirmed after completing the period of probation on the post of Superintendent Grade-I  5.
In the meanwhile on 641984, the State Respondents published a final seniority list of Superintendents Grade-III and in that seniority list, the Petitioner was shown at Serial No. 2 while the Respondent No. 3 was placed at Serial No. 3.
Similarly on 2061988, another tentative seniority list was published in relation to Superintendents Grade-I and in that list, the name of the Petitioner was shown at Serial No. 2 while the Respondent No. 3 was shown at Serial No. 3.
6.
Thus, upon perusal of the facts narrated above, it will be evident that in all the seniority lists, the Petitioner was senior to the Respondent No. 3 and in all the events that took place in relation to the service career of the Petitioner vis-a-vis the Respondent No. 3, the Petitioner always got promotion before the Respondent No. 3.
7.
It is pleaded that the next avenue of promotion from the post of Superintendent Grade-I was that to the post of an Establishment Officer which fell vacant on 121988.
8.
Let it be recorded that on 11101988, the learned counsel had wanted some time to file an affidavit stating that there were no departmental rules for the promotion to the post of an Establishment Officer.
Thereafter, an affidavit was filed and in Paragraph 3 thereof, it was stated that the post of an Establishment Officer is not governed by any departmental service rules and that the said post is a Class II post.
It has further been stated that there are other Class II rules known as the Punjab Animal Husbandry (State Service Class-II) Rules, 1981, but the post of an Establishment Officer has not been included in the said Rules.
Therefore, the learned counsel for the Petitioner states that the promotion to the post of an Establishment Officer is not on the basis of selection but it is based on seniority.
9.
Before proceeding further, let it also be recorded that the final seniority list of Assistant Grade III circulated on 241981 is Annexure P-1 and the Order by which the Petitioner was promoted as Superintendent Grade I, is Annexure P-2.
The Order dated 1481986 certifying that the Petitioner had completed the period of probation as Superintendent Grade I on 16.1.1986 is Annexure P-3, while the seniority list circulated on 641984 showing the Petitioner at Serial No. 2 and the Respondent No. 3 at Serial No. 3 in the seniority list of Superintendents Grade-III, is Annexure P-4.
Similarly, the seniority list dated 2061988 is Annexure P-5.
10.
The learned counsel for the Petitioner states that since the Petitioner was senior to the Respondent No. 3 and had been promoted earlier to the Respondent No. 3 on all occasions, he therefore, had a legitimate expectation that he would be promoted to the post of an Establishment Officer no sooner the said post fell vacant on 121988 but to his utter chagrin, he was shocked when he came to know, that instead of promoting him on that post, the State Respondents had promoted the Respondent No. 3 to the post of an Establishment Officer.
This order is Annexure P-8 which has been impugned in this writ Petition.
11.
Let it be recorded that Notices were served upon the Respondent No. 3 on 28.11.1988 as is evident from the office records but notwithstanding such service of Notice, he has chosen not to contest this writ petition nor has he filed any counter affidavit in the instant case.
12.
The State Respondents, on the other hand, have filed a counter affidavit/written statement wherein they have admitted the statements made by the Petitioner in relation to the factual events that took place.
However, their main contention is that the Respondent No. 3, who was second in the panel, had been promoted as an Establishment Officer for his better record of service.
They have further stated that the Petitioner scored the following overall assessments :-  "Year Overall Assessment 1979-80 Average  1980-81 Good 1981-82 Good 1982-83 Good 1983-84 Good 1984-85 Average  1985-86 Average  1986-87 Good 1987-88 Good" 13.
As compared to the Petitioner's track record, the Respondents have stated that the Respondent No. 3, on the other hand, scored the following overall assessments :-  "Year Overall Assessment 1979-80 Outstanding 1980-81 Outstanding 1981-82 Outstanding 1982-83 Outstanding 1983-84 Outstanding 1984-85 Outstanding 1985-86 Outstanding 1986-87 Outstanding 1987-88 Outstanding" 14.
The Respondents have also stated that the basis of the outstanding performance of the Respondent No. 3, and also on the basis of aforementioned ACRs of the Petitioner vis-a-vis the Respondent No. 3, they promoted the Respondent No. 3 instead of the Petitioner.
15.
The aforementioned contentions of the Respondents are considered only to be rejected.
This Court does not find any rationale behind the stand of the Respondents to accept the aforementioned contention because if the overall assessment of the Respondent No. 3 was outstanding in the years 1981-82 to 1985-86, then there is no explanation as to way they did not promote him during those periods and instead, promoted the Petitioner and placed him always above in the seniority lists although his ratings during the periods 1981-82 to 1986-87 were said to be only "good".
If the ratings of the Respondent No. 3 were outstanding, then, they should have promoted him at those stages itself but they did not do so, and now, it is too late for the Respondents to justify such an action.
That apart, it is also noticed that the resort of the Respondents to the ACRs is obviously misconceived because if the Respondent No. 3's ACRs were outstanding and the Petitioner's ACRs were not as outstanding as his, there is also no explanation coming forth as to whether the Respondents communicated these ACRs to the Petitioner.
That apart, the overall assessment for the period which is really relevant was not adverse insofar as the Petitioner is concerned.
They are all "good".
Even if it is contended that as compared to "good", the Respondent No. 3 was "outstanding" even then, this was the exact position even in the years 1982 to 1986 when promotion was given to the Petitioner.
There is, therefore, no explanation coming forth as to why the Respondents at those stages, promoted the Petitioner and put him above the Respondent in the seniority list.
It, therefore, appears to this Court that the written statement is not at all satisfactory and amounts to mechanically creating a defence against the Petitioner.
16.
It is well known that non-communication of entries which are considered to prejudicially affect an employee must be communicated to such an employee within a reasonable time so that he may know as to what went against him and so that he is in a position to improve himself.
In the instant case, no such procedure has been resorted to.
In support of the aforementioned contention, this Court draws inspiration from the judgments of the Hon'ble Supreme Court passed in the case of Baidyanath Mahapatra v. State of Orissa and another, AIR 1989 SC 2218; Vijay Kumar I.A.S.
v. State of Maharashtra and others, AIR 1988 SC 2060; State of UP v. Yamuna Shanker Misra and another, 1997(2) SCT 234 (SC) : 1997 Supreme Court Cases (L&S) 903; Swatanter Singh v. State of Haryana and others, 1998(1) SCT 513 (SC) : 1997 Supreme Court Cases (L&S) 909 and Sukhdeo v. Commissioner, Amravati Division, Amravati and other, 1996(3) SCT 613 (SC) : (1996)5 Supreme Court Cases 103.
17.
That apart, this Court also takes notice of the arguments of the learned counsel for the Petitioner made on the basis of his subsequent affidavit to the effect that for the post of an Establishment Officer, there were no rules prevalent nor adopted by the Government.
This has not been answered by the Respondents.
That being the position, this Court proceeds to accept the submission to the effect that for promotion to the post of an Establishment Officer, the criteria was to be based on seniority-cum-merit and not merit-cum-seniority.
In the process of adjudicating the promotion to that post, therefore, it was incumbent upon the Respondents to have given due weightage to the seniority.
Although it is well known that promotion cannot be claimed as a matter of right by virtue of seniority alone, an officer who is found unfit, may be passed over and a junior may be promoted but, in the instant case, this Court finds that if the Respondent No. 3 was more meritorious and the Petitioner less in merit, the same was obviously based on the ACRs which have been quoted above and which were never communicated to the petitioner.
Moreover, if the Petitioner was less meritorious, then there is no explanation as to why did the Respondents promote him over the Respondent No. 3 in each of the earlier stages.
This Court, therefore, has no option but to hold that the action of the Respondents in promoting the Respondent No. 3 abruptly, in the year 1988 was neither fair nor justified and the reason being given out now is too feeble an explanation to accept because the Respondents promoted the Petitioner on the basis of "good" ratings on more than one occasion even as against the then availability of "outstanding" ratings of the Respondent No. 3.
They cannot now say that one was "good" and the other "outstanding" and that too, when there are no Rules to guide this particular promotion.
The Respondents were, therefore, not correct in their approach in relation to the Petitioner and the Respondent No. 3.
18.
As a consequence of what has been stated above, this Court is of the opinion that the Respondents must re-consider the entire matter and pass an order promoting the Petitioner on the post of an Establishment Officer with effect from the date when the Respondent No. 3 was promoted.
However, taking into consideration the fact that the Respondent No. 3 was promoted in the year 1988 (i.e. almost Seventeen Years ago), the effect of this order should not be construed that it is a direction to revert the Respondent No. 3.
This is all the more so because the Petitioner, who was appointed in the year 1961, should have, by efflux of time be deemed to have retired by now (Year 2005).
That being the position, this Court directs that he would be entitled only to re- calculation of his monetary benefits and arrears of pay.
None of these calculations should prejudice the Respondent No. 3 and no steps should be taken to recover any money/pay that he may have been paid.
It is expected that the respondents will do the needful within a period of six months.
The writ petition stands disposed of an closed.
Petition allowed.
