Mrs. Rewa Gandhi, Member - On 161999 after hearing the learned counsel, we inter alia made order to the following effect :-  The Chief Secretary to Government Haryana vide order dated 441996 ordered regularisation of work charge/casual/daily wage employees and also ad hoc employees for which different conditions were laid.
(i) In the case of work-charge employees, it was provided that in case the employee has completed 3 years continuous service as on 3111996, he is to be considered for regularisation.
(ii) In case of a casual/daily wage employee, it was provided that casual and daily wage employees who have completed 3 years service on 3111996 and were in service on 3111996, they shall be considered for regularisation.
It was, however, provided that in case a daily wage worker has worked for a minimum period of 240 days in a year and the break in service in any year is not more than one month at a time and has in this manner been in service for 3 calendar years as on 3111996, he shall be considered for regularisation of service.
(iii) Lastly it was provided that in case of daily wagers, who have completed 3 years of service on class-III posts on 3131996 and were in service on 3111996, they shall be regularised provided they fulfil the requisite qualifications and were originally appointed on Class III post and the posts are available.
2.
In the present case, we are concerned with the workman-petitioner Naresh Kumar who falls in category (ii).
According to the statement of Shri VM Handa, Executive Engineer, this workman remained on daily wages from 191991 to 31101991, 1121991 to 3061992 and then from 191993 onwards.
Taking this total period of service rendered by the workman, it is clear that upto 30th June, 1992, he had rendered 8 months service and then from 191993 to 31121995, he had rendered two years and 5 months service.
He was, therefore, entitled to regularisation of service as on 3111996 on which date he was in service.
A doubt has, however, been expressed by Shri Handa by referring language of the provision in (ii) above to the effect "that if the workman had worked for minimum period of 210 days in each year and the break in service in any year is not more than one month at a time" and in this manner he had completed 3 years' service, he is to be considered for regularisation.
We feel that the above provision has no relevance in the present case.
The above provision takes into account the definition of continuous service in section 25-B(2) of the Industrial Disputes Act which by legal notion provides that in case a workman has been in service for 240 days in a calendar year, he shall be treated to have rendered one year service.
3.
In the case of the workman Naresh Kumar, however, we have mentioned above the actual service rendered by him, which is admitted by the department.
In our view he is entitled to regularisation of service from 111996, in view of the fact that he had completed three years of actual service by that date and he was in service on 3111996."
4.
When the matter came up on the adjourned date i.e. 1181999, a request in writing received from the Executive Engineer PWD, Public Health Division, Sohna, dated 1181999 was entertained wherein he stated that he could not obtain the instructions of the State Government and thus sought adjournment.
The matter was accordingly adjourned for today.
Ms Promila Nain, Advocate, has appeared today and she is assisted by Shri VM Handa, Executive Engineer who reiterated the position earlier taken by him but did not produce any instruction received from the Government.
We, therefore, proceed to decide this matter taking in view the equity and the circumstances in the interest of justice.
5.
In view of what we have already stated in our order dated 161999, we have no doubt that the purport of the Government order mentioned above is that a casual/daily wage employee should have rendered 3 years service by 31st January, 1996 and he should be in service on that date for his consideration for regular appointment.
No doubt, it was clarified that in case a daily wage worker had worked for a minimum period of 240 days in a year, and the break in service is not more than one month at a time, and has in this manner been in service for 3 calendar years as on 3111996, he shall be considered for regularisation of service.
In out view the clarification so made is to introduce the definition of "continuous service" given in Section 25-B(2) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.
In any case, in case a workman has put in total service of actual period of 1095 days i.e. 3 years preceding 3111996 and is in service on that day he would be entitled for consideration for regular appointment.
6.
In the present case, we find that the workman, according to the admission of the respondent, has put in service from 1101991 to 31101991, 1121991 to 3061992 and then from 191993 onwards.
In this manner, he had rendered service for more than 3 years.
Let us take the case of an employee who rendered service for 200 days every year for as many as ten years which would mean that the total period of service rendered by him was more than 9 years.
If the interpretation placed by Shri VM Handa, Executive Engineer on the Government order mentioned above, is accepted, such an employee would not be entitled to regularisation of service inspite of the fact that he was in service on 3111996.
This position certainly cannot be accepted.
7.
In this situation, we direct the respondents to consider the case of the petitioner for regularisation of his service in terms of the above order of the government and, on his being found suitable for regularisation of service, order to that effect should be passed within three months from today.
The writ petition is disposed of accordingly.
Petition allowed.
