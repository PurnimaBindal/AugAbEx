VK Bali, J - While invalidating the order of termination, Labour Court, even though reinstated the workman with continuity of service but denied him the back wages.
It is against this part of the order that the present petition has been filed.
2.
The Labour Court while dealing with the crucial issue, came to a firm finding of fact that order of termination dated March 28, 1985 was not a simple order of termination but was passed in the light of the letter, Ex. P2, which is stated to be a letter sent by the Employment Exchange informing the employer that the name of petitioner was wrongly sent.
The Labour Court further held that while terminating the services of the petitioner, the management did not pay any retrenchment compensation to him and thus violated the provisions of Section 25-F of the Industrial Disputes Act.
Even though the findings were as mentioned above, the back wages were denied to the petitioner on the ground that workman had succeeded in his cause only on technical ground and thus was not entitled to back wages.
This part of the order of Labour Court is in sharp contrast to the dictum laid down by this court in Hari Palace, Ambala City v. Presiding Officer, 1979 PLR 720 followed in Kanwar Rohit v. The Presiding Officer, 1992(2) RSJ, 425 and in number of other judgments.
3.
The writ is thus allowed and the petitioner is held entitled to all the back wages.
The same be paid to him within two months from today.
No order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
