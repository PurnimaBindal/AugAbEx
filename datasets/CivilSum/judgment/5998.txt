KS Garewal, J - This judgment shall dispose of three connected petitions filed by Shashi Kanta and others (CWP No. 1466 of 1989), Ved Parkash and others (CWP No. 14956 of 1990) and Jagan Nath and others (CWP 14739 of 1993).
The facts, as noted below, have been taken from CWP No. 1466 of 1989.
2.
Shashi Kanta and 40 others are Clerks working with various Government schools in Haryana under the Director of School Education/Department of Education.
According to the petitioners, there were two cadres of Clerks in the Education Department, one was the cadre of the Clerks who are working at the head office and the other consisting of Clerks working in the field.
Out of latter category as well there were two types of Clerks, one set of Clerks belonging to the State Government and the other to the provincialised cadre.
It was on October 1, 1957 that Clerks working in the District Boards had their services provincialised when the Boards were taken over by the Government with effect from that date and all such Clerks were absorbed in the Government service and formed a separate cadre called provincialised cadre.
3.
The petitioners had been directly recruited to Government service and theirs was the State cadre.
According to the petitioners, before February 1, 1969 their pay scales were the same with the pay scales of the provincialised cadre, Rs. 60-175/-.
After February 1, 1969 both cadres got the same pay scales of Rs. 110-225/- but after April 1, 1979, the provincialised cadre was placed in the pay scale of Rs. 525-1050/- while the State cadre had slightly lower pay scale of Rs. 400-600/- (selection grade Rs. 480-760/-).
4.
After January 1, 1986 the provincialised cadre enjoyed the pay scale of Rs. 1400-2600/- whereas the State cadre was only of Rs. 950-1500/-.
5-6.
The petitioners represented that all Clerks, whether belonging to the provincialised cadre or to the State cadre had similar duties, responsibilities, qualifications and their posts were also inter-changeable but their pay scales were different.
It was contrary to the principle of equal pay for equal work.
The petitioners filed CWP No. 7867 of 1988 and this Court directed vide order dated September 12, 1988 that the petitioners' representation be considered and decided by the respondents.
Thereafter, vide order dated December 23, 1988 the petitioners' representation was considered and rejected and they were driven to file the present writ petition.
7.
The respondents in their reply pleaded that the provincialised cadre was a diminishing cadre and no new posts had been created in the said cadre.
The Clerks working in the said cadre usually got promotion after about 20 years service but Clerks belonging to the provincialised cadre did not get promotion chances even after 25 to 28 years of service.
It was to provide the latter category promotion chances that the State Government, after careful consideration allowed the latter category of Clerks personal grade of Rs. 160-400/- w.e.f.
October 1, 1975 and on the basis of this they were given the pay scale of Rs. 525-1050/- w.e.f.
April 1, 1979 and Rs. 1400-2600/- w.e.f.
January 1, 1986.
8.
According to the respondents, the Clerks working in the provincialised cadre constituted a separate category which was a reasonable classification within the ambit of the Constitution of India.
On January 1, 1986 only about 15 clerks remained in the provincialised cadre and had been allowed the personal pay of Rs. 1400-2600/- w.e.f.
January 1, 1986.
9.
This Court in Krishan Kumar and 39 others v. State of Haryana and another, 2005(1) SCT 59 (P&H) : LPA No. 104 of 1998 while dealing with the question of parity of pay scales between the State cadre and the provincialised cadre considered the entire case law on the subject and came to the following conclusion :-  "We are in complete agreement with the learned Single Judge that the decision of the State Government to grant the pay scale of Rs. 700-1250 as a measure personal to the members of the provincialised Cadre, who had completed 22 years service does not suffer from any constitutional infirmity.
The principle of equal pay for equal work cannot be invoked for directing the State to grant similar pay scale to the members of the State Cadre because the two sets of employees belong to distinct cadres which are governed by separate service rules."
10.
The judgment of the Supreme Court relied upon by the learned counsel for the petitioners, reported as J.B.T.
Rajkiya Adhyapak Sangh and others v. State of Haryana and others, 1991(3) RSJ 111 was distinguished in the following terms :-  "It is not clear from the order of the Supreme Court whether the service of JBT Teachers belonging to the State Cadre were governed by one set of rules and those belonging to the provincialised Cadre were governed by another set of rules.
Therefore, it is not possible to read that order as laying down a proposition of law that the State cannot treat the members of two cadres differently in the matter of grant of pay scale."
11.
In Krishan Kumar's case the claim of the appellants, who were members of the State cadre seeking parity in pay scale vis-a-vis members of the provincialised cadre was not accepted and their appeal was dismissed.
In view of the above, I find no merit in this petition and the same is hereby dismissed.
No costs.
Appeal dismissed.
