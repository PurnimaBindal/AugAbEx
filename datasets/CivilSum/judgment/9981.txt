Ashok Bhan, J - Petitioner was an employee of the Haryana State Co-operative Development Federation Limited at Chandigarh which is a State within the meaning of Article 12 of the Constitution of India as it is financed and controlled by the State of Haryana.
The Managing Director is appointed by the State Government and all orders of appointments and promotions after a particular level have to be got approved from the Registrar, Co-operative Societies, Haryana.
Petitioner was confirmed as Joint Auditor w.e.f.
411977.
The petitioner was promoted from Joint Auditor to Auditor in March, 1978.
The promotion of the petitioner was also approved by the Registrar Co-operative Societies Haryana.
Letter of approval to this effect has been attached as Annexure P/3 with this writ petition.
2.
Petitioner submitted resignation from service on 951990 (copy Annexure P/4) and a request was made that his resignation be accepted on 9.8.1990.
Petitioner changed his mind and a request was made by him on 381990 (Annexure P/5) that he be permitted to withdraw his resignation; further prayer made was that he be allowed to join the duties forthwith.
Petitioner vide communication dated 10.8.1990 (Annexure P/6) was informed that the request of the petitioner to withdraw the resignation could not be acceded to as the resignation of the petitioner had already been accepted by the Board of Administrators on 2961990.
Petitioner in this writ petition has impugned Annexure P/6 being illegal, unjust and unconstitutional inter alia on the ground that the petitioner had tendered his resignation with a request that the same be accepted on 9.8.1990 and the respondent had no jurisdiction to accept the same before 9.8.1990 and the respondent could not unilaterally terminate the same before the said date; that the respondent could not accept the resignation of the petitioner before the date it was to be given effect to.
3.
In the written statement filed, the stand taken by the respondents is that the resignation of the petitioner had been accepted by the Board of Administrators in its meeting held on 2961990; the final decision to accept the resignation of the petitioner was taken on 1561990 with immediate effect subject to the approval of Board of Administrators and to pay the petitioner the salary for the remaining period of the notice i.e. 16.6.1990 to 9.8.1990 and the petitioner was relieved of his duties as Auditor of the respondent-Federation w.e.f.
1561990 and the petitioner was intimated accordingly vide letter No. Estt/E-90/1868-73 dated 1561990.
This decision of the federation taken on 1561990 was approved by the Board of Administrators in its meeting held on 1561990.
A copy of the letter dated 1561990 has been annexed as Annexure R/1 with the written statement; it was further pleaded that the petitioner acquiesced for the acceptance of the resignation w.e.f.
1561990 and got himself relieved, that in fact, subsequently petitioner wrote a letter dated 19.6.1990 addressed to the Managing Director of the respondent-Federation, requesting for the release of his dues and in that letter itself, the petitioner clearly stated that he had been relieved of his office on 1561990.
Copy of this letter has been attached as Annexure R/2; it was further pleaded that the respondent acted within its powers to accept the resignation before the receipt of information regarding the intention of the petitioner to withdraw the same; that the letter of withdrawal of resignation dated 381990 was of no consequence as the resignation of the petitioner stood already accepted by the respondent-Federation on 1561990, and further that the petitioner stood relieved of his duties with effect from 1561990; that the resignation of the petitioner could be accepted even prior to the date from which the resignation was to become effective and the petitioner could not withdraw the same after its having been accepted.
3.
I have heard the learned counsel for the parties at length.
4.
The point in issue is not res integra and in fact stands concluded by various judgments including that of the Supreme Court of India and of this Court.
In Dr. (Mrs.) Sumitra Devi v. State of Haryana, 1982(3) SLR 21, IS Tiwana, J as he then was, on somewhat similar circumstances posed the following two questions which were debated before him and arise for consideration in the present case as well :-  1.
Whether an employee who intimates to resign with effect from a future date can withdraw the same before the date is reached; and  2.
Whether the respondent-authorities had any jurisdiction to accept the resignation of the petitioner with effect from a date prior to the date about which the petitioner had expressed her intention to resign ?
Both these questions were answered in favour of the employee and while concluding in para 6, it was held as under :-  "The act of resigning being undisputably a unilateral act, the employee resigning can by his free volition fix the date from which he wishes to resign.
The acceptance of such a resignation by the competent authority to my mind does not mean anything more than the assent of such authority.
Thus I am of the view that the authority accepting the resignation cannot either change its date from which it is sought to be effective or in other words advance the date of its acceptance, unless the rules and conditions of service permit such a course.
In a given case it may, however, be open to the competent authority to issue a counter notice for a shorter duration in terms of the rules or conditions of appointment in case such authority wants to get rid of the employee at an earlier date."
5.
The impugned order in the said writ petition accepting the resignation was annulled.
The only distinction in the present case and in Dr. (Mrs.) Sumitra Devi's case (supra) is that in the present case, resignation had already been accepted before it was withdrawn whereas in that case, the resignation was accepted after the same had been withdrawn.
IS Tiwana, J (as he then was) while recording the finding on the two points debated before him did not base his finding only on the fact that the resignation was accepted after the same had been withdrawn by Dr. (Mrs.) Sumitra Devi the petitioner in that case.
6.
Otherwise also it makes no difference as to whether the resignation is accepted before its withdrawal or after its withdrawal where a future date is fixed by the employee for making the resignation effective and withdraws the resignation with effect from a date prior to the date about which the petitioner had expressed his intention to resign.
7.
The apex Court in Balram Gupta v. Union of India and another, AIR 1987 Supreme Court 2354, while dealing with somewhat similar case answered the point in favour of the employee.
In that case, the employee sent his letter of resignation on 24121980 mentioning therein that the resignation be made effective w.e.f.
3131981.
The resignation was accepted by the employer on 2011981.
The resignation was withdrawn by the employee on 3111981 i.e. after the acceptance.
The employee was not permitted to withdraw his resignation.
Employee challenged the action of his employer in not granting him permission to withdraw his letter of resignation.
Under these circumstances, the Supreme Court held as under :-  "The appellant having completed 20 years service wrote a letter dated 24121980 to the appointing authority seeking voluntary retirement on 3131981.
He wrote that the notice period of 3 months be treated from 111981.
By an order dated 2011981, he was allowed to retire voluntarily from service prospectively w.e.f.
from afternoon of 3131981.
In the meantime on 3111981 the appellant withdrew his notice on the ground that on account of persistent and personal requests from the staff members he had changed his mind.
The appellant was relieved by order dated 3131981 in which it was also mentioned that his withdrawal application was considered and was found not acceptable.
Held, that it could not be submitted that once notice was given it became operative immediately.
If it was received by the Government and automatically brought about the dissolution of contract after the expiry of the notice period.
The dissolution would be brought about only on the date indicated ie 31st March, 1981, upto that the appellant was and is a Government employee.
There is no unilateral termination of the same prior thereto.
He is at liberty and entitled independently without sub-rule (4) of Rule 48A of the Pension Rules, as a Government servant, to withdraw his notice of voluntary retirement.
In this respect it stands at par with letter of resignation."
8.
The point in issue thus stands covered by the judgment of this Court in Dr. (Mrs.) Sumitra Devi's case (supra) and of the apex court in Balram Gupta's case (supra).
9.
Another point raised by the learned counsel appearing for the respondents is that the resignation of the petitioner was accepted on 1561990 vide Annexure R/1 and the petitioner was relieved from his office in pursuance to the order Annexure R/1 with a stipulation that he would be paid salary for the remaining period of the notice, that is from 16.6.1990 to 9.8.1990, that is the date fixed by the petitioner for making the resignation effective.
However, salary for the period from 16.6.1990 to 9.8.1990, was ordered to be adjusted against an amount outstanding against the petitioner; that the petitioner had acquiesced to this order and got himself relieved from his duties and for that reason he is not entitled to be taken back in service.
As against this, the case of the petitioner is that he did not acquiesce to the order Annexure R/1 and in fact lodged protest by writing a letter Annexure R/2 although in Annexure R/2, the petitioner basically complained for non-payment of the salary and other benefits to which he was entitled to.
10.
In view of the law laid down by this Court and the Supreme Court of India in the judgments referred to in the earlier part of this judgment, the resignation of the petitioner could be made effective with effect from 9.8.1990 only because the petitioner had sought retirement with effect from the said date and the resignation could not be accepted before that date and made effective with the stipulation that the petitioner shall be paid salary for the remaining period of notice i.e. from 16.6.1990 to 9.8.1990.
In my view, it cannot be inferred that the petitioner had in any way acquiesced to the passing of the order Annexure R/1.
He raised a mild protest in his letter Annexure R/2 complaining that he had been relieved from the office on 1561990 even before the maturity of the resignation letter which was to be made effective from 9.8.1990.
Even if it is accepted for the sake of argument that the petitioner did not lodge any protest against his being relieved on 1561990, that is prior to 9.8.1990 that is the date from which his resignation was to be made effective as per his request, would not in any way amount to his consent for being relieved from the office prior to the date fixed by him for making his resignation effective.
It would further made no difference even if he was paid salary from 16.6.1990 to 9.8.1990 by way of adjustment towards the outstanding dues payable by him to the State government.
11.
For the reasons stated above, this writ petition is accepted, the impugned order Annexure P/6 is quashed and it is held that the petitioner was within his rights to withdraw the resignation any time prior to 9.8.1990.
Accordingly, the petitioner is ordered to be reinstated in service with all consequential benefits.
No costs.
Petition allowed.
