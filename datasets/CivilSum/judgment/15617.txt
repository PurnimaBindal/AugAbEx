Swatanter Kumar, J - Additional affidavit filed on behalf of respondent No. 2 in Court today, is taken on record.
I have heard the Learned Counsel for the parties.
2.
The limited question that falls for consideration in this case, is whether the respondents have jurisdiction to recover the excess amount paid on account of medical reimbursement with interest.
3.
There is no dispute to the fact that the petitioner had submitted the medical reimbursement bills as he had undergone the open heart surgery at Escorts, New Delhi.
These bills were duly proceed by the concerned department and amount was sanctioned and ultimately it was disbursed to the petitioner in accordance with rules, at All India Institute of Medical Sciences rates.
However, later it came to the notice of the respondents that some excess amount was paid to the petitioner.
There may be some jurisdiction for recovery of the excess amount for which the respondents are at liberty in accordance with law.
4.
The question of interest being recovered from the petitioner does not even squarely arises in the present case.
The contention of the Learned Counsel appearing for respondents, in this regard, deserves to be rejected at the very outset.
In this regard, a reference can be made to judgment of Hon'ble Supreme Court of India in the case of Om Parkash Gargi v. State of Punjab and others, JT 1996(10) SC 36.
The petitioner was paid the amount without any misrepresentation and the error, if any, was on the part of the respondents.
As such, there appears to be no justification on the part of the respondents to recover the excess amount with interest.
5.
Resultantly, this petition is allowed limited to the extent that the respondents shall not be entitled to recover interest on the excess payment.
The petition is accordingly disposed of.
Petition allowed.
