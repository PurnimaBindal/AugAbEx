GS Singhvi, J - The main object sought to be achieved by the policy of compassionate appointment/ex-gratia employment is to relieve the family of the deceased employee of the grave financial hardship caused to it due to the sudden loss of the bread winner.
It is intended to provide immediate succour to the family of the deceased employee.
Therefore, the policy of compassionate appointment cannot be treated as a source of employment and the dependent of the deceased employee cannot claim appointment as of right.
In support of this proposition, we may refer to the decisions of the Supreme Court in Umesh Kumar Nagpal v. State of Haryana, JT 1994(3) SC 525 : 1994(3) SCT 174 (SC); State of Haryana v. Naresh Kumar Bali, JT 1994(1) SC 184 : 1994(3) SCT 590 (SC); Haryana State Electricity Board v. Naresh Tanwar, JT 1996(2) SC 542 : 1996(2) SCT 778 (SC); Jagdish Prasad v. State of Bihar, 1996(1) SCC 301 : 1996(2) SCT 207 (SC); Haryana State Electricity Board v. Hakim Singh, JT 1997(8) SC 332 : 1998(1) SCT 511 (SC); and Hindustan Aeronautics Ltd v. A Radhika Thirumalai, JT 1996(9) SC 197 : 1997(1) SCT 329 (SC);.
Reference may also be made to the decision of this Court in Gurdarshan Singh v. State of Punjab, 1998(1) RSJ 557.
2.
Having noticed the basic principle which the Court has to keep in mind while marking adjudication of the claim for ex-gratia employment, we may advert to the facts of this petition which has been filed by brother of the deceased for directing the respondents to appoint him on compassionate ground.
Petitioner's brother, Shri Surinder Singh, died on 22111991 while he was working as Draftsman in Punjab Public Works Department.
The application submitted by the petitioner for ex-gratia employment was rejected by the Chief Engineer (GW), Public Health, Patiala on the ground that he does not fall within the ambit of the policy framed by the government.
3.
Shri BS Sidhu relied on the certificate Annexure P-3 issued by the Deputy Commissioner and the affidavit Annexure P-4 filed by the petitioner to show that he is dependent of late Shri Surinder Singh and is willing to serve the family of the deceased.
Learned counsel submitted that in view of these documents the petitioner is entitled to be appointed on compassionate ground as of right.
He further submitted that the instructions issued by the government vide circular letter No. 11/27/94-2PP1/2364 dated 521996 and letter No. 11/54/94-2PPI/16950 dated 1.10.96 cannot be relied upon because Smt. Hardeep Kaur, widow of Shri Surinder Singh has contracted second marriage with one Darshan Singh soon after the demise of her husband.
Learned counsel made strenuous efforts to persuade us to interpret the instructions issued by the government so as to bring the petitioner's case within the zone of eligibility by arguing that after the remarriage of Smt. Hardeep Kaur, he is entitled to be treated as dependent of Shri Surinder Singh.
4.
We have thoughtfully considered the submission made by Shri Sidhu but do not find any merit in the petitioner's claim to be appointed on compassionate ground.
A conjoint reading of the circular letters dated 521996 and 1101996 shows that brother of the deceased employee can be treated as the latter's dependent only if the government servant was un-married at the time of death.
However, the petitioner cannot derive any benefit from these instructions because late Shri Surinder Singh was married and his wife was alive at the time of his death.
Therefore, in terms of the policy framed by the government, the petitioner cannot be considered for appointment as a dependent of late Shri Surinder Singh.
We are also of the view that re- marriage by the widow of the deceased cannot entitle the petitioner to seek employment under the existing policy of ex-gratia employment.
Any such direction by the court would amount to re-writing of the instructions issued by the government and we do not find any cogent reason for doing so.
For the reasons mentioned above, the writ petition is dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
