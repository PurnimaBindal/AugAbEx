ML Singhal, J - Plaintiff-Ram Chander Gupta was appointed as Master in Punjab Education Department on 281961.
On re-organisation of the State of Punjab, he was allocated to the State of Haryana.
He worked in different capacities from time to time in the State of Haryana.
While working as Master in the grade of Rs. 585-1050, he was promoted as Lecturer (Economics) by the Director of Schools, Haryana, Chandigarh vide memo No. 28/28-83-EI-EIV (5) dated 26121985 w.e.f.
7.10.1981.
He was to be posted in a Secondary School in the department of Economics on the basis of seniority-cum-merit.
His pay was fixed in the grade of Rs. 700-1250, which was further revised to Rs. 1640-60-2600-EB-75-2900 w.e.f.
1186.
He was again promoted as Head Master of High School in the grade of Rs. 2000-3500 vide DSE Haryana Order No. 4/1/86-EI (47) dated 30986.
He resumed his duties as Head Master in Government High School, Binjalpur on 6101986.
His pay was fixed at Rs. 2450/- w.e.f.
6101986 on the post of Head Master at Rs. 2525/- w.e.f.
141987.
Fixation of pay on the post of Head Master at Rs. 2450/- w.e.f.
6101986 and 2525/- w.e.f.
141987 was verified by D.E.O.
Ambala as per the provisions of Rule 44(c) of Civil Services Rules.
Fixation of pay as aforesaid, was accepted by the plaintiff.
As per the plaintiff, if an employee, in the time scale of pay, is promoted to officiate on a higher post, his pay on that post, to which he joins on promotion is fixed and determined at the stage next above his pay drawn by him in the lower post.
Plaintiff while working as Lecturer in school cadre was holding the post of less responsibility to that of Head Master and as such he was entitled to pay fixation under Rule 44(c) of the Civil Service Rules, when he was promoted to the post of Head Master.
Pay slip was also issued by the District Education Officer, Ambala vide Audit No. 87 dated 27787.
He continued to draw pay in the pay scale of Rs. 2450/- w.e.f.
6101986.
Plaintiff was himself Drawing and Disbursing Authority.
He was required to draw salary on the basis of authority/pay slip issued by District Education Officer, Ambala, every year.
On 141987, he was drawing the basis pay of Rs. 2525/-.
He was due for next increment on 141988.
Pay slip for Rs. 2600/- was to be issued in the grade of Rs. 2000-3500 on 141988.
District Education Officer, Ambala, however, in violation of the provisions of law, provided for increment and pay fixation, issued him pay slip at Rs. 2525/- w.e.f.
141988 on 3051988.
Plaintiff represented against the issue of pay slip at Rs. 2525/- w.e.f.
141988 to the District Education Officer.
His representation was declined.
W.e.f.
141991, pay slip at Rs. 2825/- was to be issued but vide letter dated 2551991, D.E.O.
Ambala issued pay slip for 2750/-.
This order passed by District Education Officer was against the law of the land and the principles of natural justice.
On these allegations the plaintiff filed suit for declaration against the State of Haryana to the effect that he was entitled to pay fixation at Rs. 2450/- (basic pay) w.e.f.
6101986 on his promotion from the post of Lecturer, School cadre, in H.E.S.
III to the post of Head Master, Government High School, Binjalpur and at Rs. 2525/- w.e.f.
141987 and so on, as in the State of Haryana, the post of Head Master of Government High School is of higher responsibility than the post of Lecturer of school cadre in HES- III declared vide DPI Memo No. 1572/G.E.I.
(I) KW-22/11-68 dated 23121968 etc.  2.
Defendant-State of Haryana contested the suit of the plaintiff.
It was submitted that the post of Lecturer is an ex-cadre post and the pay of Lecturer on promotion as Head Master is to be fixed according to the provisions of Note 10 of Rule 44(c) of Civil Services Rules.
His pay was fixed as such.
The pay slip at Rs. 2525/- was issued w.e.f.
141988 due to the re-fixation of pay as per the directions of Director of Education, Haryana, Chandigarh letter 3/62-81 Estt (2) dated 621984.
It was denied that the action of D.E.O.
Ambala in this regard was discriminatory in any way.
It was denied that the pay slip at Rs. 2750/- was not issued in accordance with the rules.
On the pleadings of the parties, the following issues were framed :-  1.
Whether the plaintiff is entitled to his pay to be fixed as Rs. 2450/- (basic) on 6101986 on his promotion from the post of Lecturer to the post of Head Master in view of the facts mentioned in the plaint ?
OPP 2.
Whether the pay fixation proforma issued by Sectional Officer of District Education Officer under Letter No. Audit/88/124 dated 3051988 and audit/81/Nil dated 2551991 is illegal, inoperative, arbitrary, null and void and not binding upon the plaintiff, if so to what effect ?
OPP 3.
Whether the plaintiff is entitled for mandatory injunction, as alleged in the plaint ?
OPP 4.
Relief.
3.
Learned Additional Civil Judge (Senior Division), Ambala vide order dated 3181996, dismissed the suit of the plaintiff in view of his finding that plaintiff was working on an ex-cadre post as Lecturer and on promotion as Head Master, his pay was to be fixed according to Note 10 of Rule 44 of Civil Services Rules, which is that in case of a person, who proceeds from lower cadre post to a higher ex-cadre post, the benefit of pay drawn in the ex-cadre post will not be allowed for the purpose of fixation of pay on promotion/reversion to a higher cadre post.
On reversion from the ex-cadre post the pay of the official will be fixed under rule 4.4(c) with reference to the pay admissible in the lower cadre post.
As the pay of the plaintiff was fixed as per Note 10 of Rule 44 of Civil Services Rules, the pay slip at Rs. 2525/- issued by District Education Officer, Ambala w.e.f.
141988 was rightly issued as per circular (Ex. D/1).
4.
Having felt aggrieved, plaintiff went in appeal.
The appeal was dismissed by the Additional District Judge, Ambala vide order dated 1781998.
Still not satisfied, the plaintiff has come up in Regular Second Appeal to this court.
5.
It was submitted by the learned counsel for the appellant that there is no evidence that the post of Lecturer was an ex-cadre post and when he was promoted as Lecturer, it was no where written that he was promoted against an ex-cadre post.
It was submitted that at the time of his promotion as Head Master, it was not recorded that he was being promoted from the post of Lecturer, which was an ex-cadre post.
Note given under Rule 44 of the Civil Services Rules reads as under :-  (i) Notwithstanding anything contained in these rules, where a Government employee holding a post in a temporary or officiating capacity is promoted or appointed in a substantive, temporary or officiating capacity to another post carrying duties and responsibilities of greater importance that those attaching to the post held by him, his initial pay in the time scale of the higher post shall be fixed at the stage next above his pay drawn by him in the lower post provided it is certified by the Head of Department or Head of the Office where he is appointing authority in respect of class-III and IV Government employees in which the Government employee was holding the lower post that he would have continued to officiate in the lower post but for his promotion/appointment to the higher post.
(ii) The provisions of sub rule (2) of rule 414 shall also be applicable in any case where the initial pay is fixed under this clause.
In cases, where a Government employee is, immediately before his promotion or appointment to a higher post, drawing pay at the maximum of the time scale of the lower post, his initial pay in the time scale of the higher post shall be fixed in the same manner as provided in sub-clause (1) above.
6.
Rule 44 (c) of Punjab Civil Services Rules, reads as under :-  "The expression "if he holds a lien on a permanent post" occurring in this ciause should be held to include the lien on a permanent post to which a Government employee is appointed in a provisional substantive capacity under rule 314 (d) and the expression "Substantive pay in respect of the old post" occurring in it should be held to include his substantive pay in respect of that provisional substantive appointment.
This clause should, therefore, be held to permit the substantive, pay in respect of a provisional substantive appointment being taken into account in determining his initial play in another post to which he is appointed.
When the initial pay of a Government employee in a post is, thus fixed, it will not be affected even if during the tenure of his appointment to that post he reverts from his provisional appointment."
7.
To begin with, w.e.f.
7.10.1981 he was promoted as Lecturer.
As Lecturer, his pay was fixed in the grade of Rs. 700-1250 as per rule 4.4(c) and 414 of Civil Services Rules.
He was promoted as Head Master from the post of Lecturer.
As Lecturer, he was drawing Rs. 2420/- and he was fixed at Rs. 2450/- in the scale of Rs. 2000-3500 according to Note 10 of Rule 44 of Civil Services Rules, Vol.
I Part-I.

The post of Head Master was a post of higher responsibility than the post of Lecturer.
As per letter Memo No. 1572-G.ET-(1)-KW-22/11-68 dated 23121968 from the DPI, Haryana, Chandigarh addressed to all the District Education Officers in the State, the duties and responsibilities attached with the post of Head Master of Govt. High School in the scale of Rs. 300-600 are of greater importance that those attached with the post of Lecturer of the Higher Secondary School in terms of note 8 of Rule 44 of Civil Services Rules, Vol.
I, Part I.
The plaintiff was a Social Studies Master in Government High School, Barara, Ambala, when he was promoted and adjusted as Lecturer in Economics (school cadre) Men's branch in the grade of Rs. 700/1250 plus usual allowances as sanctioned by the Haryana Government.
11.
From the post of Lecturer (school cadre), he was promoted to the post of Head Master, Government High School.
It would bear repetition that the post of Head Master of Government High School is of greater responsibilities vis-a-vis the post of Lecturer of the Higher Secondary School.
Faced with this position learned counsel for the State of Haryana submitted that Head Master's is the promotional post from the post of Master and not from the post of Lecturer.
Plaintiff was promoted as Lecturer from the post of Master because of his MA qualifications.
If that was so, he could not have been made to suffer because of his promotion to the post of Head Master.
He would have drawn higher pay if he had been promoted from the post of Master to the post of Head Master.
He was basically a Master but was put as Lecturer with a view to utilize his MA qualification.
There is no evidence whether he was Lecturer substantively or he was merely holding the post of Lecturer with his lien on the post of Master.
It was submitted by the learned counsel for the State of Haryana that the plaintiff while working as Lecturer was considered as Master for the purpose of the service and he cannot take benefit both ways i.e. for getting promotion as Head Master from the Masters cadre to be treated as from cadre but for the fixation of pay to be treated as pay scale of a Lecturer on promotion to the post of Head Master.
12.
In this case, the plaintiff was entitled was entitled to the pay fixation because of promotion to the post of Head Master from the post of Lecturer (School cadre) on the principle that the post of Head Master was a post of higher responsibility vis-a-vis the post of Lecturer (School cadre).
In this case, the matter was sought to be raked up by the State after the plaintiff had drawn pay for quite some time.
13.
For the reasons given above, this appeal succeeds and is accepted and the judgment and decree of both the courts below are set-aside the plaintiff- appellant's suit is decreed for declaration to the effect that he was entitled to be fixed at Rs. 2450/- (basic pay) on 6101986 on his promotion from the post of Lecturer (school cadre) HES III to the post of Head Master of Government High School, Binjalpur and further at Rs. 2525/- w.e.f.
141987 in view of the fact that the post of Head Master of Govt. High School, in Haryana is of higher responsibility than the post of Lecturer School cadre in the Haryana Education Department and for mandatory injunction directing the respondent-State of Haryana to restrain itself from reducing his basic pay already fixed i.e. Rs. 2450/- on 6101986 and 2525/- w.e.f.
141987 and so on and that the pay slips issued by the Section Officer, office of District Education Officer Ambala under letter No. Audit/88/124 dated 3051988 and audit/91/Nil dated 2551991, are illegal, inoperative, arbitrary, null and void.
No costs.
Appeal allowed.
