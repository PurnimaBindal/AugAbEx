Motilal B Naik, J - The order impugned in these two writ petitions is one and the same and, therefore, both the writ petitions are disposed of by a common order.
2.
The main grievance of the petitioners is that they were retired from service of the Grama Swarajya Sangham, Gooty, Anantapur district (for short 'the Sangham') after completing 30 years of service, which is contrary to rules.
3.
It is the case of the petitioners that they were served with orders in Ref.
No. GSS/GTY/Retirement/8788 ES, dated 2041987, of the 2nd respondent indicating that the petitioner in W.P. No. 1755/89 would be completing 30 years of service by 28101987 and the petitioner in W.P. No. 1756/89 would be completing 30 years of service by 23101987.
Petitioners contend that this is contrary to rules governing the service conditions of the employees of the Sangham and, therefore, orders of the 2nd respondent dated 2041987 are liable to be quashed.
Further it is stated that pursuant to above orders, relieving orders were issued and retirement was effected.
It is also stated that some other employees, who were retired along with the petitioners, pursuant to their completing 30 years of service, had approached this Court and filed Writ Petitions 4203/88, 6567/88, 6589/88, 6620/88, 6624/88, 6625/88, 8352/88, 10294/88 and 10840/88.
Apart from these writ petitions, two more employees, by name V Pullaiah and N Krishna Murthy had also filed writ petitions in this Court challenging orders dated 2041987.
This Court, while admitting all those writ petitions, granted interim directions to continue the petitioners therein and by virtue of such directions, those employees were being continued.
4.
It is contended by the petitioners that Rule 21(2) of the Rules governing the service conditions of the employees of the Sangham deals with condition of retirement of employees of the Sangham.
The said rule provides that every employee shall retire on completion of 58 years of age.
However, if an employee who has put in 30 years of service, can also retire by giving three months' notice to the management of the Sangham.
Thus, according to this rule, an employee has to retire only after the completion of 58 years of age.
In case if an employee is inclined to retire even prior to the completion of 58 years of age he can opt for retirement after completion of 30 years of service by giving three months' notice.
It is, however, stated by the petitioners that on 16.1.1986, a resolution was unanimously passed by the 21st General Body meeting of the Sangham amending the existing rule to the effect that an employee should retire on attaining the age of 58 years or after completion of 30 years of service, whichever is earlier.
It is stated that pursuant to the above resolution, impugned orders were issued retiring the petitioners and other employees, who have completed 30 years of service.
It is further contended that in the General Body meeting held on 16.1.1986, not only the petitioners were present, but other 12 employees who had filed the aforementioned writ petitions were also present.
Those 12 employees, as indicated in the foregoing paragraph, filed several writ petitions questioning the retirement order dated 2041987 and obtained interim directions, pursuant to which they continued in service.
5.
It is to be seen that in the 25th General Body meeting held on 3121988 at the office of the Grama Swarajya Sangham, Gooty, Anantapur district, resolution No. 164 was moved regarding the retirement age of the employees without reference to completion of 30 years of service.
The General Body unanimously resolved to restore the retirement age as existed prior to the amendment brought in on 16.1.1986, that is to say, Rule 21(2), as it stood prior to the amendment, would govern the age of retirement of the employees of the Sangham.
Thus, according to this amendment, an employee has to retire after the completion of 58 years of age.
However, an option is given to the employee to retire on completion of 30 years of service by giving three months notice to the management.
6.
It is the case of the petitioners that pursuant to the amendment brought in by resolution No. 164, dated 3121988, the petitioners made several representations to the respondents to reinstate them into service, as they were hardly 49 years old on the date of their retirement pursuant to retirement orders dated 2041987.
It was pleaded by the petitioners that there was no justification in not extending the benefit of retirement on completion of 58 years of age inasmuch as the said benefit was extended to the employees, who are similarly placed and who were retired from service along with the petitioners.
7.
On behalf of the respondents, a detailed counter has been filed inter alia contending that the petitioners are not entitled for reinstatement pursuant to resolution No. 164, dated 3121988.
It is further contended that by the time the resolution came into force, the petitioners were retired from service and necessary retiral benefits were also paid to them and, therefore, they are not entitled for reinstatement.
8.
Sri K Somakonda Reddy, learned counsel for the respondents, has strongly opposed the writ petitions on the question of maintainability.
He has stated that when the writ petitions are not maintainable, the question of granting the relief does not arise.
His main contention is that the 2nd respondent is not 'the State' within the meaning of Article 12 of the Constitution of India and, therefore, writ petitions do not lie to the High Court.
He has taken me through various decisions on this aspect viz., Ramana v. Authority of India, AIR 1979 SC 1628; Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Majib, 1980(3) SLR 467 (SC); Tekraj v Union of India, 1988(3) SLR 560 (SC), Administrative Staff College v. DP Seshachalam, 1989(1) A.P.L.J.
371, Sri Konaseema Co-op.
Central Bank Ltd v. N Seetharama Raju, AIR 1990 AP 171; M Chilli Babu v. Bharatiya Vidya Bhavan, 1991(3) A.L.T.
385; Chander Mohan Khanna v. NCERT, 1992(1) SCT 100 : 1991(6) SLR 69 (SC); and PB Ghayalod v. Maruti Udyog Ltd, 1991(1) SCT 382 : 1991(6) SLR 72 (Delhi).
Sri Somakonda Reddy, therefore, prayed for dismissal for the writ petitions.
There is no dispute as far as the principle evolved in the decisions cited by Sri Somakonda Reddy.
But, I am afraid, those decisions have no relevance to the facts of the present case, for some other employees of the Sangham who were also retired pursuant to orders of the 2nd respondent dated 2041987, filed several writ petitions and obtained interim directions from this Court and their services were continued by the Sangham by resolution No. 164, dated 3121988.
For the purpose of clarity, resolution No. 164, dated 3121988, is extracted below :  "3.
Omitted being in regional language."
A reading of above resolution would indicate that continuance of service of employees who approached this Court and obtained interim orders, was ratified.
When continuance of other employees who were retired pursuant to orders dated 2041987, was ratified by the resolution, it is not open to the respondents to say that the petitioners cannot maintain writ petitions in the High Court.
On this count alone, I reject the contention of the respondents that the writ petitions are not maintainable.
9.
Since I have taken the view that when the respondents extended the benefit of continuing the other employees in service pursuant to interim directions of this Court in the writ petitions filed by those employees, there is no justification in denying the same benefit to the petitioners, I am not inclined to refer to the decisions cited by Shri Somakonda Reddy, as I consider that they are not relevant in the facts and circumstances of the case.
10.
The other contention raised by Sri Somakonda Reddy, learned counsel for the respondents, is that the petitioners were party to the resolution dated 16.1.1986 whereby amendment was brought in service conditions of the employees and, therefore, it is not open to the petitioners to question the impugned orders.
I am afraid, this cannot be the proper view of the issues involved in the writ petitions.
It is interesting to note that the other employees, who filed several writ petitions, were also party to the resolution dated 16.1.1986 and when those employees were retired pursuant to orders dated 2041987 of the 2nd respondent and they were later on continued in service pursuant to orders of this Court, the same benefit cannot be denied to the petitioners.
It is also interesting to note that those employees who were party to the resolution dated 16.1.1986 also figured in the resolution dated 3121988 whereby the original retirement condition was restored.
Yet, those employees were being continued in service while the petitioners are not extended the same benefit.
This, in my view, cannot be permitted, as apparently it looks that the interest of those employees is safeguarded while the petitioners' interest is left at lurch.
It is not the case of the respondents that the petitioners attained 58 years of age nor is it their case that there are charges against the petitioners.
The only ground is that the petitioners were retired pursuant to resolution dated 16.1.1986 and their cases cannot be entertained.
If that is the criterion, the respondents could have rejected the claims of other employees also.
It is because the other employees filed writ petitions and could obtain interim directions from this Court, the said directions were implemented by the respondents by resolution dated 3121988 while giving different treatment to the petitioners by denying to extend the said benefit to the petitioners, who could not get interim directions from this Court.
11.
Under these circumstances, I consider it appropriate to direct the respondents to reinstate the petitioners into service.
The respondents are accordingly directed to reinstate the petitioners into service within fifteen days from the date of receipt of this order and continue them till they attain the age of 58 years.
However, it is made clear that the petitioners would be entitled to pay and allowances only from the date of their joining duty on reinstatement.
They are not entitled to any wages for the interregnum period.
It is also made clear that the petitioners are entitled to the difference in amount of retiral benefits, if any, after retirement on attaining the age of superannuation.
12.
The writ petitions are accordingly allowed.
No order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
