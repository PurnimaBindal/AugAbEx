MM Aggarwal, J - Petitioner was working as Patwari in the revenue department of the Haryana State.
According to him, he had to undergo renal transplant in Ram Saran Dass Kishori Lal Charitable Hospital, Amritsar.
He was admitted on 2932002.
Renal transplant was conducted on 852002 and he was discharged on 1752002.
He incurred expenditure of Rs. 1,58,813/-.
Thereafter he had been getting post operative treatment as outdoor patient from AIIMS.
He had submitted bills of Rs. 1,40,156/- for such treatment for the period from 2482002 to 16.1.2003 but these bills were not paid by the respondents.
This is civil writ petition for issuance of directions to the respondents to reimburse the medical expenditure incurred by the petitioner.
2.
According to the State, this Ram Saran Dass Kishori Lal Cheritable Hospital, Amritsar was a private hospital, which was not recognized by the State of Haryana and no reimbursement could be made for the treatment taken in that hospital.
Further as far as the bills of Rs. 1,40,156/- are concerned, the claim filed was time barred and even at the time the alleged expenditure was incurred on medicines etc. the disease of the petitioner had not been declared as chronic and as such the amount spent on outdoor treatment was not admissible.
State had filed details showing that reimbursement of Rs. 2,10,656/- was made and even the petitioner had submitted seven bills at least from 16122003 to 1452004, which were paid whereas bills for reimbursement of the medicines as prescribed by AIIMS were submitted on 1862004 although this was for treatment taken for the period from 2482002 to 16.1.2003.
It had been pleaded on behalf of the State that the bills should have been submitted within a period of six months as per instructions.
3.
Counsel for the petitioner had relied on judgment of this Court reported in Prem Singh Deswal v. State of Haryana and others, 2004(4) SCT 660 : 2005(1) SLR 495 in which it had been held that where treatment for kidney transplant had been taken from a specialist hospital then the State should reimburse the amount spent by the petitioner.
Another Division Bench judgment of this Court reported in Surjit Singh v. Managing Director, PRTC and others, 2005(2) SCT 136 : 2005(4) SLR 237 had been relied to the effect that post operational follow up treatment in continuation to the indoor treatment was also liable to be reimbursed and there was no justification to deny such reimbursement.
Counsel for the petitioner relied on another judgment of Rajasthan High Court reported in Kailash Prasad Gupta v. State of Rajasthan and others, 2005(3) SCT 232 : 2005(4) SLR 573 that the Government must make relaxation and the medical reimbursement should not be denied on hypertechnical grounds.
4.
Counsel for the respondent relied on a judgment of this Court reported in Ramesh Chander v. State of Haryana, 2000(2) SCT 536 that when a person gets treatment from an unapproved hospital then he is to show that the approved hospital was too far or did not fulfil the requirement of the patient and unless he was able to show that, he is not entitled to reimbursement of treatment taken from unapproved hospital.
Counsel for the respondent relied on another judgment of this Court reported in Inder Pal Khod v. State of Haryana, 2000(2) SCT 981 wherein bills were submitted late and then it was held that the respondent-State was justified in rejecting the claim, when the bills were not submitted within stipulated time.
5.
In the year 2002, the specialist doctor of Ram Saran Dass Kishori Lal Charitable Hospital, Amritsar was known to be an expert for kidney transplant.
If this hospital was not recognized by the respondent-State, then the petitioner could be given reimbursement at the rate applicable for such treatment in the Government Hospitals at the relevant time.
The bills in this case for this renal transplant had been submitted in time.
It will be appropriate for the State to make payment of the bills for the medical expenses incurred by the petitioner in Ram Saran Dass Kishori Lal Charitable Hospital, Amritsar at the government approved rates i.e. PGI or AIIMS, at the relevant time.
6.
As far as bills for outdoor treatment of AIIMS and amount spent on medicines while taking treatment there are concerned, the disease of the petitioner was declared chronic disease only vide letter dated 20.3.2003 copy Annexure P-4.
The bills pertained to the period from 2482002 to 16.1.2003 and these were submitted on 1862004.
Disease of the petitioner had not been declared as chronic during the time from 2482002 to 16.1.2003.
Even when petitioner had submitted seven bills from 16122003 to 1452004, still he did not submit this bill of Rs. 1,40,156/- till 1862004.
Under these circumstances, respondents were justified in rejecting the claim of the petitioner as far as these bills were concerned.
From the above discussion, this writ petition is partly allowed and respondents are directed to make payment of the bills of renal transplant and treatment taken in Ram Saran Dass Kishori Lal Charitable Hospital, Amritsar at the approved rates of PGI/AIIMS/Govt.
Hospital prevalent at that time.
Needful be done within a period of three months from today.
Petition partly allowed.
