VS Aggarwal, J - By this common judgment both the civil writ petitions bearing No. 2933 and 2934 of 1997 can conveniently be disposed of together as the questions in controversy in both the writ petitions are indentical.
For the sake of convenience, facts from CWP No. 2933 of 1997 are being mentioned.
2.
Haryana State Co-operative Supply and Marketing Federation Limited, Chandigarh, seeks quashing of the award passed by the Presiding Officer, Labour Court, Ambala, dated 3101996.
By virtue of the impugned award it was held that the workman is entitled to reinstatement and continuity of service with full back wages.
3.
The relevant facts are that an industrial dispute was raised by the workman Ishwar Chand.
The conciliation proceedings had failed and this led to the reference that was made to the Presiding Officer, Labour Court.
The workman had been appointed as a Chowkidar on 111991.
He claimed that his services were terminated on 19.9.1992, in an illegal manner without following the provisions of the Industrial Disputes Act.
He was not paid any retrenchment compensaiton and the principle of last come first go was not followed.
In reply the petitioner management had asserted that the workman only had continued working with the management till 3131992.
He was only appointed for a specific work and for a specific period.
The learned Labour Court vide the impugned award held that the retrenchment had not been effected in accordance with Section 25-F of the Industrial Disputes Act.
The workman was shown to have abandoned the job on extraneous considerations on 3131992 while, in fact, he had served the department even in June, 1992.
It was held that the provisions of Section 25-F of the Industrial Disputes Act had not been complied with.
4.
The petitioner assailed the said award on the ground alleging that during the period of 12 months prior to 3131992 the workman worked only for 142 days and not for 240 days preceding the date of termination.
Otherwise also, it was asserted that the award is based on incorrect appreciation of facts.
5.
A perusal of the impugned award reveals that it had specifically been recorded that though, as per petitioner, respondent workman abandoned his job on 3131992 but he was shown to be present on 26.6.1992.
On the basis of the said fact, it was rightly concluded that the workman had not abandoned his job on 3131992.
In the face of these findings which are not shown to be erroneous and based on no material.
It is patent that the plea of the petitioner that the workman had not worked after 3131992 is incorrect and also the assertion that he has not worked for 240 days during the period of 12 months preceding the date of alleged termination is without basis.
6.
There is another way of looking at the matter.
When the findings of fact have been arrived at by the Labour Court and the same are based on appreciation of evidence then this Court in exercise of its extraordinary jurisdiction will not minutely re-apraise the same.
It is not shown, as already noted above, that the said findings are not based on any evidence.
In that view of the matter, the petitioner was bound to observe the strict provisions of section 25-F of the Industrial Disputes Act.
7.
During the course of arguments, both the respondents workmen were present.
They were ready to forego the back wages but only prayed that their continuity of service be not disturbed.
8.
Keeping in view the said concession and the reasons given above, both the writ petitions are disposed of with the direction that the workmen will not be entitled to back wages but they be reinstated immediately i.e. w.e.f.
931998.
They would be entitled to continuity of service.
Ordered accordingly.
