Jawahar Lal Gupta, J. (Oral) - The petitioner had joined service with the Department of Election in the State of Punjab on June 26, 1995.
In the year 1986 the petitioner was promoted as a Senior Assistant.
On June 28, 1996 the petitioner was promoted as Superintendent Grade-II.
Thereafter he was overlooked for promotion to the post of Superintendent Grade-I Vide order dated January 19, 1999, a copy of which has been produced as Annexure P-7 with the writ petition, the 2nd respondent was promoted as Superintendent Grade-I The petitioner complains that his claim was not considered.
The promotion of respondent No. 2 was, thus, illegal.
He prays that the order at Annexure P-7 be quashed.
A writ of Mandamus be issued directing respondent No. 1 to promote him as Superintendent Grade-I with effect from the date the 2nd respondent was promoted.
2.
The 1st respondent contests the petitioner's claim.
It has been inter- alia pointed out that the petitioner had earned `Average' reports from the years 1990-91 to 1995-96.
In fact, in the report for the year 1991-92 he was graded as `Below Average'.
He had earned two `Good' reports which were for the years 1996-97 and 1997-98.
As against this, the 2nd respondent had, during the years 1990-91 to 1997-98, earned four `Good' reports and four `Very Good' reports.
It has been further averred that the petitioner's claim was considered in the light of the instructions dated July 21, 1997.
On the basis of the reports for the five years the 2nd respondent was found to be better.
Thus, he was promoted.
3.
The petitioner has filed a replication.
It has been inter-alia pointed out that vide order dated March 18, 1993 the petitioner had been granted the proficiency step-up and his pay was raised from Rs. 2050/- to Rs. 2100/-.
He was also allowed annual increment w.e.f.
January 1, 1993 raising his pay from Rs. 2100 to Rs. 2150/-.
4.
Learned counsel for the parties have been heard.
5.
Mr. Maanipur, learned counsel for the petitioner, contends that the respondent Authority having allowed the increment on account of proficiency step-up in the year 1993, the adverse report recorded in the year 1991-92 is of no relevance.
He further contends that promotion had to be made on the basis of seniority-cum-merit.
The 2nd respondent could not have been preferred to the petitioner.
Thus, he submits that the impugned order deserves to be quashed.
The claim as made on behalf of the petitioner has been controverted by the counsel for the respondent-Authority.
6.
The 1st respondent has no doubt given the details of the reports of the petitioner as well as the 2nd respondent from the year 1987-88 onwards.
However, in paragraph 11 of the written statement it has been specifically averred that the claim for promotion to the post of Superintendent Grade-I was considered on the basis of the Confidential Reports for the preceding five years.
During the period from 1993-94 to 19978-98 the petitioner was graded as `Average' for 3 years and `Good' during 2 years.
As against this, the 2nd respondent was graded as `Good' in the reports for the years 1993-94 and 1994-95.
For the remaining three years viz. 1995-96 to 1997-98, he was graded as `Very Good'.
Thus, his performance was better.
Consequently, he was preferred.
This factual position clearly shows that the petitioner's case was not influenced by the adverse remarks conveyed to him for the year 1991-92.
In fact, the consideration was based on the record starting from the year 1993-94 onwards.
Secondly, it is apparent that the overall record of the 2nd respondent relating to the five years was much better than that of the petitioner.
In this situation, it cannot be said that the State had erred in preferring the 2nd respondent to the petitioner.
Seniority-cum-merit does not mean that a senior person has a right to be promoted irrespective of the lack of merit.
It only implies that seniority is the primary consideration.
However, even merit has to be kept in view.
In this case the respondent- Authority has shown a predilection for merit.
It cannot be said to have acted arbitrarily.
It has only taken a possible view.
It calls for no interference under Article 226 of the Constitution.
No order point has been raised.
In view of the above, we find no merit in this petition.
It is, consequently, dismissed.
In the circumstances of the case, the parties are left to bear their own costs.
Petition dismissed.
