RS Mongia, J (Oral) - The petitioner, who was working as a Lecturer in English in Government College (Evening Shift), Bahadurgarh, District Rohtak, filed the present writ petition impugning the notice dated 28th September, 1983 (Annexure P.1) issued by the Commissioner and Secretary to Government, Haryana, Education Department, prematurely retiring the petitioner after the expiry of three months of the date of issue of the notice.
2.
It is not necessary to go into the detailed facts of the case, as the case is covered in favour of the petitioner by a Division Bench judgment of this Court reported in KK Vaid v. State of Haryana, 1990(1) S.L.R.

The petitioner was prematurely releired on the basis of Haryana Government instructions dated 13th August, 1983, wherein it was laid down that the extension beyond the age of 55 years may be granted to the officials/officers, with the condition that more than 70% of the last 10 Confidential Reports are 'Good' or above.
Thew instructions were struck down by the Division Bench in KK Vaid's case (supra).
That being so, the basis for prematurely retiring the petitioner does not exist any more.
The Division Bench had also observed in para 10 of the judgment, as follows :  "The impugned order Annexure P5 also appears to suffer from arbitrariness.
It is beyond comprehension as to how and why the State Government takes an average entry in the service record of its employees as something adverse to them.
The word 'average' means nothing more than medium or ordinary.
There may well arise three situations while examining the service record of an employee for purpose of his premature retirement.
He may be positively good or positively bad and may neither be good nor bad.
It is only the last category which can be rated or evaluated as average.
Though it is interesting to note in the light of these instructions that the Haryana Government expects all of its employees not only to be above average, but something more also, i.e. good or above, yet it appears difficult to hold that an average entry has to be taken as an adverse entry.
It is only in the case of employees who are positively bad that the Government may be justified in retiring them at an early age in terms of clause (d) of rule 326 referred to above."
5.
From the Annual Confidential Reports, which have been reproduced above for the last about 10 years, it would be seen that from 1983 onwards, the petitioner had earned four 'good' reports.
In other words, before he was sought to be retired prematurely, the petitioner had earned reports which were graded 'good' consistently for the last four years.
That being so, to prematurely retire such a person would be wholly arbitrary.
6.
For the foregoing reasons, the writ petition is allowed, the order of prematurely retiring the petitioner, dated 28th September, 1988 (Annexure P.1) is quashed.
However, there will be no order as to costs.
7.
It may be observed that the petitioner had argued the case in person at the motion stage.
Since I was allowing the writ petition, I did not think it necessary to issue actual date notice of hearing to the petitioner.
Let the office inform the petitioner about the decision of the case.
Petition allowed.
