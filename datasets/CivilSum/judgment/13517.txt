Paritosh K Mukherjee, J - This writ petition which is of 1987 origin comes before us in the presence of the learned counsel for the parties.
2.
Sri Ravi Kant, learned counsel appearing on behalf of the petitioner submits that clause 6 of the impugned circular dated May 10, 1984 clarifies the position by which the petitioner has been discriminated in a hostile manner.
3.
Short facts giving rise to the instant writ petition inter alia are as follows :-  Petitioner retired from service on July 31, 1975, i.e. before the Government Circular dated June 25, 1979 came into force granting certain benefits to the pensioners who retired on or after March 31, 1979.
4.
According to the petitioner, as per liberalised pension rules which were applicable to the petitioner at the time of retirement from service, the average pay used to be worked out on one year's pay.
The maximum eligibility period was fixed at 33 years.
Accordingly, the pension was to be worked out at the ratio 33/80.
5.
Further according to the petitioner, the aforesaid Clause 6 of the circular suffers from the vice of discrimination inasmuch as it denied the benefits to those employees who retired from service prior to March 31, 1979.
However, the State Government issued yet another circular on Sept 7, 1979, granting further benefits to the employees who retired on or after 31st March, 1979.
According to the aforesaid circular, instead of the erstwhile formula of 33/80, a new formula was adopted.
The pension was to be computed according to the three slabs, mentioned in the aforesaid Government Order/Circular which is as follows :-  1.
Up to Rs. 1,000/- of the eligible average emoluments.
50% of the average emoluments.
2.
For the next Rs. 500/- of the average emoluments for eligibility of pension.
45% of the average emoluments 3.
Remaining average emoluments in excess thereof.
40% of the average emoluments.
6.
According to the petitionr as a result of the aforesaid circular, the employees, who retired after March, 1979 were granted greater benefits than to which the petitioner was entitled.
The pension was worked out on the application of the formula contained in the Government order/Circular dated June 25, 1979 which is under :-  1.

In view of the aforesaid calculation, the pension of the petitioner was fixed at Rs. 426/- per month, which amounts to only 50% of the basic pay.
Petitioner was not granted benefit of the dearness allowance and pay, as ordained under the Government Order dated June 25, 1979.
8.
According to the petitioner in view of the decision of Supreme Court in the case of DS Nakara v. Union of India (AIR 1983 SC 130), the aforesaid Government order in so far as it denied the benefit to the pensioners who retired prior to a certain date, as fixed under the Government order was held to be a case of hostile discrimination.
In fact, the need for greater enhanced pension was all the more necessary and expected in case of those employees, who retired in the early seventies.
Denial of those benefits to those employees, therefore, amounted to grave discrimination, according to the judgment in DS Nakara's case (supra) of the Supreme Court.
9.
No counter affidavit has been filed on behalf of the contesting respondents.
10.
Accordingly, after giving anxious consideration to the facts of this case, this Court is of the view that Clause 6 of the aforesaid circular dated May 10, 1984 causes discrimination as it has arbitrarily fixed up the point of date of pensionary benefits to the retired employees, and, thus it is not fitting with the liberalised pension rules.
As such, in our view, there is a good deal of force in the submissions of Sri Ravi Kant, learned counsel for the petitioner and the writ petition may be allowed to that extent.
11.
In view of the above, this writ petition is disposed of by directing the Accountant General U.P., Allahabad, respondent No. 2 to take into consideration the observation of the Supreme Court taken in its judgment (supra), and, to refix the pension of the petitioner at par with the employees who had retired after March 31, 1979.
Such determination to be made as quickly as possible, preferably within a period of six months from the date of communication of the order.
12.
In case the aforesaid authority is not in a position to pay pension to the petitioner at par with the employees who had retired after March 31, 1979, the authority is directed to give strict reasons in support thereof so that the petitioner could ventilate his grievance at subsequent stage.
13.
The writ petition succeeds and is allowed as indicated above.
However, there shall be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
