SS Saron, J - This writ petition has been filed by the Pepsu Road Transport Corporation (for short `the Corporation') against the award of the Labour Court dated 21121998 Annexure P-4 whereby it held the order dated 891993 Annexure P-2 retiring the respondent No. 2-workman w.e.f.
3041988 to be invalid insofar as it makes the said order operative with retrospective effect.
At the same time, it held the same to be valid insofar as its prospective part was concerned i.e. the order stating the workman to be retired from the date of the said order.
2.
The Corporation had filed C.Misc.
No. 31544 of 2001 for staying the operation of the impugned Award during the pendency of the petition.
This Court on 27.9.2002, recorded that counsel for the parties are agreed that instead of disposing of stay application, the matter be listed for final hearing.
Accordingly, the writ petition and stay application were ordered to be listed for final hearing.
3.
The facts leading to the present case are that the respondent-Workman was appointed as a Gunman with the Corporation w.e.f.
16.4.1971.
He was vide order dated 8.9.1973 Annexure P-2 retired from service but w.e.f.
3041998.
The order of retirement dated 891993 indicates that at the time of appointment of the Workman, his date of birth was given as 28111937 besides mentioning he was an Ex-Service man.
The Workman was asked to submit his discharge book which he produced and as per the discharge book, his date of birth was 141928.
Keeping in view these circumstances, it was observed that the Workman had deceived the Corporation by giving wrong date of birth in his application.
Therefore, keeping in view the date of birth in his discharge book to be correct, he was retired w.e.f.
3041988.
It was also ordered that the workman would get services benefits i.e. gratuity, GPF/CP Fund etc. upto 3041988 only.
The aforesaid order dated 891993 passed by the General Manager of the Corporation was assailed by the Workman before the learned Labour Court, which passed the impugned order dated 21121998 referred to above.
4.
I have learned counsel for the parties and with their assistance gone through the records of the case.
5.
Learned counsel appearing for the Corporation contends that the Labour Court has gravely erred in holding the order dated 891993 to be invalid insofar as it is operative with retrospective effect.
On the other hand, learned counsel appearing for respondent contended that Labour Court has passed a just and reasonable order and there is no error of jurisdiction in the same.
6.
I have considered the respective contentions of the parties.
It is not in dispute that the actual date of birth of respondent Workman is 141928 and therefore, in any view, he has rightly been retired from service w.e.f.
3041988 by passing an order of retirement after the workman had attained the age of superannuation.
The view that has been taken by the learned Labour Court while passing the impugned award, is on the wrong assumption that this was a case of termination from service of the Corporation.
This indeed is not the correct position.
The workman has been retired from service from the due date on which he was due for retirement on the basis of his actual date of birth.
It is not a case of termination from service of the workman but of retirement on having attained the age of superannuation.
The workman gave his wrong date of birth as 28111937 at the time of joining the Corporation.
In fact, his actual date of birth is 141928.
When this fact came to the notice of the Corporation it passed the order dated 891993 retiring the workman from his due date i.e. 3041988.
In these circumstances, there is nothing wrong in retiring the workman from the date of his attaining the age of superannuation.
Therefore, the findings of the learned Labour Court invalidating the order dated 891993 of the Corporation insofar as it is operative with retrospective effect is without any basis and is not sustainable.
The Labour Court wrongly held the aforesaid order dated 891993 to be one of termination.
It is, in fact, one of retirement of the workman from the due date.
7.
Therefore, the order dated 21121998 Annexure P-4 of the learned Labour Court is not liable to be sustained and is accordingly quashed and as per the order dated 891993, Annexure P-2, the workman would be deemed to have retired from service w.e.f.
3041988 and he would be entitled to service benefits like gratuity, GPF, CP fund etc. upto 3041988 only.
8.
The learned counsel for the respondent Workman then contends that the salary received by him for the period from 3041988 to the date of order i.e. 891993, should not be recovered.
However, learned counsel appearing for the petitioner Corporation contends that same is liable to be recovered from the Workman as he gave a wrong date of birth.
9.
In my view order the order dated 891993 Annexure P-3 limits the service benefits to that of gratuity GPF, CP Fund etc. upto 3041988 only and it does not make any mention that salary is to be recovered for the excess period that the Workman has worked.
In case the salary for the excess period was to be recovered from the workman it would have been mentioned in the order dated 891993 of the Corporation.
In any case, if the Corporation has to now recover any amount it may proceed in accordance with the principles of natural justice and thereafter may or may not recover the excess amount in accordance with law.
Therefore, the contention of the learned counsel for the petitioner that the Corporation is entitled to recover salary for the excess period, is without any merit and is even otherwise not discernible from its own order dated 891993 Annexure P-2.
Consequently, the writ petition is allowed and the order dated 21121998 Annexure P-4 of the Labour Court is set aside and quashed.
The respondent- workman would be deemed to have retired from service w.e.f.
3041988 in accordance with the order dated 891993.
The petitioner Corporation will not be entitled to recover the salary paid to the workman for the excess period from 3041988 to 891993 for which he has worked except in accordance with the principles of natural justice as indicated above.
No costs.
Petition allowed.
