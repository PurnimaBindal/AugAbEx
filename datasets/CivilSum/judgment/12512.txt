MR Agnihotri and RS Mongia, JJ.
- Briefly stated, after passing the Matriculation examination in June, 1967, the petitioner acquired the qualification of Prabhakar from the Punjabi University, Patiala in December, 1969.
She was appointed as a J.B.T.
Teacher on March 30, 1977 on ad hoc basis and continued working as such, by periodic extensions of six months till March 26, 1980.
Thereafter, she was appointed on regular basis through a Departmental Selection Committee.
2.
The State of Punjab had issued a policy decision on July 20, 1957, which, inter alia, provided for giving higher pay scale to an incumbent on acquisition of higher qualification.
This policy decision has been considered by Hon'ble the Supreme Court in the case of State of Punjab and another v. Kirpal Singh Bhatia, 1975(2) S.L.R.
621, and it was held, that the incumbent of the post is entitled to higher pay scale on acquisition of higher qualification, even though he may not be working against the post requiring higher qualifications.
3.
In another case, Rajinder Kumari v. State of Punjab, 1988(4) S.L.R.
297, it was held by a Division Bench of this Court that the benefit of the policy decision was available even to an employee who might be initially appointed on ad hoc basis, but continued in service till regularisation.
4.
The petitioner had also filed a similar petition (C.W.P.
11085 of 1988) claiming the same relief as was claimed by Smt. Rajinder Kumari.
The said writ petition along with other petitions was disposed of by passing the following order:  "Mr. GK Chatrath, counsel for the petitioners, states at the bar that CWP Nos. 9492, 9493, 11085, 3427, 11106, 11107, 11088, 11811 of 1988, 5206, 14049, 14065, 14079, 16142, 16724 of 1989, 3286 and 9188 of 1990 are covered on all fours on facts and law by a Division Bench judgment of this Court reported as Rajinder Kumari v. State of Punjab, 1988(4) SLR 297.
Mr. OP Goyal, learned Addl.
Advocate-General appealing for the respondent-State is not in a position to state as to whether these petitions are covered or not and seeks time to ascertain the facts.
Prima facie we accept the statement of the counsel for the petitioners and dispose of the writ petitions in terms of Rajinder Kumari's case (supra).
However, it will be open to the State to move an application for recalling of the order in case it comes to the conclusion that the cases are not covered by the aforesaid decision.
The petitioners be paid the salaries in accordance with their qualification(s) as revised from time to time, without delay.
Sd/- G.C.
Mital,  Acting Chief Justice.
Sd/- H.S.
Bedi,  2951991  Judge."
5.
The judgment being inter parties assumed finality so far as the State Government was concerned and when the same was not implemented the petitioner alongwith 10 others filed C.O.C.P.
No. 419 of 1992 in this Court on May 3, 1993.
However, the contempt petition was dismissed as the State pleaded non-applicability of the ratio of Rajinder Kumari's case to the case of the petitioner.
6.
The petitioner has once again approached this Court impugning the order of the respondents dated November 5, 1992 (Annexure P/4) by which she has been denied the grant of higher pay scale admissible under the policy decision of July 20, 1957.
7.
Learned counsel for the petitioner contended that the petitioner having completed more than three years of continuous ad hoc service was entitled to regularisation in view of the State Government's Instructions.
If she had been appointed on regular basis by direct recruitment before her case was considered for regularisation and her previous ad hoc service was continuous, then the petitioner's case was on better footing than that of an employee who was simply working on ad hoc basis and was later on regularised in view of some instructions of the State Government.
8.
After hearing the learned counsel for the parties, we are of the considered view that the petition deserves to be allowed inasmuch as the ratio of the judgment in Rajinder Kumari's case (supra) is squarely applicable to the present case.
A person who is initially appointed on ad hoc basis and later on his services are regularised, would be entitled to the benefit of the policy decision dated July 20, 1957.
It is wholly immaterial whether the regular appointment in service is by virtue of regularisation in view of some instructions of the Government or by direct recruitment.
The petitioner was appointed on regular basis, through by direct recruitment, therefore, she would be entitled to the same treatment as was given to Rajinder Kumari.
Otherwise, also, she was entitled to regularisation in view of Government instructions, having put in more than three years' ad hoc service.
It may also be observed here that the State Government had not filed any application for recalling the order of the Division Bench dated 2951991 allowing the writ petition of the petitioner.
9.
For the foregoing reasons, we allow this petition, quash the impugned order dated November 5, 1992 and direct the respondents to extend the same benefit regarding the grant of higher pay scale to the petitioner in accordance with Punjab Government's policy decision dated July 20, 1957, as was given to Rajinder Kumari (Petitioner in C.W.P.
No. 4515 of 1986), as also to other petitioners, whose writ petitions were disposed of on May 29, 1991, along with C.W.P.
No. 11085 of 1988 filed by the petitioner.
There will be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
