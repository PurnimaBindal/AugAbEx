THB Chalapathi, J - Invoking the jurisdiction of this Court under Article 226 of the Constitution of India, the petitioners are seeking issuance of a writ of certioriari to quash the orders dated 261994, 2771994 and 1311995 passed by the 2nd respondent promoting respondents 3 to 29 with retrospective effect from the years 1981, 1982 and 1983 and also to quash the order dated 27.6.1995 granting further promotion to respondents 3 to 29 as Superintendent Grade-II.
2.
The petitioners 1 to 5 have been promoted and working as Senior Assistants while the 6th petitioner is working as Senior Scale Stenographer whereas respondents 3 to 29 are working as Clerks in the Irrigation Department.
The 3rd respondent namely On Parkash Kaushal was promoted as Accounts Clerk/Senior Assistant on 8.5.1986.
He made a representation on 441994 that another clerk by name Tara Chand, who had been recruited as Clerk later than him, was given promotion earlier to him as Senior Assistant.
On the basis of the said representation, the 3rd respondent was given promotion with retrospective effect from 121982 by an order dated 261994.
Likewise the 4th respondent, who claimed to be senior to one Mohinder Singh, was also given retrospective promotion with effect from 28.8.1973.
According to the petitioners, retrospective promotion given to the respondents is arbitrary and mala fide and while giving retrospective promotions to the respondents, their seniority was ignored.
3.
The 2nd respondent also issued office order dated 1311995 giving retrospective promotions to respondents 5 to 29 as Senior Assistants with effect from the year 1981 to 1982.
Further according to the petitioners, respondents 3 to 29 were considered for promotion at the appropriate time, but they were found not fit to be promoted because of their bad service record or they have foregone their promotions.
Respondent No. 5 was promoted as Senior Assistant, but he refused to accept the promotion.
4.
The petitioners 1 and 2 were promoted as Senior Assistants on 3081982 while the 3rd petitioner was promoted as Senior Assistant on 22.9.1989 while petitioners 4 and 5 were promoted as Senior Assistant on 1321992 and petitioner No. 6 was promoted as Senior Scale Stenographer on 891981.
By giving retrospective promotions to respondents 3 to 29, chances of the promotion of the petitioners were adversely affected and while giving retrospective promotions to the respondents, no notice was given to the petitioners.
Therefore, the petitioners are seeking a writ of certiorari to quash the orders dated 621994 (Annexure P.1), 2771994 (Annexure P2), 1311995 (Annexure P.3).
5.
It is on the other hand, contended on behalf of the respondents that the petitioners were junior to the private respondents.
It is averred in the written statement that in his representation respondent No. 3 stated that Tara Chand who was junior to him was promoted as Accounts Clerk earlier to him.
The representation of the 3rd respondent was considered by the competent authority and accordingly he was allowed his proper place in the seniority at Sr. No. 53-A and he was promoted with effect from the date his junior was promoted.
So is the case of respondent No. 4.
Therefore, the petitioners cannot challenge the orders which have been passed validly by the competent authority in respect of respondents 3 and 4, respondents 5 to 20 have been given promotion with effect from the dates when their juniors have been promoted and therefore, the writ petition is liable to be dismissed.
6.
Respondents 3 and 14 filed a separate written statement.
The 3rd respondent contended that his service record had been very good throughout.
There was nothing adverse against him and he was given all promotions on the basis of his service record.
When his juniors were promoted on 7121981 and 121982, he made a representation and on his representation he was rightly given the date of promotion from the date his juniors were promoted.
The 14th respondent in the written statement averred that he was senior to the petitioners and they were promoted as Accounts Clerks on 3081982 and he had not foregone his promotion nor his claim for promotion was rejected on the basis of service record.
7.
Respondents 4 to 8, 17, 18, 20 and 24 filed a separate written statement contending that they were rightly promoted from the retrospective effect from the dates when their juniors were promoted and they were seniors to the petitioners.
Therefore, the writ petition is liable to be dismissed.
8.
Annexure P-1 is one of the orders challenged in this writ petition.
It shows that the 3rd respondent Om Parkash made a representation on 441994 stating that one Tara Chand who was junior to him was promoted as Accounts Clerk earlier to him and therefore, he has to be given promotion from the date his junior was promoted.
His junior was promoted on 121982.
The promotion of Tara Chand was within the knowledge of the 3rd respondent.
Though Tara Chand was promoted on 121982, the 3rd respondent did not make any grievance of it till 441994.
He took more than 12 years to make a representation against the promotion of Tara Chand and the Chief Engineer/K.A.D.
Irrigation Department, accepted his representation under Annexure P.1.
Annexure P2 is another order dated 2771994.
It is also under challenge in this writ petition.
It shows that Arjan Singh, respondent No. 4 made a representation on 741994 that his Junior Mohinder Singh was promoted as SDC on 29.8.1974 and further promoted as Accounts Clerk on 541983.
It is the claim of Arjan Singh that he should be promoted earlier to Mohinder Singh as SDC as Mohinder Singh was junior to him.
Therefore, he virtually wants to be promoted as SDC with effect from 29.8.1973 and as Accounts Clerk from 541983.
He made the representation only on 441994 after lapse of 21 years from the date of promotion of Mohinder Singh and his representation was accepted by the Chief Engineer/K.A.D.
on 2771994 vide Annexure P2.
Like that respondents 5 to 20 were given retrospective promotions with effect from 3081982 on the ground that they were senior to some of those persons promoted earlier.
9.
Admittedly, the seniority list of Senior Clerks was circulated in the Order No. 499/4NGE 2/94 and 13301-65/4 NGE 2/94 dated 18.2.1986, but the aforesaid employees made representations only in the year 1994 and they were given retrospective promotion after lapse of 13 years and more.
Further Annexure P.5 dated 1851986 clearly shows that Moti Ram, respondent No. 5 and Janak Raj were ignored due to their unsatisfactory service record.
Annexure P4 dated 18121986 also shows that certain employees including Kishan Singh have foregone promotion.
At any rate, the so-called juniors of respondents 5 to 29 have been promoted in the years 1981 and 1982 and respondents 5 to 29 have been promoted some time in 1986, but after keeping quiet for over 12 years from the date of promotion of their junior, they made the representations to the Chief Engineer claiming seniority on the ground that their juniors have been promoted earlier to them and they should be deemed to have been promoted from the date their juniors have been promoted.
When there was an inordinate delay, it was a ground to reject the representation of the respondents.
The Chief Engineer should not have entertained the representations of respondents 3 to 29 since the same have been made after lapse of more than 12 years from the date their juniors have been promoted and after lapse of 8 years after their promotion.
It is now settled by the Apex Court that seniority of the employees should not be disturbed or unsettled after lapse of several years.
10.
In Roshan Lal v International Air Port Authority of India, AIR 1981 SC 597, the Apex Court held as follows :-  "The respondents were appointed as Air Port Officers in 1975 and the present writ petitions were filed in 1978.
We do not think we will be justified in reopening the question of the legality of the appointment of the respondents as Air Port Officers several years after their appointments.
We also take notice that the prayer in the writ petitions also is confined primarily to the seniority list and the consequences flowing from the seniority list."
11.
In Jagdish Lal v. State of Haryana, JT 1997(5) SC 387 : 1998(1) SCT 26 (SC), it has been held by the Apex Court as follows :-  "That apart, as this Court has repeatedly held, the delay disentitles the party to the discretionary relief under Article 226 or 32 of the Constitution.
It is not necessary to reiterate all catena of precedents in this behalf.
Suffice it to state that the appellants kept sleeping over their rights for long and elected to wake up when they had the impetus from Vir Pal Chauhan and Ajit Singh's ratio.
But Vir Pal Chauhan and Sabharwal's cases, kept at rest the promotion already made by that date and declared them as valid they were limited to the question of future promotions given by applying the rule of reservation to all the persons prior to the date of judgment in Sabharwal case which required to be examined in the light of law laid in Sabharwal's case.
Thus earlier promotions cannot be re-opened.
Only those cases arising after that date would be examined in the light of the law laid down in Sabharwal's case and Vir Pal Chauhan's case and equally Ajit Singh's case.
If the candidate has already been further promoted to the higher echelons of service, his seniority is not open to be reviewed."
12.
The Court cannot be oblivious of the fact that the private respondents, made earlier successive representations for promotion i.e. prior to 1986, but of no avail.
They did not seek their remedy in any court of law.
There is nothing in the rules which permit the respondents to make successive representations for promotion.
The respondents have not placed any material before me to show that they made any representation for giving seniority.
13.
The learned Counsel for the private respondents submitted that the respondents have been making representations time and again and the last representation was made in the year 1994.
The Court cannot be oblivious of the fact that the respondent made the representation just a few days before the retirement of the Chief Engineer and the impugned order has been passed on the eve of the retirement of the then Chief Engineer.
It does not appear from the record that any opportunity has been given to the petitioners who have been earlier promoted to show cause why the seniority should not be revised.
Without affording any opportunity to the petitioners before disturbing their seniority, the order giving retrospective promotion cannot be sustained.
I am, therefore, of the opinion that the writ petition deserves to be allowed and the impugned order Annexure P-4 is liable to be quashed.
14.
Accordingly, the writ petition is allowed and the impugned order Annexure P-4 is quashed.
It is also brought to my notice that some of the respondents have been promoted on the basis of the revised seniority and they also retired from service.
This order will not effect the right of these respondents who have been promoted and retired from service and the State shall not take any action for recovery of the monetary benefits from those respondents on the basis of this judgment.
No order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
