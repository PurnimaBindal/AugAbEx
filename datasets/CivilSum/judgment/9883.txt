Sat Pal, J. - In this writ petition, the petitioner has prayed for a writ in the nature of mandamus directing the respondents to give him the pay scale of HVS Class-I service of Rs. 1400-2100 and other financial benefits from the date when he was appointed as Deputy, Director (Feed and Fodder), on 4.7.1983.
2.
The petitioner who possesses the qualification of B.Sc.
(Agriculture) joined the service as Agricultural Inspector in the Agricultural Department of the Government of Haryana on 5.7.1971.
He was sent on deputation to the Animal Husbandry department with effect from 1.8.1979.
While working in the said department, the petitioner was selected by the Haryana Public Service Commission for appointment on the post of Deputy Director (Feed and Fodder) on the basis of an advertisement published in the Daily Tribune dated 16.7.1986.
The Government of Haryana appointed the petitioner as Deputy Director (Feed and Fooder) in the scale of Rs. 1200-1850 in the HVS Class-I vide order dated 2741987 (copy Annexure P2).
It may be relevant to note here that in between the petitioner was appointed as a Deputy Director (Feed and Fodder) on ad hoc basis in his own pay-scale on 4.7.1983.
3.
Mr. Ahuja, learned counsel appearing on behalf of the petitioner submitted that the petitioner was appointed against the post of Deputy Director (Feed and Fodder) which was a cadre post under the Haryana Veterinary Service Class-I Rules, 1993.
In this connection, he drew my attention to the letter dated 851968 (Annexure P-9) and submitted that as on 1111966 there were three posts of Deputy Directors General, Agriculture and Animal Husbandry in the Animal Husbandry Department of Haryana in the pay scale of Rs. 350-1200.
He further submitted that letter dated 1091982 (copy Annexure P-1) shows that the pay scale of the Deputy Director and other allied posts in PVS Class-I in the Animal Husbandry Department was Rs. 600-1300.
This scale was revised to Rs. 1200-1850 with effect from 18.4.1979 and was further revised to Rs. 1400-2100 w.e.f.
121981.
The learned counsel contended that since the petitioner was also appointed to the post of Deputy Director in HVS Class-I (same as PVS Class-I) he was entitled to pay scale of Rs. 1400-2100 w.e.f.
2741987.
He also submitted that the Deputy Directors working in the Veterinary Department were getting pay scale of Rs. 1400-2100 and the petitioner who was holding the post of Deputy Director in the non-Veterinary Wing of the department, should not be deprived of the scale of Rs. 1400-2100 merely because he was not working in the Veterinary wing of the department.
In support of his submission, the learned counsel has placed reliance on the following DB judgments of this Court :-  1.
Harkishan v. State of Punjab, 1987(5) SLR 539.
2.
Haryana State Biologists Association v. State of Haryana, 1994(4) RSJ 444 : 1994(3) SCT 198.
3.
Jatinder Singh and another v. State of Punjab, 1997(2) RSJ 381.
4.
Learned counsel for the petitioner also placed reliance on a judgment of the Supreme Court in Employees of Tannery and Footwear Corporation of India Limited v Union of India, 1991(2) SLR 131 : 1991(2) S.C.T.
339, to contend that the employees working in one corporation of the Government of India were entitled to the same scale equal to the employees working in another Corporation of the Government of India, if the nature of their work was similar.
5.
Mr. Khanna, learned AAG, submitted that the petitioner was appointed against an ex cadre post for which the pay scale of Rs. 1200-1850 was duly mentioned in the advertisement, persuant to which the petitioner had applied for the post.
He, therefore, contended that the petitioner was not entitled to the pay scale claimed by him.
In support of his submission, the learned AAG placed reliance on a judgment of the Supreme Court in State of Haryana and another v. Ram Chander and another, 1997(3) RSJ 403.
6.
I have given my thoughful consideration to the submissions made by the learned counsel for the parties and have perused the records of the case.
7.
The point as to whether the petitioner was appointed against ex-cadre post or a post under the Haryana Veterinary Class-I Rules 1930, came up for consideration before a learned Single Judge of this Court in C.W.P.
No. 7893 of 1993 filed by the petitioner himself which is reported as RS Malik v. State of Haryana and others, 1995(4) All Instant Judgments 454.
The findings on this point given by the learned Single Judge are given below :-  "A look at the contents of Annexures P1 and P2 does give an impression that the petitioner was recruited under the Haryana Veterinary Service Class-I Rules, 1930 and in the proforma filed by the Haryana Public Service Commission for recruitment to the post of Deputy Director (Feed and Fodder) also reference to the H.V.S.
Class-I Rules has been made; after having carefully gone through the record I am fully convinced that the various functionaries of the Animal Husbandry Department and the Haryana Public Service Commission had not applied their mind before making recruitment to the post of Deputy Director (Feed and Fodder).
The reference has been made in the order Annexure P-1 are in fact the Punjab Veterinary Service Class-I Rules, 1930.
Vide communication dated 141953 the Punjab Government specified 9 posts as forming part of the cadre and a look at this notification clearly shows that the post of Deputy Director (Feed and Fodder) is not one amongst them.
In fact, none of the posts of the Deputy Directors find any reference in the appendix published with the said notification.
The document creating the posts of Deputy Director (Feed and Fodder) has not been placed before the Court but the letter written by the Director, Animal Husbandry to the Secretary on 1161985 clearly shows that the post of Deputy Director (Feed and Fodder) was an ex-cadre and an isolated post.
Even otherwise in the absence of any rule governing recruitment to the post of Deputy Director (Feed and Fodder), that post will have to be treated as an ex-cadre post."
8.
After going through the records, I also find that the findings of the learned Single Judge hereinabove are based on the records and accordingly I hold that the petitioner was appointed against the post of Deputy Director (Feed and Fodder) which is an ex-cadre post and does not fall under the Haryana Veterinary Class-I Rules, 1930.
Since the post of Deputy Director (Feed and Fodder) is an ex-cadre post and in the advertisement the pay scale of this post was clearly stated as Rs. 1200-1850, the claim of the petitioner for the higher pay scale of Rs. 1400-2100 is not legally sustainable.
9.
As stated hereinabove, the petitioner possessed the qualification of B.Sc.
(Agriculture) whereas the minimum qualification prescribed for the post of Deputy Director (Veterinary Wing) is Bachelor of Veterinary Science and Animal Husbandry.
Since the minimum qualifications prescribed for the two posts are different, the claim of the petitioner for the higher pay scale of Rs. 1400- 2100, which scale is being given to the Deputy Director, Veterinary, is not tenable.
In this connection, reference may be made to the judgment of the Supreme Court in Shyam Babu Verma v. Union of India, 1994(2) SCT 296 : 1994(1) SC 574.
It was observed by the Hon'ble Supreme Court as under :-  "The nature of work may be more or less same, but scale of pay may vary based on academic qualification or experience which justifies classification."
The judgments in the case of Harkishan (supra) and Jitender Singh (supra) and Haryana State Biologists Association (supra) are not of any assistance to the petitioner as the facts in those cases were different.
In the case of Harkishan (supra) a Division Bench of this Court held that it was irrational to place a junior post and a higher post in the same scale of pay but the facts of the present case have got no similarity to the facts of that case.
In the case of Jitender Singh (supra) it was found by a Division Bench of this Court that the State Government had failed to show as to why Class II officers working in the Statistical wing have not been given same pay scales as were being given to Class II officers working in the administrative wing of the Department but in the present case reasons for the pay scales prescribed for the post of Deputy Director (Feed and Fodder) have been given by the State Government.
Similarly, in the case of Haryana State Biologists Association (supra) it was found on the facts of that case that the pay scale for the post of Biologists and that of HCMS-II doctors had been the same throughout but it was suddenly disturbed without the authority of law but in the present case, as stated herein above, the post held by the petitioner is ex cadre post for which qualifications are different than the qualifications prescribed for the post of Deputy Director in the Veterinary wing of the Department.
In view of the above discussion, I do not find any merit in the petition and the same is dismissed with no order as to costs.
Petition dismissed.
