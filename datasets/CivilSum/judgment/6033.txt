Dr. Mrs. Sarojnei Saksena, J - Petitioner has filed this writ petition under Articles 226 and 227 of the Constitution praying a writ of mandamus directing the respondents 1 and 2 to give the deemed dates of promotion to the petitioner as were given to his immediate junior respondent No. 3 on the basis of the punjab Government Circular letter No. 3180-SW1-70/893 dated 11.1.1971 (Annexure P-13).
2.
The petitioner alleged that he was selected by the Subordinate Services Selection Board, Punjab in the year 1961 as Assistant Sub Inspector of Police (at that time his name was Tarsem Lal).
He assumed his name as Dharminder Singh in the year 1969.
He was sent to Police Training School at Phillaur.
After one year's training.
he was posted at the Government Railway Police Station, Ludhiana.
The petitioner is a member of Scheduled Caste.
He was confirmed on the post of Assistant Sub Inspector with effect from 541964.
On 181964 Chanchal Singh, Sub Inspector of Government Railway Police, Punjab, proceeded on leave preparatory to retirement, thereby his post fell vacant and petitioner was promoted as Officiating Sub Inspector with effect from 181964, but he was reverted to his substantive post of Assistant Sub Inspector vide order dated 26.11.1964 with effect from 23.11.1964 (Annexure P-2).
In this order of reversion, it was specifically mentioned that he is being reverted in order to make room for upper pass Assistant Sub Inspector.
Swaran Singh, who was neither a Scheduled Caste nor an upper pass Assistant Sub Inspector, was promoted as Officiating Sub Inspector.
Thereafter, twelve Junior Assistant Sub Inspectors not belonging to Scheduled Castes and mostly nonupper pass were promoted to officiate Sub Inspectors and thereafter as Inspectors and petitioner was thereby superseded.
The petitioner served as an Assistant Sub Inspector till 1981970.
Even after passing the upper pass Course, he submitted representation against his reversion order which was dismissed and then he filed Civil Writ Petition No. 1239 of 1970.
On being noticed, the State of Punjab in its reply gave assurance that the petitioner would be considered along with other upper pass Assistant Sub Inspectors when the case for promotion will be taken up.
In view of this assurance, his petition was dismissed as not pressed (Annexure P-17).
3.
The petitioner submitted representation (Annexure P-5) complaining that 12 Assistant Sub Inspectors were promoted, but he was not considered.
He made another representation (Annexure P-6) and submitted that as per Government's Circular letter No. 6028-WG-II-58/2146 dated 29.1.1959 and another Circular No. 6486-5-WGI-63/19193 dated 12.9.1963 and Articles 16(4) and 335 of the Constitution of India, 10% of the total vacancies had to be kept reserved for the Scheduled Castes, but these instructions were ignored in petitioner's case.
As per these instructions, out of 100 vacancies, 22 vacancies were reserved for Scheduled Castes.
The government considered the case of promotion of Sarvshri Gurcharan Singh, Arjan Dev Bhandari and Smt. Parkash Kaur Gill after about 15 years and were promoted, but petitioner's case was not considered despite his another representation (Annexure P-7).
4.
On 11.1.1971 vide letter No. 3180-WGI-70/893 Secretary to Government of Punjab, Punjab Welfare of Scheduled Caste and Backward Classes issued instructions to all Heads of the Departments regarding reservation in promotion for the members of the Scheduled Castes and Backward Classes in service and post under Punjab Government (Annexure P-13) in pursuance of the Apex Court's judgment in Hira Lal v. State of Punjab (Civil Writ Petition No. 271 of 1966).
Thereafter, respondents 1 and 2 promoted, respondents 3 to 5 as Deputy Superintendent of Police.
Respondent No. 3 was promoted as Sub Inspector w.e.f.
22.4.1968, promoted as Inspector w.e.f.
2351974 and promoted as Deputy Superintendent of Police w.e.f.
23.5.1980, though respondent No. 3 was junior to the petitioner.
5.
Petitioner's contention is that he is entitled to the following deemed dates of promotions :-  (i) As Sub Inspector w.e.f.
5.4.1967; (ii) As Inspector w.e.f.
5.4.1973 and  (iii) As Deputy Superintendent of Police w.e.f.
541979.
As per the seniority list, petitioner's position was at No. 46 as compared to the position of respondent No. 3, who was at No. 52.
6.
During the pendency of this Writ Petition vide order dated 2071983 the Deputy Inspector General of Police, Railway Punjab, Patiala, gave deemed date of promotion as Officiating Inspector of Police w.e.f.
2081975 vide Annexure P-19 and the petitioner was also given proforma promotion as Deputy Superintendent of Police (provisional) w.e.f.
571982 vide Annexure P-20.
7.
Petitioner's contention is that respondents are not justified in promoting respondents No. 3 and 5 to the posts of Deputy Superintendent of Police particularly when the petitioner was senior to them as Assistant Sub Inspector, Sub Inspector and Inspector.
These orders are violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
His representation could not have been dismissed only on the ground of laches.
Respondents 1 and 2 are wrongly seeking justification in promoting the petitioner when his junior respondent No. 4 was promoted though he is entitled to be promoted at par with respondent No. 3.
He is also claiming all the arrears for the period for which he has been given deemed date of promotion and for which he could not work on the post due to the fault of the government.
8.
In its reply, respondents 1 and 2 have objected that the petitioner's case for restoration of his seniority in the rank of Inspector of Police against the reserved vacancy of Scheduled Caste from the date of his eligibility for list 'F' in accordance with Government instructions issued vide letter No. 3180-SW-I-70/893 dated 11.1.1971 was considered.
In view of these instructions, petitioner's contention were considered.
His name was brought at 'F' list (a list of Sub Inspectors considered eligible for promotion to the rank of Inspector) w.e.f.
2081975 instead of 1911977, that is the date from which he completed 5 years services as Sub Inspector.
He was also given deemed date of promotion as Officiating Inspector of Police from the same date, i.e., 2081975 instead of 1121977.
In consequence of re-fixation of seniority in the rank of Inspector of Police, the petitioner's name was considered for further promotion to the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police and he was given proforma promotion as Superintendent of Police (Provisional) w.e.f.
571982, that is the date on which his next junior Shri Dewan Chand-respondent No. 4 was promoted.
9.
It is denied that the petitioner was reverted to his substantive rank as Assistant Sub Inspector to make room for regular promotion of upper school Course pass Assistant Inspectors to the rank of Sub Inspector.
He could not be allowed to continue to officiate as Sub Inspector as he had less than 4 years service and a chance of promotion was given to him when Chanchal Singh Sub Inspector proceeded on leave, but that did not entitle him to claim seniority over his senior.
His working during the period from 141962 to 30.9.1962 and during the year 1964-65 was not satisfactory and he was warned to improve his working.
These warnings were also conveyed to him.
Swaran Singh was rightly promoted as Sub Inspector because he had passed Intermediate School Course in 1955 whereas the petitioner had passed the said Course in 1962.
Swaran Singh, Sub Inspector was brought on promotion list 'E' (provisional) w.e.f.
23.11.1964 whereas petitioner was not brought on this list.
Nine Sub Inspectors, who had passed Upper School Courses prior to the petitioner and brought on provisional list 'E' were promoted as Sub Inspectors earlier to the petitioner.
Sub Inspector Swaran Singh was senior to the petitioner and was an exemptee, who could not pass his Upper School Course being over-age, was promoted as Sub Inspector on 23.11.1964 and again on 1.12.1968.
The petitioner passed his Upper School Course in August, 1969 and afterwards served as Sub Inspector till 1981970.
His representation was rejected, but he did not prefer any appeal against that.
10.
It is admitted that the Government instructions provided for reservation for 10% vacancies for the members of Scheduled Castes in promotions, the name of the petitioner was not brought on promotion list 'E', as provided under Punjab Police Rules Rule 1310, as he did not pass the Upper School Course.
Since he did not fulfil the condition for eligibility for promotion, he could not be promoted as Sub Inspector against a reserved vacancy.
11.
Petitioner was brought on list 'E' w.e.f.
2081970 and as such, he was not eligible for promotion to the rank of Sub Inspector prior to the date of admission to list 'E' because earlier to this date he did not fulfil the minimum qualification/condition for considering his case for admission to list 'E' and subsequent promotion as Sub Inspector of Police.
In compliance of the instructions (Annexure P-13), name of the petitioner was brought on list 'F' w.e.f.
2081975 instead of 1911977, that is the date on which he completed 5 years service as Sub Inspector and was given deemed date of promotion as Officiating Inspector of Police w.e.f.
2081975 instead of 1121977 and consequently, he was also promoted as Officiating Deputy Superintendent of Police w.e.f.
571982 the date on which Dewan Chand respondent No. 4 was promoted.
12.
About the seniority, respondents 1 and 2 contended that Gurcharan Singh respondent No. 3 originally belonged to Jalandhar Range whereas the petitioner belongs to Government Railway Police.
The seniority and promotion upto the rank of Sub Inspector of Police is to be regulated at range rolls respectively as provided under Rule 121(4) of the Punjab Police Rules.
The petitioner has to claim his promotion in accordance with his seniority in the Government Railway Police and he cannot claim promotion in comparison with officers of other ranges.
Thus, he cannot claim seniority and promotion as given to respondent No. 3, who belonged to Jalandhar range.
As per his seniority in the Government Railway Police, petitioner's name was brought on list 'E' w.e.f.
2081975 and he was promoted as Sub Inspector w.e.f.
this date.
Respondents 3 and 4 belonged to Jalandhar Range and the petitioner cannot compare his seniority with them as he belongs to Government Railway Police Range.
It is denied that he is entitled to get the difference of pay and allowances in view of his deemed date of promotions now given during the pendency of the Writ petition.
However, it is admitted that he will be entitled to the benefit of pay fixation from the deemed date of promotion as Inspector of Police.
13.
During arguments, respondent's learned counsel submitted that now the petitioner is no more in police service as he has sought his voluntary retirement which has been accepted by the department.
14.
Petitioner's learned counsel has made only two submissions.
(1) As per seniority list of Inspectors, petitioner's name was at serial No. 46 and the name of respondent No. 3 was at serial No. 52; therefore, while giving deemed date of promotion to the petitioner, he should have been promoted, if not earlier, at least on the dates on which respondent No. 3 was promoted.
This contention of the Deputy Advocate General, Punjab is not tenable that he was given promotion on the same date on which respondent No4 was given deemed date of promotion as Deputy Superintendent of Police.
His second contention is that no doubt during the pendency of the writ petition, the petitioner has been given deemed date of promotion as Inspector w.e.f.
2081975 and as Deputy Superintendent of Police, w.e.f.
571982, he is entitled to all differences of pay as he was not allowed to work on these promotion posts by the government and it was not because of any fault of the petitioner.
He has relied on various authorities which will be discussed later on.
15.
Learned Deputy Advocate General, Punjab, appearing for respondent No. 1 submitted that no doubt petitioner and respondents 3 and 4 belong to Scheduled Castes, but respondents 3 and 4 were working in Jalandhar range, while petitioner was working in Government Railway Police.
Respondent No. 3 was appointed as Assistant Sub Inspector on 2351962 while petitioner was appointed on 541961 and respondent No. 4 was appointed on 1541964.
Respondent No. 3 was confirmed on this post on 2351965 while petitioner was confirmed on this post on 541964, but respondent No. 3 passed Upper School Course in March, 1968 while petitioner passed this Course in August, 1969.
Respondent No. 3 was brought on promotion list 'E' on 15.4.1968, while petitioner was brought on this list on 2081970.
Respondent No. 3 was given deemed date of promotion as Sub Inspector w.e.f 22.4.1968 (12111970 actual), while petitioner was promoted as Sub Inspector w.e.f.
2081970.
Respondent No. 3 was brought on promotion list 'F' (executive) w.e.f.
2351970 deemed date, (1911977 actual) while during the pendency of the writ petition, the petitioner is brought on this promotion list w.e.f.
2081975, deemed date (1911977 actual).
Respondent No. 3 was promoted w.e.f.
2351970, deemed date (721977 actual) while petitioner was promoted as Inspector w.e.f.
2081975, deemed date (1121977 actual).
Respondent No. 3 was confirmed w.e.f.
18.4.1981, while petitioner was confirmed w.e.f.
27.7.1982.
Respondent No. 3 was promoted as Deputy Superintendent of Police w.e.f.
23.5.1980, deemed date (1321982 actual) and he died on 11101987.
Petitioner was promoted as Deputy Superintendent of Police w.e.f.
571982, deemed date.
During the pendency of the writ petition, respondent No. 4 was also given deemed date of promotion as Deputy Superintendent of Police w.e.f.
571982.
16.
Respondent's learned counsel also contended that Upper School Course Pass is a condition precedent for promotion to the rank of Sub Inspector under Punjab Police Rules, Rule 1310.
From the above submissions, it is evident that Gurcharan Singh - respondent No. 3 was promoted as Sub Inspector w.e.f.
22.4.1968 and thus, he became senior to the petitioner and got promotion list 'F' w.e.f.
2351974 as compared to 2081975 granted to the petitioner.
Consequently, respondent No. 3 was promoted as Deputy Superintendent of Police earlier to the petitioner on the basis of seniority, which was above the petitioner.
17.
Learned counsel also submitted that earlier the petitioner filed Civil Writ Petition No. 1239 of 1970 demanding back date of promotion as Sub Inspector, which was dismissed by the High Court on 3081970.
18.
He also argued that at the relevant time when the petitioner's case for grant of list 'F' was under consideration, the eligibility was to be considered and the eligibility condition for grant of this list was that a Sub Inspector should have completed 5 year's service in that rank.
The petitioner completed 5 year's service in August, 1975 and accordingly, he was granted 'F' list w.e.f.
2081975, while respondent No. 3 completed 5 year's service as Sub Inspector in 1973 and was given 'F' list w.e.f.
2351974.
He also valiantly argued that the seniority upto the rank of Sub Inspector is maintained at range level.
Petitioner belongs to Government Railway Police whereas respondent No. 3 belongs to Jalandhar range.
Therefore, they got promotion upto the rank of Inspector in their respective ranges under Rule 121(4) of the Punjab Police Rules.
He also submitted that no doubt, in the seniority list, petitioner was at Serial No. 46, while respondent No. 3 was at serial No. 52 at the time of their entry to list 'F', but respondent No. 3 was given deemed date of promotion of Sub Inspector w.e.f.
2351974 (721977 actual) as he passed Upper School in Jalandhar Range earlier on the basis of 1971 instructions of Government assigning seniority to the Scheduled Caste candidate.
As such, he became senior to the petitioner and thus, their seniority was changed.
19.
Respondent's learned counsel also submitted that no doubt during the pendency of the writ petition, the petitioner has been given deemed dates of promotion as Inspector and as Deputy Superintendent of Police, but he is not entitled to get arrears of pay in consequence of these promotions.
This fact can only be considered for fixation of pay.
In support of his contention, he relied on State of Haryana and others v. OP Gupta, 1996(2) SCT 294.
20.
Only two points arise for consideration in this case :- (1) whether the petitioner was senior to respondent No. 3 and therefore, he should have been promoted as Inspector as well as Deputy Superintendent of Police when respondent No. 3 was promoted; (2) whether the petitioner is entitled to all the arrears/difference of pay as he has been given deemed dates of promotion on the posts of Inspector and Deputy Superintendent of Police.
21.
So far as seniority of petitioner and respondent No. 3 is concerned, the relevant rule 121(4) of the Punjab Police Rules is as under :-  "Inspectors shall be borne on a provincial roll and shall receive provincial constabulary numbers.
Sergeants shall be borne on a separate provincial roll and shall receive separate provincial constabulary numbers.
Sub-Inspectors and Assistant Sub-Inspectors shall be borne on range rolls and shall receive range constabulary numbers."
22.
Admittedly, the petitioner was appointed as Sub Inspector in Railway Police, while respondents 3 and 4 were appointed as Sub Inspectors in Jalandhar Range.
Till they are promoted as Inspectors, their seniority lists are maintained rangewise.
The petitioner was appointed as Assistant Sub Inspector on 541961, while respondent No. 3 was appointed as Assistant Sub Inspector on 2351962, but petitioner was confirmed on this post on 541964, while respondent No. 3 was confirmed on this post on 2351965.
Another important condition is that the Assistant Sub Inspector must complete 5 year's service on that post before he can be considered on 'E' list and must also pass Upper School Course.
Respondent No. 3 cleared this Course in March, 1968, while petitioner cleared this Course in August, 1969; therefore, respondent No. 3 was brought on promotion list 'E' w.e.f.
15.4.1968, while petitioner was brought on this list w.e.f.
2081970.
Thus, petitioner became junior to respondent No. 3 and consequently, respondent No. 3 was given deemed date of promotion as Sub Inspector on 22.4.1968, while petitioner was promoted as Sub Inspector on 2081970.
Respondent No. 3 was brought on promotion list 'F' w.e.f.
2351974, deemed date, while petitioner was brought on this list w.e.f 2081975, deemed date, though actually both of them were brought on this list on 1911977.
During the pendency of the writ petition, subsequent orders are passed in compliance with Government Circular (Annexure P-13).
Respondent No. 3 was promoted an Inspector w.e.f.
2351974 (deemed date) and petitioner was promoted as Inspector w.e.f.
2081975 (deemed date), though actually respondent No. 3 was promoted as Inspector w.e.f.
1121977 earlier.
Consequently, respondent No. 3 was confirmed on this post w.e.f.
18.4.1981, while petitioner was confirmed on this post on 27.7.1982.
Respondent No. 3 was promoted as Deputy Superintendent of Police w.e.f.
23.5.1980 (deemed date), while petitioner was promoted as Deputy Superintendent of Police w.e.f.
571982 (deemed date).
23.
It is evident from Rule 121(4) of the Punjab Police Rules that the seniority of Sub Inspectors is to be maintained at range-level and therefore, the seniority of the petitioner and respondent No. 3 was different in their ranges.
Secondly, as per Rule 1310 of the said Rules Upper School Course Pass is a condition precedent for promotion to the rank of Sub-Inspector.
Respondent No. 3 passed this Course in March, 1968, while petitioner passed this Course in August, 1969.
Further for getting entry to promotion list 'F', the eligibility criterion was that he should have completed 5 year's service in the rank of Sub-Inspector.
As respondent No. 3 passed Upper School Course before the petitioner could clear this Course and consequently, he was earlier brought on promotion list 'E' than the petitioner; therefore, he was promoted as Sub-Inspector w.e.f.
22.4.1968, while petitioner could be promoted as Sub Inspector on 2081970.
Petitioner was to complete 5 year's service on the post of Sub Inspector for being promoted as Inspector.
As respondent No. 3 completed 5 year's service as Sub Inspector earlier than the petitioner, respondent No. 3 was promoted as an Inspector w.e.f.
2351974, while petitioner was promoted as Inspector w.e.f.
2081975.
Hence, it is obvious that as the petitioner was not eligible to be appointed as Inspector earlier than the respondent No. 3 because of the above referred two eligibility criteria, he became junior to respondent No. 3.
24.
Petitioner's learned counsel has relied on Ram Chander v. State of Haryana, 1996(4) SCT.
42 , wherein it is held that if the petitioner is reverted and all others who are reverted with him, even his junior are promoted, but petitioner is denied promotion, petition cannot be thrown due to delay only.
25.
In Shri Vidya Ram v. State of Haryana, 1995(3) SCT 50 , it is held that if promotion is to be given by following rule, seniority-cum-fitness, the person who is senior should ordinarily be promoted despite better merit of a junior.
26.
In this case, the petitioner is challenging his reversion when he was provisionally promoted on the post of Sub Inspector when in August, 1964 Shri Chanchal Singh, Sub Inspector, Government Railway Police, Punjab, proceeded on leave preparatory to retirement and subsequently, he was reverted to his substantive rank as Assistant Sub Inspector w.e.f.
23.11.1964.
Even this contention is not sustainable.
The petitioner was promoted to officiate as Sub Inspector, which was just a fortuitous chance given to him.
He cannot claim seniority over his senior.
He could not be given regular promotion on this basis because at that time his name was not brought on 'E' list as provided under Rule 1310 of the said Rules and as at that time he was not fulfilling eligibility condition, he was rightly reverted and Swaran Singh was promoted on this post as Swaran Singh passed the minimum School Course in 1955, while petitioner passed this Course in 1962; therefore, Swaran Singh was brought on 'E' list w.e.f.
23.11.1964 whereas at that time petitioner was not brought on this list.
Hence, Swaran Singh, being senior to petitioner, was rightly promoted.
27.
So far as arrears of salary etc. are concerned, petitioner's counsel has relied on Arun Kumar Chatterjee v. South Eastern Railway and others, 1985(1) S.L.R.
500, SI Bhim Singh v. State of Haryana, 1995(3) SCT.
680 and Sudershan Kumar v. State of Haryana, 1997(2) SCT.
279.
28.
In Arun Kumar Chatterjee's case (supra), it is held that if promotion is illegally denied to a government servant and he is held entitled to promotion from due date, then he is also entitled to all consequential benefits.
In SI Bhim Singh's case (supra), it is held that when the petitioner is given retrospective promotion, he is entitled to all the monetary benefits.
The same view has been held in Sudershan Kumar's case (supra).
But in OP Gupta's case (supra), the Apex Court has considered this aspect and has held that the facts of Union of India v. KV Jankiraman, JT 1991(3) SCT 317 are distinguishable and the Apex Court re-affirmed the view held in Paluru Ramakrishnaiah and others v. Union of India and another, JT 1989(1) SC 595, wherein the direction issued by the High Court was upheld that "there has to be "no pay for no work", i.e., a person will not be entitled to any pay and allowance during the period for which he did not perform the duties of higher post, although after due consideration, he was given a proper place in the gradation list having been deemed to be promoted to the higher post with effect from the date his junior was promoted.
He will be entitled only to step up the scale of pay retrospectively from the deemed date but is not entitled to the payment of arrears of the salary."
The same ratio was re- interated in Virender Kumar v. Avinash Chandra, JT 1990(3) SC 503.
It is also made clear in this judgment that unless the seniority list is prepared and finalised and promotions are made in accordance with the Rules on the basis of the above seniority list, the question of entitlement to work in the promotional posts does not arise and consequently, the payment of arrears of salary does not arise since, admittedly, the respondents had not worked during that period.
29.
The facts of Paluru Ramakrishnaiah and OP Gupta's cases (supra) are quite distinguishable.
The petitioner had never refused to work on higher post if during the pendency of the litigation deemed date of promotion is given to him and on the due date of promotion, he was deprived of his right to work on higher post, he cannot be denied the arrears of salary when retrospective promotion is given to him, the condition that he would not be paid difference in arrears of salary on promoted post could not be imposed as it is not sustainable in law.
Rule of 'No work no pay' would not be applicable.
In those cases notional promotion was given under the orders of the Court.
The promotions lists were required to be prepared, but in this case the petitioner was working in the police department and during the pendency of the writ petition he had been given deemed date of promotion on the post of Inspector with effect from 2081975, though actually he was promoted on this post on 1121977.
Thus, during this period from 2081975 to 1121977, he was working on the post of Sub Inspector though he was entitled to be promoted as Inspector.
Hence, the petitioner is entitled to get arrears of salary of the grade of Inspector from 2081975 to 1121977 (if not paid earlier).
30.
Again during the pendency of this writ petition, the petitioner is given deemed date of promotion as Deputy Superintendent of Police w.e.f.
571982.
When he filed the petition, he was not promoted on this post.
He has retired on 2.9.1992 as is evident from the judgment in Civil Writ Petition No. 15205 of 1995.
Hence, the petitioner is also entitled to arrears of salary of the grade of Deputy Superintendent of Police from 571982 till his date of retirement.
31.
Resultantly, the writ is partly allowed.
Mandamus is issued to the respondents to release the arrears of pay and allowances admissible to the petitioner on the post of Inspector w.e.f.
2081975 and on the post of Deputy Superintendent of Police w.e.f.
571982 and re-fix his pay after giving him the benefit of increments due on promoted post (if not paid earlier).
The petitioner shall also be entitled to any further consequential benefits admissible to him as a result of promotion.
Petition partly allowed.
