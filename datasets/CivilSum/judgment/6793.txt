GS Singhvi, J - This petition is a typical example of cases where the employees first seek to subvert the administrative discipline and when they find that their efforts have proved futile, they approach the Court for the avowed purpose of protection of their legal and constitutional rights.
While working as Assistant Sub inspector of Police the petitioner earned adverse report for the following periods :-  (i) 1071983 to 29.10.1983 conveyed to him vide Annexure P1, dated 1761984.
(ii) 141984 to 3131985 conveyed to him vide Annexure P6 dated 2491985.
(iii) 141985 to 3131986 conveyed to him vide Annexure P9 dated 1041986.
Against the communication of adverse remarks vide Annexure P1, the petitioner made representation Annexure P2.
It was considered by the then Deputy Inspector General of Police, Hisar Range, Hisar as would appear from Annexure P3 dated January 30, 1986.
After a period of over three years of rejection of his representation, the petitioner filed a review application dated 521989.
At the same time, he made presentations dated 721989 and 921989 in respect of adverse remarks for the periods 141984 to 3131985 and 141985 to 3131986 respectively.
The review application and representations came to be accepted by the Deputy General of Police, Hisar Range, Hisar, as would appear from communications Annexures P5, P8 and P11.
The Deputy Inspector General of Police, Hisar Range, Hisar also approved the petitioner's name for promotion as Sub Inspector of Police on ad hoc basis under Rule 131 of the Punjab Police Rules and order to this effect was issued on 371990.
Subsequently on the basis of representation dated 731991 made by the petitioner, order Annexure P14 came to be issued giving deemed promotion to the petitioner as officiating Sub Inspector of Police w.e.f.
28.10.1983 i.e. eight years prior to the date of issue of the order Annexure P14.
2.
It appears that the petitioner's days of ascendancy in the service ended in the year 1992 because the Director General of Police, Haryana suo motu took cognizance of the patently illegal orders passed by the then Deputy Inspector General of Police, Hisar Range, Hisar expunging the adverse remarks made in the Annual Confidential Reports and he ordered that those adverse remarks should be reconstructed in the confidential reports of the petitioner.
This is borne out from Annexure P15 dated June 13, 1992 and Annexure P16 dated 1761992.
After issue of orders Annexures P15 and P16, the Deputy Inspector General of Police, Hisar issued notice Annexure P17 dated 1811993 for removal of petitioner's name from List 'E' and for his reversion to the post of Assistant Sub Inspector of Police.
Immediately thereafter the petitioner moved this Court and challenged the orders passed by the Director General of Police for reconstruction of the adverse remarks in his confidential reports.
A Division Bench of the Court allowed the writ petition on March 20, 1993 and quashed the orders passed by the Director General of Police with liberty to the respondents to pass appropriate order in accordance with law.
Thereafter, the Director General of Police issued notice Annexure P19 dated 1961995 and called upon the petitioner to show cause as to why orders dated 691990 and dated 6121990 passed by the Deputy Inspector General of Police may not be set aside and consequential service benefits given to him may not be withdrawn.
The petitioner made representation Annexure P20 and submitted that there was no justification to set aside the orders passed by Deputy Inspector General of Police.
After considering the reply filed by the petitioner, the Director General of Police, Haryana, issued the impugned order Annexure P21 dated 17111995 and set aside the orders passed by the Deputy Inspector General of Police, Hisar on 691990 and 6121990.
3.
The first and foremost argument advanced by Shri Malik, learned counsel for the petitioner, is that after a lapse of two years of the passing of the orders expunging the adverse remarks by the Deputy Inspector General of Police, it was not at all justified for the Director General of Police to order reconstruction of the adverse remarks in complete violation of the principles of natural justice and once the Court has quashed the orders passed by the Director General of Police, there can be no reason or justification to again reconstruct those adverse remarks.
Shri Malik argued that the adverse remarks made against the petitioner suffered from malice in law and fact and, therefore, even though the orders passed by the Deputy Inspector General of Police on 691990 and 6121990 may not be strictly in accordance with law, the Director General of Police was duty bound to consider the representation made by the petitioner against the adverse remarks and then decide the same on merits.
Learned counsel submitted that no period of limitation has been prescribed for filing representation against the adverse remarks and, therefore, the mere delay in the submission of representation by the petitioner cannot be made a ground to nullify the orders passed by the Deputy Inspector General of Police.
Lastly, the learned counsel argued that no notice was given by the Director General of Police in respect of order dated 691990 and, therefore, to that extent, the impugned order should be declared as contrary to the principles of natural justice.
4.
We have thoughtfully considered the submission of the learned counsel and have carefully examined the record of this case.
As per petitioner's own statement, he had made representation Annexure P2 immediately after receiving communication Annexure P1 regarding adverse remarks for the period from 1071983 to 29.10.1983 and this representation was rejected by the Deputy Inspector General of Police, Hisar Range, Hisar, on 2011986.
Thereafter there was a complete silence on the part of the petitioner for a period of over three years.
Against the adverse remarks for the years 1984-85 and 1985-86, the petitioner is not shown to have made any representation till 721989 and 921989.
Suddenness with which the petitioner became alive to the possibility of the expunction of the adverse remarks should have alarmed the then Deputy Inspector General of Police, Hisar Range, Hisar.
Instead of taking note of the silence of the petitioner for a period of three years after rejection of his previous representation against the adverse remarks for the period 1071983 to 29.10.1983 the officer concerned readily obliged the petitioner by expunging the adverse remarks without giving any reason for revising earlier decision dated 2011986.
For some unknown reasons, the same very officer felt impelled to oblige the petitioner by expunging the adverse remarks for the two subsequent years.
Complete absence of reasons in the communications Annexures P5, P8 and P11 can lead to only one inference, namely, that the then Deputy Inspector General of Police, Hisar Range, Hisar, had expunged the adverse remarks for extraneous reasons and with an ulterior motive to favour the petitioner.
The petitioner got undue benefit of these three communications as is evident from the fact that soon after expunging of adverse remarks the petitioner was given officiating promotion as Sub Inspector and then he got benefit of retrospective promotion.
In this manner the petitioner gained advantage of promotion over all those persons who had been promoted as Sub Inspectors between 28.10.1983 and 371990.
This showering of benefit on the petitioner could not have been possible but for the wholly arbitrary and unjustified orders passed by the then Deputy Inspector General of Police, Hisar Range, Hisar and but for the intervention of the Director General of Police, Haryana, the petitioner would have got away with this booty.
5.
Perusal of notice Annexure P19 and order Annexure P21 issued by the Director General of Police shows that the concerned authority has objectively applied its mind to the provisions contained in the administrative instructions regarding representation against the adverse remarks and the need for compliance of such instructions.
The Director General of Police has rightly made the following observations in the impugned order :-  "It is dangerous to allow officers to go on making representations whenever the situation is favourable to them and any ex post facto attempt to improve his personal file be resisted."
We are also of the opinion that the Director General of Police has rightly rejected the plea of the petitioner that guidelines issued by the Government are not binding.
It has rightly been observed that the guidelines issued by the Government by way of instructions are binding in the absence of statutory rules.
6.
Refusal of the Director General of Police to consider the representations of the petitioner on merits on the ground that they were made after lapse of a number of years is also justified.
During the period of over three years calculated with reference to the year of the communication of the adverse remarks, a number of officials must have been promoted as Sub Inspectors and it would be wholly inequitable to unsettle the rights after lapse of such a long time and that too on the representation of a person who, at one stage, succeeded in polluting the administrative set up by manipulation and mechanism of getting adverse remarks expunged even though in one case his representation stood rejected three years prior to the making of second representation and in other cases he did not even make such representation.
7.
The argument of Shri Malik regarding violation of the principles of natural justice also merits rejection.
No doubt notice Annexure P19 does not refer to the order dated 6.9.1996 but it is clear to us that the petitioner was fully conscious of the fact that the Director General of Police had earlier passed order Annexure P16 to reconstruct all the adverse remark and he has not suffered any prejudice due to absence of indication in the show cause notice about the proposal of respondent No. 2 to reconstruct adverse remarks for the person from 1071983 to 29.10.1983.
8.
There is an additional reason why this Court should not exercise jurisdiction under Article 226 of the Constitution of India for setting aside the impugned order.
Issue of a (sic) in favour of the petitioner would amount to protect the undue gain received by the petitioner at the hands of the Deputy Inspector General of Police and in our view, there is no reason for us to exercise equitable jurisdiction in a case like the present one.
9.
For the reasons mentioned above, we dismissed the writ petition.
However, dismissal of the writ petition will not preclude the petitioner from making representation to the competent authority against his reversion on the ground that persons junior to him have been promoted as Sub Inspector in the meanwhile.
