SS Nijjar, J. (Oral) - The petitioner joined service in the Punjab State Electricity Board as a Stenotypist on 2881976 in the pay scale of Rs. 110- 250 + Rs. 25/- as Special Pay.
Stenotypist and Lower Division Clerk (hereinafter referred to as "the LDC"), at that time, had a common cadre/seniority.
The seniority of the petitioner was fixed at Sr. No. 3461.
The pay scales were revised w.e.f.
1.1.78 to Rs. 400-660.
Thereafter again the pay scale was revised w.e.f.
1186 to Rs. 950-1800.
In the meantime, the petitioner was promoted as UDC after passing the Departmental Accounts Examination on 5786 in the pay scale of Rs. 510-800 revised to Rs. 1200-2100 w.e.f.
1186.
The pay of the petitioner was accordingly fixed in the aforesaid scale w.e.f.
2571986.
The pay scale of Stenotypist/LDC was revised from Rs. 400-660 to Rs. 950-1800 w.e.f.
1186.
The pay scale of the petitioner, after promotion as UDC was more than the scale of the LDC On 3101990, the P.S.E.B.
revised by scale of LDC retrospectively w.e.f.
1186 by Circular (Annexure P-1) as under :-  40% of total No. of LDC Rs. 1500-2640  40% of total No. of LDC Rs. 1200-2100 20% remaining LDC Rs. 950-1800 2.
By order dated 7101992 (Annexure P-2), LDCs at Seniority Nos. 3121 to 3651 were given the pay scale of Rs. 1500-2640 w.e.f.
111988.
The name of the petitioner figured at Sr. No. 270 of the list.
However, in the aforesaid order, it was mentioned that the LDCs who had been performing the duties of UDCs, would not be released the pay scale.
As a UDC, the petitioner was put in the pay scale of Rs. 1200-2100.
On subsequent promotion, LDCs who were otherwise junior to the petitioner, were given the pay scale of Rs. 1500-2640.
The petitioner made a representation seeking the rectification of the anomaly, by pointing out that LDCs who had subsequently passed the examination or had even failed to pass the examination, had been given a higher pay scale.
On 15121995, the PSEB issued another list (Annexure P-3) by which LDCs at Sr.Nos.
3502 to 3541 were granted pay scale of Rs. 1500-2640 w.e.f.
1187.
On 27111991 and on 341992 Circulars (Annexures P-4 and P-5) were issued granting the scale of Rs. 1640-2925 to LDCs on completion of 16 years of service.
The petitioner was even denied this payscale on the same ground that he had been promoted in the pay scale of Rs. 1200-2100 as UDC.
In 1993, the Union of the petitioner espoused his case.
However, on 1871994 the respondents-Board issued Circular (Annexure P-6) to the effect that LDCs promoted as UDCs after 111986 cannot be given opportunity to exercise the option for fixation of their salary by treating the post of LDC as induction post.
It was also stated that anomalies in particular cases will be considered by the authorities by relaxing the rules on representation being received within four months.
The petitioner submitted representation (Annexure P-7) on 15111994 highlighting the grievance as noticed earlier.
On 2451999, the petitioner through the Union served notice (Annexure P-8) for grant of pay scale of Rs. 1500-2640 w.e.f.
1187 i.e. from the date the persons junior to him had been granted this pay scale and Rs. 1640-2925 w.e.f.
28.8.1992 on completion of 16 years of service.
On 30121999, the State Government referred the dispute for adjudication under section (10)(d) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 (hereinafter referred to as "the Act") to the Industrial Tribunal, Punjab, Chandigarh.
After the pleadings were completed, the Industrial Tribunal on 2942002 disposed of the reference by award (Annexure P-11) with a direction to the Management of PSEB to decide the representation dated 15111994 (Annexure P-7) and the notice dated 2451999 (Annexure P-8).
On 30112002, the respondents-Board passed the order (Annexure P-12) holding that the petitioner is not entitled to the pay scale having been promoted after 1186 as UDC.
3.
The respondents-Board have filed the written statement.
It is stated that the petitioner himself opted not to accept the time bound pay scale and instead he decided to accept the pay scale of UDC with effect from his promotion as such.
The UDCs who preferred to accept the time bound LDC pay scale and opted to continue in the same were allowed to continue in the LDC pay scale.
The petitioner cannot now come back and say that he should be given the same benefit of both the pay scales.
The petitioner having exercised the option cannot now make any grouse of it.
4.
Mr. Mahajan has pointed out to the replication filed by the petitioner and submitted that the petitioner had been promoted as UDC after qualifying the Departmental Accounts Examination w.e.f.
571983.
It was subsequent to the promotion of the petitioner as UDC that the Board revised the pay scales in the year 1988 with retrospective effect from 111986.
Therefore, the respondents cannot possibly plead that the petitioner has either waived his legal right or that he is estopped from claiming the pay in the same scale which is being given to his juniors.
5.
We have considered the submissions of the learned counsel for the parties and perused the paper-book.
6.
Mr. Khera has vehemently argued that the respondents-Board are justified in not giving the revised pay scale to the petitioner as he had already taken advantage of the higher pay scale of Rs. 1200-2200.
We are wholly unimpressed with the submissions made by the learned Sr.
Counsel.
It is settled proposition of law that in the matter of pay, the seniors cannot be made to accept a lower payscale.
Furthermore, the question of waiver or estoppel does not even arise in the facts and circumstances of the present case.
As noticed earlier, the pay was revised subsequent to the petitioner having been promoted as UDC upon successful completion of departmental examination.
The petitioner cannot be granted a pay scale lesser than his juniors who had not even qualified in the departmental examination.
Since the reference had been made to the Labour Court, it would perhaps entail a remand of the case back to the Labour Court as no decision has been given by the Labour Court on merits.
But we are of the considered opinion that this would be an exercise in futility.
No evidence has been examined by the Labour Court.
It is not disputed that the persons who are juniors to the petitioner are enjoying a higher pay scale.
The reasoning adopted by the respondents-Board is wholly arbitrary, and therefore, the same is violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India.
7.
In view of the above, the writ petition is allowed.
The impugned order 30102002 (Annexure P-12) is hereby quashed.
The respondents-Board are directed to grant to the petitioner the pay scale of Rs. 1500-2640 w.e.f.
111987 and Rs. 1640-2925 w.e.f.
28.8.1992.
The respondents are directed to pay the consequential benefits to the petitioner within a period of three months of the receipt of a certified copy of this order.
No costs.
Petition allowed.
