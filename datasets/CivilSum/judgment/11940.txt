Jawahar Lal Gupta, J. (Oral) - The petitioner a teacher in the Education Department is aggrieved by the order of August 5, 1991 by which his request for retirement on completing 20 years of service has been declined.
A few facts may be noticed.
2.
The petitioner claims that he joined service as Science Master on September 24, 1963 on ad hoc basis.
He was appointed on regular basis with effect from October 12, 1964.
On July 16, 1987 he served a three months' notice seeking voluntary retirement on the Director of Public Instruction (Schools) Punjab, respondent No. 2.
His request having not been accepted, the petitioner filed C.W.P.
No. 6800 of 1991.
Vide order dated May 7, 1991, the Court directed the respondents to decide the representation dated July 16, 1987 followed by reminders dated October 16, 1987 and March 15, 1989 within three months by passing a speaking order.
It is in pursuance to the directions of this Court that respondent No. 2 has rejected the petitioner's claim vide order dated August 5, 1991.
A copy of this order has been produced as Annexure P-5 with the writ petition.
A perusal of this order shows that the petitioner's request has been declined on the following there grounds :-  (i) The petitioner has not served the three month notice while in service after completion of 20 years service before seeking voluntary retirement.
(ii) The petitioner was not eligible to be granted voluntary retirement under the instructions because a case of moral turpitude was going on against him in the Court of Judicial Magistrate, Rajpura in the year 1974; and (iii) The petitioner does not fulfil the condition of 20 years' qualifying service.
3.
The correctness of the three grounds has been challenged by the petitioner in this petition.
He has produced the notice dated July 16, 1987 as Annexure P1 to the writ petition to show that the provisions of the rules had been duly complied with.
It has been further pointed out that the petitioner had remained under suspension on account of the pendency of a criminal case from February 1, 1974 to March 5, 1979.
After the decision of the case, the petitioner was reinstated.
It was, however, directed that he will not be paid anything beyond the subsistence allowance already disbursed to him.
The legality of this order was challenged by the petitioner by filing a civil suit.
The suit was decreed by the court on May 5, 1989.
As a result, the petitioner was to be paid full salary.
Respondent No. 2 had issued an order dated October 20, 1989 directing the payment of his salary.
It has been further pointed out that the petitioner having joined service in the year 1963, had completed almost 24 years of service in July, 1987 when he had served the requisite notice.
It has been further stated that the petitioner had been granted the two proficiency step-ups in the scale of Rs. 2000-3500 with effect from 111986 on the premises that he had completed 18 years of service on the appointed day.
Still further, it has also been pointed out that the petitioner had been permitted to cross the efficiency bar in the scale of Rs. 620-1200 at the stage of Rs. 700/- with effect from 111981.
On these premises, it is maintained that the impugned order is wholly illegal and arbitrary.
4.
No written statement has been filed.
5.
I have heard Mr. Gopal Mahajan for the petitioner and Mr. Arihant Jain for the respondents.
6.
The provision for premature retirement is contained in the rules called the Punjab Civil Services (Premature Retirement) Rules, 1975.
By an amendment on November 29, 1979 it was provided that an employee can seek premature retirement after completing 20 years of qualifying service by giving notice, for not less than 3 months in writing to the appropriate authority.
The rule does not prescribe a particular form of notice.
It was in accordance with this provision that the petitioner had given a notice of three months vide letter date July 16, 1987.
In this letter, it had been specially stated that "I hereby give three months notice to seek voluntary retirement."
Thereafter, the petitioner had written yet another letter dated October 16, 1987 pointing out that he had completed 23 years of service on October 11, 1987.
Consequently, he gave notice "to seek voluntary retirement with immediate effect".
The observation in the order that the petitioner "did not give three months notice dated 1671987 while in service as claimed" does not appear to be well founded.
The letter as mentioned above clearly disclosed the petitioner's intention to seek voluntary retirement by giving three months notice.
This was followed by the letter dated October 16, 1987 in which the petitioner expressed his intention to retire with immediate effect.
In such a situation, it cannot be said that the petitioner had not complied with the provision of the rule or that he had ailed to serve the requisite notice on the respondents.
It is also not the case of the respondents that the petitioner was either under suspension or even facing any departmental action in any proceeding.
Accordingly, I am unable to sustain the first ground on which the request of the petitioner was declined.
7.
The order then observes that the petitioner was not eligible for the grant of voluntary retirement under sub-clause (b)(vii) of the instructions, because moral turpitude was going on against the petitioner in the Court of Judicial Magistrate, Rajpura, in the year 1974.
It has also not been disputed before me that in the year 1987 when the petitioner sought premature retirement, no case was pending against him.
The proceedings in the case had culminated in the year 1979 and the very premises for observing that a case of moral turpitude was going on against the petitioner is unfounded.
Equally irrelevant is the observation that the petitioner did not join his new post of appointment and was thus absent from duty with effect from October 8, 1987.
No proceedings have been initiated against the petitioner on that count till today.
At least none have been pointed out by the learned counsel for the respondents at the hearing.
Accordingly, even the second ground for the rejection of petitioner's request cannot be sustained.
8.
It is then stated that the petitioner had not completed the qualifying period of 20 years service.
This also does not appear to be tenable.
Admittedly, the petitioner had been appointed as a Science Master on September 24, 1963.
His services were regularised on October 12, 1964.
In July, 1987, the petitioner had completed more than 23 years of service.
Learned counsel for the respondents has not been able to show that the petitioner had not completed the requisite period of service before making the request for premature retirement.
It is further clear that the petitioner's claim for the payment of salary for the period of suspension from February 1, 1974 to March 5, 1979 was decreed by the civil Court in Case No. 163 vide its judgment dated May 5, 1989.
A photocopy of the judgment has been produced by Mr Gopal Mahajan and is placed on record as Mark 'A'.
The petitioner having been paid full salary for the period of suspension, it is not understood as to on what basis the respondent has come to hold that the petitioner has not completed the requisite period of 20 years of service for seeking voluntary retirement.
9.
Accordingly, I find that the order dated August 5, 1991 (Annexure P-5) suffers from patent mistake of fact and law.
It cannot, as such, be sustained.
It is accordingly quashed.
The petitioner shall accordingly stand retired from service with effect from October 31, 1987.
He will be entitled to the grant of all pensionary benefits in accordance with the rules.
The needful shall be done within three months from the date of receipt of a copy of this order.
The petitioner shall also be entitled to his costs which are assessed at Rs. 2000/-.
Order accordingly.
