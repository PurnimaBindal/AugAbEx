GC Garg, J - Respondents were initially appointed as Linemen on work charge basis in the Bhakra Beas Management Board (for short "BBMB"), in the pay scale of Rs. 400-600, which was revised and raised to Rs. 950-1800 w.e.f.
111986.
Regular employees who were allegedly performing the same and similar duties as the respondents were, however, placed in the pay scale of Rs. 1200-2100.
Aggrieved by this disparity in the pay scale, the respondents first approached the Board for redressal of their grievance.
Their request having not been accepted, they filed a writ petition in this Court under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India.
Stand of the appellant-Board in the written statement was that the respondents were appointed/promoted as Work-charge Linemen during the years 1979 to 1985 in the pay scale of Rs. 400-660 as applicable to the work charge employees.
Writ petitioners on absorption on regular basis ceased to be work charge employees and have been placed in the pay scale of Rs. 1200-2100 w.e.f.
111989 i.e. the grade payable to the regular employees.
They are thus estopped from making any grievance in that behalf.
It was stated that work charge employees are governed by the Certified Standing Orders whereas the regular employees are governed by the Punjab Civil Service Rules.
The age of retirement of Class-III employees is 58 years whereas work-charge employees continue till the age of 60 years.
It was further stated by reference to a policy decision, a copy of which is also annexed with the written statement, that the Board has decided "to adopt the Punjab Govt. Irrigation Department Pay Scales for work-charge employees and also Pay Scales meant for regular employees in its Irrigation (Civil Wing) and Punjab State Electricity Board's pay scales for work-charge and regular employees in the Power (Electrical) Wing of the respondent-Board."
On merits it was pointed out that there is a qualitative difference in the degree of reliability and responsibility between the regular and work charge employees who are governed by different sets of service rules.
2.
Learned Single Judge on a consideration of the matter, allowed the writ petition by order dated 2011993 and directed the appellants herein to place the respondents in the pay scale of Rs. 1200-2100 w.e.f.
111986 instead of 111989.
It was also ordered that their pay shall be revised and consequential benefits granted to them.
Hence this appeal at the instance of BBMB challenging the order of the learned Single Judge.
3.
Learned counsel for the appellants submitted that the work charge and regular employees are governed by altogether different conditions of service with different scales of pay since the very inception and the degree of responsibility and duties of work charge and regular employees are also different because of the nature of their appointment and conditions of service.
Learned counsel further submitted that the learned Single Judge completely ignored the law laid down by the Apex Court in Federation of All India Customs and Central Excise Stenographers v. Union of India, AIR 1988 SC 1291 which provides that the nature of work done cannot be judged by mere volume of work as there may be qualitative difference as regards reliability and responsibility.
It was also submitted that the learned Single Judge granted relief by placing reliance on the letter of the Punjab State Electricity Board, a copy of which was annexed with the written statement as Annexure R-1/1, which could not be done.
Learned counsel for the respondents, on the other hand, submitted that where exactly the same and similar duties are performed by the employees, though belonging to two different categories, they are entitled to be treated equal in the matter of pay scales.
Learned counsel placed reliance on a judgment of the Supreme Court in Shri Alvaro Noronha Ferriera and another v. Union of India and others, JT 1999(3) SC 224 : 1999(2) SCT 685 (SC) and State of Haryana and another v. Ram Chander and another, 1997(4) S.L.R.
1.
4.
After hearing learned counsel for the parties and perusing the record, we are of the opinion that this appeal deserves to succeed.
The sole question that falls for consideration before us is whether the respondents, who were work charge employees during the period 111986 to 31121988 are entitled to the pay scale of Rs. 1200-2100 for the period 111986 to 31121988, which was granted to the regular employees of the Board.
5.
In the first instance, we deem it appropriate to notice that objections regarding estoppel, delay and laches and concealment of material facts raised by the BBMB before the learned Single Judge did not find favour with the Court and we are not going into these objections as they were not seriously pressed at the time of hearing.
6.
Before we proceed to go into the merits of the controversy, it would be appropriate to notice the relevant portion of the judgment of the learned Single Judge to highlight as to how and under what circumstances, the relief was granted to the respondents, which reads thus:  "Coming to the merits of the controversy, it appears that the petitioners are working as linemen.
They were initially placed in the pay scale of Rs. 400-600 which has since been revised and raised to Rs. 950-1800 with effect from January 1, 1986.
It is also correct that they have been placed in the scale of Rs. 1200-2100 with effect from January 1, 1989.
It is also the admitted position that persons appointed as linemen on regular basis were placed in the scale of Rs. 1200-2100 with effect from January 1, 1986.
Was there any basis for treating the members of the work-charged establishment differently from those who were in the regular cadre ?
The petitioners have categorically averred that they are performing exactly the same and similar duties as are being performed by others who were placed in the scale of Rs. 1200-2100.
The respondents have made a vague averment that there is a qualitative difference in the degree of reliability and responsibility.
It has not been shown that there is any difference in the matter of qualification, the duties or the responsibility between the members of the work-charged establishment and those working on regular basis.
No date has been furnished to show a valid basis of any classification.
Further the fact that years after their recruitment from November 15, 1979 to December 31, 1985, the petitioners have been placed in the regular establishment with effect from January 1, 1989 shows that they possessed the qualifications required for the post of linemen and were thus eligible and suitable for appointment as linemen in the regular cadre.
It has been stated that the respondent-Board is following the decisions of the Punjab State Electricity Board.
A reference to the order dated April 26, 1990 passed by the Punjab State Electricity Board, a copy of which has been produced as Annexure R-1/1 with the written statement, shows that even the Board had held that "the employees borne on work-charged establishment would be entitled to the revised scales of pay applicable to the regular employees".
In such a situation, I am unable to discover any valid basis for the impugned action of the respondents."
7.
From the above, it would be seen that the relief had been granted to the writ petitioners primarily after coming to the conclusion that the work charge and regular employees though belong to two different categories, but there could not be any distinction between the two sets of these employees in the matter of pay scale.
Further, the learned Single Judge had relied upon the policy decision, Annexure R-1/1 for granting the relief prayed for by the respondents.
8.
As noticed above, the respondents were working as Linemen and were initially placed in the pay scale of Rs. 400-600 which was revised and raised to Rs. 950-1800 w.e.f.
111986, on the same pattern as available to their counterparts in the Punjab State Electricity Board.
The respondents were brought on the regular cadre w.e.f.
111989 and from the said date they were placed in the pay scale of Rs. 1200-2100 admissible to linemen borne on regular establishment, on the pattern of Punjab State Electricity Board adopted by the BBMB.
Linemen on the regular establishment prior to 111986 were in the pay scale of Rs. 450-800 as against the pay scale of Rs. 400-600 of the work-charged establishment.
The pay scale of the regular employees was revised to Rs. 1200-2100 w.e.f.
111986 whereas that of the work charged-establishment was revised to Rs. 950-1800.
It was only w.e.f.
111989 that the writ petitioners were brought on the regular establishment and granted the same grade i.e.1200-2100 w.e.f.
111989.
Learned Single Judge, however, by the order under appeal, granted the pay scale of Rs. 1200-2100 to the respondents for the period 111986 to 31.12.1989 also on the ground that there cannot be disparity in the matter of pay scale as the work charge and regular employees were performing exactly the same duties.
This conclusion, in our opinion, cannot be sustained.
Work charge employees cannot and perhaps can never be equated with those who are on regular establishment at least for the purpose of granting pay scales, for they form a quite different and distinct class and they are employed for a specific purpose and time.
They are even paid under different heads.
Regular establishment is of permanent nature whereas the work charge employees continue depending upon the quantum of work and the funds for executing it.
For complete answer to this question, the observations of the Supreme Court in State of Rajasthan v. Kunji Raman, AIR 1997 SC 693 : 1997(1) SCT 497 (SC) can be read with advantage, which read thus :-  "A work-charged establishment as pointed out by this Court in Jaswant Singh v. Union of India 1979(4) SCC.
440 : AIR 1980 SC 115, broadly means an establishment of which the expenses, including the wages and allowances of staff, are chargeable to "work".
The pay and allowances of employees who are borne on a work-charged establishment are generally shown as a separate sub-head of the estimated cost of the works.
The work-charged employees are engaged on a temporary basis and their appointments are made for the execution of a specified work.
From the very nature of their employment, their services automatically come to an end on the completion of the works for the sole purpose of which they are employed.
Thus a work-charged establishment is materially and qualitatively different from a regular establishment."
9.
A little later, it was further observed as under :-  " A work-charged establishment thus differs from a regular establishment which is permanent in nature.
Setting up and continuance of a work-charged establishment is dependent upon the Government undertaking a project or a scheme or a work and availability of funds for executing it.
So far as the employees engaged on work-charged establishments are concerned, not only their recruitment and service conditions but the nature of work and duties to be performed by them are not the same as those of the employees of the regular establishment.
A regular establishment and a work-charged establishment and the persons employed on those establishments thus form two separate and distinct classes.
For that reason, if a separate set of rules are framed for the persons engaged on the work-charged establishment and the general rules applicable to persons working on the regular establishment are not made applicable to them, it cannot be said that they are treated in an arbitrary and discriminatory manner by the government.
It is well-settled that the Government has the power to frame different rules for different classes of employees."
10.
From the reading of the above observations of the Apex Court it is quite clear that the work-charged employees and those who are on the regular establishment are two separate and distinct categories in almost all respects, may it be the degree of responsibility, conditions of service or even the question of granting the pay scales.
Learned Single Judge, with utmost respect to his Lordship, was, in our opinion, not correct in coming to the conclusion that since the work-charged employees are performing the same duties as are being performed by the regular employees, they are to be treated at par and are thus entitled to the same pay scale as applicable to the regular employees.
11.
Above apart, it is the admitted case of the parties that the BBMB adopted the policy decision framed by the Punjab State Electricity Board in the matter of granting pay scales to its work-charged employees.
A copy of that policy decision has been placed on record as Annexure R-1/1.
Reliance was also placed on this letter for granting the relief.
Relevant portion of Annexure R-1/1 reads thus :-  "The Punjab State Electricity Board is pleased to decide as under :- (i) The employees borne on work-charged Establishment would be entitled to the revised scales of Pay applicable to the regular employees, where corresponding posts/scales of pay on regular Establishment exist as per provisions contained in concluding para of Ist Schedule attached to Office Order No. 129/PRC/Fin-1988 dated 11111998.
(ii) The categories of work-charged employees who are not drawing the identical pay scales available to the similar regular categories will be allowed revised replacement scales corresponding to their pre-revised scales as under :- Sr.No.
Existing Scales  Corresponding replacement Scales xxx  xxx  xxx  4(i)  400-600)400-600) 950-1800 7(i) 450-800 1200-2200 xxx  xxx  xxx  12.
It has been specifically provided in the above policy as adopted by the BBMB that the employees borne on work-charged Establishment would be entitled to the revised scales of pay applicable to the regular employees, where corresponding posts/scales of pay on regular establishment exist as per provisions contained in the Ist schedule.
The regular employees were in the pay scale of Rs. 450-800 and they were given the corresponding pay scale of Rs. 1200-2100 w.e.f.
111986 whereas the work charge employees including the respondents were in the pay scale of 400-600 and they were granted the pay scale of Rs. 950-1800 corresponding to the scale of Rs. 400-600, w.e.f.
111986.
The respondents were brought on the regular establishment w.e.f.
111989 and they were also granted the pay scale of Rs. 1200-2100 w.e.f.
111989.
The respondents thus could not be granted the pay scale of Rs. 1200-2100 during the period 111986 to 31121988, as they were not on the regular establishment of the BBMB and were working only on work-charged basis.
The case of the respondents is thus fully covered by clause (ii) and not clause (i) of the policy as noticed above and it cannot be said that they were discriminated in the matter of grant of pay scale or they were treated with disparity in that behalf.
The conclusion of the learned Single Judge in that behalf thus deserves to be set aside.
The service conditions are different and even the age of retirement is also different.
13.
No support can be had by the respondents from the judgment of the Supreme Court in Ram Chander's case (supra) and Shri Alvare Noronha Ferriera's case (supra) as these have no application to the facts and circumstances of the present case.
The former was a case where there was a difference of academic qualifications for appointment of the two categories of employees, but the nature of job, reliability and degree of responsibility were the same for the two categories as was in the case of one category.
In the latter case, the parties were holding the same posts but were posted at different places and were being paid different pay scales.
It was in that backdrop held that they were entitled to one and the same pay scales.
This is not the position in the present case.
14.
For what has been stated above, this appeal is allowed and the writ petition is dismissed.
No costs.
Appeal dismissed.
