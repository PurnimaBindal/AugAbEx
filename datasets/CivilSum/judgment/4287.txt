NK Sodhi, J - This petition under Article 226 of the Constitution has been filed by one Shri Mukhtiar Singh, retired Assistant Sub Inspector of Police, Haryana, challenging the order dated February 23, 1987 whereby he has been compulsorily retired from service under Rule 9.18(2) of the Punjab Police Rules, 1934 (for short 'the Rules').
2.
Brief facts giving rise to this petition which are not in dispute may first be noticed.
Petitioner was enrolled as a Constable in the Police force on 22.4.1958.
He was thereafter promoted as a Head Constable and confirmed as such on February 20, 1975.
He was further promoted as Assistant Sub Inspector on March 12, 1979 and thereafter confirmed on this post on January 31, 1983.
He was, however, not allowed to cross the efficiency bar three times, firstly w.e.f.
April 1, 1983 then w.e.f.
April 1, 1984 and lastly on April 1, 1985.
In the year 1984, the petitioner received a show-cause notice from the Director General of Police and Inspector General of Police, Haryana requiring him to show-cause as to why he be not compulsorily retired from service.
Alongwith the show-cause notice he was also sent a resume of the entries recorded in his character roll and also the confidential reports recorded in his personal file.
A detailed reply to the show-cause notice was sent by the petitioner which was not considered satisfactory by the competent authority and, thus, the impugned order of compulsory retirement was passed on February 23, 1987.
His date of birth is 15.4.1938 and in the normal course he would have superannuated at the age of 58 years on April 15, 1996.
The service career of the petitioner has, thus, been cut-short by a period of more than 9 years.
It is this order which has been challenged in the present writ petition.
3.
On notice issued to the respondents, they have put in appearance through Assistant Advocate General, Haryana and have also filed a detailed written statement.
The order of compulsory retirement is sought to be justified on the basis of the past service record of the petitioner.
It is averred that the service record of the petitioner has been rather poor and his honesty had been doubted quite often by the reporting Officers and that the petitioner was invariably described as a very mediocre official.
4.
Shri RK Malik, learned counsel for the writ petitioner, while challenging the impugned order forcefully contended that since the petitioner had been promoted as an Assistant Sub Inspector in March, 1979 and was later confirmed on the same post in January, 1983, his earlier past record should be completely ignored for the purpose of considering his compulsory retirement.
He urged that under Rule 131 of the Rules all promotions in the police force from one rank to another and from one grade to another in the same rank are made by selection tampered by seniority, efficiency and honesty being the main factors governing the selection.
The argument is that the entire adverse record prior to the date of his promotion has to be ignored and could not be taken notice of by the competent authority while prematurely retiring him.
Reliance in this regard was placed on the judgment of the Apex Court in Baikuntha Nath v. Chief Medical Officer, AIR 1992 SC 1020 : 1992(2) SCT 92 (SC) and particularly on sub-para 4 of para 32 of the judgment and also on Baidyanath Mahapatra v. State of Orissa, 1989(4) SLR 220.
He also sought to draw support from a recent Full Bench judgment of this Court in State of Punjab v. Kulwantbir Singh, 1993(2) SCT 567 (FB) : 1993(2) RSJ 307.
I find no merit in this submission.

5.
It will be seen that the honesty of the petitioner was doubted in 1971 and again for the period 1974-75 the remarks were "Bad reputation for honesty" and for the period 1.4.75 to 30975 the reporting officer recorded 'Is positively corrupt'.
Then again for the period 1.4.1984 to 3181984 which is subsequent to his promotion as Assistant Sub Inspector remarks of the reporting Officer are 'honesty doubtful' although similar remarks for the period 2151984 to 911985 were expunged.
All through the petitioner has been described as a mediocre official.
Overall picture that emerges from the aforementioned record is that the petitioner even after his promotion in the year 1979 did not make any improvement much less significant improvement in his performance and his honesty was again doubted by the reporting Officer for the period 1.4.1984 to 3181984.
I cannot agree with Shri RK Malik that merely because the petitioner was promoted in the year 1979 which promotion was based on merit and selection his entire adverse record in the past stands was held out and the same could not be taken note of by the competent authority while considering his case for pre-mature retirement.
In Baikuntha Nath's case (supra) their Lordships of the Supreme Court while considering the question whether it was permissible to the Government to order compulsory retirement of a Government servant on the basis of material which included uncommunicated adverse remarks and after considering the entire case law on the subject laid down the following principles :-  "(1) An order of compulsory retirement is not a punishment.
It implies no stigma nor any suggestion of misbehaviour.
(ii) The order has to be passed by the Government on forming the opinion that it is in the public interest to retire a government servant compulsorily.
The order is passed on the subjective satisfaction of the Government.
(iii) Principles of natural justice have no place in the context of an order of compulsory retirement.
This does not mean that judicial scrutiny is excluded altogether.
While the High Court or this court would not examine the matter as an appellate Court, they may interfere if they are satisfied that the order is passed (a) mala fide, or (b) that it is based on no evidence, or (c) that it is arbitrary in the sense that no reasonable person would form the requisite opinion on the given material in short; if it is found to be a perverse order.
(iv) The Government (or the Review Committee, as the case may be) shall have to consider the entire record of service before taking a decision in the matter of course attaching more importance to record of any performance during the later years.
The record to be so considered would naturally include the entries in the confidential records/character rolls, both favourable and adverse.
If a government servant is promoted to a higher post notwithstanding the adverse remarks, such remarks lose their sting, more so, if the promotion is based upon merit (selection) and not upon seniority.
(v) An order of compulsory retirement is not liable to be quashed by a Court merely on the showing that while passing it uncommunicated adverse remarks were also taken into consideration.
That circumstance by itself cannot be a basis for interference."
It is clearly laid down that when a Government servant is promoted to a higher post in spite of the adverse remarks, such remarks lose their sting but nevertheless while considering the case for compulsory retirement the entire record of service attaching more importance to the remarks of the reporting officer during the later years has to be considered and that this court while examining an order of compulsory retirement is not required to sit as an appellate court and can interfere only on the limited grounds mentioned in (iii) quoted above.
Baidyanath Mahapatra's case (supra) was also considered in Baikuntha Nath's case and not followed.
The Full Bench judgment of this Court also while following the Apex Court's judgment in Baikuntha Nath's case laid down similar principles.
6.
It is true that adverse remarks of the petitioner prior to the date of his promotion in the year 1979 have lost their sting but to determine as to how much sting is taken away will depend upon the facts and circumstances of each case.
In the instant case, one cannot lose sight of the fact that adverse entries about the petitioner relating to his integrity and honesty were being repeated time and again and even after his promotion he was considered to be of doubtful honesty.
In such a situation, it cannot be said that the impugned order of the competent authority based as it is on an overall picture of the adverse entries suffers from any infirmity so as to warrant any interference by this Court in the exercise of its extraordinary jurisdiction under Article 226 of the Constitution  7.
In the result, the petition is dismissed with no order as to costs.
Petition dismissed.
