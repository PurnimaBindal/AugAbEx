Swatanter Kumar, J (Oral) - Learned counsel for the petitioners submits that the services of respondent No. 1 have already been regularised, as such, this petition is not pressed on his behalf.
Ordered accordingly.
2.
In regard to petitioner No. 2, it is stated that regularisation of his services were declined inter-alia mainly on the two grounds firstly, he was not matriculate and secondly he was overage.
3.
Both these grounds are stated to be factually incorrect.
The petitioner No. 2 claims to be matriculate.
Copy of the matriculate certificate is appended with the writ petition as Annexure P/7.
With regard to his age, the date of birth of the petitioner is stated to be 4.4.1967.
This date of birth is duly confirmed in Annexure P/7, the certificate issued by the Central Board of Secondary Education.
4.
Having failed to make any headway, learned counsel for the State contended that there was no post available as such, the petitioner could not be regularised.
5.
Once a policy is formulated by the State, then it is under obligation to regularise the services of the employees, who satisfies the ingredients provided therein.
In the said policy, it is nowhere stated that there should be regular post available for regularisation of the services.
Even if there is any such stipulation, the same is not sustainable in terms of the judgment of the Hon'ble Supreme Court in the case Dhirendra Chamoli and another v. State of UP, 1986(1) L.L.J.
134, wherein it is clearly held that non- availability of post is no ground for rejecting regularisation of the employees.
If they are otherwise eligible.
6.
For the reasons afore-stated we allow this writ petition qua petitioner No. 2 with mandamus directing the respondent to consider the case of the petitioner for regularisation of his services in terms of its policy within a period of three months from today.
7.
No costs.
Petition allowed.
