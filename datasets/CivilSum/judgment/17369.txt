THB Chalapathi, J - This appeal is directed against the decree and judgment of the learned Additional District Judge, Faridkot in Civil Appeal No. 25 of 1988, dated 11.8.1990.
2.
The defendants are the appellants.
3.
The respondent-plaintiff filed the suit for a declaration and mandatory injunction stating that he is a permanent employee of the defendants and is entitled to his pay and allowances and increments with effect from 31101977 and the acceptance of his resignation by the second defendant in his letter No. ES(1) Pb.
85/9192 dated 14111985, is illegal, void and arbitrary and to direct the defendants to pay his salary and allowances.
4.
The plaintiff was appointed as a Pharmacist on 941956 and was also confirmed.
He was transferred from Civil Hospital, Gidderbaha to Rural Dispensary, Makhu, on 16111976, but the plaintiff did not joint his services on transfer but he applied for earned leave from 20.11.1976 to 14.2.1977 and joined duty as Pharmacist on 15.2.1977.
According to the plaintiff, due to domestic circumstances and ailment of his old parents, he submitted his resignation on 29101977, but he did not receive any communication either accepting or rejecting his resignation.
Therefore, he withdrew his resignation on 1971985.
It is only after this, he received communication on 14111985 from the second defendant who accepted his resignation.
The plaintiff, therefore, filed the suit stating that the acceptance of his resignation is illegal.
5.
In the written statement, the defendants contended that the plaintiff is estopped by his own conduct in filing the suit and that the suit is also not maintainable for want of notice.
It is submitted that the plaintiff was appointed as Pharmacist on 941956 (forenoon) and that the services of the plaintiff were provincialised with effect from 1111970 and that the plaintiff was transferred from Gidderbaha to Rural Dispensary, Makhu, on 16111976 but the plaintiff absented himself from duty from 17.11.1976 to 14.2.1977 and from 8.3.1977 to 23.10.1977 and he submitted resignation on 29101977 effective from 31101977 and that a formal acceptance of his resignation was passed on 14111985.
It is also admitted that the plaintiff made a request for withdrawal of his resignation 1971985 but the request of the plaintiff for withdrawal of the resignation was rejected as it became effective from 31101977 and, therefore, the plaintiff is not entitled to any relief.
6.
On the basis of the pleadings, the learned trial Judge framed appropriate issues and on consideration of the evidence on record, decreed the suit.
Aggrieved by the same, an appeal was filed by the defendants unsuccessfully.
Hence this second appeal.
7.
The facts are not disputed.
When the plaintiff was transferred from Civil Hospital, Gidderbaha, to Rural Dispensary, Makhu, he went on leave and remained absent for about one year.
Thereafter he submitted his resignation on 29101977, effective from 31101977.
When the plaintiff himself in his resignation letter clearly stated that it comes into effect with effect from a particulars day, he cannot he allowed to withdraw the same subsequently.
The petitioner waited for 8 years to withdraw his resignation.
When the plaintiff himself stipulated that the resignation would take effect from a future date, he was at liberty to withdraw the same before the expiry of the said date but when once it becomes operative, he has no right to withdraw the resignation after expiry of the date mentioned in the letter of resignation, irrespective of the fact whether it is accepted or not by the Government.
In such circumstances, the plaintiff had no right to withdraw the resignation subsequently, though the acceptance of the same was not communicated to him.
From 1977 till 1985, the plaintiff kept quiet and did not report for duty.
When the plaintiff himself was absent of his own and did not withdraw his letter of resignation though became effective from 31101977, his silence clearly amounts to abandonment.
Therefore, the plaintiff has no right, in my opinion, to claim that he withdrew his resignation before acceptance and, therefore, he should be deemed to have been in service.
8.
It has been held by the Apex Court in Nand Keshwar Prasad v. Indian Farmers Fertilisers Co-op.
Ltd., 1998(5) SCC 461 : 1999(1) S.C.T.
276, that the retirement mentioned in the letter of resignation must take effect from the date mentioned therein.
On the facts of this case, I am of the opinion that both the Courts erred in decreeing the suit of the plaintiff.
The appeal is accordingly allowed and the decrees and judgments of the Courts below are hereby set aside and the suit filed by the plaintiff-respondent is hereby dismissed.
Appeal allowed.
