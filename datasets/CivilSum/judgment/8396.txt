THB Chalapathi, J - The petitioners filed this writ petition for issuance of a writ of mandamus directing the respondents to regularise his services as per the decision of the Supreme Court and also the instructions of the State Government.
2.
The petitioners had been appointed on daily wages in the office of respondent No. 3 on muster rolls without issuing any order of appointment during the years 1988 to 1994.
According to them, they remained in service till 31th of May, 1994 and thereafter they have not been given any muster roll.
Some other similarly situated persons approached this Court and their writ petitions were allowed.
The services of the petitioners were not regularised, but the services of the persons junior to them have been regularised.
In order to seek regularisation of their services, the petitioners filed a writ petition bearing No. 11060 of 1988.
By an order dated 2nd June, 1993 this Court passed the following order :-  "Petitioners No. 2 and 3 have put in less than 2 years service.
No orders of regularisation can be passed in their case.
Petition on their behalf is dismissed.
It is pleaded that all other petitioners have put in more than two years service prior to the filing of the present petition.
Learned Counsel for the State contends that their case for regularisation shall be considered in view of the instructions of the State as also the judgment rendered by the Supreme Court in State of Haryana v. Piara Singh, 1992(5) SPJ 1 : 1992(3) SCT 201 (SC) and if they fulfil all the conditions as mentioned in the instructions and the judgment aforesaid, their services shall be regularised.
I order accordingly.
However, till such time the case of the petitioners is considered for regularisation, their services shall not be terminated.
The petition is disposed of."
After passing the said order, the services of the petitioners were not regularised.
The petitioners also filed Civil Misc.
Application No. 3826 of 1994 to implement the order of this Court.
A direction dated 2541994 was given to implement the order dated 22101992 within three months.
In spite of that, the service of the petitioners were not regularised.
According to the petitioners, their services are to be regularised in view of the order of this Court.
Since no order has been passed by the respondents, the petitioners filed this writ petition.
3.
On behalf of the respondents, a written statement is filed admitting that the petitioners were employed as casual labourers purely on daily wage basis and it is also contended that the petitioners are paid for the days they are present on duty.
Their services are not regular and since they are only casual labourers on daily wages, they are not entitled for regularisation.
They further stated that in compliance with the order of this Court referred to above, the case of the petitioners was considered for regularisation in the light of the decision of the Supreme Court in State of Haryana v. Piara Singh and the representations of the petitioners were rejected.
According to the respondents they have not completed 240 days in a year during the period of five years from 1988 to 1993 and therefore, they are not entitled to have their services regularised.
Therefore, their writ petition is liable to be dismissed.
4.
In their replication the petitioners reiterated their pleas taken in the writ petition.
5.
There is no dispute of the fact that the petitioners have been working as Casual Labourers on daily wages.
The Government of Haryana on the basis of the decision of the Supreme Court in State Haryana v. Piara Singh, 1992(3) RSJ 235 issued instructions on 2751993 vide Annexure R-1 in regard to regularisation of work charge/casual/daily rated employees.
According to those instructions the casual and daily rated employees who have completed 5 years of continuous service on 31st March, 1993 and were in service on that date shall be regularised.
On regularisation, they shall be put in the time scale of pay applicable to the lowest Group `D' cadre in the Government and they would be entitled to all other allowances and benefits available to regular Govt. servants of the corresponding grade.
Subsequently, a clarification was issued by the Chief Secretary to Government, Haryana in his circular letter dated 18.3.1994 which is marked as Annexure R-2.
According to this letter, the Government has decided that those daily wages employees who have worked for 240 days in a year should be regularised, but there should not be a break in service for more than 30 days at a time in a year.
Thus it is clear that for getting the benefit of regularisation of services in the Policy of the Government, the employee must have worked for a period of 240 days in a year without any break of more than 30 days.
6.
It is, therefore, to be seen whether the petitioners fulfilled the conditions laid down in the circular and the letter referred to above so as to enable them to have the benefit of regularisation.
The following chart shows the actual number of days the petitioner worked in a year during the period 1988 to 1993.

The 3rd petitioner worked for 167 days during the year 1988-89 whereas the 4th petitioner worked for 84 days during the year 1989-90.
The 5th petitioner worked for 19 days only in the year 1988-89 and the 6th petitioner worked for 225-1/2 days.
Thus none of the petitioner completed 240 days in the year 1988-89.
7.
The learned counsel for the petitioners placed reliance on a decision of this Court in Giri Raj and others v. State of Haryana and others reported in, 1997(2) RSJ 506 to which I am a party.
In that case the petitioners have fulfilled the condition of working 240 days in a year.
The break in service for more than 30 days was held not to come in the way of regularisation of the petitioners and in that case the break was not on account of fault of petitioners therein but due to banning of muster rolls for that month.
In the decision it has also not been held that those petitioners who have not completed 240 days are entitled to regularisation.
The learned counsel for the petitioner further relied upon a decision of this Court in Ram Dhari v. The Chief Wild Life Warden, Haryana and another, 1996(1) RSJ 246 : 1996(1) SCT 615 (P&H)(DB).
In that case, the petitioner completed more than 240 days during the period of five years from 1988 to 1993 and that too without any break in service.
Since the petitioner in that case worked for more than 240 days and there was no break in service during the period of five years his writ petition was rightly allowed by this Court directing the respondents to consider his case for regularisation.
Since in the present case, the petitioners have not completed 240 days in a year, they cannot take advantage of the above decision.
The other decision relied upon by the petitioners in Anand Kumar v. Haryana Urban Development Authority etc., reported in 1995(1) R.S.J.
230 : 1995(1) SCT 677 (P&H)(DB) also cannot be pressed into service since in that case the break in service more than one month occasioned due to the circumstances beyond the control of the daily wagers/casual employee.
8.
In this case, the petitioner have not fulfilled the primary condition that they should be in service for 240 days or more in a year.
Since that condition is not fulfilled, they cannot take any benefit of the instructions issued by the Haryana Government vide Annexure R-1 and R-2.
Since they have not completed 240 days' service during the period of five years prior to 3131993, they are not entitled to have their services regularised.
I do not, therefore, see any merit in this writ petition.
9.
The result is, the writ petition fail and is, accordingly, dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
