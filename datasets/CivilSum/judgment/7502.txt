MM Kumar, J - This petition filed under Article 226 of Constitution by one Johar Singh prays for issuance of a writ of certiorari quashing order dated 2391996 (Annexure P-8) passed by respondent 3 reverting the petitioner from the post of Mixer Operator to the post of Chowkidar.
A further prayer has also been made directing the respondents to reinstate the petitioner on the post of Mixer Operator w.e.f.
1571991 with a direction to pay all the arrears of salary and other consequential benefits.
2.
To start with in 1984 petitioner was appointed as Beldar in the pay scale of Rs. 770-1350/-.
He was promoted as Mixer Operator on 16.4.1985 in the pay scale of Rs. 1000-1800/- where he worked till 31121985.
On 111986 the petitioner was reverted as Beldar without any opportunity of hearing and notice.
On 2571991 the petitioner was again promoted as Mixer Operator w.e.f.
1571991 in the revised pay scale of Rs. 1000-1800/- as is evident from Annexure P-2.
However, by an order dated 2491991 he was placed under suspension as Chowkidar (Annexure R-4).
He was reinstated on 2311995 (Annexure P-3) on the post of Chowkidar.
His suspension period from 181991 to 2311995 was treated as duty period and he was held entitled to claim his full wages for the suspension period (Annexure P-4).
On the basis of some representation made by the petitioner on 3071995 the Superintending Engineer vide letter dated 28111995 (Annexure P-5) issued directions to the Executive Engineer, Ghanauli Construction Division, SYL Canal Project, Ropar to give effect to the office letter dated 2571991 which has given promotion to the petitioner as Mixer Operator w.e.f.
1571991 and necessary report in that regard was to be submitted to the office.
On 911996, the Executive Engineer appraised the Sub Divisional Officer about the order of the Superintending Engineer and sought its compliance by directing the Sub Divisional Officer to accept the joining report of the petitioner as Mixer Operator w.e.f.
1571991.
A copy of the joining report was also sent along with letter dated 28111995 (Annexure P-5).
As a consequence, the petitioner was promoted on the post of Mixer Operator (Technician) as is evident from order dated 18.7.1996 (Annexure P-7) and he was granted benefit of fixation of his pay in the revised master scale.
He was also paid arrears of salary w.e.f.
111996 to 30111996.
The petitioner had taken charge of the post of Mixer Operator w.e.f.
111996 at Ghanauli Construction Division, SYL Canal Project, Ropar and was paid salary in the pay scale of Rs. 1000-1800/- with special pay of Rs. 150/- P.M. which is permissible to a Mixer Operator.
He has also got increments from 1571991 to July, 1996.
These facts are evident from the affidavit dated 491997 which the petitioner has filed in support of the petition.
3.
After having permitted the petitioner to work as a Mixer Operator from 111996 to 30111996 and paying him salary for the aforementioned period, the Superintending Engineer, Construction Circle No. 1, SYL Canal Project, Chandigarh sent a communication stating that the petitioner was to work as a Chowkidar.
In reply to this letter, the Executive Engineer, Ghanauli Construction Division, SYL Canal Project, Ropar sent a reply to the Superintending Engineer stating that on one hand a direction has been issued by the Superintending Engineer for promoting the petitioner as Mixer Operator and on the other hand it has been directed to continue the petitioner as a Chowkidar.
The reply sent by the Executive Engineer makes an interesting reading and the relevant part of the same is extracted below :-  "Thereafter, vide your office letter No. 4236/5-E, dated 29.8.1995, detailed information had again been sought with respect to this official.
A reply thereto had been sent to you vide letter No. 2876/7-E, dated 20.9.1995.
After that, vide your letter No. 5640/5-E, dated 28111995, it had been ordered to meticulously comply with letter No. 3942/5-E, dated 2571991.
Therefore, in compliance with your orders, this official had been appointed as Mixer Operator.
But again vide your letter No. 4183/4-A/W, dated 2391996 you have directed to continue this official on the post of Chowkidar, which is totally contrary to your above mentioned letter No. 3942-43/5-E, dated 2571991 and letter No. 5640/5-E dated 28111995."
4.
The petitioner challenges order dated 239/1996 (Annexure P-8) by filing the instant petition.
5.
In the written statement the respondents have taken the stand that the petitioner has already availed the alternative remedy of invoking the jurisdiction of the Assistant Labour Commissioner which may result into a reference of the dispute between the petitioner and the management to the Labour Court.
It has been pointed out that this is an alternative efficacious remedy available to the petitioner which he should have pursued but withdrew the same for the reasons best known to him.
Further, the stand of the respondents is that the order of promotion of the petitioner dated 2571991 could not be implemented because of the closing of the project and it has been asserted that the petitioner never worked as Mixer Operator after 3181985 (Annexure R-2).
According to the respondents when the petitioner was reinstated with effect from 2311995 (Annexure R-5) and his period of suspension from 181991 to 2311995 was regularised, no objection was raised by him that he was working on the post of Mixer Operator.
However, it has not been denied that the salary of the petitioner was fixed by the Executive Engineer on the post of Mixer Operator and arrears were paid from 111996 to 30111996.
However, the order was withdrawn by the Superintending Engineer while passing order dated 2391996 (Annexure P-8) which is subject matter of challenge in the instant petition.
6.
It is appropriate to mention that on 3052003 detailed arguments in this case were heard and the orders were reserved.
On 2752003 the petitioner filed an application for bringing on record order Annexure P-10, transferring the petitioner from SYL Canal Project, Ropar to Upper Bari Doab Canal Circle, Amritsar as Chowkidar.
The operation of the aforementioned order was stayed by this Court on 2752003 by directing that the petitioner shall be allowed to work at Ropar.
Thereafter, another application was filed on 1072003 being CM No. 14934 of 2003 through which order dated 872003 was brought on record showing that despite the said order passed by this Court, the petitioner was relieved from SYL Canal Project, Ropar.
On 2472003 it was ordered that the CM as well as the writ petition were to be heard again on 2972003 and even contempt proceedings were issued against one Amar Nath Singh, Executive Engineer and Surinder Singh Dhingra, S.D.O.
Those contempt proceedings have been separately registered and are continuing.
7.
Mr. SD Sharma learned counsel for the petitioner has vehemently argued that the order dated 2391996 (Annexure P-8) passed by the Superintending Engineer is liable to be set aside on the ground that no show cause notice was issued to the petitioner directing his reversion from the post of Mixer Operator to that of Chowkidar.
According to the learned counsel the Superintending Engineer on 28111995 (Annexure P-5) himself had issued direction to the Executive Engineer for giving effect to the order of promotion of the petitioner to the post of Mixer Operator and in pursuance to the direction the petitioner has also filed joining report as Mixer Operator.
He was actually promoted as Mixer Operator on 18.7.1996 (Annexure P-7).
Learned counsel has maintained that the petitioner was granted benefit of fixation of his pay in the revised master scale and was also paid arrears of salary with effect from 111996 to 30111996.
Learned counsel has further submitted that any order which results into reduction of pay or which has adverse civil consequences cannot be issued without associating the affected person.
Learned Counsel has placed reliance on a celebrated judgment of the Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court in State of Orissa v. Dr. (Miss) Binapani Dei, AIR 1967 SC 1269, Mohinder Singh Gill v. Chief Election Commissioner, AIR 1978 SC 851, Menaka Gandhi v. Union of India, AIR 1978 SC 597 and Union of India v. Tulsiram Patel, AIR 1985 SC 1416.
Referring to the observations made in the judgment of the Supreme Court in Tulsiram Patel's case (supra), learned counsel has argued that the principles of natural justice are part of Article 14 of the Constitution and the founding fathers have given these principles the highest status which is even superior to fundamental rights.
Learned counsel has further maintained that once the principles of natural justice are shown to have been violated then no alternative remedy is required to be availed by the petitioner as per the stand taken by the respondents in their written statement.
8.
Mr. Sukant Gupta, learned State counsel has submitted that according to a Full Bench judgment of this Court in Manohar Lal v. State of Punjab, 1983(2) SLR 658, it has been held that remedy provided to a workman to claim a reference under Section 10 of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 is an effective efficacious alternative remedy and it bars invoking the jurisdiction of this Court under Article 226 of the Constitution.
Learned counsel made a big attempt to argue that the petitioner has not submitted his joining report but did not stress the same because of the fact that the record speaks to the contrary.
9.
I have heard the learned counsel for the parties.
I am of the considered view that the impugned order dated 2391996 (Annexure P-8) passed by the Superintending Engineer reverting the petitioner from the post of Mixer Operator to that of Chowkidar is unsustainable in the eyes of law because it has been passed in flagrant violation of the principles of natural justice.
It is well settled that the principles of natural justice are integral part of rule of law and no State action which has adverse civil consequences could be completed without associating those who are likely to be adversely affected.
The judgment of the Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court in Dr. (Miss) Binapani Dei's case (supra) has completely abridged the distinction between the administrative order or other orders by observing that as long as an order results into civil consequences the affected party must be granted an opportunity to show case and hearing.
Similar principles have been reiterated in the cases of Manohar Singh and Menaka Gandhi (supra).
In the case of EP Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu and another, 1974(4) SCC 3 the Supreme Court referred to the new and dynamic interpretation given to the concept of equality which is the subject matter of Article 14 of the Constitution.
On its march towards perfecting the principles of natural justice as part of Article 14 of the Supreme Court in Tulsiram's case (supra) observed as under :  "The principles of natural justice have thus come to be recognized as being a part of the guarantee contained in Article 14 because of the new and dynamic interpretation given by this Court to the concept of equality which is the subject-matter of that Article.
Shortly put, the syllogism runs thus : violation of a rule of natural justice results in arbitrariness which is the same as discrimination; where discrimination is the result of State action, it is a violation of Article 14; therefore, a violation of principle of natural justice by a State action is a violation of Article 14.
Article 14, however is not the sole repository of the principles of natural justice.
What it does is to guarantee that any law of State action violating the will be struck down.
The principles of natural justice, however, apply not only to legislation and State action but also where any tribunal, authority or body of men, not coming within the definition of "State" in Article 12, is charged with duty of deciding a matter.
In such a case, the principles of natural justice require that it must decide such matter fairly and impartially."
10.
If the facts of the instant case are examined in the light of the principles laid down by the Supreme Court in the aforementioned judgments, it becomes evident that the order of reversion dated 2391996 (Annexure P-8) could not have been passed by the Superintending Engineer without issuing show cause notice to the petitioner and hearing him because the petitioner who was working in pay scale of Rs. 1000-1800/- was reverted to the post of Chowkidar which is in much lower pay scale.
The petitioner was promoted to work as Mixer Operator from 111996 to 30111996.
He was also paid the salary for the aforementioned period.
When the Superintending Engineer, Construction Circle No. 1, Canal Project, Chandigarh sent a communication through the Executive Engineer Ghanauli Construction Division, SYL Canal Project, Ropar stating that the petitioner was to work as a Chowkidar, the Executive Engineer, Ghanauli Construction Division, SYL Canal Project sent a letter to the Superintending Engineer, Construction Circle No. 1, Canal Project, Chandigarh on 15111996 (Annexure P-9) stating that on the one hand a direction has been issued vide Annexure P-5 dated 28111995 by the Superintending Engineer to promote the petitioner by giving effect to the order dated 2571991 (Annexure P-2) and on the other hand direction was issued on 2391996 to continue the petitioner on the post of Chowkidar.
The fact that the petitioner has been promoted as Mixer Operator is patent from the following observations made by the Executive Engineer in his letter dated 15111996 :  "Thereafter, vide your office letter No. 4236/5-E, dated 29.8.1995, detailed information had again been sought with respect to this official.
A reply thereto had been sent to you vide letter No. 2876/7-E, dated 20.9.1995.
After that, vide your letter No. 5640/5-E, dated 28111995, it had been ordered to meticulously comply with letter No. 3942/5-E, dated 2571991.
Therefore, in compliance with your orders, this official had been appointed as Mixer Operator.
But again vide your letter No. 4183/4-A/W, dated 2391996 you have directed to continue this official on the post of Chowkidar, which is totally contrary to your above mentioned letter No. 3942-43/5-E, dated 2571991 and letter No. 5640/5-E dated 28111995."
11.
It is, thus, admitted that the petitioner was promoted as Mixer Operator with effect from 1571991 in compliance to the order dated 28111995 (Annexure P-5) passed by the Superintending Engineer and it was duly compiled with.
The joining report of the petitioner as Mixer Operator was obtained and was sent vide officer letter No. 468/2-A dated 29121995.
The pay of the petitioner was fixed on the post of Mixer Operator from 1571991.
He was paid arrears with effect from 111996 to 30111996.
Therefore, the order dated 2391996 (Annexure P08) passed by the Superintending Engineer directing the Executive Engineer to post the petitioner as Chowkidar is violative of Article 14 of the Constitution.
It is further evident from the endorsement that the petitioner was to be treated as Chowkidar retrospectively and recovery was to be effected, if found due.
12.
The argument of the learned State counsel that there is an alternative efficacious remedy available to the petitioner based on a Full Bench judgment of this Court in Mohinder Singh Gill's case (supra) has not impressed me because the alternative remedy may successfully be pleaded as a bar to the maintainability of a writ petition but in a case where the principles of natural justice are violated such an argument cannot be sustained.
It is well settled by a catena of judgments that in cases where there is wanton disregard to the principles of natural justice then the plea of the alternative remedy cannot be set up to defeat the claim of a person who has invoked the writ jurisdiction under Article 226 of the Constitution.
In this regard reliance can be placed on judgments of the Supreme Court in the cases of AV Venkateswaran v. Ramchand Sobhraj Wadhwani, AIR 1961 SC 1506; Surya Dev Rai v. Ram Chander Rai, 2004(1) RCR(Civil) 147 (SC) : 2003 SCC 675; Whirlpool Corporation v. Registrar of Trade Marks, 1988(8) SCC 1 and Harbans Lal Sahnia v. Indian Oil Corporation Ltd, 2003(2) SCC 107.
Therefore, there is no substance in the arguments raised by the learned State counsel and the same is hereby rejected.
For the reasons stated above, this petition succeeds.
Accordingly, order dated 2391996 (Annexure P-8) is set aside and as a result thereof, the petitioner is entitled to be reinstated on the post of Mixer Operator with all consequential benefits including salary as Mixer Operator from 1571991, the date from which date benefit of fixation has been given to him.
The respondents shall comply with these directions expeditiously preferably within a period of three months from the date of the receipt of a copy of this order.
Petition allowed.
