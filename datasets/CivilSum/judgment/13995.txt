JS Narang, J - This judgment would dispose of two Civil Writ Petitions No. 14919 of 2002 and 403 of 2003, as common question of law and somewhat similar facts are involved in the aforestated petitions.
The facts are being taken from Civil Writ Petition No. 403 of 2003.
2.
Petitioner joined as JBT Teacher on February 3, 1956 and superannuated on July 31, 1991 as Head Teacher from Government Primary School Khurda, District Kaithal after rendering 35 years 5 months and 29 days of service.
He acquired Bachelor of Arts (BA) qualification on June 9, 1971.
In pursuant thereto and as a sequel to letter dated July 27, 1957 issued by the erstwhile State of Punjab, the petitioner claimed higher pay scale on account of higher qualification.
Since no relief was granted by the Government, a petition under Article 226/227 of the Constitution of India, was filed which was registered as CWP No. 18986 of 1995, and the same was finally allowed vide judgment dated April 22, 1988.
In view of the relief granted, the petitioner was sanctioned JST scale as per the higher qualifications with effect from December 12, 1973, vide communication No. E-I/2000/97 dated 2212000.
3.
However, it may be noticed that the JST scale had already been fixed by virtue of a letter dated January 28, 1998, issued in pursuant to the general orders dated October 26, 1995 issued by the Director, Primary Education, Haryana.
The petitioner had issued a notice of contempt for implementation of the judgment passed in the aforestated petition filed by the petitioner.
The respondents stated that the petitioner had been granted relief and the monetary effect was to be granted only for a period of 38 months prior to the date of filing of the aforesaid petition which was filed on December 21, 1995.
Thus, by no stretch of imagination, the relief was granted as the petitioner had retired with effect from July 31, 1991.
Consequently, nothing was found due and payable.
The resultant effect had been duly explained by the District Primary Education Officer, Kaithal, vide a communication dated February 15, 2000, addressed to the Director, Primary Education, Haryana, which has been made subject matter of challenge by the petitioner, copy Annexure P3.
4.
Notice of motion was issued vide order dated January 14, 2003 and that the opportunity to file written statement had also been granted.
However, no written statement has been filed.
5.
Learned counsel for the petitioner has argued that in view of the relief granted by this Court in the previous writ petition filed by the petitioner, the respondents have fallen into error in declining the relief on the premises that the petitioner had since retired on July 31, 1991, as such the arrears are not grantable as the petition had been filed in the year 1995 and the prior period of 38 months commenced much after the retirement of the petitioner.
It is argued that the petitioner would be entitled to the arrears to the extent of 38 months prior to the date of his retirement.
It is also argued that the pension of the petitioner was required to be revised upon the application of the JST scale as he had acquired the higher qualification in the year 1971 and the Government granted the sanction for application of JST scale accordingly.
It is the admitted case that the petitioner had been granted the aforestated scale with effect from December 12, 1973, thus, in pursuant thereto the pension of the petitioner was also required to be re- fixed and revised accordingly.
Resultantly, the petitioner would be entitled to the revised pension accordingly.
The respondents under the garb of the impugned order, copy Annexure P3, had declined arrears on the premises that the petitioner had retired on July 31, 1991 and whereas the petition had been filed in the year 1995.
De hors of that, the petitioner would be entitled to be paid the revised pension on account of the applicability of the JST scale with effect from December 12, 1973.
The petitioner is being paid the pension as per the old scales and not as per the revised scales as aforestated.
In support of his contention, he has placed reliance upon the judgments of this Court are noticed as under :-  (1) Ram Chander v. State of Haryana, 1998(4) Service Cases Today 492 and (2) Jaimal Singh v. State of Haryana and others, Civil Writ Petition No. 222 of 2002, decided on July 22, 2002 6.
On the other hand, learned Deputy Advocate General, Haryana, has argued that the petitioner is not entitled to the arrears and the same have been correctly declined by the respondents.
In fact, his claim stood negated on the ground that the petitioner had superannuated on July 31, 1991, and that the petition for enforcement for JST scale was filed in the year 1995.
The relief in respect of arrears has been granted for a period of 38 months prior to the filing of the petition which was filed on December 21, 1995.
This period of 38 months could be counted only upto the date much after the date of superannuation, resultantly, the arrears have been correctly declined.
It is further argued that the revision in the pension as claimed by the petitioner is also not grantable as he had superannuated much earlier and the relief has been granted only for a period after the date of superannuation.
Resultantly, the petitioner would not be entitled to any revision of pay scale as well.
Civil Writ Petition No. 14919 of 2002  7.
The petitioner superannuated on March 31, 1992 after rendering 35 years and 10 months service.
However, it is not clear that the petitioner had also filed any such similar petition under Article 226/227 of the Constitution of India, as had been filed by the petitioner in Civil Writ Petition No. 403 of 2003.
However, upon the representation of the petitioner the Accountant General, Haryana, addressed two communications to the petitioner copy Annexure P4 dated 25.6.2001 and Annexure P5 dated 861997, vide which it has been clearly and categorically observed that no arrears are payable and that the pay has been revised notionally, as such, the pensionary benefits can only be revised in case of relaxation of rule 624 of the Punjab Civil Services Rules Vol. II, and it has been advised that the matter be taken up with the department for seeking relaxation of the rule and forwarding the case accordingly.
The relief is grantable similarly as we have opined in Civil Writ Petition No. 403 of 2003.
However, the arrears in this case upon the revision of the pension shall be payable for 38 months prior to the date of filing of the representation which was filed on September 2, 2002.
8.
After hearing learned counsel for the parties, we are of the view that the respondents have correctly declined the payment of arrears in pursuant to the judgment of this Court in Jaimal Singh's case (supra).
Admittedly, the petitioner had superannuated much prior to the period of 38 months directed to be counted prior to the date of filing of the petition.
However, so far as revision of the pension is concerned, from the perusal of the communication relied upon by the petitioner, it transpires that the JST scale had been granted to the petitioner with effect from December 12, 1973, though after superannuation but the resultant effect is that the scale stood revised notionally, which has certainly affected the scale, re-fixation and resultantly the revision of the pension.
Thus, this petition is partly allowed and the respondents are directed to revise the pension of the petitioner with effect from the date of superannuation and the arrears in respect thereof shall be payable from the date when the earlier petition had been allowed.
The writ petition stands disposed of in the above terms.
Petition partly allowed.
