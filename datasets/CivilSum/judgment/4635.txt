S Parvatha Rao, J - The petitioner seeks a Writ in the nature of Mandamus or any other Writ, Order or direction, directing the respondent to continue him as Principal of BHH Degree College for Women in Guntur declaring the resolution dated 2691993 as illegal etc.  2.
The petitioner states that he was appointed as Principal of the BHH Degree College for Women and that he had taken charge on 27.2.1991 and that he had been working as such from the inception of the College.
He states that the Secretary of the College took from him a letter of resignation dated 1491993 stating that it would come into force with effect from 1111993 and that on 2091993 the Secretary again obtained another letter from him that the resignation was to come into force from 1101993 onwards.
He further states that on 24.9.1993 he wrote a letter to the Secretary withdrawing his letters of resignation dated 1491993 and 2091993 obtained from him, and informed the Secretary that he would continue to work in the College as Principal.
He states that the College was closed for Dasara vacation from 28.9.1993 onwards and that he was not given any letter communicating to him the acceptance of his resignation or any copy of the Committee resolution alleged to have been passed on 2691993 accepting his resignation.
According to him only on 9101993 a messenger delivered a photostat copy of the resolution without anybody signatures.
The petitioner filed a photostat copy of the proceedings of the Committee of the BHH Degree and Junior Colleges for Women held on 2691993 in the college premises, alongwith the Writ Petition.
It shows that 8 persons, attended the said meeting.
Item No. 3 in the Agenda is.
"To consider the letter of the resignation sent by the Principal."
It also shows that under Resolution No. 3 the Committee inter alia resolved :  "...in view of the unconditional withdrawal of allegations and expression of apologies and tendering of resignation by the Principal, the resignation maybe accepted relieving the Principal forthwith.
The Executive is authorised to give effect to the resolution and make alternative arrangements such as release of advertisement and other steps for the appointment of I/C Principal until permanent arrangement is made."
3.
The petitioner contends that the said resolution is a concocted one and that "it is neither valid under law nor enforceable in view of its apparent infirmities" and therefore seeks the relief mentioned earlier, in the present Writ Petition.
4.
The Writ Petition was admitted on 14101993 and in WPMP No. 19759 of 1993 this Court directed by order dated 14101993 that status quo as on that day should be maintained.
5.
The Secretary and Correspondent of the said College gave his counter affidavit dated 5121993 denying the allegations of the petitioner and stating that resignation letters dated 791993 and 2091993 were submitted by the petitioner himself voluntarily and that the petitioner never sent the withdrawal letter before his resignation was accepted.
He further states that the petitioner was appointed by a resolution of the Managing Committee dated 2421991 on ad hoc basis and that when he was so appointed, he was a retired person having already reached the age of superannuation, and therefore he was not eligible for appointment on a permanent basis, and that the Government would not release teaching grant to such a person.
He also states that the petitioner had no right to continue in service permanently because he was appointed only on a temporary basis and that the management had the right to terminate his service without notice at any time and without assigning any reasons, but that the management did not terminate his service in exercise of that right.
According to the Secretary, the petitioner himself voluntarily resigned through his resignation letter dated 791993 to come into effect from 1111993 followed by another resignation letter dated 2091993 stating that the resignation shall come into effect from 1101993.
The petitioner also addressed a letter to the Nagarjuna University on 25.9.1993 intimating that he would be relinquishIng the post of Principal from 2791993 and that Smt. MA Rajyalakshmi, who was the Principal in-charge would act as Chief Superintendent for conducting B A, BCom, and BSc, September 1993 examinations commencing from 2791993.
The Secretary further states that a meeting of the Managing Committee of the College was convened on 2691993 and one of the subjects for consideration was the resignation letters of the petitioner and in view of the said letters of resignation of the petitioner and his letter dated 25.9.1993 addressed to the Nagarjuna University, the Managing Committee unanimously passed a resolution dated 2691993 accepting the resignation of the petitioner with immediate effect.
He also states that the resolution of the Managing Committee was intimated to the petitioner who was waiting in the Principal's room on the same day ie, on 2691993 itself.
He also states that on 2791993 the petitioner handed over all the records, rules, accounts, cash books, pass books cheque books etc., to Smt. A Rajyalakshmi teacher in Zoology, who was appointed as Principal in-charge until alternative arrangement were made.
A letter dated 2791993 drafted by the petitioner himself and typed evidencing the handing over of the charge was signed by the petitioner and Smt. M Rajyalakshmi.
The Secretary states that since then Smt. M Rajyalakshmi has been acting as Principal in-charge of the college and that the petitioner voluntarily handed over the charge to Smt. M Rajyalakshmi and he did not raise any dispute about it till 3101993.
The Secretary contends that the meeting of the Managing Committee held on 2691993 is perfectly valid.
He states that the meeting was attended by 8 members whereas the quorum was only 5.
He denies that the meeting was attended by only 4 members.
He also states that the Nagarjuna University did not nominate its representative.
The Secretary states that the petitioner sent letter dated 3101993 withdrawing his resignation letter with false allegations that his resignation was not accepted and that acceptance of his resignation was not communicated to him.
The Secretary replied to that letter on 8101993 stating that his resignation was already accepted and that it was given effect by the petitioner himself by handing over charge to Smt. M Rajyalakshmi on 2791993.
The reply letter dated 8101993 was sent on the same day to the petitioner by registered post with acknowledgement due, but was returned with an endorsement dated 18101993 that the petitioner 'left.'
He further states that when he and the acting Principal Smt. A Rajyalakshmi were not present in the College, the petitioner trespassed into the college on 18101993 and tampered with the attendance register of the staff by making interpolations as if he was attending the college and functioning as Principal till the date of obtaining the order of the High Court.
This is an utterly high -handed act on the part of the petitioner.
The Secretary submits that the Writ Petition is without any merit whatsoever.
6.
The first contention raised by the learned counsel for the petitioner is that on 24.9.1993 itself the petitioner withdrew his resignation letters before his resignation was accepted and therefore the acceptance of the so called resignation of his is a make believe one.
He further contends that the said resolution of the Managing Committee dated 2691993, even assuming that the meeting of the Managing Committee was properly convened, can only be treated as 'termination' of his services - the purported termination is invalid and can have no legal effect whatsoever because it is in violation of Sections 79 and 83 of the Andhra Pradesh Education' Act, 1982-(hereinafter referred to as 'the Act') as no prior approval of the competent authority was obtained and no notice and opportunity were given to him.
But the Secretary and Correspondent has stated that the petitioner did not withdraw his resignation.
In his additional counter affidavit dated 821994 he has also stated that the statement of the petitioner that he sent a letter dated 24.9.1993 withdrawing his earlier resignation letters is absolutely false and invented for the purpose of this case and he denies that he received any such letter and he also denies that he signed any acknowledgement to the effect that he received such letter which the petitioner for the first time asserted in his reply affidavit dated 1611994.
The material on record clearly establishes that the petitioner is not speaking the truth when he states that he sent the letter dated 24.9.1993 withdrawing his resignation to Secretary and Correspondent.
7.
There are several significant circumstances which improbalise and indeed establish that the petitioner did not withdraw his resignation before 2691993.
The first is that the petitioner does not dispute that he sent letter dated 25.9.1993 to the Controller of Examinations, Nagarjuna University stating as follows :  "As I am relinquishing the post of Principal from 27993, Smt. M Rajyalakshmi, Principal in charge will act as Chief Superintendent for September, 1993 BA, BCom, BSc Examinations.
This is for your kind information."
This letter was signed by the petitioner himself.
If the petitioner really withdrew his resignation by letter dated 24.9.1993 addressed to the Secretary, there is no explanation whatsoever for the petitioner addressing this letter dated 25.9.1993 to the Controller of Examinations.
The second circumstance is as follows : There is also a communication dated 2791993 from the Secretary to the Principal with a request to hand over charge to Smt. M Rajyalakshmi, which is as follows :  "Smt. M Rajyalakshmi, MSc, Lecturer in Zoology is appointed Principal incharge of the college with effect from 28.9.93 till alternative arrangements are made consequent upon the resignation of the Principal Sri G Kailasapathy Rao.
She is requested to take charge from him.
The outgoing Principal has handed over all records, files, accounts, cash books, pass books, cheque books etc. and Sri G Kailasapathy Rao is relieved of all his college duties from the afternoon of 2791993."
This was signed by the Principal with the date in his own hand as 2791993 after endorsing in his own hand 'handed over charge' and was also signed by Smt. M Rajyalakshmi under the date 2791993 endorsing thereon 'taken over charge'.
The Secretary also signed on it with the date 2791993.
The original of this letter is produced before me by the, learned counsel for the respondent.
In the xerox copy produced by the petitioner alleged to have been attested as true copy by the Secretary of the College, it is additionally typed "For Exams purpose only handed over Charge."
This obviously is not a true copy of the original which is produced before me in which there was no mention that for exams purpose only charge was handed over.
I am inclined to agree with the learned counsel for the respondent that the signature of the Secretary in the xerox copy produced by the petitioner is a forged one.
The rubber stamped address under the signature of the Secretary/Correspondent gives the address of the College as "2/17, Brodiepet, Guntur-2".
In the true copy filed by the petitioner.
'Brodiepet' is spelled in the rubber stamped address as 'Brodiepet' which shows that different rubber stamp was used in the copy furnished by the petitioner.
The same spelling 'Brodipet' is found in the rubber stamped address in the xerox copy of the letter dated 24.9.1993 produced by the petitioner to establish that it was received by the Secretary and Correspondent on 2491993.
The Secretary in his additional affidavit dated 821994 stated that the said copy of the letter dated 24.9.1993 (which was filed on 721994 in the second set of material papers) is a concocted one and denied his signature thereon.
He also categorically stated that no such letter dated 24.9.1993 was sent by the petitioner withdrawing his earlier resignation letters and that the said letter is concocted for the purpose of this case.
The third circumstance is that the petitioner in his own hand wrote under the date 2691993 in the minutes book of the Managing Committee of the College the agenda item No. 3 which is as follows :  "To consider the letter of resignation sent by the Principal."
The minutes book is produced and placed before me by the learned counsel for the respondent and this is found at page 57 of the said book.
It is stated in the additional counter affidavit of the Secretary and Correspondent of the college dated 821994 that :  "The Agenda for the meeting dated 26993 including the subject of resignation was written by the petitioner himself."
In the additional reply affidavit dated 1321994 filed by the petitioner, this fact is not disputed.
If the petitioner really sent the letter dated 24.9.1993 withdrawing his resignation, it is inconceivable that he would have written the said Item No3 of the agenda himself.
The fourth circumstance is that in the letter dated 3101993 addressed by the petitioner, no mention was made of his letter dated 24.9.1993 withdrawing his resignation.
In his reply affidavit dated 1611994 the petitioner had, committed that he sent the letter dated 3101993.
A xerox copy of the said letter is filed by the respondent.
The tenor of the said letter dated 3101993 is, such that, if really the petitioner addressed the letter dated 24.9.1993 withdrawing his resignation, it was impossible for him not to have mentioned it.
On the other hand, the tenor of the said letter dated 3101993 positively indicates that he did not address the alleged letter dated 24.9.1993 to the Secretary.
In the letter dated 3101993 he wrote inter alia as follows :  "...I begged you to continue me at least till NU Examinations are over.
But alas.
You wanted me out of the way by hook or crook and did not care for the interests of the institution or the students and staff.
The only thing you were interested in is to get me out of the way forthwith.
Your hasty action of 27091993 automatically cancels my resignation letters (which were any way forced on me by you).
Further, I am not served any relieving orders till date.
According to your version my resignation is linked with Sri Somayaji episode.
Hence, request you to please treat my resignation letters as withdrawn and continuing in my office."
Certainly the petitioner would not have written so if he withdrew his resignation by letter dated 24.9.1993.
I am of the view that the letter dated 3101993 itself is an after thought because even by that time the petitioner admittedly handed over charge to the acting Principal Smt. M Rajyalakshmi after handing over all records, files, accounts, cash books, pass books, Cheque books etc., as is evidenced by what he signed on 2791993 with his endorsement 'handed over charge' referred to above.
The petitioner admits this when he states in his reply affidavit dated 1611994 :  "It is true that Smt. M Rajyalakshmi has been acting as in-charge from 27993 i.e., during vacation."
He also admits that he was bodily present at the time of commencement of the meeting on 2691993, in his reply affidavit dated 1611994.
In the circumstances, it is not possible to believe the petitioner when he states that he was not communicated the decision taken at the meeting accepting his resignation.
8.
It is also to be noticed in this connection that according to the respondent, the college was on Dasara vacation only from 5101993 to 24.10.1993 and the University Degree Examinations for September candidates were conducted in the college premises from 2791993 to 14/15101993.
In additional counter affidavit dated 821994, Secretary states that after obtaining status quo order dated 14101993, the petitioner came to the college and tampered with the attendance register by marking his attendance from 28.9.1993 to 4.10.1993.
In his first reply affidavit dated 1611994 itself the petitioner admitted that he went to the college on 18101993 on receiving the orders copy of this Court to apprise the Secretary about the same and that he waited for two hours i.e. upto 1200 noon and as the Secretary did not come, he asked the Attender to get the attendance register and signed for 18101993 just to prove" that he was there in the college on that day.
He also stated that the signature was otherwise absolutely unnecessary because it was still vacation for the college.
In his additional reply affidavit dated 1321994, he does not deny that he in fact marked his attendance from 28.9.1993 to 4.10.1993 and he does not explain how he marked his attendance from 28.9.1993 to 4.10.1993 when according to him the Dasara vacation commenced from 2791993 and he admittedly handed over charge to Smt. M Rajyalakshmi though according to him it was for conducting the examinations.
I am therefore, satisfied that the stand of the petitioner that he withdraw his resignation letters prior to the acceptance of the same by the respondent and that he did not hand over charge to consequent upon his resignation as an outgoing Principal cannot be believed.
9.
The learned counsel for the petitioner submits that the respondent will have to obtain prior approval of the competent authority under Section 83 of the Act before the petitioner's resignation is accepted by the Management in view of the decision of the Division Bench of this Court in [Dr. Sr. Y Philomena v. Prl.
Secretary to Govt., 1992(3) ALT 324 (AP)].
In that case, the Division Bench held as follows :  "Removal termination or discharge of staff members can be made only with the prior approval of the competent authority after following the rules and regulations prescribed by itself and approved by the competent authority.
That statutory authority is entitled to refuse approval if it considers that the action proposed is against the principles of natural justice or contrary to the rules and regulations adopted by the management itself.
Orders of such authorities are subject to prior approval under Section 83 and appeals under Section 79 or 80 or 89 or revision under Section 90 of the Act."
I fail to see how these observations of the Division Bench support the contention of the learned counsel for the petitioner.
In the present case, the petitioner was not dismissed or removed or reduced in rank or suspended by way of any punishment or otherwise and it is not even the case of the learned counsel that Section 79 of the Act is attracted to the facts of the case.
Section 83 of the Act deals with retrenchment of employees and it is as follows :  "Where retrenchment of an employee is rendered necessary by the management or competent authority consequent on any change relating to education or course of instruction or to any other matter, such retrenchment may be effected with the prior approval of the competent authority or the next higher authority as the case may be."
This provision was interpreted by two other Division Benches of this Court.
In Vasavi College of Engineering v. A Suryanarayana, 1992(1) SCT 626 (AP) (DB) : 1991(3) ALT 335 a Division Bench of this Court held as follows :-  "We hold that if the service of a teacher or other employee of a private educational institution is sought to be terminated by dismissal or removal, it shall be preceded by any inquiry as provided in Section 79 and such removal shall be effected only with the prior approval of the competent authority.
Like-wise in the case of any termination of service other than those covered by Section 79 of the Act, prior appellant of such competent authority or the next higher authority is made a condition precedent for any other termination of service under Section 83 of the Act.
xxx xxx xxx  In the view which we take that Sections 79 and 83 complement each other and exhaust the entire area of termination of service, punitive or otherwise, it is not necessary for us to consider further refinements sought to be discovered by counsel for the employer that only such retrenchment of employees consequent on 'any change relating to education or course of instruction or to any other matter' fall within Section 83 of the Act.
We are not persuaded to accept the specious argument that 'any other matter' must be relatable to change relating to education or course of instruction and cannot take in termination of service or any other reason.
The natural meaning to be given to the phrase 'any other matter' seems to us to be exclusive of change relating to education or course of instruction."
In P Rama Devi v. Govt. of AP, 1993(4) SCT 315 (AP) (DB) : 1993(1) ALT 179 (AP)] another Division Bench of this Court took a similar view holding : "The word 'retrenchment' employed under Section 83 of the Act means the termination by the employer of the service of employee for any reason whatsoever, otherwise than as punishment inflicted by way of disciplinary action.
That is made more clear by the Act in view of the separate provision contained under Section 79 thereof dealing specifically with dismissal, removal or reduction in rank or suspension etc., for employees of private institutions."
10.
It has to be noticed that in Vasavi College of Engineering (supra) the Division Bench was dealing with a case of termination of service of a temporary employee found to be unfit for regular appointment and the contention advanced on behalf of the management was that Section 83 has no application to such cases because that was not really a case of retrenchment which means removal or termination because of surplus age.
There was a positive act of the management by passing an order terminating the services of the employee in that case.
That was not a case of resignation by the employee himself.
So also in the case of P Rama Devi (supra).
An employee who was appointed in a leave vacancy was sought to be terminated without any prior approval of the competent authority after the return of the employee who went on leave.
The Division Bench held that in much as the termination was not by virtue or any simpliciter proceedings and was termination simpliciter in view of the fact that the employee was holding a post temporarily during the absence of the permanent incumbent who went on leave, the same amounts to retrenchment within the meaning of Section 83 of the Act and that prior approval of the competent authority was necessary.
Thus, this is also a case where the services of the employees were terminated by the employer and not a case of resignation.
In these two cases the question of the employee himself terminating his own services by resignation and whether such cases are also attracted by Section 83 of the Act, did not arise.
11.
I am of the view that cases of resignation by the employees are not covered by Section 83 of the Act.
When an employee submits his resignation there is no termination of the service of that employee by any act of the employer-acceptance of the resignation does not amount to an order of termination by an employer.
'Retrenchment of Employees' contemplated under Section 83 of the Act must involve removal from service or termination of service against the wish of the employee.
When the employee himself submits his resignation he himself puts an end to his services, and not the management.
The retrenchment that requires prior approval under Section 83 is that which is rendered necessary by the management or competent authority.
Even if 'retrenchment' takes in every mode of termination by the management other than that covered by Section 79, in my view it cannot take in termination of services by employee himself by submitting his resignation I and, therefore, of the view that the termination of the service of the workman is not by the employer and is caused by the workman himself by resigning, it will not be attracted by Section 83 of the Act.
This is brought out by the language of Section 83 itself when it says as :  'Where retrenchment of any employee is rendered necessary by the management."
In that view of the matter I hold that Section 83 of the Act is not attracted to the facts of the present case.
12.
I refrain from referring to the authorities dealing with the import of the expression 'retrenchment' in Section 2(oo) and Section 25F of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 because of the observations of the Division Bench in Vasavi College of Engineering (supra) that :  "Retrenchment' in such a context as defined under Section 2(oo) of the Industrial Disputes Act as it stands at present, cannot be of any assistance in understanding the scope of 'retrenchment' occurring in Section 83 of the AP Education Act."
13.
The learned counsel for the petitioner questions the validity of the resolution of the Committee dated 2691993 on the ground that the meeting was not properly convened and that the requisite number of members did.
not attend the meeting and that the resolution was not passed with the proper majority.
But I am of the view that question cannot be raised in writ proceedings.
The petitioner has to question the same in civil proceedings.
It has been held by a Full Bench of this Court in Sri Konaseema Co-op.
Central Bank Ltd v. N Seetharama Raju, AIR 1990 AP 171 as follows :  "Even if for some reason it can be held that the appellant-Society is an 'authority' within the meaning of Article 12, it does not follow therefrom that every activity of its is amenable to writ jurisdiction."
14.
In the circumstances, the Writ Petition is dismissed.
No costs.
Petition allowed.
