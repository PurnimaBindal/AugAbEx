Ashutosh Mohunta, J - Chander Bhan, a motor driver of Haryana State Electricity Board, remained absent from duty from 16.10.1980 to 2231981 and the Superintending Engineer (Operations) of the HSEB ordered the stoppage of one increment with cumulative effect vide order dated 911984.
This order was challenged before the Authority under the Payment of Wages Act, 1936 who vide his order dated 11101984 held that the order dated 911984 was null and void and consequently awarded wages worth Rs. 5,240/- to the respondent.
2.
Against the order dated 11101984 passed by the Authority under the Payment of Wages Act, 1936 an appeal was filed by the HSEB before the District Judge, Sirsa, who vide his judgment dated 1521985 dismissed the appeal holding that as stoppage of one increment with cumulative effect was a major penalty and as no inquiry was held, therefore, the order imposing such a punishment was null and void.
Resultantly, the appeal was dismissed.
It is against this judgment that the present appeal has been filed.
3.
A perusal of the above mentioned facts clearly shows that stoppage of one increment with cumulative effect was ordered vide order dated 911984 by the Superintending Engineer (Operations), HSEB and as stoppage of one increment with cumulative effect was a major punishment and no inquiry was held before awarding such a punishment, therefore, the punishment awarded was clearly liable to be set aside.
4.
Mr. Jain, learned counsel for the respondent, has relied upon the judgment in Punjab State and others v. Ram Lubhaya, 1983(2) SLR 410, wherein it has been held that stoppage of increment with cumulative effect is a major punishment and no such punishment can be awarded unless a regular inquiry is conducted.
To the same effect is the judgment in Kulwant Singh Gill v. The State of Punjab, 1991(2) SCT 30 (SC) : 1991(1) RSJ 413 wherein it has been held by the Apex Court that stoppage of increment with cumulative effect would fall within the meaning of Rule 5(v) which require the conducting of a regular inquiry.
In the present case, as no regular inquiry was held before awarding the punishment of stoppage of one increment with cumulative effect, hence, the orders were clearly illegal.
5.
On the other hand, Mr. Ojha has relied upon the judgment in Sarwan Singh v. The State of Punjab and others, 1985(2) SLR 76, to contend that stoppage of increment with cumulative effect is not a major penalty.
6.
Keeping in view the dictum in Kulwant Singh Gill's case (supra) wherein it has clearly been held by the apex Court that stoppage of increment with cumulative effect would have to be construed as a major penalty and would necessarily require conducting of a regular inquiry, the judgment reported in Sarwan Singh's case (supra) does not hold good.
In view of the above, there is no merit in the present revision and the same is dismissed.
Petitiion dismissed.
