R K Patra, J - By this petition under Article 226 of the Constitution of India the petitioner seeks for a direction to the State of Orissa represented by the Secretary, Higher Education and Director, Higher Education (opposite parties 1 and 2) to grant him senior scale of pay and selection grade of pay of lecturer with effect from 1-1-1986 and 11-10-1987 respectively.
2.
The case of the petitioner is that he joined as a lecturer in Oriya in D. A. V. College, Titilagarh, an aided education institution on 11-10-1971.
Later he was transferred to KBDAV College, Nirakarpur where he served till 31-7-1986.
He was subsequently transferred to U N College, Soro and to Rairangpur college and is continuing as such in that college.
As per the U G C guidelines, the case of every lecturer for senior scale and selection grade/reader scale is to be considered if he/she has completed 8 years of service after regular appointment and he/she has consistently satisfactory performance appraisal reports.
He claims that he has unblemished service record, inasmuch as, nothing adverse against him was ever communicated.
Although he is entitled to senior scale and selection grade scale of pay, he was not given that benefit, but lecturers junior to him have been favoured with the same.
3.
The opposite parties 1 and 2 have filed counter affidavit.
Their case is that para 13 of the U G C Guidelines issued in Government Order No. F-1- 21/87 SI dated 22-7-1983 deals with grant of senior scale, selection grade/reader scale of pay to the lecturers.
It provides that "every lecturer would be placed in senior scale of Rs. 3,000/- to Rs. 5,000/- and in the selection grade/reader scale of Rs. 3,700/- to Rs. 5,700/- if he/she has :  (a) completed 8 years of service after regular appointment, with relaxation as provided in para 12 thereof; (b) participation in the refresher course/summer institutions, each of approximately 4 weeks' duration or engaged in other appropriate continuing education programmes of comparable quality as may be specified by the U G C; (c) consistently satisfactory performance appraisal reports."
Keeping in view the above provision the State Government in the Education and Youth Services Department issued resolution No. 9491-SYS dated 10-3-1990 (Annexure-4) laying down the criteria and conditions of eligibility of placement in the senior scale and selection grade (reader).
In paragraph-4(b) of the said resolution, it has been sentenced (prescribed ?)
that a lecturer for being eligible for consideration by the screening committee for being placed in the senior scale shall satisfy the following conditions :-  "(a) xx xx xx (b) A consistent satisfactory performance appraisal reports."
The screening committee which was constituted for the purpose of consideration found that the petitioner has adverse remarks in the C. C. A. for the years 1987-88, 1988-89 and 1989-90.
In view of the aforesaid adverse entries, the petitioner has not been considered to be placed in the senior scale of pay and selection grade scale of pay and his juniors who have an adverse entry have been placed in the senior scale and selection grade scale of pay.
4.
Shri Das, learned counsel for the petitioner stated that in the meantime the petitioner has been granted his senior scale of pay by Government notification dated 21-8-1996 and he has retired in October, 2001 on attaining the age of superannuation.
His submission is that the adverse entries having been not communicated to him cannot be utilised to deny him the senior scale or selection grade scale.
His further submission is that since he has already been given senior scale of pay with effect from 21-8-1996, the adverse entries, if any, have lost their significance and as such, he should be given selection grade scale with effect from 11-10-1987.
In this connection, he placed reliance on the decisions of this Court in Shuvansu Kumar Mohanti v. State of Orissa, (1990) 89 Cut LT 634 : (1990 Lab IC NOC 111) and judgment dated 11-11-1996 in CJC No. 6884 of 1993 (Subir Das v. State of Orissa) and decisions of the Supreme Court in Vijay Kumar v. State of Maharashtra, AIR 1988 SC 2060 : (1989 Lab IC 45); Baidyanath Mahapatra v. State of Orissa, AIR 1989 SC 2218 : (1989 Lab IC 2089); Amar Kant Choudhary v. State of Bihar, AIR 1984 SC 531 : (1984 Lab IC 291); Gurdial Singh v. State of Punjab, AIR 1979 SC 1633 : (1979 Lab IC 1186); State of UP v. Yamuna Shankar Misra, 1997(2) SCT 234 (SC) : (1997) 4 JT (SC) 1 : AIR 1997 SC 3671 and Brij Nath Pandey v. State of UP, 2000(4) SCT 957 (SC) : (2000) 9 JT (SC) 464.
5.
Upon hearing counsel for parties, we have no hesitation to hold that the writ petition is bound to succeed on a short ground.
Admittedly, during the pendency of this writ petition vide Government Notification dated 21-8-1996 the petitioner has been placed in the senior scale of pay.
From this it follows that the adverse entries, if any, against him have lost their sting'.
We may note that the adverse entries for the relevant period which have been extracted in the counter affidavit do not speak his dishonesty or lack of integrity.
A three-Judge Bench of the Supreme Court in Baikunth Nath Das v. Chief District Medical Officer, 1992(2) SCT 92 (SC) : AIR 1992 SC 1020 : (1992 Lab IC 945) in paragraph 32(iv) of the judgment has observed as follows :-  ".......If a government servant is promoted to a higher post notwithstanding the adverse remarks, such remarks lose their sting, more so, if the promotion is based upon merit selection and not upon seniority."
A somewhat similar question came up for consideration before the Supreme Court in Brij Nath Pandey v. State of UP, (2000) JT (SC) 464.
In that case, the question arose whether after the concerned officer was allowed to cross the efficiency bar, his past adverse remarks can stand on the way of his fresh consideration for promotion.
The Court observed as follows :  ".
. . . . . . . . . . . . .In our view this contention of the appellant is correct and the adverse entries in 1985-86 and 1986-87 cannot come in the way of the appellant for further promotion once he was allowed to cross the efficiency bar on 20-6-92."
6.
For the reasons aforesaid, we have no hesitation to hold that the adverse entries, if any, against the petitioner have paled into insignificance in view of the subsequent Government order granting his senior scale of pay.
Therefore, those adverse entries no more stand on his way for getting the selection grade scale of pay from due date.
We order accordingly.
The State Government is hereby directed to pass appropriate orders keeping in view the observations made above within one month of receipt of writ and pay him the differential amount as expeditiously as possible preferably by the end of November, 2002.
The Writ Petition is allowed.
Petition allowed.
