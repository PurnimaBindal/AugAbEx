Virender Singh, J - Suresh Kumar Monga, the respondent herein is the plaintiff in the civil suit and the State of Haryana who is appellant herein is the defendant (hereinafter to be referred to as plaintiff and defendant respectively).
2.
In short, the facts of the case are that the plaintiff has been a member of Indian Administrative Service in State of Haryana.
He was a member of Haryana Civil Services (Executive Branch) prior to his appointment.
He was promoted in the Selection Grade of HCS (Executive Branch) vide Haryana Government Notification No. 33/1/88-S(I) dated 1981983 w.e.f.
1591982.
Since the vacancy of Selection Grade of HCS against which the plaintiff was required to be considered and promoted became available in the year 1978, he made a representation to the defendant-State in the matter of Selection Grade to him in HCS cadre from 1978 when the vacancy had become available.
The defendant found the representation of the plaintiff as correct and promoted him to the Selection Grade of HCS (Executive Branch) w.e.f.
20.3.1978 vide Haryana Government order No. 33/1/8-S(I) dated 781995.
The promotion of the plaintiff was ante dated from 1591982 to 20.3.1978 as per the order dated 781995.
Consequent upon ante dated promotion of the plaintiff, refixation of pay of the plaintiff was made, The defendant vide memo No. HCS (S-42)/95- 2SIII dated 1491995 refixed the pay of the plaintiff from 20.3.1978 onwards in the selection grade of HCS.
Thereafter, the plaintiff vide order dated 20.9.1995 wrote to all the departments where he remained posted from March, 1978 onwards for withdrawal of arrears of pay and allowances as per the entitlement of the plaintiff.
He got the arrears of Rs. 1,41,47141 pertaining to the period 1977-78 to 1990-91 and during the period of 1995-96 since the arrears of pay and allowances of the plaintiff were delayed from 5 to 17 years, the plaintiff submitted a representation dated 1221997 requesting the defendant to allow him interest at the rate of 18% per annum for with-holding these arrears due to non-sanctioning of selection grade in time.
He also sent reminders and ultimately on 1921998, the request of the plaintiff was declined for allowing interest on pay and allowances.
Thus, the plaintiff was constrained to file the present suit.
3.
The learned trial Court vide judgment and decree dated 13.2.2002 partly decreed the suit declaring that the impugned order dated 1921998 was illegal, null and void and held that the plaintiff is entitled to interest at the rate of 12% per annum on an amount of Rs. 1,41,47141 from 20.3.1978 till its actual payment.
4.
Defendant-State thereafter preferred an appeal against the said judgment and decree of the learned Civil Judge (Junior Division), Chandigarh and the same was dismissed vide impugned judgment dated 262003 passed by the learned Additional District Judge.
Hence, this appeal by the State of Haryana.
5.
I have heard Ms. Geeta Mathuria, Assistant Advocate General, Haryana.
The judgments of both the Courts below have also been perused by me minutely.
6.
The main argument advanced by the learned State counsel is that both the Courts below have failed to appreciate that the vacancies of Selection Grade of HCS (Executive Branch) were not available prior to the judgment in LPA which was finally decided on 23111994 and as such the cause of action had accrued to the plaintiff-respondent for the first time on 23111994 as the grant of Selection Grade was linked with the decision of LPA.
She then contends that there was no delay in granting the Selection Grade after the decision of the LPA and as such the plaintiff-respondent is not entitled to any interest on account of delayed payment.
Learned State counsel then contends that in case the relief claimed by the plaintiff-respondent is granted to him, it would amount to lending handle to the other persons similarly situated.
7.
I do not agree with the contentions raised by the learned State counsel.
The learned trial Court while referring to LPA which was ultimately decided in 1994 has observed as under :-  "This plea of the defendant is most strange since Nathi Ram Goel who was the petitioner in C.W.P.
No. 1174 of 1981 has been granted Selection Grade vide order dated 30787 even before the decision of the LPA but in view of this LPA the representation of the plaintiff has been kept pending from 28388 till 31795.
Even the LPA has been dismissed vide judgment Ex. D3.
On 231194 again defendant has taken a period of eight months to decide the representation of the plaintiff and to pass the necessary Order Ex. PW1/E vide which the plaintiff has been promoted to Selection Grade w.e.f.
20378 is dated 31795.
It is most unusual that the department has promoted Nathi Ram Goel and five of his senior officers to the Selection Grade on 30787 but in view of the pendency of LPA in Nathi Ram Goel's case has withheld the promotion of the plaintiff till the year 1995.
The order Ex. D2 vide which Nathi Ram Goel has been promoted reveals that the said order is subject to decision of LPA No. 1195 of 1985.
Defendant has failed to give any explanation as to why a similar order could not be passed in the case of the plaintiff i.e. subject to decision of LPA Hence I find no merit in this plea of the defendants and the same is unsustainable.
An inordinate delay has been caused in the case of the plaintiff by the defendant in passing the order of promotion resulting in great financial loss to him.
The plaintiff was entitled to the promotion in the Selection Grade from 20378 but has been granted the same on 31795.
Though the plaintiff has been paid the arrears due to him but he has suffered a loss of interest as he could have invested the money soundly if the same would have been received by him in time."
8.
The finding returned by the learned trial Court is reaffirmed by the learned Appellate Court.
9.
Both the Courts below have rightly granted the interest at the rate of 12% per annum on the above said amount of Rs. 1,41,47141P.
No question of law muchless substantial question of law is involved in this case.
The present appeal is consequently dismissed being devoid of any merit.
However, any observation made herein would not construe as precedent for other cases.
Appeal dismissed.
