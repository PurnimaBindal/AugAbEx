RS Mongia, J - This judgment will dispose of C.W.P.
No. 2141 of 1982 and C.W.P.
No. 3313 of 1982.
2.
Facts of C.W.P.
No. 2141 of 1982 :  The petitioners were appointed as Masters in the Education Department of the State of Punjab on 26.11.1956, 681954 and 481956 respectively.
In 1964, the State of Punjab created a number of posts of School Lecturers, to be filled from amongst the Masters and other qualified persons who possessed the qualifications of MA The petitioners appeared in the open competition and they were selected for the posts of Lecturers through the Punjab Public Service Commission and were appointed as such on 8th April, 1964.
While holding the posts of the Lecturers, the petitioners were provisionally appointed to the posts of Head Masters on 2221973, 1951970 and 15121972 respectively.
The dates of their birth as given by the petitioners in Annexure P-8 are 1931923, 981928 and 2481930 respectively.
3.
Facts of C.W.P.
No. 3313 of 1982:  The petitioner was appointed as a Master on 31st Aug, 1964 and was selected as Lecturers in History vide order dated 6th Dec, 1973.
He represented that he should be considered and promoted as Lecturer from the date of his passing the MA Examination.
This representation of the petitioner was accepted and he was declared to have been promoted as Lecturer in History with effect from 19th June, 1967.
Since candidates from the Punjab Public Service Commission were not available for being appointed as Head Masters in various schools, it was decided by the Director, Public Instructions (Schools) to fill up the posts of Head Masters provisionally from amongst the Masters in their own pay and grade.
Since the petitioner was holding lien on the post of Master, he was also provisionally posted on the post of a Head Master in his own pay scale on 30th Sep, 1974, vide Annexure P2.
The petitioner had made a representation dated 22nd Aug, 1978 for fixation of his seniority in School Lecturers' cadre.
Accordingly, his seniority was fixed at Serial No. 708 in that cadre.
Accordingly, his seniority was fixed at Serial No. 708 in that cadre, whereas respondent No. 3's seniority in the School Lecturers cadre was fixed at Serial No. 792.
4.
It may be observed that both the petitioner and respondent No. 3 belong to Scheduled Castes category.
The date of birth of the petitioner is 1511943.
5.
The Masters are eligible for promotion to the posts of Head Master as well as to the posts of principles.
The Lecturers are also eligible for promotion to the posts of Principals.
As observed above sometime the Lecturers were also offered the posts of Head Master in exigency of service.
By letter dated 24th January, 1978 as also by letter dated 10th May, 1978, respondent No. 2 had asked for the options from all the Lecturers whether they were working as Lecturers or were holding the post of Headmasters and as to whether they would like to be confirmed in the cadre of Lecturers or in the cadre of Masters.
Further whether for the purpose of promotion to Class II service, they would like to maintain the seniority in the cadre of Lecturers or they would be liked to be considered for the post of Principal from cadre of Head Masters.
It is the case of all the petitioners that they had opted to remain in the cadre of Lecturers and be considered for promotion to the post of Principal from that cadre.
It has been further alleged that vide letter dated 2nd February, 1981, the Director Public Instructions (Schools) had asked the concerned District Education Officers to send the Confidential Reports in respect of the petitioners so that their cases could be considered for promotion to PES Class-II.
Petitioner Nos. 2 and 3 in C.W.P.
No. 2141 of 1982 were promoted on 12th October, 1981 to PES Class-II (Schools and Inspection Cadre) as Principals and School Lecturers respectively.
However, the order of promotion was cancelled on 17th November, 1981 (Annexure P10) before these petitioners could join the promoted ranks.
As far as the petitioner in the other writ petition is concerned, he was not promoted to PES Class-II, on the ground that he was working as a Head Master and was not in line of promotion in the Lecturers' cadre.
It is this action of the respondents of not promoting or cancelling the promotion that has been challenged in these writ petitions.
6.
It may be noticed at the outset that petitioners in C.W.P.
No. 2141 of 1982 have since retired.
All the petitioners had pleaded that they had given options to be treated in the cadre of Lecturers though this fact has been denied by the respondents that any options were given.
However, one fact is clear that the options were asked for from the petitioners in the year 1978.
In the year 1981 in the case of the writ petitioners in the first writ petition and in the case of writ petitioner in the second case it was in the year 1981 and also on 25.1.1982 that the respondents had asked for the records of the petitioners for being considered for promotion to PES Class-II on the basis of their turn in the Lecturers' cadre.
From this a reasonable inference can be drawn that the petitioners had in fact opted for being considered in the cadre of Lecturers; otherwise there was no occasion to send for their confidential reports for being considered for promotion to PES Class-II from the Lecturers' cadre.
The consideration of the petitioners for promotion could not be denied on the ground that they were holding the posts of Head Masters.
As observed above, they were provisionally holding the posts of Head Masters, but they were never treated to have severed their connections on the posts of Lecturers.
7.
For the foregoing reasons, these writ petitions are allowed.
The order of cancellation of promotion dated 17th November, 1981 (Annexure P10) of petitioner Nos. 2 and 3 in C.W.P.
No. 2141 of 1982, is hereby quashed, and they would be deemed to be promoted to PSE Class-II with effect from 12th October, 1981 when their order of promotion was issued.
As far as petitioner No. 1 is concerned, his case may be considered for promotion retrospectively from the date any of his junior in the Lecturer cadre was promoted in PSE Class-II.
Similarly, the case of petitioner in C.W.P.
No. 3313 of 1982 be also considered for promotion to PSE Class-II from the date his junior was promoted in the Lecturers' Cadre.
The petitioners would be entitled to all consequential benefits.
There will be no order as to costs.
Order accordingly.
