THB Chalapathi, J - The petitioner filed this writ petition for issuance of a writ of mandamus directing the respondents to regularise his services as per the decision of the Supreme Court and also the instructions of the State Government.
2.
The petitioner has been appointed on daily wage in the office of respondents No. 3 on muster rolls without issuing any order of appointment during the years 1980 to 1994.
According to him, he remained in service till 31st of May, 1994 and thereafter he has not been given any muster roll.
Some other similarly situated persons approached this Court and their writ petitions were allowed.
The services of the petitioner were not regularised, but the services of the persons junior to him have been regularised.
In order to seek regularisation of his services, the petitioner filed a writ petition bearing No. 2339 of 1990.
By an order dated Feb 26, 1990 this Court passed the following order :-  "The petitioner is seeking regularisation of his services in view of Piara Singh's case (1988(4) SLR 739) without making any representation.
He may make a representation to the authority, who will pass appropriate order on the same within six months thereof.
Status quo regarding service to continue from the date of representation.
This petition is disposed of accordingly."
After passing the said order, the petitioner filed a representation to the respondents, but they removed him from service.
That necessitated the petitioner to file a contempt petition which has been disposed of on the statement of the Counsel for the respondents to take this petitioner in service.
3.
On behalf of the respondents, a written statement is filed admitting that the petitioner was employed as casual labourer purely on daily wage basis and it is also contended that the petitioner is paid for the days he is present on duty.
His services are not regular and since he is only casual labourer on daily wage, he is not entitled for regularisation.
He further stated that in compliance with the order of this Court referred to above, the case of the petitioner was considered for regularisation in the light of the decision of the Supreme Court in State of Haryana v. Piara Singh and his services were not regularised since he has not completed 240 days in a year during the period of five years from 1988 to 1993 and therefore he is not entitled to have his services regularised.
Therefore, his writ petition is liable to be dismissed.
4.
In his replication, the petitioner reiterated his pleas taken in the written statement.
There is no dispute of the fact that the petitioner has been working as Casual Labourer on daily wages.
The Government of Haryana on the basis of the decision of the Supreme Court in State of Haryana v. Piara Singh, 1992(3) RSJ 235 : 1992(3) SCT 201 (SC), issued instructions on 2751993 vide Annexure R-1 in regard to regularisation of work charge/casual/daily rated employees.
According to those instructions, the casual and daily rated employees who have completed 5 years of continuous service on 31st March, 1993 and were in service on that date shall be regularised.
On regularisation, they shall be put in the time scale of pay applicable to the lowest Group `D' cadre in the Government and they would be entitled to all other allowances and benefits available to regular Govt. servants of the corresponding grade.
Subsequently, a clarification was issued by the Chief Secretary to Government, Haryana in his circular letter dated 18.3.1994 which is marked as Annexure R-2.
According to this letter, the Government has decided that those daily wage employees who have worked for 240 days in a year should be regularised, but there should not be a break in service for more than 30 days at a time in a year.
Thus it is clear that for getting the benefit of regularisation of services in the Policy of the Government, the employee must have worked for a period of 240 days in a year without any break of more than 30 days.
5.
It is, therefore, to be seen whether the petitioner fulfilled the conditions laid down in the circular letter referred to above so as to enable him to have the benefit of regularisation.

6.
The learned Counsel for the petitioner placed reliance on a decision of thin Court in Giri Raj and others v. State of Haryana and others, 1997(2) RSJ 506 to which I am a party.
In that case, the petitioners have fulfilled the condition of working 240 days in a year.
The break in service for more than 30 days was held not to come in the way of regularisation of the petitioners and in that case the break was not on account of the fault of the petitioners therein, but due to banning on muster rolls for that month.
In the decision, it had also not been held that those petitioners who have not completed for 240 days are entitled to regularisation.
The learned counsel for the petitioner further relied upon a decision of this Court in Ram Dhari v. The Chief Wild Life Warden, Haryana, 1996(1) RSJ 246 : 1996(1) SCT 614 (P&H)(DB).
In that case, the petitioners completed more than 240 days during the period of five years from 1988 to 1993 and that too without any break in service.
Since the petitioner in that case worked for more than 240 days and there was no break in service during the period of five years, his writ petition was rightly allowed by this Court directing the respondents to consider his case for regularisation.
7.
In this case, the petitioner has not fulfilled the primary condition that he was in service for 240 days or more in a year.
Since that condition is not fulfilled, he cannot take any benefit of the instructions issued by the Haryana Government vide Annexure R-2.
Since he has not completed 240 days service during the period of five years prior to 3131993, he is not entitled to have his services regularised.
I do not, therefore, see any merit in this writ petition.
8.
The result is, the petition fails and is, accordingly, dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
