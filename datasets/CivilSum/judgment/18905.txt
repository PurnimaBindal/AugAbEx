JS Narang, J - This judgment shall dispose of CWPs No. 780, 829 of 1987, 10643, 10791 and 11459 of 1998, as the common question of law is involved in all the said petitions.
CWP No. 780 of 1987 had been listed for regular hearing on the board of Hon'ble JS Khehar, J and vide order dated 2291999, it had been released from his Lordship's board and was ordered to be heard with CWP No. 10643 of 1998.
Facts are being taken from CWP No. 10643 of 1998.
2.
The petitioner was appointed as a Clerk in the respondent-department on 1471976.
He passed the Assistant Grade Examination on 981985 and as per the respondents, the result thereof was received on 491985.
The petitioner was promoted as Assistant on 3011986.
According to the averments made in para 3 of the writ petition, certain vacancies in the rank of Assistants were available.
There were seven persons who were senior to the petitioner as Clerks but had not qualified the Assistant Grade Examination which was a pre- requisite for being promoted as an Assistant.
Some seniors to the petitioner in the rank of Clerk passed the Assistant Grade Examination in March, 1986 and were promoted on 551986.
For some reasons, the petitioner was reverted as Clerk on 421987 (copy Annexure P3).
The said order is subject matter of challenge in Civil Writ Petition No. 780 of 1987 filed by the petitioner.
That case, as observed above, has been received by transfer and is being disposed of with this judgment.
Suffice it to say that on 2691995, copy Annexure P4, the petitioner was re-promoted as Assistant with effect from the date of his reversion i.e. 2421987.
However, the petitioner was allowed the arrears and the payment was made subject to the decision of Civil Writ Petition No. 780 of 1987.
The petitioner was again served with a show cause notice dated 141998, copy Annexure P8, as to why he should not be reverted.
The main reason of effecting reversion of the petitioner mentioned in the show cause notice is that since he was earlier reverted vide order dated 421987 and there is no provision for retrospective promotion, he was liable to be reverted.
The petitioner had filed his reply to the show cause notice vide Annexures P10 and P11 dated 24.4.1998 and 28.4.1998, respectively.
However, the said reply did not find favour with the official respondents and the impugned order dated 671998, copy Annexure P12, reverting the petitioner has been passed.
The said order has been made the subject matter of challenge.
Notice of motion was issued and reversion of the petitioner was stayed.
Respondents have filed the reply and the petitioner has filed replication.
3.
We have heard the learned counsel for the parties.
4.
The facts which need be highlighted are that petitioner passed the Assistant Grade Examination on 981985.
Some posts of Assistant had fallen vacant and became available in January, 1986 when petitioner was promoted against the said posts along with some others.
Some persons who were Senior as Clerks were not promoted along with the petitioner as they had not qualified the examination, which they passed in March 1986 and they were promoted on 551986.
5.
The question that arises for consideration is that if on the date when the vacancies are available, the promotions are sought to be made and eligible persons are available and some of the persons who may be senior are not available on that date, the result would be that the juniors who are qualified would steal march over the seniors, being eligible for promotion.
If the seniors get the requisite qualifications and become eligible later on, the juniors who might have been earlier promoted against the available vacancies, are not supposed to become senior to the said juniors.
It may be emphasised that the instructions had been issued by the Government in the Personnel and Administrative Reforms and Development Department vide communication dated 1861987, copy Annexure P6.
It reads as under (relevant portion) :-  "It is intimated that a junior person who had already passed the Assistant Grade Examination and who had been promoted as Assistant is not to be reverted in order to give promotion to a senior person on passing the Assistant Grade Examination."
To the same effect is the communication dated 29.4.1988, Copy Annexure P7, from the same department to Director Education Department, Punjab, Chandigarh.
The relevant portion reads as under :-  "xx xxx xxx xx It is intimated that as per the policies of the Government if a person passes the test earlier and is promoted as Assistant then senior person who passes the test subsequently and becomes eligible for promotion, is not to be given promotion by reverting junior person.
A person who passes the test later on can only be given promotion when the vacancy arises.
Therefore, if vacancies are decreased then the persons who are promoted last of all can only be reverted."
6.
It may be again highlighted here that the petitioner was promoted on 3011986 as Assistant along with seven other Clerks.
The persons which were ten in number who were senior as Clerks but having qualified the Assistant Grade Examination later, were promoted on 551986 (Refer to Annexure P2).
If for some reasons, some seniors have been left out who might have passed the examination later than the petitioner or for any reason the vacancies decreased in the rank of Assistant, it is the later promotee who will have to go first.
Admittely in the present case, about ten Clerks were promoted as Assistants vide order dated 551986.
The instructions from the Personnel Department dated 17/1861987 and 29.4.1988 (Annexures P6 and P7) to which reference has already been made above is in consonance with what we have observed above, which is not even being disputed now before us.
Is it that some persons who might be otherwise senior to the petitioner in the rank of Clerks and had qualified Assistant Grade Examination later than the petitioner are to be given promotion prior to the petitioner or the petitioner be asked to make room for any of them ?
As observed above, if for some reasons, the reversions are to be effected because of paucity of posts or posts having been decreased, the principle i.e. "last come first go" would apply, meaning thereby, whosoever was promoted later will have to be reverted, irrespective of his seniority in the rank of Clerk.
7.
The learned counsel for the State has argued that the petitioner had no right to continue on the post of Senior Assistant when senior persons to the petitioner who were otherwise eligible for promotions were working as Clerks.
The petitioner could have been promoted to the rank of Senior Assistant only when he had passed the Assistant Grade Examination and had also acquired the requisite experience and a regular vacancy was available on that day.
Thus, the petitioner had been rightfully reverted to the post of Clerk vide order dated 421987.
(The said order of reversion is the subject of matter of challenge in CWP 780 of 1987).
8.
Learned counsel for the petitioner had filed CM 23829 of 1999 along with various documents reflecting the status of newly created posts and the resultant vacancies occurring from time to time.
The State has also filed reply to the said application.
As such, the status of newly created posts vis-a-vis vacancies of Senior Assistants from time to time can be duly elicited from the said documents and the averments contained in the application.
9.
Our attention was drawn to the copy of the order dated 26121984 issued by Government of Punjab to the Chief Engineer, Copy Annexure P14.
This document shows that apart from some other posts, 18 posts of Assistants and Accounts Clerks were created in the concerned department.
Subsequently, vide office order dated January 4, 1995, copy Annexure P15, six Senior Accounts Clerks had been promoted as officiating Superintendent Grade-IV meaning thereby 8 more posts of Assistants became vacant (the posts of Senior Accounts Clerk and the posts of Assistants are equivalent).
By virtue of these two documents, 26 posts became available in the rank of Assistant/Senior Accounts Clerks.
It was on 7th of January 1985 by virtue of an office order of the same date, 23 Clerks were promoted as officiating Assistants/Senior Accounts Clerks.
A copy of the order dated 711985 has been annexed as Annexure P16, a perusal of which shows that the promotion of these 23 persons had been made on provisional basis till the said persons pass the Assistant Grade Examination under the Punjab State Assistant Grade Examination Rules, 1984 (hereinafter referred to as the Rules).
Out of these 23 persons, a person had refused to accept the officiating promotions which is evident from the office order dated 1521985, copy Annexure P17, vide which 10 more Senior Clerks were promoted as officiating Assistants/Senior Accounts Clerks.
These 10 persons were also not qualified as they had not passed the Assistant Grade Examination prescribed under the rules.
It was specifically mentioned that these promotions had been made till the regularly selected persons are appointed and the promotees shall not have any claim vis-a-vis seniority etc. Again vide office order dated March 22, 1985, copy Annexure P18, five more Senior Clerks were given officiating promotions to the rank of Assistants, Accounts Clerks and in this very order, it has been noticed that four Senior Clerks who had been given officiating promotions vide order dated 711985 (sic) had foregone their promotions.
In other words, 12 persons out of 33 persons who had been given the officiating promotions vide order dated 711985 and 1521985 Annexures P16 and P17 respectively, had not accepted the promotions.
In nutshell, against 26 vacancies which had occurred upto January 4, 1985, had been filled up by way of promoting unqualified persons on officiating basis.
The petitioner along with other seven persons were promoted on January 30, 1986, copy Annexure P19 and vide the same order six persons who had been earlier given officiating promotions and were not qualified were reverted.
Thereafter, vide order dated May 5, 1986, copy Annexure P20, 10 more persons who were fully qualified by way of passing the requisite examination later than the petitioner, had been promoted, though otherwise the said ten persons were senior to the petitioner as Clerks.
By the same order dated 551986, seven unqualified persons were reverted.
Another order dated 2661986, copy Annexure P21 was passed vide which Shri Surinder Pal Singh (petitioner in CWP No. 11459 of 1998) who had qualified the examination was promoted.
Thus the total mathematical projections which emerged by virtue of the communication, referred to above, against 26 posts, 19 qualified persons had been promoted up to 1961986.
So far as the petitioner is concerned, he had been promoted vide order dated 3011986 and at that time 26 vacancies were available because after him 10 more persons were promoted who definitely qualified the Assistant Grade Examination later than the petitioner though otherwise senior to the petitioner in the rank of Clerk.
