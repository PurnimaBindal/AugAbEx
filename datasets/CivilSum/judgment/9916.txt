NK Kapoor, J - This is unsuccessful plaintiff's regular second appeal.
2.
Mahesh Kumar, ex-Constable, filed a suit for declaration to the effect that order dated 641984 passed by Superintendent of Police, Rohtak discharging him from police services under Punjab Police Rule 1221 is illegal, null and void and without jurisdiction.
As per facts, it was stated that he was enrolled as a Police Constable Bugler on 751980 and was posted in Police Line, Rohtak.
His record remained unblemished and he was awarded a cash award of Rs. 50/- for his good performance.
It is on 641984, when he had completed 4 years of service that he was discharged under Rule 1221, which was not applicable.
3.
Defendant put in appearance and filed written statement.
By way of preliminary objections it was stated that suit of the plaintiff does not lie in the present form and suit is not maintainable for non-joinder of the necessary parties.
On merit it was stated that plaintiff was recruited as a Bugler on 751980, he was sent for training but he did not show any interest in his training and so on the basis of the report of the Principal he was discharged from service under Rule 1221 vide order dated 961981.
Subsequently, on his representation he was again re-enlisted w.e.f.
2471981.
Again his overall record was not found to be satisfactory and so he was discharged vide order dated 641984 in terms of Punjab Police Rules 1221.
This way the defendant prayed for dismissal of the suit.
4.
The trial Court on the basis of evidence found no illegality in the order of discharge dated 641984.
Consequently, this issue was decided in favour of the defendant and against the plaintiff.
Resultantly, the suit of the plaintiff was ordered to be dismissed.
5.
The lower appellate Court once again examined the matter but found no ground to reverse or vary the judgment and decree of the trial Court.
Accordingly, the appeal too was dismissed.
6.
With a view to seek the reversal of the concurrent findings recorded by the Courts below, the learned counsel for the appellant after refering to the facts briefly, has argued that the Courts below have in fact erred in law in mis-construning the relevant provisions of the Punjab Police Rules.
According to the counsel, the petitioner joined the police service on 751980.
He had unblemished record and infact a commendation certificate has also been issued, yet for no good reason his services were terminated vide order dated 641984 when he had already completed 4 years of service.
This being so, the plaintiff's services could not be dispensed with in terms of Rule 1221 and so the judgments and decrees of the Courts below are liable to be reversed.
Counsel in support of his contentions placed reliance upon the following decisions:-  (1) Gurdev Singh, Constable v. The State of Punjab and others, 1982(2) SLR 365; (2) Punjab State and another v. Smt. Kamlesh Kumari, 1988(1) SLR 614; (3) Punjab State through Secretary to Government of Punjab, Home Department, Chandigarh and another v. Joginder Singh, Ex-Constable, 1989(3) SLR 665; and (4) Shishpal v. State of Haryana and others, 1991(4) SLR 9 : 1991(2) SCT 727(P&H).
7.
I have heard the learned counsel for the appellant, perused the judgments of the Courts below as well as the other documents referred to by the counsel.
Admittedly, petitioner was inducted in service as a Bugler on 751980.
He was sent for training at Police Training College, Madhuban.
He was ordered to be discharged under Punjab Police Rule 1221 vide order of the Superintendent of Police dated 961981.
This order of the discharge is not under challenge.
Subsequently, the plaintiff made a representation to the Superintendent of Police who took a compassionate view of matter and agreed to re-enlist him vide order dated 23.7.1981.
The operative part of the order reads as:-  "Therefore, in view of the above observations, I am inclined to give Sh. Mahesh Kumar another chance and recruit him again as a Constable.
He will be sent to PTC Madhuban for Buglar course commencing from 1st August, 1981.
Undertaking given by Sh. Mahesh Kumar is kept on the file."
8.
A bare perusal of the aforesaid part of the order makes it abundantly clear that Mahesh Kumar plaintiff was again taken as a Constable and pursuance to which he joined on 2471981.
Thus, during the period i.e. 961981 to 23.7.1981 he was not in service.
Since, he was re-enlisted from 2471981 the order of discharge dated 641984 is within the period of three years and so has been validly passed under Punjab Police Rule 1221.
In view of these clear facts none of the judgments cited by the counsel for the appellant have any applicability to the facts of the present case.
9.
In Gurdev Singh's case (supra) the Court was examining the provisions of Rules 12.21 and 1222 and it is in this context it was held that whereas the Superintendent of Police has power to discharge a Constable within three years in terms of Rule 1221, a Constable who had obtained a certificate, under Rule 1222 could not be dealt with under Rule 1221 i.e. to say he could not be removed.
As noticed earlier, the decision rendered by the Court has hardly any applicability in the context of the present case.
10.
Similarly, in Punjab State and another v. Smt. Kamlesh Kumari, 1988(1) SLR 614, Court was examining the provision of Rule 1221 in the context of Articles 16 and 311 and so the Court held that it can go behind an ostensibly innocuous order to find out the real nature of the order i.e. whether it is punitive in nature or otherwise discriminatory.
There is neither any pleading nor proof on record.
Otherwise too, this judgment stands impliedly overruled in view of the Full Bench decision in Sher Singh v. State of Haryana and others, 1994(1) PLR 456.
Similarly, the case Punjab State through Secretary to Government of Punjab, Home Department, Chandigarh and another v. Joginder Singh, Ex-Constable, 1989(3) SLR 665 is as per the facts of its own case and in no way helps the case of the appellants in any manner.
Similarly, the appellant cannot derive any benefit from the decision of this Court in Shishpal's case (supra) as the present case is not of reinstatement.
11.
In the present case fresh appointment has been made and so the period of three years is to be calculated from the date of fresh appointment to find out whether the order of discharge is in terms of Rule 1221.
The Court below on appraisal of the evidence have returned a positive finding to the effect that the order of termination is in conformity with the provision of Punjab Police Rule 1221.
This finding is not proved to be vitiated in any manner.
Consequently, finding no merit in this appeal the same is dismissed.
No order as to costs.
