AK Mathur, CJ.
- During the pendency of the appeal, the appellant-employee died and his heirs and legal representatives were brought on record.
2.
This is an appeal directed against the judgement dated March 19, 1981 passed by the learned single Judge in CR No. 9873(W) of 1978 whereby the learned single Judge dismissed the writ petition holding that whether the age given in the Matriculation Certificate is the correct date of birth or given in service record, are all questions of fact which this Court under Article 226 of the Constitution would not inquire.
3.
Brief facts which are necessary for disposal of this appeal are that the petitioner-appellant retired from service of Allahabad Bank in December 1978 on attaining the age of superannuation after having been given two extensions of one year each.
The writ petitioner filed the represent petition for a direction to the Bank to correct his date of birth in service record in conformity with the Matriculation Certificate.
The affidavit-in-opposition filed by the respondent Bank pointed out that the petitioner's date of birth had been recorded while he entered the Bank's service and that all the dates were filled in the petitioner himself.
Therefore, whatever date of birth had been recorded in the service record shall govern the service of the petitioner and subsequent application filed by him for correction of date of birth at the fag end of the service career cannot be entertained.
Learned Single Judge dismissed the writ petition holding that these are disputed questions of fact and that cannot be gone into under Writ jurisdiction.
4.
We have heard the learned Counsel for the parties.
It is an admitted fact that the petitioner while entering into service gave his date of birth as 1.1.1918.
Since the entries were made in his own hand-writing and he served the Bank with those entries and did not wake up to correct that date of birth till he attained the age of superannuation, therefore, now he cannot be permitted to turn back at the fag end of his career to challenge the entry in his service record of the date of birth.
The view taken by the learned Single Judge appears to be correct and there is no ground to interfere in the appeal.
5.
The appeal is hence dismissed.
6.
There will be no order as to costs.

Sinha, J -  7.
Although, I agree with the Judgment and Order pronounced by Hon'ble the Chief Justice, I would like to add a few words of mine.
8.
The appellant herein admittedly on the basis of the entries made in his Service Record was to superannuate in 1976.
He accepted the said fact and obtained an order of extension of service for one year.
He again obtained the second extension whereafter only he filed the writ application.
9.
The right to get the date of birth corrected either on the basis of Matriculation Certificate or otherwise is not a legal right far less a constitutional right.
10.
Even if such a right was a legal one the same could be waived and in fact has been waived as the same was not in public interest.
See Graphite India Ltd & Ann v. Durgapur Projects Ltd & Ors., reported in 1999(7) SCC 645.
11.
Furthermore, the appellant filed the writ application at the fag end of his career on the basis of a Matriculation Certificate.
The employer was not bound to correct his date of birth on the basis thereof has been held by a Division Bench of this Court in Calcutta Post Trust & Ors. v. Ajit Kumar Deb, reported in 1996 Lab.
I.C.
167 : 1996(4) SCT (Cal.)
(DB)(P&H).
12.
This aspect of the matter has also been considered by this court in Chittaranjan Das v. Durgapore Project Limited, reported in 99 CWN 897 and in Babu Ram Pandey v Mining & Allied Machinery Corporation & Ors., reported in 100 CWN 230.
13.
Yet again in Pratul Kr.
Mukherjee v Steel Authority of India & Ors., reported in 1996(1) C.H.N.
347 upon taking into consideration a large number of decisions including the decision of Dy.
Commissioner of Police, Special Branch & Ors. v. Bhupesh Chandra Karanjai, reported in 1993(2) C.L.J.
74, upon which strong reliance has been placed by Mr. Chakraborty this court noticed :  "Moreover, in the instant case the claim of the petitioner is a stale one.
Such a stale claim made after a long time may not be entertained by the employer.
This Court in such a situation would also refuse to exercise its extraordinary jurisdiction under Article 226 of the Constitution of India.
In Bhupendra Nath Chatterjee v. State of Bihar, reported in 1977(3) SCC 491, the Supreme Court has held that the date of birth recorded in the service records should be accepted as correct.
The said decision has recently been followed by a Division Bench of this Court in Steel Authority of India Ltd v. Abani Kumar Moitra, reported in 1995(1) C.L.J.
105."
14.
It had also taken into consideration in the Judgment the decision of the Apex Court to the effect in Union of India v. Harnam Singh, reported in 1993(2) SCC 162 : 1993(3) S.C.T.
120, State of Tamil Nadu v. TV Venugopalan, reported in 1994(6) SCC 302 : 1994(4) SCT 375 (SC), The Chief Medical Officer v. Khadeer Khadri, reported in AIR 1995 SC 850 : 1995(2) SCT 402 (SC) and Union of India v. Kantilal Hematram Pandya, reported in AIR 1995 SC 1349 : 1995(2) SCT 520 (SC)  15.
In a recent decision in The Chairman, Ad hoc Committee v. Subhendu Bikash Den & Ors., reported in 1998(4) S.L.R.
87 : 1998(4) SCT 467 (Calcutta), the same view has been reiterated by a Division Bench of this Court.
16.
We, therefore, find no merit in this appeal.
Appeal dismissed.
Appeal dismissed.
