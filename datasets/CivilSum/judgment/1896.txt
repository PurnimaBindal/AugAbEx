Facts are taken from Civil Writ Petition No. 8172 of 1990.
2.

4.
In the present writ petition, challenge is to order dated 1651990 on the ground that vide order dated 261989, while revising the pay scales, certain benefits were given and thereafter, petitioners had been enjoying the benefit of higher pay scale as a matter if right and that right cannot be taken away by issuing fresh order with retrospective effect, adversely affecting the petitioners and that too without affording them an opportunity of hearing.
It is stated that the decision taken by the State Government for confining the pay scale of Rs. 4100-5300/- to HCMS (Class I and II), Engineers and Deputy Superintendents, who had completed 12 years of service, to 20 per cent of the posts in the cadre is arbitrary and unreasonable.
5.
Upon notice of the writ petition respondents 1 to 4 in their written statement have stated that pay scales of Engineers and other important services of the State Government like Doctors (HCMS), Deputy Superintendent of Police, Civil Services (HCS) have been revised from time to time keeping in view the overall administrative requirements, job requirements and other related factors.
Further according to them, the members of the H.C.S.
(Executive Branch) in the State of Haryana were granted, as per rule 25(4) of the Punjab Civil Services (Executive Branch) Rules, 1930, the selection grade as fixed by the Government from time to time for 20 per cent of the cadre posts.
According to the respondents, whenever selection grade is granted to posts included in any cadre for any service, it is always confined to certain proportion of such cadre posts.
Further according to them, even in the case of Engineers, whenever selection grade was granted, it was confined to number of posts in that cadre.
According to the respondents, vide order dated 261989, AEE/AE/SDO/SDE Class I and Class II of the Engineering Branch were provided uniform pay scales of Rs. 2200-4000, 3000-4500 after 5 years and Rs. 4100-5300 after 12 years.
The pay scales were the same as were available to HCS (Executive Branch), viz. Rs. 2200-4000, Rs. 3000-4500 (Sr.
Scale) after five years, Rs. 4100-5300 (Selection grade) after 12 years confined to 20 per cent of the cadre posts.
Respondents have stated that while issuing order dated 261989, the Government by mistake failed to mention that the grade of Rs. 4100-5300 would be a selection grade and limited to 20 per cent of the cadre posts and would be given after 12 years of regular satisfactory services as is the case with HCS officers.
This omission when came to notice of the State was duly rectified by issuing order dated 1651990 (Annexure P-6).
Respondents have stated that if the omission had not been rectified, it would have resulted into utter administrative confusion and chaos because as from 151989 the pay scale of Executive Engineers and Superintending Engineers would have been Rs. 3000-4500 and Rs. 4100-5300 respectively.
According to the respondents, as per order dated 261989 every SDE with 12 years' service would have reached the pay scale of a Superintending Engineer without even being considered fit to hold the post of Executive Engineer and such an anomaly would have ruined the administrative machinery and structure inasmuch as the S.D.E.
with moderate and average record would have drawn salary higher than that of the Executive Engineer and equal to that Superintending Engineer under whose direction, supervision and administrative control, they were required to work.
Additionally, there would not have been any incentive for any SDE to really improve his performance to achieve better position/promotion as he would have been assured of higher salary and scale of Superintending Engineer without working for it.
Respondents have stated that earlier order dated 261989 was not in accordance with the decision of the Government and as such, the error had to be rectified and this action did not require any opportunity to be given to the petitioners before its issuance.
They have stated that the action of the Government in correcting the mistake was fair and reasonable.
6.
Learned counsel appearing on behalf of respondents has stated that the matter has come to be covered by a decision of the Hon'ble Supreme Court in Civil Appeal No. 6585 of 2000 in the case of State of Haryana v. Ishwar Singh and others.
Counsel submitted that order dated 1651990 which is the subject matter of the dispute in the present writ petition, has been held to be valid by the Hon'ble Supreme Court and therefore, the writ petition deserves to be dismissed.
7.
In answer to these submissions, learned counsel appearing on behalf of petitioners has cited detailed guide-lines, viz. No. 8/6/2001-5 PR (FD) dated 2042001, issued by the Finance Department, Government of Haryana, in matters involving pay revision and issue of anomalies related thereto.
A specific reference has been made to paragraphs 7 and 14 of the said guide-lines to contend that vide order dated 261989 the Government had decided to revise the pay scales of State Government employees broadly following the central pattern of pay scales.
This was a complete switch over from a "State-specific pay scale structure" to a "Centre-specific system/structure".
Counsel submitted that one of the relevant aspects encountered in the process was that the Fourth Pay Commission had reduced the number of pay scales and recommended only about 30 standard pay scales, and the system of grant of selection grade was also discontinued.
It is contended that adding of words "selection grade" and confining it to 20 per cent of the cadre posts was against the very instructions issued by the State Government.
8.
After hearing the learned counsel and going through the record, I do not find any merit in the contention of counsel for the petitioner.
The guide- lines cited by the counsel for the petitioners were issued by the Finance Department in the State of Haryana to remove certain doubts and to explain the subject of revision of pay scales and the issues pertaining to the so-called anomalies for the benefit of all concerned.
The said guide-lines are not at all relevant to the controversy raised in the writ petition.
9.
As regards order dated 1651990 (Annexure P-6), impugned in the writ petition, the matter was considered by the Supreme Court in Civil Appeal No. 6585 of 2000 arising out of SLP (Civil) No. 1463 of 2000.
Their Lordships of the Supreme Court while allowing the appeal directed that the selection grade Rs. 4100-5300/- shall be restricted to 20 per cent of the posts in the cadre as provided in clarification dated 1651990.
The order of the Supreme Court reads as under :-  "Special leave granted.
This is an appeal by the State of Haryana against the judgment of the High Court of Punjab and Haryana dated 23.9.1999 in CWP No. 18043/98.
The parity in pay was granted to the doctors/respondents category along with Executive Branch, by an order dated 2689 and when Selection Grade posts were also sanctioned at par with the Executive Branch.
The order unfortunately did not mention that the selection grade posts were restricted to 20% of the posts.
This mistake was, however, rectified by an order dated 16590 of the Government with retrospective affect 1589.
The High Court has now held that Selection Grade is to be granted without restriction to 20% of the posts in the particular cadre.
It is well known that in every department Selection Grade is restricted to certain percentage of the posts such as 20% in the present case.
So merely because the percentage was not mentioned in the earlier order dated 2689, the High Court could not have awarded Selection Grade to every member in the cadre.
The High Court should have seen that the mistake in the order dated 2689 was rectified.
We, therefore, allow the appeal and set aside the order of the High Court and direct that the Selection Grade scale of Rs. 4100-5300/- will be restricted to 20% of the posts in the cadre as provided in the clarification dated 16590.
The appeal is allowed accordingly.
There shall be no order as to costs."
Reading of the order of the Supreme Court makes it very clear that while taking decision for revision of pay scales of doctors and engineers in the year 1989, the intention of the Government was to bring pay scales at par with those of H.C.S.
(Executive Branch).
However, while issuing order dated 261989, the parity in pay scales was not clearly brought out and for that matter, order dated 1651990 was issued and the omission that crept in order dated 261989 was rectified.
The action of the State Government in correcting this mistake was absolutely just, fair, reasonable and in accordance with the principles of natural justice.
The submission of learned counsel for the petitioners to the contrary is not acceptable.
10.
Faced with this situation, learned counsel for the petitioners contended that if the intention of the Government was to bring parity in scales at par with those of H.C.S.
(Executive Branch), then why parity has not been maintained in the pay scales as revised with effect from 111996.
This contention urged by the counsel is not the subject matter of the writ petition.
Moreover, it is well-settled that determination of pay scale is primarily the function of the Executive and not the Judiciary and, therefore, ordinarily, this Court would not enter upon the task of job evaluation which is generally left to the expert bodies like Pay Commissions etc.
In case petitioners feel that they have been unjustly treated in the matter of pay scales revised with effect from 111996 as per recommendations of the Fifth Pay Commission, they are always at liberty to take appropriate steps in that direction.
11.
Learned counsel for the petitioner then contended that petitioners are not liable to refund the amount which they have received during the period from 151989 to 1651990, i.e. the date of issuance of order dated 1651990, because the amount paid to them for the said period was not on account of any misrepresentation or fraud committed by them.
In this regard, counsel cited a judgment of the Supreme Court in Sahib Ram v. State of Haryana, 1995(1) SCT 668, wherein their Lordships of the Supreme Court opined that if the salary on revised pay scale was paid to the applicant and it was not on account of any misrepresentation that the benefit of higher pay scale was given to him, then the applicant cannot be held to be at fault and the amount paid is not to be recovered from him.
12.
Likewise, in Union of India v. M Bhaskar and Others, 1996(4) SCT 56, the Supreme Court held that if an employed was wrongly given a pay scale by the Authorities and later on he was not held entitled to it, the amount already paid on account of pay scale wrongly given should not be recovered because it would cause hardship to him.
No judgment to the contrary has been cited by the counsel for the respondents.
13.
In view of the law laid down by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in the afore- mentioned two judgments and the fact that amount, if any paid to the petitioners in pursuance of order dated 261989 was not on account of any misrepresentation or fraud committed by them but was on account of mistake of the part of the Government, the amount if actually paid to the petitioners before issuance of order dated 1651990 is not liable to be recovered from them.
14.
Learned counsel for the petitioners further contended that while admitting the writ petitions this Court had stayed the operation of order dated 1651990 and the petitioners have been paid the amount in accordance with the pay scales mentioned in order dated 261989.
He contended that if the amount is now ordered to be recovered from the petitioner, the same shall cause great hardship to them.
He contended that some of the petitioners may have died or some may have retired and if the recovery of the amount already paid is ordered to be made from the legal representatives or from the pension of the retired petitioners, that would cause great hardship to them.
He contended that as far as these two categories are concerned, respondents may be directed not to recover the amount already paid to them.
In answer to these submissions, counsel for the respondents contended that the mistake was rectified by the State Government vide order dated 1651990 and considering the hardship which the employees may face on account of dues recoverable from them, it was specifically mentioned in para 6 of the said order that any dues recoverable from the employees on account of order dated 1651990 shall be recovered in easy instalments subject to a maximum of 36 instalments.
Counsel contended that petitioners cannot be allowed to take benefit of the interim order which was granted in their favour at the time of admission of the writ petition.
He contended that on account of the said order, the State Government has been burdened with an extra expenditure of Rs. 20 lacs per month.
15.
After hearing the counsel on this aspect of the case, I am of the view that petitioners are not entitled to any equitable consideration.
Petitioners are drawing the extra amount because of interim order passed by this Court, though the mistake that crept in order dated 261989 was corrected by issuing ordered dated 1651990.
However, considering that some of the petitioners may have died and burden to return the amount may fall on their legal representatives, it would be in the fitness of things if the matter in this regard is left to the State Government to take a decision regarding waiving off the amount already paid to them.
As regard the petitioners who may have retired or are serving, they are liable to pay the amount which they continued to receive on account of interim order passed by this Court, because the interim order, if any passed, is always subject to the final decision of the writ petition.
Resultantly, the writ petition is disposed of with the following directions to the respondents :-  (1) The writ petition so far as it challenges the selection grade of Rs. 4100-5300 after 12 years of regular satisfactory service and limited to 20 per cent of the cadre posts, is without any merit and order dated 1651990 to that extent is held valid.
(2) Amount, if actually paid to the petitioners on account of order dated 261989 but before issuance of order dated 1651990, shall not be recovered.
(3) The Government shall take a decision within three months from the date of receipt of the order in regard to waiving off the amount qua the petitioners who have since died.
(4) The pension received by the petitioners who have since retired and the family pension received by the legal representatives of the deceased- petitioners shall be refixed in accordance with order dated 1651990.
(5) Any dues recoverable from the petitioners, who may have retired or are serving, on account of order dated 1651990 shall be recovered in easy instalments to be fixed by the Government.
There shall be no orders as to costs.
Order accordingly.
