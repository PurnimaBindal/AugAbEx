TS Doabia, J - Heard.
Learned counsel for the petitioner has argued vis-a-vis one point only i.e. withdrawal of increments.
2.
Vide Annexure-L dated 2531974, the petitioner was allowed two increments.
These were allowed in terms of the earlier decision taken by the State Government on 12111959.
This decision is to the effect that an employee passing accounts examination would earn two increments.
This was an above incentive to the employees passing accounts examination.
As indicated about all employees passing these tests became entitled to two increments in terms of the decision taken by the State Government on 12111959.
These two increments given to the petitioner are sought to be withdrawn by order (Annexure-P) dated 2881985.
It is basically this order which is being challenged under Article 226 of the Constitution of India.
3.
Learned counsel for the petitioner submits that there is no valid reason for withdrawal of the order which was passed on 2531974.
There is merit in the contention of the petitioner.
In Annexure-P, it is mentioned that by the grant of these two increments, the petitioner would start getting more emoluments than his senior colleague.
I am of the view that this reason cannot come to the rescue of the respondents to uphold the impugned order.
The petitioner was entitled to these two increments in terms of the decision taken by the State Government on 12111959.
4.
If this is the position, then the conclusion that the petitioner was rightly given the increments is inevitable.
Apart from this, the petitioner was not given opportunity of hearing.
The order (Annexure-P) is set aside.
The petitioner would continue to get the benefits conferred vide order (Annexure-L) passed on 2531974.
5.
There would be no order as to costs.
Security, if paid, be refunded to the petitioner as per rules.
