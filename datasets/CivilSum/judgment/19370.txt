Amarjeet Chaudhary, J - In this writ petition filed under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India, the petitioner has prayed for issuance of a writ in the nature of certiorari quashing the order dated 2491994, copy of which is Annexure P-15 to the writ petition, passed by respondent No. 2 and further for issuing a direction to the respondents to release full pension to him by giving the benefit of Army service rendered by him as per Government of India's Memo dated 3151988, copy Annexure P-11 to the writ petition.
2.
In brief, the case of the petitioner as put forth by his counsel is that the petitioner had joined the Indian Army in the year 1969 and was released therefrom with effect from 3061975.
Later on, petitioner joined the Border Security Force (hereinafter referred to as 'BSF').
He was appointed as Assistant Commandant vide letter dated 1441976.
The petitioner served the BSF upto 2371990.
He was pre-maturely retired under Rule 17 of the Border Security Force Rules, 1969, copy of order is Annexure P-3 to the writ petition.
The BSF authorities issued a certificate to the petitioner to the effect that he had served the BSF from 1051976 to 2871990.
It is also the case of the petitioner that while discharging him from the Indian Army, the Army Authorities had paid him the gratuity etc.
The petitioner has prayed that if the Army service rendered by him is counted towards the BSF service, his pension will be increased.
It is further the case of the petitioner that he had been submitting representations, latest being dated 3171990, copy of which is Annexure P-7 to the writ petition, to the effect that the pension be calculated by adding the service rendered by him in the Army.
3.
The claim of the petitioner was rejected by the BSF authorities vide letter dated 1561988 but the same was not communicated to the petitioner.
The petitioner made another representation dated 28111990, copy Annexure P-8 to the writ petition, but to no avail.
Further, the case of the petitioner is that as he was not confirmed in the BSF, he was not given the benefit of Army service towards pension.
However, it was communicated to the petitioner by the BSF authorities that his case had been referred to the Ministry of Home Affairs and the decision would be communicated to him as and when the same is received.
The authorities vide letter dated 651991, copy Annexure P-10 to the writ petition, informed the petitioner that his case had been duly considered by the Ministry of Home Affairs but his request was not acceded to as the same was not covered under Rule 19(1) of the C.C.S.
(Pension) Rules, 1972.
Thereafter, the petitioner made another representation and served legal notice through his counsel in the month of December, 1992 followed by reminder dated 6.9.1993.
Having received no response from the respondents, the petitioner had filed Civil Writ Petition No. 5205 of 1994, which was disposed of on 26.4.1994 with the following observations :-  "Mr. Jang Bahadur Singh, learned counsel for the petitioner contends that the petitioner had served a demand notice in December, 1992, copy of which is Annexure P-12 to the writ petition but the respondents have not taken a decision on the said demand notice.
On the consideration of the matter, respondents No. 2 and 3 are directed to decide the said notice (Annexure P-12) with a well reasoned and speaking order expeditiously preferably within three months from the date of the receipt of the copy of this order.
With this direction, the writ petition stands disposed of."
Thereafter, the claim of the petitioner was reconsidered by the respondents in the light of instructions dated 3151988, copy Annexure P-11 to the writ petition.
However, the same was rejected vide order dated 2491994, copy Annexure P-14 to the writ petition.
4.
The respondents have contested the claim of the petitioner by filing written statement.
It has been categorically stated in the written statement that the petitioner was commissioned in the Indian Army on 1531970 and not in the year 1969 and was released with effect from 3061975.
The petitioner did not file rejoinder to the written statement.
5.
We have heard the learned counsel for the parties at length and perused the paper book.
6.
The undisputed facts of the case, which have emerged from the record are that the petitioner had served the Indian Army with effect from 1531970 to 3061975 and later on he joined the BSF on 1051976.
The petitioner was discharged from the BSF on 2871990 on the ground of unsuitability.
It is not in dispute that the gratuity and other retiral benefits have been released to him by the Army Authorities for the service rendered by him in the Army and the BSF authorities have also released retiral benefits admissible to him for the service rendered by him in the BSF  7.
The sole point to be considered in the present case is whether the petitioner is entitled for counting of service rendered by him in the Army from 1531970 to 3061975 towards the service rendered by him in the BSF  8.
The petitioner has relied upon Government of India, Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances & Pensions, Pension Department and Pensioner's Welfare memo No. 28/50/87-P & PW, dated 3151988, copy of which is Annexure P-11 to the writ petition.
The petitioner has also cited various instances of counting of temporary service towards the pensionary benefits.
9.
We have perused the memo dated 3151988, copy Annexure P-11 to the writ petition.
By careful scrutiny of the same, we find that the abovesaid letter does not support the claim of the petitioner.
Inasmuch as Army service can be combined with the civil service if the ex-serviceman/ex-Army officer is confirmed in the civil service or if not confirmed, he has to render ten years of temporary service and only then he will get pension under the CCS Pension Rules either on superannuation or on invalidation.
The relevant portion of memo dated 3151988 reads thus :-  "The Govt. had issued orders vide OM No. 38/16/Pension Unit/80 dt 30.12.1980 allowing the Govt. servants to get pension after completion of 20 years of service either on invalidation or superannuation.
In pursuance of Government decision on the recommendations of the Fourth Central Pay Commission, the Government has further decided vide OM No. 2/4/87-PIG dated 14th April, 1987 that a Government servant will get pension under the CCS (Pension) Rules, either on superannuation or on invalidation after rendering 10 years of temporary service in the Government."
The perusal of letter makes abundantly clear that for getting the benefit of service rendered in the Indian Army, the person should either retire on superannuation or on invalidation but in the case of the petitioner neither of the two contingencies exists.
10. .
In this view of the matter, the petitioner is not entitled to the benefit of memo dated 3151988.
The authorities have rightly rejected the claim for the petitioner.
11.
However, before parting with the judgment, we would like to observe that there may be number of similar cases and keeping in view the fact that Army officers/ex-serviceman had volunteered to render service for the Mother Land, it would be appropriate if the Government reconsiders the matter and makes some provision for the counting of military service in such cases where the total service including the service rendered in the Army exceeds ten years irrespective of the fact that incumbent retires on superannuation or on invalidation.
Finding no merit in the writ petition, we dismiss the same with no order as to costs.
