RL Anand, J - Shri Kanwal Singh Daulta, District Probation Officer, Central Jail, Hisar, has filed the present writ petition under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India for issuance of a writ in the nature of certioari for quashing the impugned order dated 20th September, 2000 (Annexure P-9) vide which the petitioner was compulsorily retired.
2.
The case set up by the petitioner is that he joined the Army on 28th August, 1963 during emergency and served the country up to 20th November, 1968.
Later on he joined as Assistant Superintendent (Jail) at Karnal on 15th November, 1974.
He was promoted to the post of Deputy Superintendent (Jail) on 19th October, 1988.
He earlier filed C.W.P.
No. 15986 of 1994 along with others for seeking the benefit of emergency service which was granted to the petitioner and he was given promotion w.e.f.
3rd September, 1981.
The petitioner received a letter dated 22nd July, 1994 in which four months ACR w.e.f.
141992 to 3171992 was conveyed and the overall assessment was given as "satisfactory".
The petitioner also received a letter dated 27th October, 1999 regarding A.C.R.
for the year 1998-99 in which he was graded as average.
The petitioner filed an appeal against the conveyed ACRs on 27th October, 1999.
According to the petitioner, he worked sincerely to the satisfaction of his superiors.
On 25th May, 2000, respondent No. 1 passed an order and promoted some junior persons but the petitioner was not considered for promotion to the post of Superintendent Jail.
The petitioner made a representation for supply of the copies of the ACRs for the years 1998-99 to 1994-1995, but no action was taken on his representation dated 24th July, 2000.
Finally, the petitioner received a rejection letter dated 14th August, 2000 from the office of respondent No. 2.
The petitioner has challenged the order dated 20th September, 2000 on the ground that this is illegal, unfair, unconstitutional, arbitrary and has been passed with ulterior motive.
As per the record he was born on 18th June, 1944 and he had completed 55 years on 18th June, 1999.
The petitioner was considered fit for further service.
He has earned more than 70% good reports.
Two ACRs of the petitioner were average those were conveyed to him.
The petitioner filed an appeal/representation which is still pending and in spite of that, the respondent No. 1 has passed the impugned order dated 20th September, 2000 (Annexure P-9) without any basis.
The petitioner further alleges that no adverse remarks were conveyed to him, except the average report.
The decision of the Government in prematurely retiring the petitioner is illegal.
The petitioner had already crossed the age of 55 years on 30th June, 1999 when his case was considered for further service.
Therefore, the impugned order is liable to be set aside.
On this premises, the petitioner has made a prayer that the order of compulsory retirement dated 20th September, 2000 be set aside.
3.
Notice of the writ petition was given to the respondents.
They filed the reply and denied the allegations.
According to the respondents, the petitioner was retired in public interest under Rule 532-A(C) of the Punjab CSR Vol. II and Rule 3.26(d) of the Punjab CSR Vol.
I Part I, as applicable to the Haryana State Government employees.
According to these rules, the State Government has the right to retire a public servant on attaining the age of 50/55 years as the case may be by giving him notice of less than 3 months in writing.
Before passing the impugned order, the annual confidential reports of the petitioner from 1988-89 to 1997-98 were considered by the Officers Committee under the Chairmanship of the Chief Secretary to Government of Haryana.
Out of his last ten years annual confidential reports, 7 were good, 2 were average and 1 report was below average.
Out of adverse reports, one pertained to the doubtful integrity of the petitioner.
So it was considered desirable by the Committee to retire the petitioner under the provisions of the above-said rules.
On merits, it is the stand of the respondents, that no legal and constitutional right of the petitioner has been infringed which could entitle him to invoke the extra ordinary jurisdiction.
It was also maintained by the respondents that the name of the petitioner was considered for promotion as Superintendent Jail in the year 1996, but he was not found suitable for promotion to the said post w.e.f.
511995 the date when Shri Daya Nand was promoted.
The integrity of the petitioner was doubtful for the year 1990-91 and, therefore, he was compulsorily retired.
With this broad defence, the respondents have prayed for the dismissal of the writ petition.
4.
In support of his case, the petitioner has placed on record some documents Annexure P-1 to P-7.
On the contrary, respondents have also placed on record documents Annexure R-1 to R-8.
5.
I have heard the learned counsel for the parties and with their assistance, have gone through the record of this case.
The point for determination is whether the impugned order has been passed in a public interest or that has been passed in illegal manner.
Before I deal with the submissions raised by the learned counsel for the parties, I would like to refer to the confidential letter dated 781991 (Annexure R-4) vide which the adverse remarks for the year 1990-91 were conveyed to the petitioner.
The remarks are as follows :  1.
An average officer, Manner of performance of duties also of average class.
2.
He did not complete and fill his journal registers and other relevant documents.
3.
Performance : un-satisfactory and shady reputation for honesty.
4.
Requires more training in accounts.
5.
Overall assessment : below average.
Annexure R-5 is the confidential report for the year 1991-92 which runs as follows :  1.
Lack of knowledge of work as average.
There is a need for improvement for the disposal of work.
2.
Your control on the subordinates is average.
3.
Overall assessment : Average.
Annexure R-7 is the confidential report for the year 1992-93 which runs as follows :  1.
Factory accounts keeping at Mohindergarh Jail has been poor.
Hence overall assessment is "Satisfactory".
2.
He should take interest in his official work at least.
6.
Learned counsel for the petitioner has submitted that the petitioner was earlier promoted at that stage.
His service record was taken note of.
Had there been any bad entry to the disadvantage of the petitioner, the petitioner would not have been promoted.
Earlier to the date of his promotion as Deputy Superintendent of Jail even there was any adverse entry that must have been waived off.
No adverse remarks for the year 1990-91 was conveyed to the petitioner.
7.
In a case of pre-mature/compulsory retirement, the position of law is now well crystalised.
Such orders are not stigmatic or punitive in nature.
The Hon'ble Supreme Court in Badrinath v. Government of Tamil nadu and others, 2000(4) RSJ 256 : 2000(4) SCT 832 (SC) has taken note of that entire period of service should be taken into consideration.
The adverse remarks which related to a distant past should not be given weight after a long distance of time.
If the adverse remarks related to a period prior to an earlier promotion they must be treated as having lost their sting and as weak material subject to the rider that if they related to dishonesty or lack of integrity they cannot be considered to have lost their strength fully so as to be ignored altogether.
The Hon'ble Supreme Court further held that the order of compulsory retirement can be passed even if the adverse remarks remained uncommunicated and against which no opportunity was given to the public servant to make a representation.
It is evident that in the year 1990-91 the performance of the petitioner was not only un-satisfactory but he was having "shady reputation for honesty".
In these circumstances, the Officers Committee was justified in taking into consideration the entry for the year 1990-91.
The ten years record of the petitioner was examined rationally.
A public servant whose honesty has been doubted has no business to stay in public service.
It will not be in the interest of the public to retain the said person with shady character and of shady reputation into service.
Faced with this difficulty, reliance has been placed on Brij Mohan Singh Chopra v. State of Punjab, 1950-1988) Vol. 1 Recent Services Judgments 546 and submitted that no order for premature retirement can be passed on the basis of the uncommunicated remarks because there is a valuable right to the public servant to make representation against the adverse remarks.
The judgment relied upon by the learned counsel for the petitioner is not helpful to me.
For my assistance and in the presence of the learned counsel for the parties, I have seen the service record of the petitioner in original.
Adverse remarks for the year 1990-91 were sent to the petitioner by registered A.D. post vide office letter No. 13086 G1/A-6 dated 781991.
There is a presumption of correctness with regard to the sending of that letter.
Though an effort was made by the learned counsel for the petitioner that his client has not received those adverse remarks, but I am not convinced with his submission keeping in view the fact that the letter was posted to the petitioner by a registered A.D. Cover.
Even otherwise, the service record of the petitioner for the year 1991-92 was "average", for the year 1992-93 it was "satisfactory", for the year 1997-98 and 1998-99 it was again "average".
No mala fides are involved on the part of the members of the Officers Committee.
They have properly considered the record of the petitioner.
8.
Reliance was also placed by the learned counsel for the petitioner to the Instruction 95 in which it was laid down that letter conveying adverse remarks should be acknowledged properly.
In the present case, it is evident that the adverse remarks for the year 1990-91 were conveyed to the petitioner.
9.
Having considered all the pros and cons of the case, I am of the considered opinion that there is merit in this writ petition.
The order of compulsory retirement of the petitioner has been passed in public interest after taking into consideration the service record of the petitioner especially for the period of ten years.
I may also make a mention that at one point of time the petitioner was even ignored for the purpose of promotion.
The writ petition is hereby dismissed with no order as to costs.
Petition dismissed.
