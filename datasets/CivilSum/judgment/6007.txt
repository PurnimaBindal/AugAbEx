GS Singhvi, J - In this petition (amended), the petitioner has prayed for quashing seniority list of Punjab Agricultural Service Class-I (for short, `PAS-I') Officers circulated vide memo dated 631985 (Annexure P7), so far as it concerns him and respondent No. 3.
He has further prayed for setting aside the order vide which the benefit of military service was given to respondent No. 3 in terms of Rule 5 of the De-mobilised Armed Forces Personnel (Reservation of Vacancies in the Punjab State Non-Technical Service) Rules, 1968 (for short, `the 1968 Rules').
Still further, he has prayed for issuance of a writ in the nature of quo warranto declaring that respondent No. 3 is not eligible to hold any post in Punjab Agricultural Service Class-II (for short, `PAS-II') or PAS-I The petitioner has also prayed for issuance of a writ of mandamus to the State Government to consider his case for promotion to the posts of Joint Director, Additional Director and Director of Agriculture and promote him on those posts w.e.f.
2451984, 21.2.1992 and 12.6.1997 respectively.
2.
The facts :-  On being selected by Punjab Public Service Commission (for short, `the Commission'), the petitioner was appointed to PAS-I on temporary basis vide memo, dated 1171974 (Annexure P1).
He was promoted as Joint Director, Agriculture (Extension and Training) vide order dated 1821985 issued by the State Government.
3.
Respondent No. 3 - Shri Avtar Singh Randhawa joined service as Agriculture Inspector which is a Class-III post on 5111962, in July, 1963 he joined Indian Army on deputation.
He was released from the Army in May, 1968.
Thereafter, he served in the parent department till January 6, 1972, when he rejoined the Army.
He was finally released from the Army some time in 1973.
In the meanwhile, the Department of Agriculture sent requisition dated 13.9.1971 to the Commission for recruitment of 13 Class-II Officers.
Of these, four posts were reserved for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes and three were reserved for Released Armed Forces Personnel.
While he was serving the Indian Army (second stint), respondent No. 3 applied for recruitment to PAS-II in pursuance of the advertisement issued by the Commission and on being recommended by the Commission, he was appointed as Chief Agricultural Office vide order dated 911973.
He joined that post on 1641973.
By an order dated 2511977, he was promoted to PAS-I Later on, the competent authority passed order dated 2311976 giving him the benefit of deemed appointment to PAS-II w.e.f.
1111963 in terms of Rule 5 of the 1968 Rules.
By another other dated 2091982, the State Government revised his promotion to PAS-I and he was given the benefit of deemed promotion w.e.f.
3111971.
During the pendency of this petition, he was promoted as Joint Director w.e.f.
17101989, as Additional Director w.e.f.
2221992 and as Director w.e.f.
23/2461997.
4.
After recruitment of respondent No. 3 to PAS-II, the State Government circulated tentative seniority list of PAS-I officers vide memo dated 2621980 (Annexure P4) showing their position as on 111979.
The petitioner's name figured in that list at S.No.
22, but that of respondent No. 3 did not find mention in it.
However, in the revised tentative seniority list prepared in pursuance of the decision of the High Court in Deep Singh Sihota v. State of Punjab, 1983(1) S.L.R.
665 which was circulated vide memo dated 2551984 (Annexure P5), the petitioner's name was shown at No. 23 and that of respondent No. 3 was shown at No. 13.
This was done because in the meanwhile, respondent No. 3 had been given the benefit of deemed appointment to PAS-II and PAS-I w.e.f.
1111963 and 3111971 respectively.
The petitioner submitted representation dated 25.6.1984 (Annexure P6) to protest against his placement below respondent No. 3 by asserting that the latter could not have been given the benefit of deemed appointment in terms of Rule 5 of the 1968 Rules.
However, the State Government did not accept his representation and in the final seniority list circulated vide memo dated 631985 (Annexure P7), inter-se position of the petitioner and respondent No. 3 was maintained.
5.
The petitioner has challenged his placement below respondent No. 3 in the final seniority list of PAS-I mainly on the ground that respondent No. 3 could not have been given the benefit of Rule 5 of the 1968 Rules.
He has averred that respondent No. 4 had joined Army service on 20.7.1963 whereas the post, with reference to which he has been given the benefit of deemed appointment in terms of Rule 5 of the 1968 Rules, was advertised by the Commission on 1851963 and the last date fixed for receipt of the applications was 1161963.
According to the petitioner, the benefit of deemed appointment can be given only to a Released Armed Forces Personnel with reference to the first opportunity available to him for appointment to the civil services after joining Indian Army and as respondent No. 3 did not get such opportunity after 20.7.1963, he could not have been given the benefit of deemed appointment.
He has further averred that as on 1.11.1973, respondent No. 3 was not even eligible to be appointed to PAS-II because he did not have the requisite experience for recruitment as Horticulture Development Officer (Grapes).
The further case of the petitioner is that even as on 911973 or 1641973, respondent No. 3 was not eligible to be appointed to PAS-II because he did not possess the qualification of Post Graduation in Agriculture prescribed under Rule 5 of the Punjab Agricultural Service (Class II) Rules, 1947 (for short, `the 1947 Rules') as amended vide notification dated 25.8.1972.
He has referred to the example of Shri Raghbir Singh, who was denied appointment to PAS-II only on the ground that he did not possess the qualifications prescribed under the amended Rule 5 of the 1947 Rules and pleaded that the official respondents cannot adopt different yard-stricks for judging the eligibility of the candidates for recruitment to PAS-II.
6.
Respondent Nos. 1 and 2 have filed written statement to contest the writ petition.
They have averred that respondent No. 3 had applied for recruitment to PAS-II in pursuance of advertisement dated 431972 issued by the Commission for recruitment of 13 PAS Class-II officers for which requisition dated 13.9.1971 was sent by the Agriculture Department and on the recommendations of the Commission, he was appointed as Chief Agricultural Officer vide order dated 911973.
They have further averred that respondent No. 3 was given the benefit of deemed appointment to PAS-II w.e.f.
1.11.1973 because Shri Ajit Singh Doabia was appointed as Horticulture Development Officer with effect from that date.
They have controverted the petitioner's assertion that respondent No. 3 was not eligible to be appointed as Horticulture Development Officer (Grapes) and have averred that he possessed the qualifications prescribed under the 1947 Rules.
Respondent Nos. 1 and 2 have also controverted the petitioner's plea that they had adopted different yard-sticks for judging the eligibility of the candidates for recruitment to PAS-II or PAS-I For the sake of convenient reference, the averments contained in paragraph 17(i) and 17(v) of the written statement filed by respondent Nos. 1 and 2 are reproduced below:-  "17(i) Not admitted.
According to Rules 5(1) of the Demobilised Armed Forces Personnel (Reservation of Vacancies in the Punjab State Non Technical Service) Rules, 1968, seniority and pay of the candidates who are appointed against the vacancies reserved under Rule 3 and who :- (i) in the case of Emergency Commissioned Officers are released according to a programme; or (ii) In the case of Short Service Commissioned Officers; are released on the expiry of the tenure of their service; or (iii) are invalidated owing to a disability attributable to or aggrated by military service; shall be determined on the assumption that they joined the service or the post, as the case may be, under the State Government at the first opportunity they had after they joined the military service or training prior to the Commission".
Further as provided in Rule 5(3) of the Rules ibid, all candidates appointed against the reserved vacancies under Rule 3 shall rank below the candidates appointed by direct recruitment in the year to which the former candidates are allowed.
According to Rule 6 of the Rules ibid, the provisions of these Rules shall have effect, notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained in any other rules.
The copy of the advertisement appended as Annexure/P/8 to the petition is not the strictly relevant document in this case.
The actual position i.e. however, that the Punjab Public Service Commission advertised two posts of Horticulture Development Officer (Grapes) on 1851963 with the following qualifications :-  (a) B.Sc.

(b) Three years' experience of development work of fruit production; (c) Adequate knowledge of Hindi or Punjabi.
In the Punjab Agriculture Service (Class II) Rules, 1947, then in vogue, the following qualifications have been prescribed for direct recruitment to Class II posts:-  "has obtained diploma or a degree of a recognised institution in the particular branch of science or subject in which proficiency is required or a candidate for the particular scientific or technical post for which he is to be recruited."
Thus, the qualifications and experience prescribed for the post of Horticulture Development Officer were not in conformity with the said statutory provisions of the Rules.
On the post of Horticulture Development Officer Shri Ajit Singh Doabia was appointed on 31101963 through direct recruitment.
Respondent No. 3 joined the military service on 20.7.1963.
He is B.Sc.
(Agri.)
with Horticulture.

7.
In his separate written statement, respondent No. 3 has pleaded that the writ petition is liable to be dismissed on the ground of laches.
He has averred that the petitioner has not explained the delay of 12 years between the date of his appointment to PAS-II and the filing of writ petition and, therefore, he should be non-suited on this ground alone.
He has justified his placement above the petitioner in the revised seniority list issued in compliance of the direction given by the High Court in Deep Singh Sihota's case (supra) and averred that Letters Patent Appeal and Special Leave Petition filed against the order of the learned Single Judge were dismissed by the Division Bench and the Supreme Court respectively.
He has also justified the decision of the government to give him the benefit of the deemed appointment to PAS-II and PAS-I by asserting that he was selected to join Indian Army much before 1161963 i.e. the last date fixed for receipt of applications in pursuance of advertisement dated 1851963.
On the issue of his eligibility to be appointed to PAS-II, respondent No. 3 has averred that he fulfilled the prescribed qualifications.
8.
I have heard learned counsel for the parties and carefully perused the record.
9.
At the out-set, I deem it proper to mention that during the course of hearing, Shri DV Sharma had produced the record containing documents in which the case of respondent No. 3 for grant of benefit of deemed appointment to PAS-II and promotion to PAS-I was dealt with and made a statement that his appointment to PAS-II ws ante-dated from 1641973 to 10111963 vide order dated 23111976 and his promotion to PAS-I was ante-dated from 21511977 to 3111971 vide order dated 2091882.
Shri Sharma also stated that the petitioner and respondent No. 3 have already retired from service.
Shri RD Bawa, learned counsel for the petitioner did not controvert either of the two statements made by Shri DV Sharma.
10.
The first question which needs consideration is whether the selection and appointment of respondent No. 3 to PAS-II is liable to be nullified on the ground that he was not eligible to be considered for appointment in pursuance of advertisement dated 431972.
According to the petitioner, the qualifications prescribed under Rule 5 of the 1947 Rules for recruitment to PAS-II was amended vide notification dated 25.8.1972 (published in Gazette dated 191972) and Post Graduation in Agriculture was made an essential qualification and respondent No. 3 did not have Post Graduate Degree in Agriculture at the relevant time.
The stand taken by respondent Nos. 1 and 2 is that the requisition for 13 posts of PAS-II which were advertised by the Commission on 431972 had been sent on 13.9.1971 and the same was not withdrawn despite the amendment of Rule 5 of the 1947 Rules and, therefore, the Commission was justified in making selection from among the candidates, who possessed the qualifications prescribed under the unamended rule.
11.
In my opinion, the selection and appointment of respondent No. 3 to PAS- II cannot be nullified on the ground that he was not qualified for that post.
The question whether or not respondent No. 3 was eligible to be recruited to PAS-II was directly considered by this Court in Kulwant Singh Grewal v. The State of Punjab and others, 1990(5) S.L.R.
518 and answered in the negative in the following words :-  "Admittedly, respondent No. 1 sent the requisition to the Punjab Public Service Commission for filing up of 13 posts in P.A.S.
Class II Service on September 13, 1971 and in the requisition it was specified that qualification for these posts was B.Sc.
(Agriculture).
The Punjab Public Service Commission advertised these posts and the last date for receipt of the applications was April 4, 1972.
These posts were advertised much prior to the amendment of Class II Rules.
The only condition prescribed was that the applicant should have the basic qualification of B.Sc.
(Agriculture).
The process of selection begins with the issuance of advertisement and ends with the preparation of select list.
Indeed, it consists of various steps like inviting applications, scrutiny of applications, rejection of defective applications or elimination of ineligible candidates, conducting examinations, calling for interview or viva-voce and preparation of list of successful candidates for appointment.
When such are the different steps in the process of selection, a candidate on making application for a post pursuant to an advertisement does not acquire any right of selection.
But if he is eligible and otherwise qualified in accordance with the relevant rules and the terms contained in the advertisement, he does acquire a vested right for being considered for selection in accordance with the Rules as they existed on the date of advertisement.
If amendment is made in the Rules during the pendency of selection, unless the amended rules suggest otherwise.
It will operate prospectively.
However, if by express language or by necessary implication, it can be inferred that the amended rules apply prospectively (retrospectively-Ed) normally would operate prospectively.
On the date of advertisement and on the last date of submitting the application, respondents 3 to 5 were eligible for applying for P.A.S.
Class II posts.
Subsequent amendment in the Class II Rules will not render them ineligible irrespective of the fact that on the date when the appointment letters were issued, the amended rules had already come into force.""
12.
In view of the above noted judgment, I have no hesitation to reject the petitioner's challenge to the eligibility of respondent No. 3 to be appointed to PAS-II.
13.
There are two other reasons why the petitioner's challenge to the appointment of respondent No. 3 to PAS-II should be negatived.
Firstly, he has approached the Court after a gap of 12 years and no satisfactory explanation has been given for not challenging the appointment of respondent No. 3 during this long interregnum.
If the petitioner was to file Civil suit in the year 1985 for quashing the appointment of respondent No. 3 to PAS-II w.e.f.
911973/1641973, the same was bound to be dismissed as barred by limitation.
Therefore, by applying the ratio of the judgments of the Supreme Court in State of M.P. and another v. Bhailal Bhai, AIR 1964 SC 1006; M/s Tilok Chand Moti Chand & others v. HB Munshi, Commissioner of Sales Tax, Bombay and another, AIR 1970 SC 898; Kamini Kumar Das Choudhury v. State of West Bengal and others, AIR 1972 SC 2060 and PS Sadasivaswamy v. State of Tamil Nadu, 1976(1) S.L.R.
53, I am inclined to uphold the objection of delay and laches raised by respondent No. 3.
Secondly, no useful purpose would now be served by entertaining the petitioner's prayer for issuance of a writ of quo warranto because respondent No. 3 has already retired from service.
14.
The second question which merits consideration is whether orders dated 2311976 and 2091982 for giving the benefit of deemed appointment to respondent No. 3 to PAS-II w.e.f.
1111963 and promotion to PAS-I w.e.f.
3111997 is ultra vires to Rule 5 of the 1968 Rules.
For appreciating the petitioner's plea that respondent No. 3 was not entitled to the benefit of deemed appointment and promotion to PAS-II and PAS-I respectively, it will be useful to notice Rule 5 of the 968 Rules.
The same reads as under :-  "5(1) Seniority and pay of the candidates who are appointed against the vacancies reserved under Rule 3 and who - (i) in the case of Emergency Commissioned Officers are released according to a programme; or (ii) in the case of Short Service Commissioned Officers, are released on the expiry of tenure, of their service; or (iii) are invalidated owing to a disability attributable to or aggravated by Military service, shall be determined on the assumption that they joined the service or the post as the case may be under the State Government at the first opportunity they had after they joined the Military service or training prior to the commission."
15.
A critical analysis of the rule reproduced above shows that a candidate, who is appointed against the vacancy reserved for Released Armed Forces Personnel, is entitled to have his seniority fixed on the assumption that he had joined service under the State Government at the first opportunity he had after joining Military service or training prior to the commission.
According to the petitioner, the advertisement with reference to which Shri Ajit Singh Doabia was appointed as Horticulture Development Officer on 1111963 was issued on 1851963 and the last date fixed for receipt of the applications was 1161963 and as respondent No. 3 had joined the Army 20.7.1963, he could not have been given the benefit of deemed appointment w.e.f.
1111963.
In my opinion, there is no merit in the plea of the petitioner.
The use of expressions "they joined the service or post" and "under the State Government" in Rule 5 of the 1968 Rules clearly show that the candidate must be eligible and available for taking up the job under the State Government on the first available opportunity.
In the present case, that opportunity could come to respondent No. 3 only on 1111963 when Shri Ajit Singh Doabia was appointed as Horticulture Development Officer (Grapes).
Therefore, the mere fact that the post on which Shri Ajit Singh Doabia was appointed had been advertised prior to the joining of respondent No. 3 in the Indian Army cannot disentitle him from the benefit of deemed appointment to PAS-II.
16.
The petitioner's challenge to the eligibility of respondent No. 3 to be appointed to PAS-II w.e.f.
1111963 on the ground that he did not have three years experience of development work in relation to Food Production also merits rejection.
The qualifications prescribed under the 1947 Rules (as they then stood), for recruitment to the post of PAS-II was as under :-  "has obtained diploma or a degree of a recognised institution in the particular branch of science or subject in which proficiency is required of a candidate for the particular scientific or technical post for which he is to be recruited."
17.
Therefore, the requirement of experience incorporated in the advertisement cannot be relied upon for denying the benefit of Rule 5 of the 1968 Rules, more-so because Rule 3 of the said Rules does not lay down any particular qualification for recruitment to PAS-II.
18.
There is one more reason for my disinclination to entertain the petitioner's challenge to the deemed appointment and promotion of respondent No. 3 to PAS-II and PAS-I respectively.
He has neither placed on record copies of orders dated 23111976 and 2091982 vide which respondent No. 3 was given the benefit of deemed appointment and promotion nor he has prayed for quashing of those orders.
In the absence of a specific prayer, I do not find any justification to nullify those orders.
No other point has been argued.
For the reasons mentioned above, the writ petition is dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
