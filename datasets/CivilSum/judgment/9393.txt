Pratap Kumar Ray, J - Heard the learned Advocates appearing for the parties.
2.
Challenging the judgement and order dated 23rd February, 2004 passed by the learned Judge in WP 2819(W) of 2004 whereby and whereunder the writ application filed by the present appellants was dismissed by rejecting their prayer for regularisation, this appeal has been preferred.
The appellants before us initially approached the Central Administrative Tribunal praying for necessary direction for appointment on temporary basis in terms of the scheme introduced by the Department of Telecommunication as circulated under No. 269/X/89-ST. New Delhi dated 7th November, 1989 on the ground that they fulfilled the conditions as stipulated thereof, namely, service of 240 days for each year.
This application was registered as OA No. 166 of 1994 whereby direction was made for consideration of the issue by the appropriate authority who in turn decided the matter negatively rejecting the prayer for confirmation of temporary status under the said scheme on the ground that the appellants submitted a contradictory statement about their performance of duties and also on the ground that the said Scheme had no applicability.
This decision was passed by the Divisional Engineer, External, Bhatpara on 2nd December, 2003.
The writ application was filed assailing the said order.
The factual matrix of the writ petitioners/appellants is very short.
It is an admitted fact that they were appointed in the year 1990.
It is also an admitted fact that they claimed their temporary status in service on elevation from their status of casual labourers in terms of the aforesaid scheme of 7th November, 1989.
However, from the judgment of the learned trial Judge it appears that scheme of 1993 so urged, as per views of the learned trial Judge, had no applicability.
Mr. Moitra, learned senior Advocate, appearing for the appellants before us has placed strong reliance on the scheme of 7th November, 1989 for the purpose of necessary relief granting temporary status in terms of the scheme, 1989.
However, this appeal has been opposed by the respondent BSNL, now a Corporation, on change of its nomenclature from its parent Organisation, Department of Telecommunication.
Since the entire issue rests on the scheme itself dated 7th November, 1989 the answer of the grievance could be available from the analysis of the scheme itself.
Hence the scheme as relied upon dated 7th November, 1989 is set out hereinbelow.
"No 269-10/39 STN New Delhi Dated 07 November, 1989  To  The Chief General Managers Telecom Circles, M.T.N.L.
New Delhi/Bombay, Metro Distt.

Details of the Scheme are furnished in the Annexure.

3.
On bare perusal of the scheme particularly paragraph 3 it appears that a total embargo about fresh recruitment of the casual labourer was made by the circular letter dated 7th November, 1989 save and except on the field of engagement of casual labourers in Projects and electrification circles only for specific works and on completion of the work the casual labourers so engaged require to be retrenched.
Another circular letter dated 22nd June, 1988 has been issued which provides that there should not be any fresh recruitment of casual labourers even for specific works for specific periods in Projects and Electrification Circle which earlier was allowed to the limited extent by the circular letter dated 30th March, 1985.
The reasoning for issuance of the scheme to confer the temporary status and regularisation of the casual labourers as issued on 7th November, 1929 got an object as it appears from the first paragraph which speaks that the casual labourers who were employed earlier and have rendered continuous service of at least one year with condition of fulfilment of clause 3 and clause 33 should be given temporary status in their service.
Under clause 33 it appears that the appointees after 30th March, 1985 should not be granted any temporary status without specific approval from the office, that is, office of the Chief General Manager, Telecom Service.
Along with the circular letter dated 7th November, 1989 the scheme was also annexed which provides that it is effective from 1st October, 1989 for the purpose of filling up the vacancies in Group D cadres in the various offices of the Department of Telecommunications by regularisation of the casual labourers who would be conferred with the temporary status of service.
Clause 32 of the said circular letter dated 7th November, 1989 speaks that in contravention of the instruction imposing a total ban for appointment of any casual labourer in terms of the letter dated 30th March, 1985, if any appointment is made no conferment of temporary status could be made in respect of those appointees and those cases should be referred to the Telecom Commission with relevant details and particulars about the action taken against the concerned officer under whose authorisation/approval the irregular engagement/non-retrenchment was made.
Hence on a bare reading of the scheme of 1989 and the circular letter of 7th November, 1989 it appears before us that it got its applicability to confer the temporary status of those casual labourers who were appointed prior to 30th March, 1985 and who were rendering continuous service from that period.
Since the appellants do not fulfil the condition precedent of applicability of the circular letter and particularly the scheme thereof for grant of temporary status in view of the admitted fact that they were appointed in the year 1990 when restriction was imposed by office letter No. 270-6/84-STN dated 22nd June, 1988 on fresh recruitment of casual labourers and their appointment itself being contrary to the circular letter of 7th November, 1989 as also office letters dated 30th March, 1985 and 22nd June, 1988 as referred to, we are of the view that the scheme has no applicability to rescue the petitioners from the present position for their elevation from casual labourers to temporary status.
It is settled law that unless and until under the service Rule and/or circular letters and/or any scheme, provision is made for elevation of the status of any employee in the service from the position of ad hoc appointee, casual appointee, temporary appointee to the status of permanent appointee, no elevation of such status could be made either by the concerned employer or by a Court of law.
Reliance may be placed to the judgment passed in the case of State of Madhya Pradesh v. Dharam Bir reported in 1998(6) SCC 165.
Long back in 1979 three judges Bench of the Apex Court has settled the law in the case of BN Nagarajan v. State of Karnataka, reported in AIR 1979 SC 1676 by holding in paragraph 5 to this effect "when rules framed under Article 309 of the Constitution of India are in force, no regularisation is permissible in exercise of the executive powers of the Government under Article 162 thereof in contravention of the rules."
Public employment has a special consideration in the matter  of fulfilment and satisfaction of the constitutional mandate enshrined under Articles 14, 16 and 19 of the Constitution of India.
Any appointment in violation of the Constitutional scheme is nullity being contrary to the basic concept of equality as enshrined under Article 14 of the Constitution of India.
Even it has been held that under the service jurisprudence breach of Articles 14, 16 and 19 should be looked into as a breach of basic structure of the Constitution of India, in the event the same is not adhered to, which has been echoed very recently in the case of State of Karnataka v. Uma Debi reported in 2006(4) SCC 1, a judgement of the Constitution Bench by holding inter alia, that adherence to the rule of equity in public employment is a basic feature of the Constitution and it has been further observed that the role of High Courts as sentinel and guardians and directions should not be permissible by the Court of law while delivering any judgment in that respect on the issue of employment wherein there would be a clear breach of Article 14 of the Constitution of India.
It is true that the present appellants were appointed as casual labourers and their service has been taken by the respondent BSNL, an erstwhile Department of Telecommunication.
It is also true that those casual labourers worked for so many years, but despite all these, the long service rendered amicus curiae will not provide any legal right for absorption and/or regularisation and/or confirmation of any status.
Reliance may be placed to the judgement passed in the case of Uma Debi (supra) as referred to.
The judgment as relied upon before us by Mr. Moitra, learned Senior Advocate for the appellants, passed in the case of Yamuna Shankar Sharma v. State of Rajasthan and others reported in 2007(1) Supreme 175 has no scope to apply.
No right could be claimed for regularisation only on the basis of rendering of long service on ad hoc basis or any other status unless and until there is a scheme formulated by the employer, BSNL.
The appellants are not entitled to get any relief from the Court of law either by passing any mandatory order granting temporary status and/or for consideration of their case.
As it appears that the scheme of 1989 has no applicability in respect of their case, we have no other alternative but to confirm the views expressed by the learned trial judge.
Hence having regard to such state of affairs, this appeal stands dismissed.
There will be no order as to costs.
4.
Before parting with the matter, we are not unmindful to the fact that some of the employees whose particulars have been given in the annexure to the application before the learned trial judge, namely, five in number who are junior appointees as casual labourers have already been given right to entry in the Master Roll.
This Court is of the view that the respondent BSNL since has not addressed that issue, they will pass a reasoned decision to that effect on the grievance as raised by the petitioners by referring the cases of other casual appointees thereof and such answer should be given by the respondent BSNL by four weeks from this date to the appellants concerned.

Prasenjit Mandal, J - I agree.
Appeal dismissed.
