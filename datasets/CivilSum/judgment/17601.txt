VK Bahli, J (Oral) - In this otherwise voluminous record of pleadings of the parties, perhaps, the only controversy that needs to be resolved, is as to whether while passing order, Annexure R-I, dated November 18, 1988, petitioner, who although had been shown senior to respondent No. 3-Pirthi Singh, was required to be heard in the matter or not.
Before, however, the question which has an obvious answer in favour of the petitioner, is actually recorded, it will be useful to see the admitted position with regard to service record of the petitioner vis-a-vis respondent No. 3.
2.
Petitioner came to be appointed as an Assistant Sub Inspector on probation on April 16, 1969 and was confirmed on the said post on April 16, 1972.
His name came to be entered in list 'E' on December 12, 1975 and he was promoted to the post of Sub Inspector on December 5, 1975 and was confirmed on January 31, 1983.
He was promoted as Inspector in February, 1984 and as Deputy Superintendant of Police on May 16, 1988.
Inasmuch as the petitioner was earlier serving in Army, having joined there at the call of the Nation when emergency was proclaimed, he was entitled to the benefits contained for the such persons in Punjab National Emergency Rules, 1965.
For counting the service rendered by him in the Army in the police department when he came to be employed initally as Assistant Sub Inspector, as mentioned above, he was given deemed dates of appointment.
He was deemed to have been appointed as ASI on June 17, 1964 and confirmed on the said post on June 17, 1967.
His name was also deemed to have been brought on List 'E' w.e.f.
March 5, 1971 and he was deemed to have been promoted as Sub Inspector on March 5, 1971 and confirmed on the said post on February 1, 1980.
Respondent No. 3, who, likewise the petitioner, was earlier in Army and likewise had also served the Nation when emergency was declared, came to be appointed as ASI on probation on March 30, 1971.
He was confirmed on the said post on March 30, 1974 and his name was brought on list 'E' on November 3, 1977 and confirmed on the said post on January 31, 1984.
He was promoted as Deputy Superintendent of Police in November/December 1988.
He was also given deemed dates as petitioner on various posts.
He was deemed to have been appointed as ASI on October 22, 1966 and was deemed to have been confirmed on October 22, 1969.
His name was deemed to have been brought on list 'E' on March 5, 1971 and he was deemed to have been promoted as Sub Inspector on March 18, 1971 on which post he was deemed to have been confirmed on April 15, 1980.
3.
Appointment of the petitioner on various posts vis-a-vis respondent No. 3, whether actual or deemed, would thus clearly manifest that he had been shown senior to respondent No. 3 on all concerned posts for over a period of about 15 years.
All of a sudden, however, order Annexure R-1 dated November 18, 1988 came to be passed wherein Respondent No. 3, in seniority, was brought above the petitioner on all posts starting from that of Assistant Sub Inspector.
Concededly, this order came to be passed without issuing notice to the petitioner for hearing him in the matter.
In the written statement filed on behalf of the respondent an attempt has been made to support the order but, as mentioned above, it is conceded position that petitioner was not heard in the matter at all.
The Court would not like to go into the reasons which are only post decisional and were not so recorded after a meaningful hearing might have been given to the petitioner.
On the sole ground, thus, that petitioner was not heard in the matter and the impugned order concedely once again, affected his service career, the same deserves to be quashed.
So ordered.
It is, however, made clear that if the authorities still might like to disturb the seniority of the petitioner vis-a-vis respondent No. 3 on any cogent ground that may be available to them, it may do so, but only after complying with the provisions of Law and hearing the petitioner in the matter.
The present petition is allowed in the manner indicated above, Parties are, however, left to bear their own costs.
