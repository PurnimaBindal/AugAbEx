SK Jain, J - The plaintiff-respondent brought Civil Suit No. 1028 of 2481988 against the State of Haryana and others for a decree of declaration to the effect that he was senior to defendant No. 5 and that he be deemed to have been promoted as electrician w.e.f.
26121985 with full back wages with interest at the rate of 12% pa.
The suit was contested on behalf of the defendants.
A joint written statement was filed on behalf of the official defendants No. 1 to 4 whereas private defendant No. 5 filed his own written statement.
It was denied that the plaintiff had become eligible for promotion.
The contention raised on behalf of defendants was that defendant No. 5 had become eligible for promotion prior to the plaintiff.
Defendant No. 5 also took the same he had become eligible for promotion after completion of four years of service when he had duly passed 'A' class wiremen certificate.
Parties fought the litigation on following issues :-  "(1) Whether the plaintiff was senior to defendant No. 5, if so, its effect ?
OPP  (2) Whether the plaintiff has no locus standi to file the suit ?
OPD  (3) Whether the suit in the present form is not maintainable ?
OPD  (4) Whether Civil Court has got no jurisdiction to try the suit ?
OPD  (5) Whether the plaintiff is estopped from filing the present suit by his own act and conduct ?
OPD  (6) Relief."
Learned trial Court found that in view of the rules a non-matriculate T/Mate who obtained 'A' Class wireman certificate and had four years experience was eligible for promotion.
Defendant No. 5 was appointed on 12101979.
He obtained 'A' Class Wireman certificate on 3111982, therefore, he had become eligible for promotion as electrician with effect from 12101983, whereas the plaintiff who was matriculate unqualified T/Mate, had become eligible for promotion after six years on 15.2.1984.
He dismissed the suit of the plaintiff vide judgment and decree dated 631991.
2.
The learned first Appellate Court in its judgment dated 18121991 has stated that in view of the statement of PW-5 read with the seniority list, the plaintiff-appellant was at serial No. 65, he having been appointed as Work Charge T/Mate on 1521978 whereas defendant No. 5 was at serial No. 99, he having been appointed as Work-Charge T/Mate on 12101979.
As per letters Ex.P.3 (also D-1) dated 31.1.1975 from the Superintending Engineer, Electric Circle, PWD (B&R), Karnal/Ambala/Rohtak to the Executive Engineer, Gurgaon, the period of experience required for promotion of ITI qualified T/Mate to the post of Electrician was two years whereas for unqualified T/Mate, it was six years.
Similarly, as per letters dated 3041980, (Ex. P3 duplicate and D-2), it had been clarified that the period of experience required for promotion to the post of Electrician would be three years for matriculate T/Mates with 'A' Class wireman certificate.
Since the plaintiff fell in the category of unqualified T/Mate, he would be eligible for promotion as Electrician after six years of service whereas defendant No. 5 who was a non-matric 'A' class Wireman certificate holder would be eligible for promotion after four years of service as T/Mate.
The plaintiff, a matriculate T/Mate had joined on 1521978 and after completing six years of service on 15.2.1984 would become eligible for promotion as electrician.
On the other hand, Dharam Vir Singh, defendant No. 5, a non-matric T/Mate, who had joined on 12101979 and held 'A' class wireman certificate would become eligible for promotion after completion of four years service on 12101983.
The vacancy for promotion as electrician fell vacant in 1985.
Defendant No. 5 was promoted with effect from that date ignoring the claim of the plaintiff who was senior to him as per seniority list Ex. P.5 which could not be done.
The learned First Appellate Court, therefore, reversed the findings of the Court below on issuance Nos. 1 to 3, allowed the appeal and decreed the suit.
3.
It is that judgment and decree dated 18121991 of the first Appellate Court which has been appealed against by the defendant and which may requires examination of its sustainability.
4.
I have seen the pleadings in the suit, the evidence adduced by the parties in the suit and the judgments of both the Courts below.
Following facts are not in dispute :-  (I) As per the rules framed for promotion of T/Mates to the post of Electrician following qualification and period of experience has been prescribed :-  Academic Qualifications Period of Experience required for promotion.
(a) ITI Qualified two years.
(b) Matric with 'A' Class wireman certificate Three years.
(c) Non-matric with 'A' class wireman certificate four years.
(d) unqualified Six years.
(II) A post of Electrician fell vacant in the circle on 26121985.
(III) the plaintiff was a Matriculate.
He was appointed as Workcharge T/Mate on 1521978 and was categorised as unqualified T/Mate.
(IV) defendant No. 5 was a non-matric.
He joined as workcharge T/Mate on 12101979.
He was also categorised as unqualified T/Mate.
He possessed 'A' class wireman certificate.
(V) That in the seniority list Ex. P-5 the plaintiff was shown at serial No. 65 whereas defendant No. 5 at serial No. 99.
(VI) The plaintiff, a matriculate unqualified T/Mate appointed on 1521978 became eligible for promotion as Electrician on 15.2.1984 after completion of six years of service, whereas the defendant, a non-matric unqualified T/Mate possessing 'A' Class Wireman certificate and appointed on 12101979 became eligible for promotion as Electrician after completion of four years of service on 12101983; and  (VII) On 26121985 when the post of Electrician fell vacant, the plaintiff was shown at serial No. 65 and defendant No. 5 at serial No. 99 of the said list and both of them were eligible for promotion.
5.
The principal question for consideration is as to whether Dharamvir respondent No. 2 who was admittedly 34 steps below the plaintiff in the seniority list could be promoted as an Electrician in supersession to respondent No. 1 in view of the fact that he had become eligible for promotion earlier to him.
6.
So far as the seniority is concerned, it is not and cannot be disputed that the second respondent was at all points of time senior to respondent No. 1.
It is no doubt true that respondent No. 2 on account of his being a non- matric T/Mate, holding 'A' Class Wireman certificate, had become eligible for promotion as Electrician after completion of four years service on 12101983 whereas the respondent No. 1, a Matriculate T/Mate, had become eligible for such promotion after completion of six years of service on 15.2.1984.
But when the occasion for promotion arose, the cases of every one eligible on that day has to be considered and if the senior-most person is eligible and suitable for promotion, he has to be promoted earlier than those who might have become eligible earlier.
7.
The crucial point to be noticed is that every one who is duly qualified is entitled to have his case considered as on the date on which a vacancy arises, depending upon his seniority.
The right to consideration depends upon the further question as to whether the person is seniormost and as to whether he has all the qualification prescribed for the post.
The fact that a junior has qualified himself by putting in the prescribed length of service earlier than his senior is not relevant.
What is relevant is as to who are all qualified on the date of occurrence of the vacancy.
The date on which qualifications are acquired is not of relevance.
The only relevant question is as to whether the seniormost person is duly qualified as on the date on which vacancy has occurred and is suitable in every other respect for promotion.
It is only if he is not duly qualified or found unsuitable that the question of considering the case of the person next in seniority would arise for consideration.
In holding the above view, I am fortified by the ratio laid down in a Division Bench judgment of Kerala High Court reported as Kunjukrishnan Nair v. State of Kerala, 1991(3) SCT 450 (Kerla) : 1991(4) SLR 633.
This being the direct authority on the aforesaid proposition, which has a bearing on the decision of this case, the same is rightly pressed into service for consideration.
8.
As mentioned hereinbefore, admittedly, the respondent No. 1 was shown at serial No. 65 in the seniority list Ex. P-5 whereas respondent No. 2 was shown at serial No. 99 of the seniority list.
Both of them were eligible for promotion as Electrician on 26121985 when the post of Electrician became available for promotion.
Therefore, the cases of all the T/Mates eligible on that day had to be considered and the seniormost T/Mate eligible and suitable for promotion had to be promoted.
9.
In view of the above discussion, the finding of the learned first appellate Court on issue No. 1 is modified accordingly.
The cases of all the T/Mates, who were eligible for promotion as Electrician on 26121985, when the post of Electrician became available for promotion, have to be considered and the seniormost T/Mate found eligible and suitable for promotion has to be promoted against the said vacancy.
Except the abovesaid modification in the judgment and decree dated 18.12.199, passed by Shri V M Jain, District Judge, Rohtak, this appeal is dismissed.
No order as to costs.
Appeal dismissed.
