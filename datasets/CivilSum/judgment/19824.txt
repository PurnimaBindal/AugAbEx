JL Gupta, J - This is a petition by constable Ishwar Singh against the order terminating his services on the ground that he was overage on 771989 when he was recruited in the Ist Bn.
of the H.A.P.
(Annexure P-4).
2.
The petitioner was admittedly born on 3041962.
He was enrolled as a constable on 2941989.
The certificate of enrolment issued to him under Rule 1222 of the Punjab Police Rules is at Annexure P.1.
The order of his recruitment had been passed by the competent authority i.e. the Superintendent of police, Hissar.
While working as such, he was given a Commendation Certificate (Class III) with cash reward of Rs. 50/-.
This was followed by another similar Commendation Certificate with a cash reward of Rs. 100/-.
It appears that vide orders dated 471989, the petitioner was transferred by the Deputy Inspector General of Police, H.A.P.
to the Ist Bn.
H.A.P.
for recruitment in the Bn.
The petitioner appears to have joined the Ist Bn.
HAP on 771989.
Soon thereafter, the order at Annexure P-4 was passed terminating his services on 2871989.
This order read as under :-  "Order No. 14212/16 Dated 2871989(X) DISCHARGE (X) Const.
Ishwar Singh s/o Shri Jogi Ram was allotted to this Battalion for recruitment vide Deputy Inspector General, Haryana Armed Police, Madhuban's Office No. 18017-20/A2/12 dated 4789 in pursuance of the instructions conveyed vide DGP's Memo.
No. 822-42/B-3 dated 18189.
He was allotted constabulary No. 1/235 vide this office order No. 13841/12 dated 17789.
However, later scrutiny of his papers had revealed that he was overage on 7789 that is the day of his recruitment in Ist Battalion and he was not fit for recruitment in Police as per above instructions of D.G.P.
(H) and allotment of earlier constabulary number was done inadvertently.
2.
Hence, in the circumstances he is discharged under PPR 1221 with immediate effect, i.e. 28789 as he is not likely to prove an efficient Police Officer.
Sd/-  Commandant Ist Bn.
HAP.
Ambala City."
3.
The relevant provisions which govern the petitioner are contained in Rule 1215 and 1221 of the Punjab Police Rules.
Under rule 1215, it is, inter alia, provided that "recruits shall be not less than 18, and not more than 27 years of age at the time of enrolment and ..........".
Rule 1221 provides as under :-  "1221.
A constable who is found unlikely to prove an efficient police officer may be discharged by the Superintendent at any time within three years of enrolment.
There shall be no appeal against an order of discharge under this rule."
It is in exercise of the power conferred under rule 1221 that the impugned order has been passed.
A perusal of the impugned order which has been passed on the ground that the petitioner had crossed the age of 27 years as fixed under rule 1215 on 7789 when he was recruited in the Ist Bn.
It has been further observed in the order that he was being discharged under rule 1221 as he was not likely to prove an efficient police officer.
4.
In the written statement, it has been admitted that he had been initially recruited by the Superintendent of Police, Hissar on 2941989 and he had worked there till 771989.
The case of the respondents further is that the petitioner was discharged under rule 1221 as he had crossed 27 years of his age on 771989.
5.
I have heared Mr. RK Malik, counsel for the petitioner and Mr. Jaswant Singh, learned AAG (Haryana) for the respondents.
The sequence of events as given above shows that the petitioner was initially recruited as a constable on 2941989.
On that day he had not crossed the age of 27 years.
Certificate as envisaged under rule 1222 was issued to him and is at Annexure P-1 of the petition.
Thereafter, two commendation Certificates at Annexures P-2 and P-3 (both the certificates do not bear any date) were given to the petitioner along with cash rewards.
In July, 1989, he appears to have been sent to the Ist Bn.
of H.A.P.
In view of this sequence of events, I find it difficult to accept the stand taken on behalf of the respondents that he was overage at the time of recruitment.
The petitioner had been recruited to the police in the rank of constable.
At that time, he was within the permissible age limit.
His eligibility being beyond doubt at the time of his initial recruitment the subsequent allocation to the Armed Police did not involve fresh recruitment.
This is all the more so in view of the fact that certificate at Annexure P.1 as envisaged under rule 1222 had been given to him.
I have no doubt in my mind that action in passing the order Annexure P-4 was totally unjustified and untenable in the circumstances of this case.
6.
There is another aspect of the matter.
The order of discharge has been passed in the purported exercise of power conferred by rule 1221.
The rule embodies what is commonly known as 'probation.'
The person recruited to the police force is on trial for a period of three years.
In case it is found that the person is not likely to prove to be "an efficient police officer", the Superintendent of Police has the power to discharge him from the Force.
The petitioner had been given two commendation Certificates soon after his recruitment.
Otherwise, nothing adverse, whatsoever, against his work or conduct has been pointed out in the written statement.
In such a situation, there was no room at all for invoking the power under rule 1221.
This power can be invoked ordinarily in a case where something positively against the person concerned has come on record of the sort having happened, there was no justification whatsoever for invoking the provisions of Police Rule 1221.
7.
I, consequently, allow this petition and quash the order at Annexure P-4.
The petitioner shall be entitled to all the consequential reliefs in the nature of arrears of salary etc.
He is also held entitled to the costs of this petition which are assessed at Rs. 1000/-  Petition allowed.
