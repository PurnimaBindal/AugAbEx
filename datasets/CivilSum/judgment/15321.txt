MM Kumar, J - This petition filed under Article 226 of the Constitution seeks issuance of a writ in the nature of certiorari quashing order dated 571985, Annexure P-3 passed by respondent No. 2 reverting the petitioner from the post of Clerk which was in the pay scale of Rs. 400-600/- to the post of peon in the pay scale of Rs. 300-430/-.
2.
Brief facts of the case are that the petitioner joined as peon in the office of respondent No. 2 on 861977.
He passed his Matriculation examination on 1461981.
He was given promotion on the post of Clerk on ad hoc basis for a period of six months on 1261984 with a stipulation that he was being promoted purely on temporary basis for a period of six months or on arrival of candidates from the Subordinate Services Selection Board, Haryana (for brevity, `the Board').
The case of the petitioner is that he satisfactorily discharged his duties as Clerk but his period of six months was illegally extended till the time the candidates selected by the Board were to join vide order dated 121985.
The petitioner has alleged that in fact it was done in order to make room for respondent No. 3 Shri Ram Sagar Mishra because on the initial date of promotion of the petitioner on 1261984, Shri Ram Sagar Mishra was not eligible as he had not passed Matriculation examination.
The petitionerhas further alleged that in accordance with the rules regulating the service conditions of Clerks, the case was required to be sent to the Board in accordance with Clause (n) of sub-rule (1) of Rule 7 of the Punjab Economic and Statistical Organisation (State Service - Class III) Rules, 1963 (for brevity,`the Rules').
3.
The stand taken by respondents 1 and 2 in their written statement is that respondent No. 3 is far senior to the petitioner because he had joined service on 8121966, although he passed his Matriculation examination on 1791984.
It has further been asserted that on account of acquiring Matriculation qualification, the petitioner was given ad hoc promotion against the vacancy belonging to direct quota post.
Those vacancies were required to be filled up on regular basis by the Board.
It is claimed that there is no violation of the Rules because the petitioner was promoted against a direct quota post and according to the practice, and ad hoc promotee is given extension beyond the period of six months, if selected candidates do not arrive from the Board.
It is further claimed that the appointing authority was competent to revert the petitioner from the post of Clerk to his substantive post of peon because respondent No. 3 who was far more senior to the petitioner in the cadre of peons had acquired the requisite qualification.
It is further claimed that ad hoc promotion given to the petitioner does not confer any right in preference to respondent No. 3.
4.
In a separate reply filed by respondent No. 3, it has been asserted that the writ petition is liable to be dismissed because of concealment of facts.
On merits, it has been submitted that the Rules have been further amended on 7.10.1977 and a Class IV Peon is required to fulfil at least 5 years experience of working in the Economic and Statistical Organisation, Haryana.
It has further been averred that vide office order dated 1821982, five peons who were all matriculates and seniors to the petitioner were promoted to the post of Clerk on temporary basis for a period of six months or till the arrival of candidates duly appointed by the Board.
In July, 1982 Sarvshri Sher Singh and Rattan Lal were promoted on the post of Clerk and in August, 1982 Shri Bhawani Prashed was promoted as such.
These three candidates were also matriculate and senior to the petitioner.
The names of the persons who were promoted on 1821982, July, 1982 and August, 1982 fall at serial Nos. 29, 31, 38, 36, 42, 10, 23 and 5.
The name of the petitioner figures at serial No. 44 and that of respondent No. 3 at serial No. 13.
Therefore, it is claimed that the petitioner has not yet acquired enough seniority for the purposes of consideration of his case for promotion.
5.
Mr. Madan Dev, learned counsel for the petitioner has vehemently argued that the order dated 571985, Annexure P-3 reverting the petitioner from the post of Clerk to that of Peon is absolutely arbitrary and violates the basic principles enshrined under Articles 14 and 16(1) of the Constitution.
The learned counsel has submitted that from the facts disclosed in the document Mark `A', it is revealed that the petitioner was reverted to accommodate respondent No. 3 because he was also promoted on ad hoc basis against a direct quota post.
According to the learned counsel on the date of promotion of the petitioner i.e. 1261984, the qualification was required to be seen and on that date respondent No. 3 was not qualified and he acquired the qualification of Matriculation on 1791984.
6.
Mr. NK Joshi, learned State counsel has submitted that there is no violation of equality clause because the petitioner being far junior to respondent No. 3 was not entitled to be considered for promotion against 20% quota meant for class IV employees.
The learned counsel has pointed out that name of the petitioner in the seniority list figures at serial No. 44 and that of respondent No. 3 at serial No. 13.
There were numbers of peons who had already been working on ad hoc basis and their cases are required to be considered ahead of the petitioner for promotion to the posts of Clerks and all of them fulfil the qualification of matric.
The learned State counsel has also heavily relied upon a Division Bench judgment of this Court rendered in Civil Writ Petition Nos. 15845 and 12404 of 1998.
The learned counsel has submitted that the aforementioned view of the Division Bench is in respect of the same department and persons are senior to the petitioner.
In that case it has been held by affirming the view taken in Ram Kumar Sharma v. State of Haryana, 1996(4) SCT 715 (P&H) : 1996(6) SLR 450 that the seniority does not get altered by virtue of passing the examination earlier and three vacancies which were available on 381982 were given to Bhawani Parshad who figures at serial No. 5, Sher Singh who figures at serial No. 10 and Rattan Lal who figures at serial No. 23.
In view thereof, the writ petition filed by Rattan Lal i.e. Civil Writ Petition No 12403 of 1998 was dismissed and his claim that he acquired Matriculation certificate on 11.2.1982 earlier to Bhawani Parshad who acquired Matriculation certificate on 2971982 was rejected.
7.
I have thoughtfully considered the respective submissions made by learned counsel for the parties and am of the view that this petition is without any merit and is, thus, liable to be dismissed.
In accordance with the Rules, 20% quota of the post of Clerks has been allocated to those who have been working on the post of peon provided they have acquired 5 years experience along with qualification of second class Matriculation.
Clause (n) of sub-rule (1) of Rule 7 of the Rules which is relevant for deciding the controversy read as under :-  "(n)(1) In the case of Clerks :- (i) 80 per cent by direct recruitment; and  (ii) 20 per cent by promotion from amongst Class IV employees and Class III employees drawing pay in a time scale less than that of clerks.
(2) The promotion to the post of Clerk from amongst Class III and Class IV employees shall, in the first instance, be made on ad hoc basis for a period of six months and if his work is found satisfactory, his case for regular appointment shall be sent to the Board for approval and notwithstanding anything contained in rule 9, the period of probation of such person shall start from the date of his regular appointment."
8.

11.
It is evident that when the petitioner was promoted on the post of Clerk on 1261984, he was outside his quota of promotee and was promoted against a post meant for direct recruit.
Even otherwise, he was far junior to respondent No. 3 who had in the meanwhile acquired necessary qualification of passing Matriculation with second class in September, 1984.
The question which has fallen for consideration before the Division Bench in the case of Bhawani Prashad v. State of Haryana and others, in Civil Writ Petition No 15845 of 1998 is `whether by passing a qualifying examination, the seniority of an employee can be disturbed when his case for promotion was to be considered'.
The answer given to the aforementioned question is in the negative.
The observations of the Division Bench in this regard read as under :-  "For considering the claim of all the three vis-a-vis the post which became available on 381982, the seniority as on 1251981 amongst the peons had to be kept in mind.
On 381982, the claim of Shri Bahwani Prashad was sustainable against the post which fell vacant on 381982.
No doubt all the three officials had become eligible to the considered for promotion to the post of Clerk but on account of Seniority the post should have gone to Bhawani Parshad and the second vacancy should have gone to Shri Sher Singh.
Therefore, Shri Bhawani Parshad was entitled to be regularised on the post of Clerk with effect from 381982 and Shri Sher Singh was entitled to be regularised with effect from 1281982, the date on which the posts became available.
In pursuance thereto, their promotions to the posts of Assistants also need to be corrected accordingly.
So far as Shri Rattan Lal is concerned, his claim over and above Shri Bhawani Parshad and Sher Singh is not sustainable.
The learned counsel for Bhawani Parashad has brought to our notice a Single Bench judgment of this court rendered in Ram Kumar Sharma v. State of Haryana, 1996(6) SLR 450, wherein it has been said that seniority does not get altered by the date of passing the qualifying examination or acquiring the eligibility.
Names have to be considered in order of seniority as fixed under the rules or the relevant executive orders.
The date of passing the qualifying examination would not determine the seniority or that the names of selected candidates shall be placed in the order in which they have acquired the requisite qualifications.
We concur with the principle enunciated in Ram Kumar Sharma's case (supra).
We, therefore, set aside this impugned office order dated 3091998 (Annexure P8) qua Bhawani Parshad which orders his reversion from the post of an Assistant to the post of Clerk.
We also set aside the cancellation of the order dated 561996 (Annexure P5) vide which the services of the petitioner had been regularised upon the post of Clerk w.e.f.
3181982.
The petition filed by Bhawani Parshad is consequently allowed with no order as to costs.
So far as CWP 12403 of 1998 filed by Rattan Lal is concerned, the same shall stand dismissed in view of the above observations."
The aforementioned observations are squarely applicable to the facts of the present case because the petitioner is far junior and there was no substantive vacancy against which the claim of the petitioner for permanent and long time promotion could have been considered.
In such a situation no violation of equality clause has been committed by reverting a junior to his substantive post and giving posting against a direct quota post to a person who is far senior like respondent No. 3.
Therefore, there is no illegality in passing of order Annexure P-3.
It is well settled that unless there is a quota post available, a person belonging to another quota cannot lay claim as a matter of right.
It is evident that no post belonging to the promotee quota was available on 1261984 when the petitioner was ordered to be promoted on the post of Clerk.
Even seniors to the petitioner like Sher Singh and Bhawani Parshad were regularised on the post of Peon w.e.f.
28182 and 3181982 and no person junior to the petitioner was considered for promotion against the substantive vacancy of promotees.
Therefore, there is neither any infringement of fundamental right enshrined under Articles 14 and 16(1) of the Constitution nor there is any violation of the Rules.
The writ petition is, thus without any merit and is liable to be dismissed.
For the reasons recorded above, this petition fails and the same is dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
