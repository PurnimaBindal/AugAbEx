AL Bahri, J (Oral) - Vide this order two writ petitions are being disposed of which have been filed by the same petitioner.
In both the petitions, i.e., CWP 6197 of 1988 and CWP 5616 of 1989 most of the facts are common.
They are given from the first writ petition.
2.
Raj Kumar, ASI was working at Ludhiana, with effect from 1st August, 1976 he was to cross efficiency bar.
Since he was not allowed to cross the efficiency bar, he filed a civil suit, which was decreed on 4th October, 1982.
Appeal filed against the same was dismissed on 1st September 1983, and further Regular Second Appeal No. 2697 of 1983 was dismissed by the High Court on 12th October, 1984.
The State of Punjab further took up the matter with the Supreme Court in S.L.P.
No. 1488 of 1985, which was dismissed on 30th March, 1987.
(a copy of the order is Annexure P-2).
3.
In 1985 the petitioner was retired from service vide order dated 24th April, 1985 (Copy Annexure P-1).
This order was challenged by the petitioner in Civil Writ Petition No. 2280 of 1985, which was allowed on 12th November, 1986 (copy of the judgment is Annexure P-6) in Civil Writ Petition No. 5616 of 1989.)
After the same was allowed, the petitioner rejoined on December 8, 1986.
After a week thereof, i.e. on 14th December, 1986, the petitioner was again relieved.
The petitioner filed a contempt petition in the High Court, and he was allowed to rejoin on February 14,1987.
4.
From March 17, 1987 to March 22, 1987 the petitioner worked with the CBI and thereafter from 23rd March, 1987 to 10th April, 1987, he worked in the Police Lines at Ludhiana.
From 11th April, 1987 to 22nd May, 1987, the petitioner worked in the University Examination Hall.
Thereafter, the petitioner worked with the CBI from 27th May, 1987 to 29th February, 1988.
Adverse remarks in the annual confidential report for the period 4th July, 1987 to 29th February, 1988 were communicated to the petitioner (copy of which is Annexure P-8).
A representation against the aforesaid adverse remarks was submitted by the petitioner (copy of which is Annexure P-9).
This representation was rejected vide order dated 27th June, 1988.
In Civil Writ Petition No. 6197 of 1988, orders Annexures P-8 and P-10 are under challenge.
5.
Vide order dated 24th April, 1989, the petitioner was retired.
A copy of this order is Annexure P-16 in Civil Writ Petition No. 5616 of 1989 which is under challenge therein.
At this stage it may be further mentioned that the petitioner was ordered to work with the Excise and Taxation Department on 28th January, 1988 and he joined the aforesaid Department on February 29, 1988 (afternoon) and the petitioner was retired therefrom.
6.
In both the writ petitions specific allegations were made against Shri AP Pandey, Senior Superintendent of Police, who was also made respondent in both these cases that he had passed the impugned orders mala fide as earlier also it was his order vide which the petitioner was retired, which order was quashed by the High Court in Civil Writ Petition No. 2280 of 1945.
Shri Pandey chose not to file replies in these writ petitioner inspite of service.
Thus, the allegations of mala fide alleged against him stand unrebutted.
7.
Rule 1317 of the Punjab Police Rules deals with the subject of recording of annual confidential reports, and an extract therefrom is reproduced as under :  "1317.
(1) Superintendents shall prepare and submit annually to the Deputy Inspector General, after obtaining the District Magistrate's remarks thereon, reports in form 1317 on the working of all Upper Subordinates serving under them.
These reports shall be submitted to reach the Deputy Inspector General on before 15h April.
Instructions on the subject of recording of annual confidential reports were issued by the State Government from time to time, and the relevant instructions on which reliance has been placed, were issued on 3rd May, 1960.
An extract therefrom is reproduced below :  "No reporting officer should record his remarks in the confidential report of an officer/official under him unless he has seen his work and conduct for at least three months.
If the officer official has served under the reporting authority for less than three months, the opinion of the officer under whom he has previously served for at least three months should be obtained."
From the perusal of the institutions, referred to above, it is quite clear that an officer, who has seen the working of his subordinate at least for a period of three months, could only record remarks in the annual confidential report.
Annexure P-8, which contains adverse-remarks communicated to the petitioner, are for the period July 4, 1987 to February 29, 1988.
During this period the petitioner was working with the CBI which fact is not disputed in the written statement filed on behalf of the State of Punjab.
It is quite clear that Shri AP Pandey, who was then working as Senior Superintendent of Police, had no occasion to see the working of the petitioner, and as per instructions aforesaid he was not required to record any remarks in the annual confidential report for the said period.
The stand taken up in the written statement by the State, in response to the allegations made in the petitioner in para 20, is that the annual confidential report was recorded in accordance with the Departmental instructions and the Civil Service Rules.
During the course of arguments no other instructions of Civil Service Rules have been pointed out to justify the record of adverse remarks in the annual confidential report of the petitioner for the relevant period, as noticed above by Shri Pandey.
8.
No doubt recording of annual confidential remarks is an administrative work of the State, and while disposing of appeal filed against such remarks, detailed reasons need not have been recorded as the appellate authority was dismissing the representation.
However, when the petitioner had challenged the jurisdiction of the officer to make adverse remarks and had referred to the rules and instructions in his representation (Annexure P-9), it was expected of the authority, to deal with such a point, while rejecting the representation.
In such circumstances the order simply rejecting the representations was rejecting not called for.
The order (Annexure P-10), thus, cannot be sustained in law.
9.
Shri Pandey, having failed in his attempt to get rid of the petitioner (as petition No. 2280 of 1985 was allowed), he wanted to take revenge against the petitioner and he passed subsequently order dated 24th April, 1980 retiring him from service.
This is Annexure P-16 in Civil Writ Petition No. 5616, of 1989.
It is significant to be noticed that at the relevant time when this order was passed, the petitioner was working with the Excise and Taxation Department, and before that he worked with the CBI No doubt, at the time when the Government servant has completed 25 years of service or attained 50 years of age, the authorities are required to consider his case for retention in service, and in that process the service record of the Government official is to be considered.
In the present case, when civil suit was filed by the petitioner, and undertaking was given on May 10, 1979, as stated, on behalf of the State Government that the previous adverse record of the petitioner would not be taken into consideration while granting promotions and, furthermore, after the previous writ petition No. 2280 of 1985 was allowed, and order of retirement of the petitioner passed in 1985 was quashed, what could be taken into consideration by the authorities in 1989 was the subsequent record of the petitioner.
Since Shri Panday had no occasion to see the working of the petitioner, who had worked with the CBI and the Excise and Taxation Department for most of the period, could not opine that it was in the interest of the State not to retain the petitioner in service, by recording the adverse entry for the period July 4, 1987 to February 29,1988, which has already been discussed above.
10.
In the facts of the present case, no other impression, except that the orders were passed by Shri Panday mala fide to vindicate his position, is possible.
At whatsoever level a Government Servant Works, he deserves fair adjudication of his working record, so that he may earn promotions and other service benefits.
When he is pitched against a higher officer, who from stage to stage so acted to harm his service career, such officer should be made responsible and to compensate the petitioner by way of costs.
11.
For the reasons recorded above, both the writ petitions are allowed with costs, which are quantified at Rs. 5,000/- (consolidated in both the petitions) to be paid by Shri AP Panday, Senior Superintendent of Police-respondent No. 3.
The impugned orders in both the writ petitions are quashed, i.e. order Annexure P-8 recording of adverse remarks and the order (Annexure P-10) dismissing the representation of the petitioner, in civil writ petition No. 6197 of 1988; and the order dated 24th April, 1989 Annexure P-16 in Civil Writ Petition No. 5616, of 1989.
A direction is given to the respondents to forthwith reinstate the petitioner with continuity of service and grant him service benefits within a period of three months, alongwith 18% interest thereon.
12.
With the directions aforesaid, both the writ petitions are allowed as above.
Petition allowed.
