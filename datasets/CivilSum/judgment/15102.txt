MM Kumar, J - The petitioner was charge-sheeted by the respondent-State on following three counts :-  "(i) that he accepted illegal gratification amounting to Rs. 1000/- on 2772001 in ITI Gohana from a student of Turner Trade Shri Suresh Kumar son of Shri Ramphal village Gangana and Mukesh Kumar son of Shri Karam Singh, village Bali Brahaman for passing them in practical subject and he was caught red handed and FIR No. 104 dated 2772001 has been registered in Police Station, City Gohana.
Therefore, his honesty has been found doubtful;  (ii) Shri Swami Sharan being gazetted officer and Principal/Chief Examination Superintendent has been found misusing his official position and guilty of dereliction of duties which shows immorality; and  (iii)That he has been found wanting in the conduct of FTT July, 2001 Examination smoothly and conduct of examination was affected."
2.
After examining oral as well as documentary evidence, the enquiry officer found that the petitioner was caught red handed while accepting illegal gratification on 2772001 for passing a student Shri Suresh Kumar of Turner trade and Mukesh Kumar in practical subject.
It was further found that he was remanded to judicial custody on 1082001 in District Jail, Sonepat and FIR No. 104 dated 2772001 under Sections 7, 13 and 49 of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 has been registered against him at Police Station Gohana.
The enquiry officer also found in respect of charge no. 2 that the petitioner violated the instructions of the Directorate and appointed Shri Ranbir Singh Mor on examination duty and he himself assumed the duty of Examiner for four trades whereas he was deputed as Chief Examination Superintendent.
Therefore, it was found that he has misused his official position by passing a student in practical examination.
It has been concluded that the petitioner has been found guilty of violating Rule 3 of Employees Conduct Rules, 1987.
Therefore, the second charge has also been proved against the petitioner.
The third charge is also proved as the petitioner has caused damage to the reputation of the department and the teachers amongst the students and public.
3.
When the enquiry report came up for consideration before the Financial.
Commissioner-cum-Principal Secretary to Government of Haryana (Industrial Training and Vocational Department) Haryana, a show cause notice was issued to the petitioner on 24102002 to which the petitioner had sent a reply on 10122003.
However, the departmental enquiry was stayed till the decision of the criminal case.
On 13.8.2005, the proceedings in the criminal case concluded and the petitioner was acquitted by the Special Judge, Sonepat on 13.8.2005 (Annexure P17).
On 2282005, the petitioner made a representation to respondent no. 1 to reinstate him in service.
However, respondent no. 1 passed an order on 342006 for retiring the petitioner compulsorily.
4.
We have heard the learned counsel at some length and are of the view that the enquiry against the petitioner has been held in a lawful manner.
The mere fact that he has been acquitted in criminal proceedings would not necessarily lead to an inference that even in departmental proceedings he would earn exoneration.
The standard of proof in the criminal case is to prove the charged beyond any reasonable doubt whereas in departmental enquiry adequacy of evidence is no ground to declare a finding in such an enquiry as illegal.
It is also well settled that the principles of Evidence Act, 1872 has no application in domestic enquiry.
For the afore-mentioned proposition, reliance may be placed on a latest judgment of the Supreme Court in the case of Chouhan Roadways Ltd v. Thiruganajambandam, 2005(3) SCC 241, Deputy Manager, AP State Road Transport Corporation v. Mohd.
Yousuf Miya, 1997(2) SCC 699, UOI v. Sardar Bahadur, 1972(4) SCC 618 and Lalit Popli v. Canara Bank, 2003(3) SCC 583.
5.
In view of the settled proposition of law, we do not find any ground to interfere in the order of premature retirement of the petitioner.
We are further of the view that in fact the respondents have taken a lenient view and allowed the petitioner to retire prematurely.
In such like cases, it is not possible to exercise equitable jurisdiction under Article 226 of the Constitution especially when the authorities have taken a reasonable view.
Hence, we uphold the impugned order dated 342006, Annexure P19 and dismiss the writ petition.
Petition dismissed.
