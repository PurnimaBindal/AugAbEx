Jawahar Lal Gupta, J. (Oral) - On November 16, 1996, the petitioner who was working as a Treasury Officer was ordered to be retired ``with immediate effect in public interest''.
The petitioner impugns this order as being totally arbitrary, unfair and discriminatory.
2.
A written statement has been filed on behalf of the respondents.
It has been stated that the petitioner's case for retention in service on attaining the age of 55 years was considered by the `Officers' Committee consisting of the Chief Secretary, Administrative Secretary and Head of Department.....'.
It has been further pointed out that after the petitioner had been promoted as Treasury Officer on July 19, 1983, he had been warned to be careful on three occasions and vide order dated December 4, 1992, a penalty of stoppage of one increment had been awarded to him.
In the confidential report for the year 1987-88, the petitioner's performance was found to be average.
In the annual confidential report for the period from November 4, 1989 to March 31, 1990, the accepting officer had recorded remarks as under:-  ``He should have called upon the Deputy Commissioner.
There cannot be more patent example of carelessness and non-co-operation than what has been said by the Deputy Commissioner.''
In the report for the period April 1, 1990 to August 13, 1990, the following remarks were recorded :-  ``Very reluctantly did he contribute towards small savings.
Family planning targets could not be achieved.
He is in the habit of making lame excuses for poor performance.''
The petitioner represented against these remarks.
It is, however, on the basis of these adverse remarks that the respondents seek to justify the order of petitioner's premature retirement.
It has also been pointed out that a charge-sheet under rule 7 of the Punjab Punishment and Appeal Rules, 1952, had been served on the petitioner in the year 1985.
3.
The petitioner had filed a replication to controvert the averments made in the written statement.
4.
After a perusal of the written statement and hearing the parties at some length, Ms. Bahri was asked to produce the file containing the annual confidential reports of the petitioner.
She was also asked to ascertain the position with regard to the charge-sheet which had been issued to the petitioner in the year 1985.
Both the files have been produced.
5.
So far as the file relating to the charge-sheet is concerned, the conclusion recorded by Mr. Hardip Singh, IAS, who had held the inquiry into the matter is in the following words:-  ``From the evidence led, it is a simplicitor case that the cheques have been passed in excess of LOC of a particular Office/division of public works department by the delinquent.
However, the department should consider the case of the delinquent in the light of the fact that this kind of financial impropriety has been shown by many other treasury officers and it would not be in the interest of justice to punish a single treasury officer and ignoring the case of others.''
Ms. Bahri, on instructions from the departmental representative who is present in Court, states that on the basis of this report, no action whatsoever is proposed to be taken against the petitioner.

7.
In view of the above record of the petitioner, it cannot be said that he was dead wood which deserved to be chopped off.
No reasonable person could have come to the conclusion that it was in public interest to retire him.
Still further, the adverse remarks which have been mentioned above are not such as to indicate that the petitioner was not suitable for being allowed to continue in service.
If he did not call on the Deputy Commissioner or he was reluctant in making contribution towards the `small savings', it did not mean that the petitioner was not a competent or good officer.
As for the observation that the family planning targets could not be achieved, it may only be mentioned that this is a problem which the whole country faces today.
Nothing has been pointed out to show as to on what basis it was found that the petitioner was in the habit of making lame excuses for poor performance.
Taking the totality of the circumstances into consideration, it cannot be said that the petitioner's record of service was such as to warrant his premature retirement.
8.
In view of the above,the order dated November 16, 1996, by which the petitioner was prematurely retired from service is set aside.
He shall be deemed to have continued in service.
The petitioner shall be entitled to all consequential benefits like arrears of salary etc.
He shall also be entitled to his costs which are assessed at Rs. 5,000/-.
