RS Mongia, J (Oral) - The petitioner was appointed as a Draftsman Grade-II on August 25, 1962 and on May 23, 1966 he was promoted as Draftsman Grade-I He was confirmed as Draftsman Grade-I in October, 1976.
The petitioner's date of birth is December 1, 1942.
The normal age of superannuation is 58 years.
As the petitioner was to complete 30 years of services on August 24, 1992, his case for retention in service after completion of 30 years' service was considered and an order dated 2341992 (copy at Ann.
P9) was passed by the Officiating Chief Engineer, Project Himank that he was of the opinion that it was in the public interest to prematurely retire the petitioner on his completing 30 years qualifying service w.e.f.
August 25, 1992.
This order was passed in exercise of the powers under Clause (j) of Rule 56 of the Fundamental Rules.
The petitioner filed a Review Petition against the order at Annexure P.9 and he was informed vide letter dated June 30, 1992 (Annexure R.3 with the written statement) that the Review Committee had already taken all aspects/factors into consideration while arriving at its decision regarding premature retirement of the petitioner in public interest.
He was further advised that if he still wanted to file any further representation, it may be addressed to the Chairman, Representation Committee, Secretariat DRDB, B wing, 4th Floor, Sena Bhawan, New Delhi.
Petitioner did file such a representation to the Chairman, Representation Committee, which was, however, dismissed on October 31, 1992 (Annexure R.4 with the written statement).
The petitioner filed the present writ petition challenging the aforesaid order of premature retirement (Ann.
P.9) and dismissal of his representation as aforesaid.
2.
It may be observed here that while the petitioner was serving in the Headquarters, Chief Engineer, Project Beacon, he was sent on deputation to Indian Road Construction Company in Libya on June 12, 1984.
He was posted to Headquarters 50 Border Road Task Force under Project 'Yatrik' vide order dated December 30, 1985 on repatriation from Indian Road Construction Company at Libya.
However, he did not report there for duty and remained absent allegedly without leave.
The Chief Engineer (Project) after conducting an enquiry against the petitioner discharged him from service vide order dated July 22, 1989 wef.
November 2, 1985 under Rules 12 and 19 of the C.C.S.
(CC & A) Rules, 1965.
Petitioner filed a representation-cum-appeal against the order of discharge.
He was reinstated vide order dated November 19, 1990 and was posted at Headquarter 754 Border Road Task Force under Project Beacon.
The interregnum period could not be regularised by the Headquarter, 754 Border Road Task Force due to non-availability of a vacancy.
The Border Road Task Force was disbanded in August, 1991 and the petitioner was posted to Headquarters Chief Engineer (P) Himank.
During October, 1991, the case of the petitioner was again taken up with the General Reserve Engineer Force record by Ex-754 Border Roads Task Force for proforma posting for the interregnum period.
On May 25, 1992, an order of proforma posting of the petitioner was issued as under :-  (a) 21185 to 30988 : H Qr.
50, BRTF(P) 'Yatrik'.
(b) 11088 to 02191 : H Qr.
49 BRTF.
(c) 3.1.91 onwards : H Qr.
754 BRTF.
3.
It has been mentioned in the written statement that after the receipt of the order dated May 25, 1992, regarding proforma posting order of the petitioner, the Headquarter Chief Engineer (P) Himank had taken up the case with the Chief Engineer, GREF, vide order dated June 26, 1992 to take expeditious action to regularise the services of the petitioner of the absence period.
The counsel for the respondents has produced from his record a letter, passed during the pendency of the writ petition dated December 11, 1993, issued by Chief Engineer (P) Himnak mentioning therein that absence period from duty in respect of the petitioner from November 2, 1985 to Jan 2, 1991 has been regularised by 50 BRTF and 49 BRTF as under :-  50 BRTF : (a) 1 day EL for 21185 (b) 38 days HPL from 31185 to 101285.
(c) 1025 days EOL from 111285 to 30988.
49 BRTF : (a) 614 days EOL from 11088 to 2191.
In paragraph 4 of the order, it has been mentioned as under :- "4.
On fixation of pay on IV Pay Commission, difference of pay and allowances from 101290 to 24.8.92 has since been claimed for Rs.
24,478/-."
It has further been mentioned that the amount, as aforesaid, along with other terminal benefits would be paid to the petitioner on finalisation of Court case.
It has further been mentioned in the order that the pay and allowances in old scale from 10121990 to July 31, 1992 have already been paid to the petitioner.
Learned counsel for the petitioner, on facts, submitted that, in fact, the petitioner was not absent upto January 2, 1991 as, in fact, he had reported for duty on June 6, 1990.
This, according to the learned counsel for the petitioner, is apparent from the order dated November 29, 1990, Annexure P.1, issued by the Director General Border Roads regarding regularisation of absence from duty, which reads as under :-  "Consequent on your reinstatement in service vide this Dte.
Letter No. 17003/Gen/DGBR/EIE dated November 1990, your absence from duty w.e.f.
21185 to 5.6.90 will be regularised against leave admissible under rules as you reported for duty to GREF Centre on 6690 but could not be allowed to join duty for your desertion/removal from service retrospectively.
Period from 6690 will be treated as on duty for all purposes.
2.
You are, therefore, advised to report for duty to GREF Centre Pune within 15 days of receipt of this letter.
On your failure to report for duty within stipulated time, you will be treated as absent from duty."
4.
Learned counsel for the petitioner argued that the appointing authority of the petitioner was the Chief Engineer (Project) whereas the impugned order prematurely retiring the petitioner had been passed by an Officiating Chief Engineer, who cannot be said to be the Appointing Authority.
He further submitted that the only blot, which, according to the respondents, in the service career of the petitioner, was the absence from 2.11.1985 to 561990, which according to the learned counsel for the petitioner, had been satisfactorily explained, which led to his reinstatement into service and also regularisation of the period when the petitioner remained absent as has been mentioned in the foregoing paragraphs of the judgment.
According to the learned counsel, apart from this, there was nothing in the record against the petitioner to justify the premature retirement of the petitioner.
He further submitted that as per order, dated November 29, 1990 (Ann.
P.1) (already reproduced above), the petitioner had reported for duty on June 6, 1990, but could not be allowed to join duty by the respondents and, therefore, he is entitled to full pay from June 6, 1990 to December 10, 1990 from which date onwards he would be paid as per order dated December 11, 1993, to which reference has already been made above.
5.
On the other hand, learned counsel for the respondents, on the basis of record, argued that the petitioner had remained absent almost for five years and it was only due to a technical reason that he was reinstated back into service and his period of absence regularised.
Such a person, who remains absent for such a long period and creates indiscipline cannot be allowed to be retained in service after he has completed qualifying service.
Premature retirement, according to the learned counsel, is not by way of punishment.
6.
After hearing the learned counsel for the parties and going through the record produced by the learned counsel for the respondents, I am of the view that this writ petition is liable to succeed.
For the view I am taking in the matter on the second point raised by the learned counsel for the petitioner, it is not necessary for me to advert to the first point as to whether the Officiating Chief Engineer could pass the impugned order or not.
It will be assumed for this case that he had the authority to pass the impugned order.
The question that arises is -- As to what was the material with the respondents to form an opinion that it was in public interest to retire the petitioner prematurely on completion of 30 years of qualifying service ?
From the record, I find that the only reason was the absence of the petitioner for about five years.
Once the petitioner was reinstated in service and his period of absence was treated on duty and that period was regularised by giving leave of the kind due, as is apparent from the order dated December 11, 1993 and the earlier orders dated November 29, 1990 and May 25, 1992 issuing proforma posting of the petitioner - Can it be said that the petitioner remained absent for that period ?
The explanation of the petitioner was found satisfactory and only then he was reinstated in service.
The period of alleged absence having been treated as the leave of the kind due, the petitioner would be deemed to be in service for that period.
Now the respondents cannot turn around and say that, in fact, the petitioner was absent during that period.
Physically, though he was absent but the fact remains is that period has been treated as leave of the kind due.
It will be contradictory that on one side the respondents are saying that the petitioner was absent and on the other hand he was on leave of the kind due.
According to me, this cannot be the basis for forming an opinion that the petitioner was to be retired prematurely in public interest.
The order of premature retirement and the orders rejecting the representation of the petitioner are liable to be quashed.
7.
It is apparent from the order dated November 29, 1990 (Annexure P.1) that the petitioner had reported for duty on June 6, 1990, but he was not allowed to join and, therefore, it was mentioned in that order that period from June 6, 1990 onwards the petitioner would be treated on duty for all purposes.
That being so, the petitioner would be entitled to full pay during the period from June 6, 1990 to December 10, 1990, from which date onwards the petitioner is being already paid as per order dated December 11, 1993.
8.
Consequently, I allow this writ petition and quash the order dated April 23, 1992 (Annexure P.9), as also the orders rejecting the representation of the petitioner at Annexures R.3 and R.4 dated June 30, 1992 and October 31, 1992 respectively.
Resultantly, the petitioner would be deemed to have continued in service from the date the order at Annexure P.9, dated April 23, 1992, was to take effect.
9.
Learned counsel for the petitioner, on getting instructions from his client, who is present in Court, fairly states that if 25 per cent of the arrears of emoluments w.e.f.
the date the order at Annexure P.9, dated April 23, 1992, was to be made effective till date are paid by the respondents within a period of three months from today, the petitioner shall forego his claim to the full arrears, but in case the payment is not made, as aforesaid, the petitioner would be entitled to the entire arrears.
I order accordingly.
Under the circumstances of the case, I make no order as to costs.
