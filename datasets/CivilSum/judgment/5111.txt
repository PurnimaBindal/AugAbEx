It was with effect from 111978, the Central Government abolished the ceiling limit corresponding to the price index of 320 points which was followed by the Government of Haryana with effect from 1121978 and at that time, the price index had touched 328 points.
A notification dated 17.5.1979 had been issued by the Government of Haryana in this regard, copy Annexure P- 1.
Pursuant thereto the total emoluments inclusive of pay, DA, Special Pay etc. ranged between Rs. 1030/- to Rs. 2450/- + Rs. 370/- subject to the ceiling of Rs. 2750/-.
The admissibility of DA at higher pay was fixed at a marginal lower level and that the absolute ceiling on the total emoluments was abolished.
However, upon the recommendation of the Pay Commission on 1.4.1979, the Government of Haryana revised the pay scales of all its employees whereas in the State of Punjab, the same had been done with effect from 111978.
Resultantly, the revised pay scale of Superintending Engineer and that of the Chief Engineer was fixed at Rs. 2000-2400 and Rs. 2250-75-2400-100-2600-150/2-2750, formula adopted was that the DA/pay was merged into the revised pay scale at a price index of 320 points with the basic pay and that one or two advance increments became allowable depending upon the length of service of an employee.
The aforestated relief grantable was subjected to some limitations which read as under :-  "i) The immediate benefit to any employee in the matter of pay fixation in the revised scale was not to be less than Rs. 30/-, nor exceed Rs. 75/- pm ii) If the amount so worked out was less than the start of the revised scale, then the pay was to be fixed at the start.
iii) If the amount so worked out was more than the maximum of the revised scale then the pay would be limited to the maximum.
iv) In case the initial pay of any employee thus arrived at fell at an intermediate level in the revised grade, then the excess over the lower stage was to be treated as personal pay to be merged in the next increment."

On the other hand, learned District Attorney, Haryana for the respondents has argued that the petitioners and their counter parts in the State of Punjab were at par till 1979 and also till the Third Pay Commission had come in and was adopted by both the States.
It is further contended that when the recommendations of the Fourth Pay Commission came in the year 1985, the Government of Haryana did not accept those but instead adopted/accepted the recommendations of the Central Pay Commission with certain modifications.
It is also contended that by virtue of adoption of the recommendations of the Central Pay Commission and modifications, the number of scales were reduced and brought down to 25 instead of 78 by way of adopting the principle of clubbing.
In this manner, the pay partity came in various departments and the ambiguities and the anomalies were taken care of.
He has further contended that the Government of Haryana had constituted an Anomaly Committee in the year 1986 and that all matters are referred to the said Committee on case to case level and so also the bunch which may arise accordingly.
29.
However, learned District Attorney has very fairly conceded that the alleged anomaly raised by the petitioners had never ever been referred to the aforestated committee as no request had ever been made by them.
Thus, taking any que from the circular letter issued by the Government of Punjab is of no consequence as the same has not been and could not be adopted by the Government of Haryana as the recommendations of the Fourth Pay Commission of State of Punjab were not adopted or accepted by the Government of Haryana.
Thus, there is a complete departure which ensued on account of adoption of the Central Pay Commission recommendations with certain modifications.
Thus, the pay parity of the petitioners with the counterparts in Punjab is not at all available to the petitioners.
30.
Learned District Attorney, Haryana has also argued that the matter relating to the special pay has also been dealt with by the Government which had been adopted from time to time by the Board and that the Dearness Allowance is not calculable thereon as the special pay cannot be termed as part of the pay in view of Rules 244 and 252 of Punjab Civil Service Rules.
Volume I, Part I which reads as under :  "R.244 - (a) Pay means the amount drawn monthly by a Government employee as - (i) the pay, other than special pay or pay granted in view of his personal qualifications which has been sanctioned for a post held by him substantively or in an officiating capacity or to which he is entitled by reason of his position in a cadre; and (ii) Overseas pay, special pay and personal pay; and (iii) any other emoluments which may be specially classed as pay by the competent authority."
"252.
Special pay means an addition, of the nature of pay, to the emoluments of a post or of a Government employee, granted in consideration of : a) the specially arduous nature of duties; or b) a specific addition to the work or responsibility and includes non- practising allowance granted to doctors in lieu of private practice."
In this regard, he has placed reliance upon the judgment of Hon'ble the Supreme Court rendered in re : Union of India v. LVS Vishwanathan etc., 1998(1) SCT 95 (SC) : JT 1997(9) Supreme Court 89.
31.
It has been further argued that so far as the anomaly alleged to have been created by virtue of the note which has appeared in the letter dated 241980, copy Annexure P-2 and also the clarificatory letter dated 1141986, Annexure P-12, the matter can be referred to the Anomaly Committee and if any ambiguity or any grey area is found, the same can be answered accordingly.
32.
After hearing learned counsel for the parties and perusal of the paper book and the documents which have been brought on record from time to time by virtue of the orders passed by this Court, I am of the considered opinion that the respondents have not been able to take a clear cut stand in respect of the sustainability of the Note-I appearing in the letter dated 241980, copy Annexure P-2 and so also the clarification issued by the Government of Haryana vide communication dated 1141986, copy Annexure P-12.
The cumulative analysis of the arguments addressed and the mathematical calculations which have been projected by the petitioners and so also by the respondents which have been noticed here above, I find that an ambiguity and anomaly does exist, so far as the merger of the ad hoc Dearness Allowance and grant of Dearness Allowance is concerned.
Thus, it does require close scrutiny by the concerned quarters before such mathematical analysis given by the respective parties are digested and the benefits accruing therefrom or otherwise are passed on to the petitioners.
33.
There is no doubt that the granting of Dearness Allowance or ad hoc Dearness Allowance or provisional payments which are passed on by the employers, need to be scrutinised by the experts in this regard.
The Courts are only to examine the correct applicability of the procedure and the process which are provided by the concerned quarters legislatively or by way of Government instructions or the rules framed in this regard accordingly.
34.
In the present case, a doubt has arisen in my mind that the clarifications which have been issued by the Government from time to time and which have been adopted by the Board have not clarified the matters in the right and correct perspective, thus they need to be examined once all over again.
It may be noticed that learned District Attorney has very aptly apprised this Court in regard to the subsequent developments which have taken place after the filing of the writ petition.
This also needs to be examined while determining the claim of the petitioners as the said averments are not forthcoming from the written statement which had been filed by the Board.
I have been informed that the Anomaly Committee is headed by the Chief Secretary to Government of Haryana and the same is still in existence and continues to render clarifications in respect of the anomalies which are raised in the individual cases or in the collective cases as the case may be.
Thus, it would be appropriate that the Note-I contained in letter dated 241980, copy Anneuxre P-2 which is being still referred to and the clarification dated 1141986, copy Annexure P-12 which is being adhered to while dealing with the cases of the petitioners shall have to go.
Resultantly the petition is allowed by granting a writ of certiorari and the aforestated are quashed.
It is further directed that the Anomaly Committee shall now consider the grievances of the petitioners which may now be spelt out by them by making detailed "submissions in writing to the Anomaly Committee headed by the Chief Secretary to Government of Haryana through proper channel within two months from the date of receipt of a certified copy of this order and that the Anomaly Committee shall take a conscious and cautious decision within two months thereafter, by following the dictum "audi alteram partem," i.e., giving them personal hearing or through an authorised representative which shall not include a counsel on both the sides.
The effect of the decision thereon shall be passed to the petitioners within one month thereafter.
Petition allowed.
