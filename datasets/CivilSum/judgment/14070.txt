Viney Mittal, J - Through the present order Civil Writ Petition No 17367 of 2001 Dharam Singh v. State of Haryana and others and Civil Writ Petition No 17379 of 2001 Kanti Lal v. State of Haryana and others are being taken up for disposal together as the common questions of facts and law are involved in the same.
2.
Dharam Singh petitioner in Civil Writ Petition No 17367 of 2001 joined in Public Health Circle, Ambala Cantt of Department of Public Health of State of Haryana at Ambala Cantt as Pump Operator w.e.f.
April 1, 1993.
Similarly Kanti Lal, petitioner in Civil Writ Petition No 17379 of 2001 also joined as Pump Operator with Public Health Circle, Ambala Cantt of Public Health Department of the State of Haryana w.e.f.
April 1, 1993.
Whereas Dharam Singh is working as a Pump Operator on daily wage basis as such till date, Kanti Lal aforesaid was transferred to Chaharwala w.e.f.
April 26, 1995 where he had earlier worked as Fitter.
Subsequently, in the year 1976, the aforesaid Kanti Lal was made a Fitter Collie and since then he is working in that capacity till date.
3.
Hereinafter, in the present order both the aforesaid persons, namely, Dharam Singh and Kanti Lal would be jointly referred as the petitioners.
4.
The claim made by the petitioners in their respective writ petitions, as aforesaid, is for regularisation of their services.
The petitioners state that the Government of Haryana formulated policies for regularisation of services of daily rated employees.
According to the aforesaid policies dated March 7, 1996/March 18, 1996, all those daily rated employees who had completed 3 years of service as on January 31, 1996 are entitled to be regularised in service.
However, as laid down in the policies, if a person had completed 240 days of service in a year and the break in service was not more than 30 days, he would be considered to have completed one year of service for the purpose of regularisation of his service under the policies.
Copies of the aforesaid policies are appended as Annexures P/1 and P/2 with the present petitions.
5.
Since the claim of the petitioners for regularisation was not being considered under the aforesaid policies, therefore, they filed a Civil Writ Petition No 6869 of 2001 in this Court.
A claim was made for regularisation of their services.
Vide order dated May 14, 2001, a Division Bench of this Court disposed of the aforesaid writ petition with a direction to the respondents to consider the representation filed by the petitioners and dispose of the same by passing a speaking orders.
A copy of the aforesaid order dated May 14, 2001 has been appended as Annexure P/4 with the petitions.
Pursuant to the order passed by the Division Bench in the aforesaid writ petition, Superintending Engineer respondent No. 3 considered the claim of the petitioners through aforesaid representation and vide order dated August 6, 2001 rejected the aforesaid claim of the petitioners by holding that the petitioners did not fulfil the eligibility condition of regularisation in terms of Government policies as their continuous service was less than 3 years as on January 31, 1996.
A copy of the aforesaid order dated August 6, 2001 passed by the Superintending Engineer-respondent No. 3 has been appended as Annexure P/5 with the petitions.
6.
Both the petitioners being aggrieved against the aforesaid order annexure P/5 have filed their respective writ petitions.
7.
In pursuance to a notice issued in the Writ Petitions, the respondents have put in appearance and filed their respective written statements contesting the claim by the petitioners.
The stand taken by the respondents in the written statements is that since the petitioners were engaged on daily wages w.e.f.
April 1, 1993, therefore, the service of the petitioners could not be regularised in terms of the notification/policy because they did not fulfil the requisite conditions laid therein.
The respondents have stated that the petitioners should have completed 3 years service as on January 31, 1996 as per Government policy but they have merely completed a period of 2 years and 10 months as on January 31, 1996.
8.
We have heard Sh. GP Singh, learned Counsel for the petitioners and Sh. Rajbir Sehrawat, learned Deputy Advocate General appearing for the respondents.
9.
Sh. GP Singh, for the petitioners has submitted that as per Government policies dated March 7, 1996 and March 18, 1996 all daily rated employees who had completed 5 or more years continuous service as on January 31, 1996 and were in service on January 31, 1996 were liable to be regularised provided they had worked for a minimum period of 240 days in each year and the break in their service in any year of service was not more than one month at a time.
Subsequently, vide the policy dated March 18, 1996 the period of 5 years was reduced to 3 years and it was laid down that all work-charge/casual/daily rated employees who had completed 3 years service as on January 31, 1996 and fulfilled the other conditions laid down in the policy dated March 7, 1996 were entitled to be regularised.
On the strength of the aforesaid policy and the proviso to the eligibility condition, Sh. GP Singh has submitted that an employee working on daily rated basis was entitled to be regularised even if he had worked for a minimum period of 240 days in each year for three years.
On that basis, it is submitted that since the petitioners in the present case were admittedly appointed on April 1, 1993 and had been in continuous employment thereafter without any break, therefore, even on January 31, 1996, the petitioners had actually completed 240 days in all the 3 preceding years.
Thus, Shri GP Singh has submitted that the petitioners are duly qualified and eligible accordingly to the conditions laid down in the policies Annexures P/1 and P/2 and therefore, their service were liable to be regularised.
10.
On the other hand Sh. Rajbir Sehrawat, learned Deputy Advocate General has submitted that in fact as per policy, a person should have actually served for 3 years as on January 31, 1996 and since the petitioners had actually been appointed w.e.f.
April 1, 1993, therefore, on the face of it they have not completed 3 years of service as on that date.
Sh. Sehrawat has further submitted that the proviso to the conditions laying that service of 240 days in a year would be deemed to be one completed year of service could not be taken to compute the period of 3 years.
11.
We have given our thoughtful consideration to the submission made by the learned Counsel for the parties and find that the present Writ Petitions deserves to succeed.
It has been clearly provided in the policy Annexure P/1 that :  "The casual and daily rated employees who have completed five years service on 31st January, 1996 and were in service on 31st January, 1996, shall be regularised provided they have worked for a minimum period of 240 days in each year and the break in service in any year is not more than one month at a time......" Subsequently the condition of 5 years was changed to 3 years vide policy annexure P/2.
12.
A perusal of the aforesaid policy makes it abundantly clear that if a person had actually worked for 240 days in a year, then it would be deemed that he has worked for full one year and in such a situation when the policy provided that a person was entitled to the regularisation of his service if he had completed 3 years of service as on January 31, 1996, then it would actually mean that a year was deemed to be completed if a person had actually served for 240 days.
Taking the aforesaid view, we find that the petitioners had been appointed w.e.f.
April 1, 1993 and had been continuously working as such without any break till the enforcement of the policies Annexures P/1 and P/2 and were in actual employment also on January 21, 1996.
Thus, the petitioners would be taken to have worked for 240 days in the year 1993, for whole year in the year 1994 and similarly again for the whole year of 1995 and were in actual service on January 31, 1996.
Therefore, the petitioners would be taken to have served for 3 years and are covered by the policies Annexures P/1 and P/2.
13.
The view we have taken to interpret the aforesaid policies finds full support from a Division Bench Judgment of this Court in Ram Dhari v. The Chief Wild Life Warden, 1996(1) SCT 615.
14.
Accordingly, we allow the writ petitions No. 17367 of 2001 and 17379 of 2001 and while quashing order Annexure P/5 direct the respondents to regularise the services of the petitioners in accordance with the Government policies Annexures P/1 and P/2.
On such regularisation the petitioners shall also be entitled to all consequential benefits as were given to regular employees from the date their services were regularised.
No costs.
Petitions allowed.
