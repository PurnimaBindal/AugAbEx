AS Nehra, J - This appeal is directed against the judgment and decree dated 1521991 passed by the Additional District Judge, Chandigarh, by which the appeal of the plaintiff-appellant was dismissed and the judgment and decree dated 23101986 passed by the trial court (dismissing the suit of the plaintiff-appellant) was upheld.
2.
Milkha Singh plaintiff-appellant filed a suit for declaration alleging that he was employed as a work-charge Electrician in July 1957 in the Irrigation Deptt.
; that, on the reorganization of the State of Punjab in the year 1966, he was allocated to the State of Haryana; that the remained on the work-charges establishment for more than 5 years; and this his services, however, were regularized as an Electrician Chargeman only from November 1974, whereas he should have been made regular and confirmed as an Electrician latest by 1962 when he had completed more than 5 years' service.
Referring to a case of work-charge employee, the plaintiff-appellant stated that the industrial Tribunal, Punjab, regularised the services of that employee who had put in three years' continuous service, whereas in the present case he had put in about 17 years' continuous service when he was regularised.
The plaintiff-appellant further pleaded that he retired from service on 31121982 on attaining the age of superannuation and that, since he had only 8 years' service as a regular Government employee, he was deprived of the pensionary benefits.
3.
The suit filed by the plaintiff-appellant was resisted by the defendant-respondent-State, stating that it was not competent as no cause of action had arisen to the plaintiff; that no legal right of the plaintiff had been violated; that the plaintiff was not entitled to the pensionary benefits because he had not put in 10 years' qualifying service; and that according to rule 1132 of the P.W.D.
Code, a work-charge employee was not entitled to any pension, leave or any other allowance.
On facts, it was stated by the defendant-respondent that the plaintiff was employed as work-charge Electrician in June, 1957; that there was a break in the service of the plaintiff from 111964 to 9111964, which was concealed by him from the Court; that the plaintiff was reappointed in the State of Punjab on 10111965 and, later on, he was allocated to the State of Haryana; that the plaintiff was promoted as Chargeman (Electrical) on 1.12.167 and his services were regularised with effect from 21111974 along with other similar employees who had completed more than 5 years' continuous service up to 141972; that it was simultaneously made clear that all the work-charge employees would remain on probation for a period of one year and, thereafter, their services would be regularized after passing specific orders; and that, therefore, the services of the plaintiff were regularized with effect from 21111974 immediately after he completed 5 years' continuous regular service.
It was also stated by the defendant-respondent that the Department of Irrigation was not covered under the Industrial Disputes Act and, therefore, the award of the Industrial Tribunal was not binding on the Department.
4.
In the replication, the plaintiff-appellant reiterated his contentions and refuted those of the defendant-respondent, stating that there was only a national break of nine days from 111964 to 9.1.1964; that he could not be denied the benefit of service from July 1957 to 1121967 where he was promoted as Chargeman (Electrical); and that he could not also be deprived of the benefit of continuous service from 1967 to 1974.
5.
On the pleadings of the parties, the following issues were framed :-  1.
Whether the plaintiff is deemed to be a permanent employee under the defendant after completing five years of service in the year 1962 or any other date found due ?
2.
If issue No. 1 is proved, whether the plaintiff is entitled to pensionary and allied benefits ?
3.
Whether the plaintiff has got no cause of action to file the present suit ?
4.
Whether the suit is not maintainable ?
5.
Relief.
6.
The trial Court decided issue Nos. 1 and 2 against the plaintiff-appellant and issue Nos. 3 and 4 were not pressed by the defendant-respondent-State.
Consequently, the suit filed by the plaintiff-appellant was dismissed by the trial Court on 23101986.
7.
The learned counsel for the appellant has argued that the appellant was entitled to be regularized in the year 1962, i.e., after completion of 5 years' continuous service.
The learned counsel for the respondent has argued that the services of the appellant could be regularized neither in the year 1966 nor in the year 1972, as there was a break in his service from 111964 to 9111964.
Faced with this situation, Shri GS Minhas, learned counsel for the appellant, submitted that under Rule 423 of the Punjab Civil Services Rules, Volume II, condonation of interruption in service is permissible subject to the conditions laid down therein and that, since the interruption in the case of the appellant was not more than one year's duration, the interruption should be condoned.
Rule 423 of the Punjab Civil Service Rules, Vol II, reads as under :-  "423.
Interruption service (either between two spells or permanent, or temporary service or between a spell of temporary service and permanent service or vice versa in the case of an officer retiring on or after the 5th January, 1961, may be condoned, subject to the following conditions, namely :- (1) The interruption should have been caused by reasons beyond the control of Government Servant concerned.
(2) Service preceding the interruption should not be less than five years' duration.
In cases where there are two or more interruptions, the total service, pensionary benefits in respect of which shall be lost if the interruptions are not condoned should not be less than five years.
(3) The interruption should not be of more than one year's duration.
In cases where there are two ore more interruptions, the total period of all interruptions to be condoned should not exceed one year."
8.
There is no force in this contention of the learned counsel for the appellant.
Such interruptions in service can be condoned by the competent authority and not by the court.
Nothing has come on the record to shown that the appellant ever applied for condonation of interruption in service.
9.
The appellant is proved to be working continuously on the job from 10111964 in the erstwhile State of Punjab.
He was allocated to the State of Haryana on reorganisation of the Punjab State and was promoted on 1121967.
His services were regularised on 21111974 and he retired on 31121982 on attaining the age of superannuation.
The learned counsel for the appellant has argued that the case of the plaintiff-appellant is covered by a Full Bench judgment of this Court in Kesar Chand v. State of Punjab through the Secretary, P.W.D.
(B&R), Chandigarh and others, 1988(2) Punjab Law Reporter 223, Mr. JV Yadav, Deputy Advocate General, Haryana appearing for the respondent-State, has fairly conceded that Kesar Chand's case (supra) is fully applicable to the facts of the present case.
10.
In view of the above-mentioned discussion, it is held that the services rendered by the plaintiff-appellant from 10111964 to 21111974 as a work-charge employee shall be taken into consideration for the purposes of granting pensionary and the allied benefits.
Resultantly, the appeal is allowed the judgments and decrees passed by the lower Courts are set aside, and the suit filed by the plaintiff-appellant is decreed.
Since the plaintiff-appellant died on 591991, therefore, his legal heirs, namely, Karnail Singh, Mohan Singh and Gurdeep Singh (sons of Dial Singh) are entitled to receive the pensionary and the allied benefits.
Appeal allowed.
