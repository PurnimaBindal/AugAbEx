GS Singhvi, J - This is a petition to quash the order dated 181997 issued by the Government of Punjab under Rule 3(1) of the Punjab Civil Services (Premature Retirement) Rules, 1975 (for short the 1975 Rules) prematurely retiring the petitioner from service.
2.
The facts on which there is no dispute between the parties are that the petitioner, who joined services as Civil Defence Instructor on 521964, received two promotions during the course of service.
He was twice allowed to cross efficiency bar and was also given the benefit of proficiency step up w.e.f.
111986.
The details of his promotions etc. are given below :  (i) promoted as Company Commander on 131968 (the date of promotion was subsequently revised and he was given deemed promotion w.e.f.
3.9.1966); (ii) promoted as District Commander w.e.f.
11.4.1977;  (iii) allowed to cross efficiency bar w.e.f.
131976 in the scale of Rs. 320-20-460/20-600; (iv) allowed to cross efficiency bar w.e.f.
131980 in the time scale of 800-30-1000/40-1200/50-1400; (v) vide order dated 3171991, he was given the benefit of proficiency step up w.e.f.
111986 in the revised pay scale.
The petitioner has challenged the impugned order of retirement on the ground of arbitrariness, non-application of mind and mala fides.
His contention is that the government has exercised the power under Rule 3(1) of the 1975 Rules without making an objective assessment of his service record and the impugned order, which is primarily based on the stale entries of the years 1968-69, 1969-70 and 1979-80 should be declared nullity on the ground of arbitrariness.
3.
The respondents have defendant the petitioner's premature retirement from service by asserting that the competent authority has, after evaluation of the entire service record, come to the conclusion that his retention in service is not in public interest.
The respondents have also pleaded that the instructions issued by the government do not preclude consideration of the service record of more than 10 years and, therefore, adverse remarks contained in the petitioner's Annual Confidential Reports for the year 1979-80 could legitimately be acted upon for the purpose of boarding him out of service.
4.
We have heard Shri R.K. Malik, learned counsel for the petitioner and Shri Rupinder Khosla, learned Deputy Advocate General and have perused the record produced by Shri Khosla.
5.
The right of the public employer to retire an employee before attaining the age of superannuation has been judicially recognised during last 40 years, as is evident from the following decisions :  (i) Shyam Lal v. State of UP, A.I.R. 1954 SC 369.
(ii) Shivcharana Singh v. State of Mysore, A.I.R. 1965 SC 280.
(iii) Union of India v. JN Sinha and another, A.I.R. 1971 SC 40.
(iv) Union of India v. MD Reddy and another, A.I.R. 1980 SC 563.
(v) Baikuntha Nath Dass and another v. Chief District Medical Officer, Baripada and another, 1992(2) SLR 2 : 1992(2) SCT 92 (SC).
In the last mentioned decision, their Lordships of the Supreme Court, after review of the judicial precedents, culled out the following principles:  (i) An order of compulsory retirement is not a punishment.
It implies no stigma nor any suggestion of misbehaviour.
(ii) The order has to be passed by the Government on forming the opinion that it is in the public interest to retire a government servant compulsorily.
The order is passed on the subjective satisfaction of the government.
(iii) Principles of natural justice have no place in the context of an order of compulsory retirement.
This does not mean that judicial scrutiny is excluded altogether.
While the High Court or this Court would not examine the matter as an appellate Court, they may interfere if they are satisfied that the order is passed (a) mala fide or (b) that it is based on no evidence or (c) that it is arbitrary in the sense that no reasonable person would form the requisite opinion on the given material; in short, if it is found to be a perverse order.
(iv) The Government (or the Review Committee, as the case may be) shall have to consider the entire record of service before taking a decision in the matter of course attaching more importance to record of and performance during the later years.
The record to be so considered would naturally include the entries in the confidential records/character rolls, both favourable and adverse.
If a government servant is promoted to a higher post notwithstanding the adverse remarks, such remarks lose their sting, more so, if the promotion is based upon merit (selection) and not upon seniority.
(v) An order of compulsory retirement is not liable to be quashed by a Court merely on the showing that while passing it uncommunicated adverse remarks were also taken into consideration.
That circumstance by itself cannot be a basis for interference.
Having noticed the relevant principles, we shall not decided whether the impugned order of retirement is liable to be invalidated on the ground that the opinion formed by the respondents on the suitability of the petitioner to be retained in service is vitiated by error of law, arbitrariness or mala fides.
However, before doing that, we deem it necessary to remind ourselves that while exercising jurisdiction under Article 226 of the Constitution, the High Court does not sit in appeal over the judgment of the government in such matters but confines the review of the decision making process to the well defined limitations, namely, whether the exercise of the power of retirement is vitiated due to consideration of irrelevant and extraneous material or there is no cogent material justifying the formation of opinion that the employee is not fit to be retained in service.
6.
A bare reading of the order Annexure P12 shows that the power vested in the government has been exercised by the government on the premises that the over all record of the petitioner is average and he has earned adverse remarks in the years 1968-69, 1969-70, 1979-80 and 1995-96.
The warning given to the petitioner in the year 1973-74, withholding of efficiency bar w.e.f.
131976 and pendency of a complaint are the other factors which have weighed with the government in deciding that the petitioner should not be retained in service after the age of 55 years.
7.
Before proceeding further, we may take note of the instructions issued by the government vide circular letter No. 16/42/78-2 PP/7300 dated 2261981, the relevant portion of which is extracted below :  "I am directed to invite your attention to Punjab Government circular letter No. 6520-2SII-75/38581 dated the 26th September, 1975 relating to the weeding out of corrupt, dishonest or inefficient officers/officials from services under the Punjab Civil Services (Premature Retirement) Rules, 1975, and to say that Government has reviewed the matter in the light of the revised policy of the Government of India and other administrative requirements.
Government has decided to lay down the following additional guidelines in the matter: (i) Although the entire service record of an employee has to be considered, premature retirement should not be ordered if during the last 5 years the work and conduct of the employee has been good or better than that.
(ii) Ordinarily no retirement should be ordered within a period of one year preceding the date of superannuation of the government employee.
(iii) If an adverse entry relating to integrity exists in the confidential reports during the 10 years preceding the review, or if after recording there has been no change in the class, status or the post of the officers that single entry should be considered sufficient for ordering premature retirement.
(iv) If the adverse report on integrity relates to the distant post or is more than 10 years old, the subsequent record of the employee should be scrutinised carefully.
If the subsequent reports vouchsafe the integrity of the employee in unambiguous term, the inference is that he has improved his conduct and it should not be necessary to order his premature retirement.
A similar view can be taken if an employee has been promoted after the recording of the adverse remarks.
I am to request that the above guidelines may please be brought to the notice of all concerned officers/officials for information and guidance."
8.
A careful analysis of the impugned order in the light of the instructions issued by the government makes it clear that while examining the petitioner's case for retention in service, the competent authority directed its attention only to the adverse reports and ignored all the good reports.
This, in our considered opinion, is contrary to principle (iv) laid down in Baikuntha Nath Dass's case (supra).
However, in order to satisfy ourselves whether on the file there is a consideration of good as well as bad reports, we minutely examined the record produced by the learned Deputy Advocate General but could not find any such consideration.
In fact, perusal of the record shows that instead of making an over all evaluation of the petitioner's service record, the government based its decision mainly on the adverse Annual Confidential Reports for the years 1968-69, 1969-70, 1979-80 and 1995-96 with greater emphasis on the adverse Annual Confidential Report of the year 1979-80 casting reflection on the petitioner's integrity completely ignoring the fact that the adverse reports of the years 1968-69, 1969-70 and 1979-80 were recorded 29, 28 and 16 years prior to the year of consideration and the same had lost their significance and relevance in the context of assessment of petitioner's suitability to be retained in service beyond the age of 55 years.
The competent authority also over-looked the fact that the petitioner was given promotion as District Commander on 11.4.1977 and was allowed to cross efficiency bar w.e.f.
131980 notwithstanding the adverse Annual Confidential Reports of the years 1968-69 and 1969-70.
Likewise, the grant of proficiency step up w.e.f.
111986 even in the face of adverse Annual Confidential Report of the year 1979-80 has been ignored.
The factum of existence of these adverse Annual Confidential Reports at the time of petitioner's promotion to the post of District Commander, at the time he was allowed to cross efficiency bar and was granted the benefit of proficiency step up has not been contested by the respondents.
It is, thus, evident that these adverse Annual Confidential Reports were not considered sufficient to withhold promotion and efficiency bar of the petitioner or to deny him the benefit of proficiency step up.
In view of this, it is difficult to find any rational reason to justify the petitioner's retirement on the basis of the adverse remarks which were recorded 29, 28 and 16 years prior to the year of retirement.
In our opinion, the petitioner's promotion to the higher post of District Commander, the decision of the respondents to allow him to cross efficiency bar and grant of the benefit of proficiency step up in spite of the adverse Annual Confidential Reports are sufficient to invoke the theory of wash out/dilution of adverse entries of those years as indicated in IVth principle laid down in Baikuntha Nath Dass's case (supra).
9.
For the same reasons, we hold that the warning (1973-74) administered to the petitioner 23 years before the passing of impugned order of retirement cannot have any bearing on the petitioner's suitability to be retained in service and these stale adverse materials could not, in law, be made basis for forming an opinion that the petitioner has outlived his utility or that he has become a dead wood or that his efficiency is impaired to such an extent that his retention in service is not in public interest.
10.
The only remaining adverse factor on which the respondent could legitimately base its decision on the question of petitioner's retention in service is the so-called adverse Annual Confidential Report of the year 1995- 96 which was communicated to him vide Annexure P.8 dated 1481996 and against which the petitioner's representation has been rejected.
We have perused the two reports recorded during the year 1995-96.
For the period between 1.4.1995 and 3.8.1995, the Reporting Officer has described him as an average officer and graded him `B'.
The second Reporting Authority has made the following remarks: "An average Commandant.
Needs to take more interest in work.
Should not spread rumour."
For the remaining period i.e. 781995 to 3131996, the Reporting Officer has remarked "An easy going officer.
Lacking inquisitive mind."
He has graded him `B'.
The second Reporting Authority has made the following remarks : "An average rank officer who needs to work hard more.
This Commandant should refrain from the habit of spreading rumours and loose talk".
As against these adverse remarks, which are by and large advisory in nature, the Annual Confidential Reports of the petitioner of the last 10 years contain the following gradings :  Year Remarks.
1985-86 B plus 1986-87 B plus 1987-88 Not available 1988-89 B plus 1989-90 B  1990-91 B plus 1991-92 B 1992-93 B 1993-94 B plus 1994-95 B 1995-96 B 1996-97 B It may be mentioned that for the year 1996-97, the first Reporting Officer has given average/adverse remarks and has graded him `C' but the second Reporting Officer who is superior in rank has expressed his disagreement with the remarks given by the first authority and has described the petitioner as a competent, dependable and intelligent officer and graded him `B'.
11.
It is, thus, evident that except for the Annual Confidential Report of the year 1995-96 which contains advisory remarks, there is nothing adverse available in the record of the petitioner which could cast reflection on his ability and integrity justifying the exercise of power of premature retirement and those entries cannot justify premature retirement in view of the fact that his other reports are good and satisfactory.
12.
On the basis of above discussion, we hold that the decision taken by the government to prematurely retire the petitioner on the basis of single advisory entry in the Annual Confidential Reports of last 10 years ignoring 5 goods and 4 satisfactory reports is legally unsustainable.
We are also of the opinion that the consideration of the adverse reports of 1968-69 (29 years old), 1969-70 (28 years old), and 1979-80 (16 years old) and the warning administered to the petitioner in the year 1973-74 (23 years old) has the effect of vitiating the decision making process and the impugned order of retirement suffers from a patent legal error warranting interference by this Court under Article 226 of the Constitution of India.
13.
For the reasons mentioned above, the writ petition is allowed.
Order Annexure P12 is quashed.
The petitioner is ordered to be reinstated in service with consequential benefits.
Petition allowed.
