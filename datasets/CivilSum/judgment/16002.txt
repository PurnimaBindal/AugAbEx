Jawahar Lal Gupta, J. (Oral) - Is the post of Assistant Librarian in the office of the Advocate General, Haryana equivalent of that of Librarian in the Haryana Civil Secretariat?
This is the short question that arises for consideration in this writ petition.
The petitioner claims that in the year 1977 he was appointed to this post and was placed in the scale of Rs. 300-600 which was also the scale for the post of Librarian in the Civil Secretariat.
Inferentially, the two posts were treated as equal and the action of the State Govt. in placing the post of Assistant Librarian in a lower scale of pay than that granted to the Librarian suffers from the vice of discrimination and is thus violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
Is it so ?
A few facts may be noticed.
2.
The post of Assistant Librarian in the office of the Advocate General, Haryana, was sanctioned by the Govt. vide order dated November 4, 1977.
A copy of the order has been produced as Annexure P-1 with the writ petition.
A perusal thereof shows that the Govt. had accorded sanction "to the creation of the post of Assistant Librarian on purely temporary basis in the grade of Rs. 300-25-600 plus usual allowances as sanctioned by the Haryana Government from time to time from the date of entertainment upto 2821978."
The petitioner who was working as a Clerk was promoted to this post on November 5, 1977.
Thereafter, there have been periodic revisions of scales of pay.
The position in this behalf has been summarised in para 5 of the written statement filed on behalf of the respondents.

pay (as a personal measure) -do- The petitioner's grievance as mentioned above is that the action of the respondents in not giving him a scale of pay equivalent to that sanctioned for the post of Librarian is discriminatory and thus violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
The respondents contest the petitioner's claim and point out that the qualifications, the nature of the duties and responsibilities attached to the two posts being different, the difference in scales of pay is just and reasonable.
3.
Mr. Surya Kant, learned counsel for the petitioner, submits that the post of Assistant Librarian was equated to that of Librarian in the year 1977.
Thereafter, nothing had happened which could entitle the State Govt. to create a disparity between the two posts.
The petitioner was entitled to parity of treatment in the scale of pay and should have been granted the same scale of pay as had been granted to the Librarian in the Civil Secretariat.
Ms. Ritu Bahri, appearing for the respondents contests this claim.
4.
The post of Assistant Librarian was administratively sanctioned by the Govt. on November 4, 1977.
At that time, no rules had been framed.
However, a graduate with Diploma in Library Science was considered as being eligible for appointment to the post of Assistant Librarian.
Accordingly, the petitioner who was a graduate with Diploma in Library Science was appointed as an Assistant Librarian on November 5, 1977.
Subsequently, rules governing the conditions of service of the employees in the office of the Advocate-General were promulgated on October 13, 1981.
The post of Assistant Librarian was included in these rules.
The qualifications for this post were laid down in Appendix 'B'.
For appointment by direct appointment, the candidates were required to possess the qualification of BA with Diploma in Library Science.
The qualifications for appointment by a method other than direct recruitment were "BA, or Matric - with Diploma of Library Science.
Three years' experience as Clerk."
As against this, the qualifications for appointment to the post of Librarian in the Civil Secretariat were laid down vide order dated May 27, 1977.
These were as under :-  "1.
Minimum educational and other qualifications.
MA with a Diploma in Library Science of a recognised University 2.
Minimum experience Three Years' service as a Liberarian A copy of this order which has been produced by Ms. Bahri during the course of hearing is placed on record as Mark 'A'.
It is, thus, clear that the minimum qualifications for the two posts are different.
While a candidate for the post of Assistant Librarian in the Civil Secretariat is required to possess a post-graduate degree with a Diploma in Library Science, and an experience of three years as a Librarian, a mere graduate with a Diploma in Library Science is eligible for direct recruitment to the post of Assistant Librarian in the office of the Advocate General.
Otherwise, even a mere graduate or a matriculate with a Diploma in Library Science with three years' experience as a Clerk is eligible for appointment by transfer or deputation.
Thus, there is a significant difference between the minimum qualifications required for the two posts.
5.
It is true that initially in the year 1977 the two posts had been placed in the scale of Rs. 300-600.
However, it is apparent that at the time of subsequent revisions, the post of Librarian has always been in a higher scale of pay.
On March 14, 1978, the pay scale of the post of Librarian was raised from Rs. 300-600 to Rs. 400-800.
A corresponding increase was not allowed to the Assistant Librarian in the office of the Advocate General.
Thereafter, with effect from April 1, 1979, the post of Librarian was placed in the scale of Rs. 1000-1500 with a special pay of Rs. 100/- while the post of Assistant Librarian was in the scale of Rs. 600-1100.
In view of the provision in the Haryana Civil Services (Revised Scales of Pay) Rules, 1980, the petitioner was given the scale of Rs. 700-1400 on July 22, 1983 when he had qualified the MA examination.
Still, the scale was lower than that of the post of Librarian in the Civil Secretariat.
The subsequent revision of pay scale was made effective from January 1, 1986.
The post of Librarian was placed in the scale of Rs. 2000-3500, while that of an Assistant Librarian was in the scale of Rs. 1640-2900.
It is, thus, clear that the disparity between the pay scales sanctioned for the two posts has continued since March 14, 1978.
The fact that the incumbent of the post of Librarian in the Haryana Secretariat was given the scale of Rs. 1400-1860 with a special pay of Rs. 200/- with effect from April 15, 1985, or even higher scales thereafter, is of no relevance.
Factually, the State Govt. has clearly treated the two posts differently since the year 1978.
6.
Even otherwise, besides a vague statement in the petition that the duties of the two posts are equal, nothing has been placed on the record to show that the Govt. had ever passed any orders equating the post of Assistant Librarian with that of a Librarian.
It is primarily for the Govt. to decide about the equivalence of posts.
The writ Court has neither the expertise nor the relevant material to determine the equivalence of posts.
If on consideration of relevant facts the Govt. finds that the post of Librarian carries higher responsibilities and status and is thus required to be placed in a higher scale of pay than that of an Assistant Librarian, the Court would be normally reluctant to interfere with the action unless it is shown to be patently unfair and illegal.
In the present case, it is clear that the qualifications required for the two posts are vastly different.
The Govt. has treated these posts differently since the year 1978.
While a person has to possess a post graduate degree for appointment to the post of Librarian, a mere Matriculate with a Diploma in Library Science and the prescribed experience can be appointed to the post of an Assistant Librarian.
The distinction between the two posts is obvious.
The petitioner never challenged it.
In this situation, it cannot be said that the action of the State in sanctioning different scales of pay is arbitrary or unfair.
7.
Accordingly, the question posed at the outset is answered in the negative.
It is held that the post of an Assistant LIbrarian in the office of the Advocate-General, Haryana, is not equivalent to that of Librarian in the Haryana Civil Secretariat.
It follows that the action of the respondents is not discriminatory or violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
8.
In view of the above, I find no merit in this petition.
It is dismissed.
In the circumstances of the case, there will be no order as to costs.
Petition dismissed.
