Swatanter Kumar, J - Sub Inspector Karam Singh, the petitioner, joined as Constable in the Punjab Police on 21.6.1971.
According to the petitioner, he was a National Athlete and won a gold medal.
He passed the Lower School Course and was placed in the promotion List C-I in March, 1979.
After confirmation as Head Constable he was sent to Intermediate School Course which he passed successfully and was consequently promoted as Assistant Sub Inspector and was placed in the promotion list "D".
Ultimately, the petitioner was placed on promotion lis "E" meant for promotion to the rank of Sub Inspector on 141987, but was not promoted as Sub Inspector.
The petitioner, however, was promoted as adhoc Sub Inspector and permitted to officiate on 23101992 and 9.11.1993 respectively.
According to the petitioner, he ought to have been promoted as Sub Inspector from the year 1987 and Inspector from 1990 and DSP from 1997 when persons who were shown junior to him were promoted.
A representation filed in this behalf by the petitioner was rejected by the respondents vide order dated 811999 Annexure P-1 to the writ petition.
The petitioner thus challenges this order in this writ petition and prays for the above noted reliefs.
2.
Upon notice, the respondents filed written statement wherein it was stated that the petitioner has not exhausted the departmental remedies available to him.
The petition has been filed after a great delay and in fact, the petitioner has no cause of action.
The order has been passed in accordance with rules.
The work and conduct of the petitioner as reflected in his Confidential Reports right from 1984 to 1988 was undesirable.
In fact, there has been orders of punishment against the petitioner forfeiting 5 years of service on different occasions.
Still, the petitioner has been granted the benefit of promotion.
The present petition is thus, misconceived and is liable to be dismissed.
3.
The main contention raised on behalf of the petitioner is that the petitioner has been ignored and has not been granted promoted in violation to the rules (i.e. Rule 1312.91) of the Punjab Police Rules.
He also relies upon a Division Bench judgment of this Court in the case of Gurcharan Singh v. State of Punjab, 1974(1) S.L.R.
62.
4.
Before we determine the merit of this contention in the light of the referred judgment, it will be important for us to note the main factors that weighed with the concerned authorities for denying promotion to the petitioner for some period.
In the written statement, the stand taken is identical as stated in the impugned order.
The language of the impugned order reads as under :-  "Copy of Endst.

5.
The authorities have applied their mind and have rejected the representation of the petitioner for grant of promotion to different posts from different dates commencing from 1987.
We are unable to see any error in the opinion of the authority in ignoring the petitioner for the relevant periods.
The above averments have not been denied as the petitioner has filed no replication.
Even in the writ petition, the petitioner has not disputed the correctness of Annexure P-1 and has rendered no explanation in that regard.
The service record as afore-noticed, does not infringe the order of the authority on the ground of arbitrariness.
The authorities have still approved the petitioner for promotion to the rank of officiating Sub Inspector with effect from 9.11.1993 which further shows the bona fides on the part of the concerned authorities.
The service which has been forfeited can obviously not be part of the service rendered on duty.
6.
As far as the Division Bench judgment of this Court in Gurcharan Singh's case (supra) is concerned, all that has been stated is as under :-  "To our mind, there is no manner of doubt that the underlying principle of rule 1316(2) is that promotions to the temporary vacancies of Sub-Inspectors and Inspectors are not hide bound by seniority but by the overriding consideration of affording equal opportunities to all eligible candidates on lists "E" and "F" for holding an independent charge in the higher rank and to test their mettle in these posts."
It is clear from the bare reading of the above finding that opportunity has to be granted to the eligible candidates on consideration.
The petitioner has admittedly been considered and ignored, more so, for particular reasons.
We are unable to appreciate as to how the judgment of the Division Bench supports the case of the petitioner in the light of the reasoning recorded in the impugned order.
The spirit behind Rule 1312 is intended to prevent reversion of an officer from the rank of Assistant Sub Inspector just because the person senior to the officer concerned is not officiating in that rank.
It no way directs the competent authority to ignore the conduct of service record of the officer.
The argument of the learned counsel for the petitioner that in all events, he must first be appointed and his matter should be tested by the departmental authorities before passing any such order, is again misconceived.
A person has no right of promotion.
The right is that consideration which has been granted to the petitioner undisputedly.
For the reasons afore-stated, we find no merit in this petition.
The same is dismissed, however, leaving the parties to bear their own costs.
Petition dismissed.
