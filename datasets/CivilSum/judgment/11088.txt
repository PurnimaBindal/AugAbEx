GS Singhvi, J.- This case is illustrative of the sufferance of a poor citizen of this country.
In the first instance he has been forced by the respondents to file this writ petition because they did not honour his constitution right of equality.
Secondly, he has been abandoned by his Counsel, who has not thought it proper to appear when the case was called for hearing.
Whether this is justified or not is a matter which I leave to the learned counsel to decide.
However, keeping in view the constitutional duty of a court to do justice between the parties and also keeping in view the fact that the accountability of the judicial system is to the people of the country and not to the members belonging to the legal profession, I have considered the claim made by the petitioner on its merit despite the absence of this Advocate.
2.
Facts of the case show that the petitioner joined service as a Physical Training Instructor on 2.5.1971.
He passed D.P.Ed.
and MA in July, 1974 and July, 1985 respectively.
After having acquired higher training and academic qualification, the petitioner made a claim for being granted the benefit of higher pay and has pleaded that despite the judgments of this court and the Supreme Court, the respondents have failed to give him relief by way of fixation of his pay in the higher scale in accordance with his higher academic and training qualifications.
He had relied on the gonvernment's letter dated 23.7.1957 which was issued by the Government of Punjab and which was adopted by the Government of Haryana.
3.
The respondents have resisted the claim of the petitioner on the ground that the policy decision contained in letter dated 23.7.1957 was followed till 30-11-1967.
Thereafter the grades were raised and with effect from 1121967 benefit of higher grade is not admissible on the basis of higher qualifications.
It has been pleaded by the respondents that Supreme Court's decision in Chaman Lal v. State of Haryana, 1987 (1) SLR 4, is not applicable to the case of the petitioner.
4.
After hearing the learned Assistant Advocate General, I am fully convinced that the claim made by the petitioner merits acceptance.
In Civil Writ Petition No 15630 of 1993 (Rattan Singh and eight others v. State of Haryana and others, 1994(3) SCT 243 (P&H) decided on 1491994. a Division Bench of this Court, to which I was a party, accepted the claim of the persons similarly situated and held that on the basis of the revision of grades with effect from 1121967 and once again with effect from 1.4.1979, the benefit of higher pay scale based on higher qualifications cannot be denied to the existing employees.
In that decision, the Division Bench has also taken note of the letter dated 931990 issued by the Government and held that the said circular cannot be used as a tool to deny the benefit of higher grade to the persons who have acquired higher qualifications.
In taking the view that even after 1121967 the persons acquiring higher qualifications are entitled to the benefit of higher pay scales, the Division Bench has relied on the following judgments :-  1) Chaman Lal and others v. State of Haryana, 1987 (4) SLR 4;  2) Punjab Higher Qualified Teachers Union v. State of Punjab, 1988(1);  3) Jetha Singh and others v. State of Punjab and another, 1988(4) SLR 300;  4) Nasib Kaur and others v. State of Punjab and others, 1988 (4) SLR 301;  5) CWP.
No. 9999 of 1939, Megh Raj and others v. State of Haryana and others, decided on 26111994;  6) CWP No 9568 of 1993, Ram Parkash and others v. State of Haryana and anthers, decided on 1971994;  7) CWP No 16289 of 1993, Shakuntla Devi and others v. State of Haryana and others, decided on 571994, and  8) CWP No 4639 of 1993, Mohiud Kathuria v. State of Haryana, decided on 1311994.
In view of the law laid down by this Court and the Supreme Court and in view of the decision of the Division Bench dated 1491994, I find every justification to accept the claim of the petitioner.
For the detailed reasons which have been recorded in the order of the Division Bench and which shall be treated as part of this judgment, the writ petition is allowed.
The respondents are directed to give relief to the petitioner by fixing his pay in the higher pay scale with effect from the date.
He acquired the higher qualifications.
However, the petitioner shall not be entitled to interest.
Costs made easy.
