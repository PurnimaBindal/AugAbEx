SS Sodhi, J - No occasion is provided here to interfere with or question the judgment of the learned single Judge.
2.
The controversy in the writ petition concerned promotion to the post of Assistant Grade II in the General Administration Cadre, in the context of Food Corporation of India (Staff) (30th amendment) Regulations, 1976 whereby three years experience in the lower cadre was prescribed for graduates and five years for non-graduates, as an essential condition for promotion to these posts.
The writ petitioners (now respondents 1 to 5) who were non-graduates had all been appointed before the present respondents 6 to 12 but on account of being non-graduates, they were shown junior to these respondents in the final seniority list published on August 20, 1982.
Similar seniority of the parties to the petition was reflected in the subsequent provisional seniority list of December 16, 1987.
3.
It is pertinent to note that no objections were raised with regard to the seniority list of August 20, 1982 or even against the provisional list of December 16, 1987.
The writ petitioners, however, came to this court instead and challenged the validity of the amendment 1976 seeking also the consequential relief of seniority over respondents 6 to 12.
4.
As regards the amendment of 1976 two High Courts, namely, those of Kerala in 1983(1) All India Service Law Journal 412 and Andhra Pradesh in Civil Writ Petitions 363 and 1168 of 1987 decided on April 20, 1987, have struck it down as being violative of Article 14 of the Constitution.
According to the learned counsel for the respondents the Special Leave Petition filed against the judgment of the High Court of Andhra Pradesh has also been dismissed.
Following these judicial precedents, the learned single Judge too held the amendment to be invalid.
The further plea on behalf of the respondents to deny the relief claimed by the petitioners, on the ground of laches was also turned down.
5.
What renders this appeal wholly misconceived is that no controversy now survives to adjudicate upon.
None of the employees affected by the decision of the learned Judge has chosen to contest it and the invalidity of the amendment of 1976 is not, in any manner, being questioned.
Such being the situation, there is no option but to dismiss this appeal with costs.
Counsel's fee Rs. 1000/-.
Appeal dismissed.
