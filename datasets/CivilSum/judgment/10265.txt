JS Khehar, J (Oral) - The petitioner was inducted into the service of the Military Hospital, Ambala Cantt, on 21.8.1961 as a Lower Division Clerk.
In November 1969 he was promoted to the post of Accountant.
2.
While the petitioner was holding the post of Accountant, during a surprise check certain discrepancies in the accounts were noticed on 1791971.
On the basis of the aforesaid discrepancies, the authorities registered a first information report.
Charges were framed against the petitioner under Section 5(1)(c) read with section 5(2) of the Prevention of Corruption Act and under Sections 409/477-A and 120-B of the Indian Penal Code.
After having been tried, the petitioner was convicted by the Special Judge, Haryana at Ambala, vide judgment/order dated 21/28.11.1978.
Dissatisfied by the judgment/order, referred to above, the petitioner preferred Criminal Appeal No. 1417 of 1978.
The said appeal was disposed of on 12.5.1982.
A persual of the order passed by this Court in the aforesaid appeal reveals that the conviction of the petitioner was upheld.
However, the sentence of imprisonment and fine was set aside with a direction that the petitioner would be released on probation for a period of one year on his entering into a bond in the sum of Rs. 2000/- with one surety in the like amount to the satisfaction of the trial Court.
3.
On the basis of the aforesaid conviction of the petitioner, the Director, Medical Services, vide order dated 631979, in exercise of powers vested in him under Rule 19(1) of the Central Civil Services (Classification, Control and Appeal) Rules, 1965 (hereinafter referred to as, the 1965 Rules') dismissed the petitioner from service.
4.
It is not a matter of dispute between the parties that an appeal is competent against an order passed under Rule 19(1) of the 1965 Rules.
The claim of the petitioner is that he preferred an appeal against the order dated 631979 on 15.3.1979.
The appeal preferred by the petitioner was submitted through registered acknowledgment due post.
A copy of the said appeal has been placed on the record of this case an Annexure P.5.
Since the appeal was not being decided by the respondents, the petitioner approached this Court by filing the instant petition impugning the order dated 631979 as also having a challenge to the inaction of the authorities in not disposing of the appeal preferred by him.
5.
Notice of motion was issued on 2191979.
The respondents entered appearance before this Court on 16.10.1979.
Written statement in the original writ petition was filed on 2921980.
In the original writ petition i paragraph 7, the petitioner had expressly stated the factum of having filed the appeal referred to above.
In the written statement filed, there was no denial to the factum of filing of the appeal.
It was, however, stated that the legality of the impugned order of dismissal dated 631979 was the subject matter of appeal.
6.
In the interregnum, an amended writ petition came to be filed wherein the same averments were asserted insofar as the factum of filing of the appeal against the impugned order dated 631979.
Written statement thereto dated 392001 has been filed recently in this Court.
The averments contained in paragraph 7 of the writ petition are unchanged.
Likewise the response thereto on behalf of the respondents remains the same.
It is, therefore, evident that despite the fact that there is a statutory appeal which has been filed by the petitioner, the same remains undecided despite the passage of more than 22 years.
Despite the fact that this Court, on the basis of the facts alleged against the respondents, had released the petitioner on probation, the appellate authority has slept over the appeal.
It is obvious that the entire controversy has to be re-determined keeping in view the order passed by this Court on 12.5.1982 whereby the sentence and fine imposed upon the petitioner were set aside and the petitioner was ordered to be released on probation.
The Competent Authority/Appellate Authority alone are vested with the said responsibility.
It is improper for this Court to determine the controversy raised by the petitioner till the appeal preferred by the petitioner is disposed of.
7.
In view of the peculiar facts narrated above, the appellate authority is directed to decide the appeal preferred by the petitioner within one month from the date of receipt of a certified copy of this order.
The petitioner shall also be entitled to Rs. 10,000/- on account of apathy shown by the respondents in not deciding the appeal preferred by him, for a period of more than 22 years.
8.
With the directions aforesaid, this petition stands disposed of.
Order accordingly.
