ML Singhal, J - One Gulab Singh was employee of Municipal Committee, Jaitu, District Faridkot.
He was tried and convicted under Sections 409/419/420/468 of the Indian Penal Code for misappropriation.
Judicial Magistrate, Faridkot acquitted him of the charge under Section 409 of the Indian Penal Code vide judgment dated 2821981, but convicted him under Section 468 of the Indian Penal Code and sentenced him to one year rigorous imprisonment and a fine of Rs. 1,000/-.
Appeal filed by him was dismissed by Additional Sessions Judge, Faridkot and the conviction and sentence was confirmed.
Gulab Singh did not relent and filed a revision in the Punjab & Haryana High Court challenging his conviction and sentence.
Revision was allowed on 0831984 and the consequence was his acquittal of all the charges levelled against him.
After his acquittal by the High Court, Gulab Singh submitted his joining report to Municipal Committee, Jaitu on 29.3.1984.
He requested to allow him to join duty as he had been acquitted of all the charges levelled against him.
He made repeated representations requesting to allow him to join duty, but to no effect.
Vide order dated 231981, he was dismissed from service with effect from 2821981.
2.
Gulab Singh challenged his dismissal from service through Civil Writ Petition No. 110 of 1985 (Gulab Singh v. The Director Local Bodies and another).
He prayed for issuance of writ of mandamus directing the Municipal Committee, Jaitu to allow him to join duty.
Municipal Committee filed reply to the writ petition stating therein that Municipal Committee had filed a petition for special leave to appeal with Criminal Misc. No. 5090 of 1984 in the Supreme Court of India.
The Supreme Court has issued notice to the petitioner.
As such, he was not entitled to reinstatement.
3.
During the pendency of writ petition, Gulab Singh died on 19101990.
His legal heirs were allowed to be brought on record vide order dated 621991.
Legal heirs of Gulab Singh were accordingly brought on record.
Since Gulab Singh had died on 19101990, as such, prayer for his reinstatement in service became redundant.
Order of dismissal was impugned on the ground that the same was without issuance of show cause notice.
Dismissal was ordered simply on account of his conviction in a criminal case.
It was submitted that as Gulab Singh had been acquitted, the order of dismissal could not survive.
Hon'ble Amarjeet Chaudhary, J, as his Lordship then was, vide order dated 2811997, took the view that the order of dismissal can not survive in view of Gulab Singh having died on 19101990.
As the petitioner was acquitted or the charges by the High Court, the said appeal abated on account of death of Gulab Singh.
Hon'ble Amarjeet Chaudhary, J further held once Gulab Singh had been acquitted of the charges, the very foundation of the order of dismissal disappeared.
It was also observed that no show cause notice had been issued to Gulab Singh before passing the order of dismissal.
Order of dismissal dated 231981 was, therefore, quashed with a direction that the legal heirs of the deceased will be entitled to monetary benefit from the date he was acquitted till his death in accordance with law.
It was directed that monetary benefits shall be released to the legal heirs of the deceased within 4 months from the date of receipt of copy of the order dated 2811997.
4.
In nutshell, Hon'ble Amarjeet Chaudhary, J held legal heirs of the deceased entitled to monetary benefits from the date Gulab Singh was acquitted till his death.
It may be recalled that he was acquitted on 831984 by this Court.
As per order dated 2811997, the legal heirs of Gulab Singh became entitled to the monetary benefits with effect from 831984 till 19101990.
5.
Municipal Committee, Jaitu filed Letters Patent Appeal No. 181 of 1997 against order dated 2811997 of the learned Single Judge passed in C.W.P.
No. 110 of 1985 and prayed that order dated 2811997 of the learned Single Judge be set aside and the writ petition filed by late Gulab Singh be dismissed with costs.
6. Letters Patent Bench comprising RS Mongia and ML Singhal, JJ vide order dated 11111997, dismissed the Letters Patent Appeal so that there was no hardship to the legal heirs of Gulab Singh, Municipal Committee, Jaitu was directed to carry out the directions of the learned single Judge within 4 months, failing which the monetary benefits to which the legal heirs of Gulab Singh were entitled, would carry interest at the rate of 12% per annum from the date money became due i.e. judgment of the learned Single Judge acquitting the writ petitioner till the date of payment.
7.
Through C.M.
No. 190 of 2000 in L.P.A. No. 181 of 1997, legal heirs of Gulab Singh had prayed that late Gulab Singh now represented by his legal heirs, was entitled to all the increments which had accrued to him since 991976 i.e. the date when he was suspended upto the date of his acquittal i.e. 831984 and this is what he was entitled to from 831984 till his death.
He was entitled to the monetary benefits in accordance with the said calculations.
It was prayed that with effect from the date of acquittal i.e. 831984 till his death i.e. 19101990, the intervening period commencing from 991976 should be taken into consideration for the purpose of fixing up his pay by adding annual increments which he would have earned on account of revision of pay scales made with effect from 111978 and 1.1.1986 as well.
It was also prayed that Municipal Committee, Jaitu be directed to pay arrears by calculating the salary of deceased Gulab Singh in the aforesaid manner along with interest at the rate of 12% per annum.
Through C.M.
No. 190 of 2000 in L.P.A. No. 181 of 1997, the legal heirs of Gulab Singh sought clarification of judgment of the learned single Judge dated 2811997 and that of the judgment passed by Letters Patent bench in the Letters Patent Appeal dated 11111997.
8.
C.M.
No. 190 of 2000 was declined by the Letters Patent Bench comprising RS Mongia was ML Singhal, JJ vide order dated 24112000.
It was observed that there was no reason that any modification/clarification be made in the order dated 11111997 of the Letters Patent Bench passed in L.P.A. No. 181 of 1997.
9.
Legal representatives of Gulab Singh had not lost heart.
Through C.M.
No. 3926 of 2001, they sought clarification/modification of the order dated 11111997 with renewed vigour.
They had prayed that the order dated 24112000 passed in C.M.
No. 190 of 2000 be recalled as in the disposal of C.M.
No. 190 of 2000 the Letters Patent Bench could not be assisted properly by their counsel.
10.
The order dated 24112000 passed in C.M.
No. 190 of 2000 was recalled vide order dated 992002 passed in CM Nos. 3925-3926 of 2001.
This is how C.M.
No. 190 of 2000 is now awaiting disposal.
11.
It has been prayed that Municipal Committee, Jaitu is required to be directed to fix the pay of deceased Gulab Singh payable on 831984 by taking into consideration the annual increments which he would have earned from the date of his suspension i.e. 991976 till his acquittal i.e. 831984 and accordingly the revised pay worked out like this is to be considered for the purpose of determining the arrears payable to them.
It has been prayed that calculations made in Annexure A-1 be taken into account by the Municipal Committee, Jaitu while fixing the pay as on 831984.
12.
Municipal Committee, Jaitu contested this prayer.
It was submitted by learned counsel for the Municipal Committee, Jaitu that prayer of legal representatives of late Gulab Singh is devoid of merit as the order of the learned single Judge disposing of C.W.P.
No. 110 of 1985 is quite clear, inasmuch as, the learned single Judge quashed the order of dismissal dated 231981 and held the legal heirs of Gulab Singh entitled to monetary benbefits from the date Gulab Singh was acquitted i.e. 831984 till his death.
It was further submitted that if the order of the learned Single Judge was not cler in any respect, legal heirs of Gulab Singh should have filed Letters Patent Appeal.
It was Municipal Committee, Jaitu which filed Letters Patent Appeal and the Letters Patent Appeal filed by Municipal Committee, Jaitu was dismissed by Letters Patent Bench vide order dated 11111997.
It was further submitted that Letters Patent Bench has clearly stated that the legal heirs of the deceased would be entitled to monetary benefits from the date Gulab Singh was acquitted by the High Court till his death in accordance with law.
It was submitted that now the legal heirs of the deceased Gulab Singh want which could be given to them by Letters Patent Bench if they had filed Letters Patent Appeal against the order of the learned single Judge (which they did not).
13.
In my opinion, there is a fallacy in the submissions made by learned counsel for the Municipal Committee, Jaitu.
When Gulab Singh was acquitted by the High Court vide its order dated 831984, he became, at once, entitled to reinstatement into service as if he was never dismissed from service.
It is quite settled that acquittal blots out the existence of guilt altogether.
Acquittal will have the effect of placing him in the same position in which he was, before registration of the case against him.
It is as if no case was ever registered against him and he was never put up on trial and he will be always deemed to be in service of Municipal Committee, Jaitu.
He is, therefore, entitled to all arrears of salary together with usual increments and usual allowances with effect from 991976 till 19101990 as if he was all along in the service of Municipal Committee, Jaitu and never placed under suspension/dismissed from service.
While calculating the salary disbursable to the legal heirs of Gulab Singh, whatever payments have been made to him those will be adjusted and the rest of the amount shall be paid to the legal heirs of the deceased Gulab Singh.
C.M.
No. 190 of 2000 is accordingly allowed.
Judgment of the learned single Judge dated 2811997 and that of the Letters Patent Bench dated 11111997 shall be deemed to have been modified/clarified accordingly.
Calculations are to be made by taking into account Annexure A-1.
Petition allowed.
