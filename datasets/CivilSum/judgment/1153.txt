RL Anand, J - Unsuccessful Smt. Prem Lata has filed the present regular second appeal and it has been directed against the judgment and decree dated 2471992 passed by the Additional District Judge, Patiala who dismissed the appeal of the plaintiff-appellant as well as the cross-appeal of the State of Punjab and affirmed the judgment and decree dated 1851990 passed by Shri JS Koery, Senior Sub Judge, Patiala.
2.
Brief facts of the case are that Smt. Prem Lata filed a suit for declaration that she is entitled to be regularised in the classical and vernacular teacher cadre as Sewing Teacheress with effect from 111973 with all rights, benefits and privileges granted to other Classical and Vernacular Teacheresses similarly situated according to the Government policy with benefits of handicapped employees having been given Rs. 50/- per month with effect from 15.6.1982 and then Rs. 75/- per month with effect from 111985.
The plaintiff also prayed that her pay be fixed by counting the service with effect from 1081970 and her pay be fixed by adding five increments instead of three increments in the scale of Rs. 570-1080 instead of Rs. 480-880 with effect from 111978.
Plaintiff further prayed for the grant of selection grade of Rs. 620-1200 with effect from 211978 with all the rights, benefits and privileges with five increments and the plaintiff challenged that withholding of the above benefits on the part of the State of Punjab and District Education Officer, Patiala was unconstitutional, mala fide, discriminatory and against the principles of natural justice.
The plaintiff also prayed that she be released the above benefits alongwith interest and defendants be called upon to release the benefits as claimed by her.
3.
It was pleaded by the plaintiff that she is matriculate and is having a three years Diploma in Sewing from the recognised Institute.
She was appointed as Social Adult Education worker by the District Education Squad (Rampura Phul), Bathinda in the year 1970 and plaintiff worked as such from 1081970 to 781971.
She was reemployed from 391971 to 10.2.1972.
She was posted at Rampura Phul in the scale of Rs. 125-350.
She was appointed as Sewing Teacheress in Government Girls Middle School, Walanwali, District Bathinda by District Education Officer, Bathinda from 1221972 to 1461972 in the scale of Rs. 125-350 in the cadre of Classical and Vernacular Teacheress.
After that she was appointed in the Government High School, Dhadhogal Kheri, District Sangrur from 511973 to 16.6.1973 in the grade of Rs. 170-350 in the cadre of Classical and Vernacular Teacheress and Government High School Balad Kalan, District Sangrur from 10.8.1973 to 921974 and from 1121974 to 791976 in Government Girls High School, Moonak, District Sangrur from 791976 to 26101976 in the same capacity.
She was relieved on 2111976 with effect from 26101976, when Mohinder Kaur was transferred from Hoshiarpur to Moonak.
However, she remained continuously out of service from 26101976 to 841980.
Later on she approached the District Education Officer, Sangrur and DPI, Chandigarh for re-employment in Sangrur District and remained out of service from 26101976 to 841980.
The plaintiff was thus forced to remain out of service.
Subsequently, she was posted in Government Middle School, Lad Wanzara, District Sangrur, in the scale of Rs. 570-1080 as Sewing Teacheress against the post of Art and Craft Teacher which is in the grade of Rs. 570-1080 with three advance increments.
The Government vide notification dated 2861983 laid down the policy in respect of Classical and Vernacular Teacheresses in the scale of Rs. 570-1080 to Rs. 620-1200 in the ratio of 70 :30 with five advance increments.
The Government vide letter dated 931984 decided that the Classical and Vernacular Teacheress shall also include Sewing Teachers.
The defendant No. 2, in view of the letter dated 931984 instructed the Headmistress Government High School, Anardana Chowk, Patiala vide memo No. E.4(85-10784) dated 271985 to fix the pay of the plaintiff by adding three increments but the plaintff was entitled to get her pay fixed by five advance increments in the pay scale.
This action of the defendant was discriminatory and was challenged in the High Court.
The High Court directed that the matter of grant of five increments to Shashtries only and denying the same to other Classical and Vernacular Teacheresses who were in the same cadre was unconstitutional and illegal.
The plaintiff being handicapped was also entitled to Rs. 75/- per month as allowance.
4.
Notice of the suit was given to the State and it was contested on the various pleas as that the suit of the plaintiff was bad for non-joinder of necessary parties; that the benefit of advance increments could only be given to Punjabi, Hindi, Arts and Crafts and Sanskrit Teacheresses, that the Civil Court has no jurisdiction to entertain the suit; and that the suit was not properly valued for the purposes of Court fees and jurisdiction.
On merits it was the stand of the State that plaintiff was transferred from Sangrur to Patiala on her request and according to the rules of the policy, whenever a Classical and Vernacular Teacheress is transferred from one District to the other, such employee loses her seniority in that District and would be placed at the bottom of the seniority of the Classical and Vernacular Teacheresses in the District in which such employee was transferred.
So, the plaintiff had no claim for the selection grade of 70 :30 ratio.
The selection grade is only given on the basis of the seniority of the Patiala district.
5.
The plaintiff filed replication to the written statement filed by the State in which she reiterated her allegations made in the plaint by denying those of the written statement and from the above pleadings of the parties, the learned trial Court framed the following issues :-  1.
Whether the plaintiff is entitled to the declaration as prayed for ?
OPP 2.
Whether the suit is bad for non-joinder of necessary parties ?
OPD.
3.
Whether the suit is properly valued for the purposes of court fee and jurisdiction ?
OPP.
4.
Relief.
6.
The parties led oral and documentary evidence in support of their case and on the conclusion of the proceedings, issue No. 1 was decided partly in favour of the plaintiff and it was held that the plaintiff would be entitled to get the benefit of fixation of pay in accordance with the Rules and Regulations with effect from 941980 when she was temporarily appointed.
However, it was further concluded by the trial Court that the plaintiff was not entitled to get her service regularized towards the seniority and benefits of service prior to 941980.
Issues No. 2 and 3 were decided against the defendants and the suit of the plaintiff was partly decreed and declaration was granted that she was entitled to get her services counted towards increments.
The suit of the plaintiff that she was entitled for adding five increments and selection grade as well as for benefit of handicapped allowance at the rate of Rs. 75/- per month etc. was dismissed for want of evidence that she was handicapped.
7.
Aggrieved by the said judgment and decree dated 1851990 of the trial Court, plaintiff Smt. Prem Lata filed appeal praying that the benefit of previous service should also be given to her besides the benefit of the five increments as per policy of the Government on account of the fact that she was handicapped and the trial Court held in an illegal manner that she had not been able to prove her disability.
8.
State also filed an appeal praying that the suit of the plaintiff has been wrongly decreed by the trial Court and that she was not entitled to any relief.
9.
Vide impugned judgment and decree dated 2471992, both the appeals i.e. of the plaintiff and the State of Punjab were dismissed.
The State did not file any further appeal.
However the plaintiff-Smt. Prem Lata has filed the present regular second appeal against the judgment and decree of the First Appellate Court dated 2471992.
10.
I have heard the learned counsel for the parties and with their assistance have gone through the record of this case.
11.
This Court is of the view that both trial Court as well as the First Appellate Court not rightly appreciated the evidence led on the record and this Court is duty-bound to disturb the findings of the Courts below.
The first point for determination would be if Smt. Prem Lata was in a position to prove that she was handicapped and that she was entitled to the benefit of the instructions dated 18.4.1973 (Ex.P-10).
The trial Court as well as the First Appellate Court had not given the relief to the plaintiff primarily on the ground that plaintiff had not been able to prove about her disability/physically handicapped aspect.
The Courts took the view that since plaintiff did not produce any certificate under the signature of CMO/Civil Surgeon and, therefore, in the absence of such evidence it cannot be said that plaintiff was handicapped.
Both the Courts in my opinion had not examined this aspect in depth.
Firstly, there was a statement of the plaintiff to that effect which has not been challenged by any cogent evidence on behalf of the defendants.
Secondly, it is specifically alleged by the plaintiff in para No. 18 of the plaint that she is handicapped one and is entitled to be given all the rights, benefits and privileges given to the handicapped employees of the Government and she is entitled to be reguarlized from 111973 as per the policy of the Government of Punjab contained in various circular letters issued by the Chief Secretary on 18.4.1973, 291973, 1551973, 20.9.1973 and 1181974 and so on.
This part of the allegations of the plaint has not been denied specifically by the State.
The only averment in the written statement on behalf of the defendants was that petitioner was appointed on priority basis in the light of the Government instructions dated 18.4.1973 when she had applied to the department after obtaining the certificate from the Civil Surgeon.
In other words, the defendant nowhere alleged that the plaintiff was not handicapped.
Rather, it was an admission on the part of the defendants that plaintiff after obtaining the medical certificate from the Civil Surgeon regarding her physical handicapness applied for the job suggesting that plaintiff was handicapped.
The things do not rest here.
Even in the application under Order 39 Rules 1 and 2 CPC moved by the plaintiff, it was specifically alleged by the plaintiff that she was claiming allowance as being given to the handicap employees as per policy of the Government as mentioned in paras 18 and 19-A of the plaint.
When reply was filed to this application, there was no categorical denial.
Rather, it was stated by the respondents that plaintiff was appointed on priority basis in the light of the Government instructions dated 18.4.1973 when she applied to the department after obtaining the medical certificate from the Civil Surgeon.
In the light of the above pleadings, it can be safely concluded that the defendants themselves admitted that plaintiff was handicapped and she obtained the certificate of the Civil Surgeon at the time of applying for job.
It is settled law of the land that admitted facts need not be proved.
Order 8 Rule 5 C.P.C. lays down that there should be a categorical denial of a fact and if there is any evasive denial, it would tantamount to as if that particular fact alleged in the plaint has been admitted by the defendants.
In the present case, the denial of the State with regard to para No. 18 of the plaint was evasive and does not conform to the provisions of Order 8 Rules 4 and 5 of the C.P.C.
Therefore, it has to be inferred and concluded that the defendants admitted that the plaintiff was handicapped and she was entitled to the handicap allowance as per policy of the Government contained in circular dated 18.4.1973.
This aspect of the case has also not been rightly appreciated by the First Appellate Court in the impugned judgment and decree which has carried miscarriage of justice to the plaintiff and the same is liable to be set aside and I order accordingly.
Now it is to be seen whether the plaintiff falls within the ambit of Classical and Vernacular Teacheress cadre and can claim the scale as claimed by her.
In para No. 9 of the judgment, the trial Court has dealt with this issue in light of the judgment of the Supreme Court in Civil Appeal No. 3709 of 1983 Avtar Singh v. State of Punjab and held that some senior teachers do not fall in the various categories such as category A and B incorporating the various classes of the employees.
12.
Learned counsel for the appellant has argued that this aspect of the case has not been rightly appreciated by the Courts below and he has drawn my attention to the citation in Sukhwinder Kaur v. State of Punjab and others, 1989(3) SLR 379 and Amarjeet Kaur, Tailoring Mistress, Government High School v. The State of Punjab and others, 1988(4) SLR 199.
I am of the view that the citation relied upon by learned counsel for the appellant can be helpful for the plaintiff.
In the cited cases, the Sewing Teachers were given the benefit of the same pay scale as was given to Classical and Vernacular Teacheresses.
13.
Here the dispute is also with regard to the pay scale as it was before this Court.
In this view of the matter I am of the view that both the Courts below were in error when they held that the plaintiff was not entitled to get the benefit of the pay scale as was being enjoyed by the Classical and Vernacular Teacheresses.
The findings of the Courts below are reversed on this point.
14.
The third aspect of the case would be from which date the plaintiff would be entitled to the grant of the benefit of the revised scale besides the increments.
In my opinion, she would be entitled to get the benefit of the scale with effect from 941980 and not earlier to that.
15.
In the light of the above discussion, I modify the finding of the Courts below on issue No. 1 and declare that plaintiff would be entitled to get grade of Rs. 570-1080 by adding five increments with effect from 941980.
Further, she would also be entitled to the handicap allowance as being enjoyed and paid to the handicap employees as per Government instructions issued from time to time.
The findings of the Courts below contrary to my findings above would be deemed to have been reversed by this judgment.
The appeal is partly allowed in terms stated above.
No order as to costs.
