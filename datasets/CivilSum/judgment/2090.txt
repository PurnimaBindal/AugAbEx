VK Jhanji, J - The petitioner who possesses Master's degree in Public Administration, joined Indian Air Force in July, 1965 during the National Emergency.
The terms of engagement required the petitioner to serve the Armed Forces for 9 years as regular and 6 years as Reserved.
This composite service after 15 years was required for attaining the age of superannuation.
However, after rendering service of little more than 9 years, the petitioner was released on 3181974.
Thus his release was before superannuation.
2.
In response to an advertisement issued by the Punjab Public Service Commission for recruitment to PCS (Executive Branch) and other allied services, the petitioner applied against the post reserved for persons released from the Armed Forces and appeared in the written test conducted by the Commission in the year 1976.
The case of the petitioner is that his result of the examination held in 1976 was not declared despite many representations made by him.
The petitioner again applied for the post of PCS (Executive Branch) and allied services in response to an advertisement in the year 1979 and appeared in the written examination held in November, 1979.
There were, in all, 69 posts out of which 13 posts were meant for the Released Armed Forces Personnel under the Demobilised Indian Armed Forces Personnel (Reservation of vacancies in the Punjab Civil Services (Executive Branch) Rules, 1972.
However, as far as allied Services are concerned, reservation for the posts is done under the rules known as Demobilised Armed Forces Personnel (Reservation of Vacancies in the Punjab State Non-Technical Services) Rules, 1968 as amended by 1st Amendment Rules, 1977.
The petitioner though secured 169 marks out of 325 in the written examination, was not called for interview.
The petitioner challenged the action of the Punjab Public Service Commission in not calling him for interview by filing a writ petition in this Hon'ble Court which was decided alongwith other writ petitions.
A Division Bench of this Court vide judgment dated April 7, 1982 (CWP No. 4939 of 1981) dismissed the writ petition after holding that an ex-serviceman discharged on fulfilling the conditions of his enrolment under rule 15(2) (b) of the Air Force Rules, 1969, does not come within the ambit of Indian Armed Forces Personnel on demobilisation under rule 2(d) of the Rules and thus the Punjab Public Service Commission was justified in excluding the petitioner from consideration against the reserved vacancy.
Feeling aggrieved in Civil Writ Petition No. 5516 of 1981, the petitioner filed Special Leave Petition No. 5474 of 1982 in the Hon'ble Supreme Court of India which was dismissed on 3.9.1982.
Thereafter, the petitioner filed a Review Petition No. 177 of 1983 vide which Special Leave Petition No. 5474 of 1982 was restored by the Hon'ble Supreme Court vide order dated 3081988 which reads as under :-  "Heard learned counsel for the parties.
The question about the applicability of the Circular of 1971 in respect of eligibility of persons who were discharged from services was not raised before the High Court when the High Court disposed of these matters.
That question has been subsequently raised by a review petition and now before this Court in a writ petition.
The question raised is material and it is proper that it is determined by the High Court.
We, therefore, set aside the judgment of the High Court and remand the matter for fresh disposal including the new question or questions which may be raised by the petitioner.
The High Court shall dispose of these matters expeditiously.
The civil appeals are disposed of accordingly.
In the circumstances of the case, there will be no order as to costs."
3.
This is how the present writ petition has come up before us.
4.
The only contention of the learned counsel for the petitioner is that even if the petitioner is not covered by the definition of Released Armed Forces Personnel, his claim against one of the reserved seats could not be defeated and his candidature, in any case, merited consideration in the light of the binding instructions contained in Circular No. 3808-SII(3)- 71/20988 dated 12.8.1971 (Annexure P-1) These instructions were reiterated by the Government of Punjab vide Circular letter No. 10/23/80-5PP/621 dated 1911981 (Annexure P-7).
His precise submission is that even if the petitioner's case fell short of failing within the ambit of 'Released Armed Forces Personnal he was entitled to be considered against one of the three reserved seats which remained unfilled due to non-availability of a Released Armed Forces Personnel.
5.
On the other hand, learned counsel for the respondents has submitted that further selections in the year 1984-85, 1987-88 in the Punjab Civil Services (Executive Branch) and allied Services Examination, have already taken place and it would not be proper to reconsider petitioner's case and reopen the selection made in the year 1979 at this stage.
6.
After hearing the learned counsel for the parties, we are of the view that this writ petition deserves to succeed.
It is not the case of the respondents that instructions dated 12.8.1971 (Annexure P-1) and 1911981 (Annexure P-7) go against the statutory rules for the reservation of seats for Ex-servicemen.
Admittedly, out of 13 seats reserved for Released Armed Forces Personnel, 10 were filled up and the remaining 3 seats were kept vacant.
If the instructions contained in Annexures P-1 and P-7 are given effect to, the net result would be that some more Ex-Servicemen would be appointed against reserved seats although they do not strictly come within the definition of 'Released Armed Forces Personnel'.
The instructions come into play only if some vacancies are left to be filled in for want of suitable persons.
Thus, we are of the considered view that the action of the Punjab Public Service Commission in not calling the petitioner for interview after he successfully passed the written examination for the post of Punjab Civil Services (Executive Branch) is clearly arbitrary and in violation of the instructions dated 12.8.1971 and 1911981, Annexures P-1 and P-7 respectively.
7.
For the reasons recorded above, this writ petition is allowed and a direction is issued to the respondents to call the petitioner for interview and consider him against one of the reserved vacancies, on the basis of his performance in the written test which he successfully cleared, whenever next examination takes place, along with others.
No costs.
Petition allowed.
