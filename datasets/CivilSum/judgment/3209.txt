Amarjeet Chaudhary, J - This order will dispose of Civil Writ Petition Nos. 14688 of 1993, 6024 and 16909 of 1994 and 5352 of 1995 as common questions of law and fact are involved in these petitions.
2.
The petitioners have prayed for issuance of a direction to the respondents to grant them Master's grade from the dates of their acquiring BEd/BT qualifications.
The relief has been sought on the basis of decision of Supreme Court in Chaman Lal and others v. State of Haryana, A.I.R. 1987 SC 1621 as also of High Court decisions in Harbans Lal v. State of Haryana, 1994(3) SCT 633(P&H) : 1994(2) R.S.J.
429, Arjun Dev v. State of Haryana , 1992(2) SCT 511(P&H) : 1992(2) R.S.J.
498 and Rattan Singh v. State of Haryana, 1995(1) SCT 711(P&H) : 1995(1) R.S.J.
533 and several other unreported judgments of this Court.
The petitioners have heavily relied upon the letter dated 23.7.1957 issued by the composite Punjab Government.
3.
In the reply, acquiring of higher qualifications by the petitioners after the issuance of instructions dated 931990 has not been disputed.
The respondent-State has taken a categoric stand that according to the communication issued by the Haryana Government Finance Department vide letter No. 7/2(i)/90 4F&I dated 931990, copy Annexure P-1 to the writ petition, it has been clarified that the pay scales admissible to the Masters i.e. BA BEd.
will be given to such teachers who have been appointed against the post for which the qualification is BA, BEd., whereas the petitioners were initially appointed as J.B.T.
teachers and they acquired BA, BEd., qualifications while working as teachers after joining Government service.
Thus, the petitioners are not entitled to the grade of the post of Masters.
4.
Learned State counsel has also argued that this Court in Civil Writ Petition No. 14736 of 1991, had not allowed this benefit to those teachers who acquired the qualification of BA, BEd.
after 931990.
5.
We have considered the submissions of learned counsel for the parties.
We hardly find any merit in the stand taken by the respondents.
6.
After perusing the letter dated 931990, we have reached the conclusion that the State Government had issued the said circular only to deny the relief to the teachers to get higher pay scale from the date they acquired higher qualification of BA, BEd.
Learned counsel for the petitioners contends that the judgment relied upon by the learned State counsel would not be applicable to the facts of the present case as the instructions aforesaid were not the subject matter of that case.
7.
This Court in Rattan Singh's case (supra) while considering the circular dated 931990 has observed as under :-  " A careful perusal of that circular shows that the issue of the same is nothing but an attempt to frustrate the rights of the existing teachers to get higher pay from the date they acquired higher qualifications.
Distinction sought to be made out between the persons appointed as Masters with higher qualifications and those acquiring higher qualifications while in service is illusory and unwarranted."
In an identical matter, the Supreme Court in PK Ramchandra Iyer and others v. Union of India and others, A.I.R. 1984 SC 541, on the principle of 'equal pay for equal work' granted the higher pay scales to the petitioners who were holding the posts of professors.
8.
In Chaman Lal's case (supra) the Apex Court had directed the respondents to give higher grade admissible to Masters to all the teachers who have acquired the B.T./BEd.
qualifications with effect from their respective dates of their acquiring the qualifications.
9.
It is unfortunate that despite the Supreme Court pronouncement in Chaman Lal's case (supra) and other pronouncements of this Court, the State Government has not granted the grade of Masters to the teachers, who have improved their qualifications while in service.
It would have been appropriate for the Government to have granted higher grades to the teachers suo moto, denial of which is violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India.
We cannot restrain ourselves by observing that if the judgment by the Apex Court or the High Court is delivered and the same achieves finality, the benefit of the said judgment is to be granted to all similarly situated persons.
10.
For the reasons stated above, the writ petitions are allowed and the respondents are directed to release to the petitioners the pay scales of Masters with effect from the date they acquired the requisite higher qualification.
The arrears of salary be paid to the petitioners within six months.
There will be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
