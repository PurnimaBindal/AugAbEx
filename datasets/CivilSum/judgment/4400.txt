Hence, it appears that before 7th November, 1996 already the corrected list wherein the name of the respondent No. 6 was incorporated was before the Managing Committee of the School.
Despite such, the Managing Committee did not issue the interview letter to the respondent No. 6.
As a resultant effect, the respondent No. 6 moved a writ application being CO No. 18351 (W) of 1996 two days before the interview held on 28th November, 1996 and SR Mishra, J (as His Lordship then was) by the order dated 5th December, 1996 directed the District Inspector of Schools concerned to hear the grievance of the respondent No. 6 herein.
The order of SR Mishra, J (as His Lordship then was) reads to this effect :  5.12.96 :- Having heard the learned Counsel for the petitioner and the State Counsel, the writ petition is disposed of with the direction to the DI of schools to consider the grievance of the petitioner.

10.
Having regard to those very nature of correspondences of the Employment Exchange it appears that the Employment Exchange by the letter dated 22nd July, 1996 has corrected the first list of incorporating the name of respondent No. 6 and for keeping the total number 20 deleted the last name of the first list, as respondent No. 6's name though legally was required to be sponsored but due to mistake of the Employment Exchange, the same could not be inserted and accordingly a list dated 21st July, 1996 was referred to showing the respondent No. 6 in the 10th position on deleting the name of one Probodh Kumar Singh.
This list ultimately became the list sponsored by the Employment Exchange.
11.
From the Recruitment Rules, it appears that Clause 6(k) is based on the language that no second list should be obtained from the Employment Exchange and further it provides that no further names/lists even in continuation of the first list should be obtained.
It appears from the Employment Exchange's list as already quoted above that there was no second list sent but simply the first list as prepared, the same was corrected and the respondent No. 6's name was properly included.
Accordingly there was only one list sent from the Employment Exchange on 22nd July, 1996 in view of the correction as made in the list dated 18th July, 1996.
It is a settled law that if there is any mistake in any decision that mistake could be corrected.
12.
No argument could be advanced that the first list has wrongly prepared out of mistake could not be corrected applying the principles of irrevocability.
The said principle has no application in this case.
As it appears from the records that Employment Exchange Authority upon detecting their mistake immediately had sent the corrected list.
If any mistake lead to action being taken upon a wholly wrong basis, so that some different action needs to be substituted, if such action does not prejudice the rights of any party in any adjudicating process, that mistake is termed as simply official mistake, which is every time revocable.
In the corrected list, name of the writ petitioner was already there, beside the writ petitioner, name of other 19 persons out of 20 persons were identical and only change was made by incorporating the name of respondent No. 6 and as a consequence thereof deletion of the last candidate's name on the basis of seniority.
Such action of sending a corrected list was never debarred by the Statute.
A paragraph from the book "Administrative Law" by Sir William Wade and Christopher Forsyth of its 7th Edition appearing at page 262 is profitable, which reads thus:  "A mistake may lead to action being taken upon a wholly wrong basis so that some different action needs to be substituted.
This happened where a local education authority agreed to pay the cost of school transport for a girl, supposing that she lived more than three miles from the school and that they therefore had a statutory duty to pay.
When it was found that the distance was less than three males, so that they had a power to pay but no duty, they refused to do so.
The Court of Appeal rejected the plea it was not taken in the exercise of any power to determine a question of legal right and could not affect the duty to exercise discretion when the true facts appeared."
Said passage was quoted on the reflection of the judgment passed in the case Rootkin v. Kent CC (1981) 1 WLR 1186, wherein on a wrong factual parameter about distance of the residence of the students from the school in question gave rise to issue of financial support, which subsequently on detecting such mistake was rectified and corrected.
The Court held that decision cannot be said as irrevocable.
Said principle is identically applicable in the present one and accordingly learned Advocate's submission that the Employment Exchange has no jurisdiction to correct the list by rectifying the mistake is not legally accepted.
13.
The Clause 6(k) of the said Recruitment process never speaks that no correction could be made but it simply prohibits that there will be no list one after another.
As for example, suppose the Employment Exchange refers a list of ten candidates, thereafter refers names of eight new candidates, and subsequently another list containing names of another few more then only the aforesaid Clause 6(k) would be applicable.
But in the instant case it appears that the original list dated 18th July, 1996 was kept intact save and except the deletion of name under Serial 20 and addition of respondent No. 6 in proper place on the basis of his seniority in the Employment Exchange register.
Hence, the list dated 22nd July, 1996 has finally signed on 22nd July, 1996 incorporating the name of respondent No. 6 and deleting the name of Probodh Kr.
Singh cannot be said as a second list in terms of Clause 6(k).
On the contrary the list dated 26th July, 1996 was the only list correcting the the list dated 18th July, 1996 before the School Committee.
14.
Under such state of affairs, the respondent No. 6 got a right to be interviewed along with other candidates, but such right was illegally denied by the School (authority).
It appears that the interview letter was sent long after the list dated 26th July, 1996 was received by the school authority.
From the writ application it appears that on 7th November, 1996 the interview letter was signed by the Secretary of the Managing Committee whereas the final list of the Employment Exchange dated 26th July, 1996 was before them long before November, 1996.
In view of such illegality, the respondent No. 6 took shelter of the writ Court praying necessary order of his appearance in the interview which was fixed on 28th November, 1996 by moving the writ on 26th November, 1996.
But unfortunately got no interim order save and except the direction for respective affidavits.
15.
Hence, the issue was sub judice before High Court.
Ultimately this matter was heard on 5th December, 1996 by SR Mishra, J (as His Lordship then was) and a direction was given to the District Inspector of Schools concerned to decide the grievance.
This time the District Inspector of Schools concerned ought to have considered the entire issue but he did not do the same.
On the contrary he approved a panel on 9th January, 1997 which was prepared by the School authority on the basis of non-existing list dated 18th July, 1996 of Employment Exchange, which became nonest due to the final corrected list dated 26th July, 1996.
As already referred to that there was an order existing passed by SR Mishra, J directing District Inspector of Schools concerned to decide the grievance of respondent No. 6, with a ride that "any decision of approval or appointment against the post in question will be subject to the decision to be taken by the District Inspector of School", hence, decision of approval of panel as passed by District Inspector of Schools was attracted by principle of lis-pendence and by the contents of the order.
More particularly, the approved decision remained as subject to final decision by District Inspector of Schools on due compliance of order passed by SR Mishra, J as passed long before according such approval of Panel.
16.
In that view of the matter, finding of the District Inspector of Schools concerned as made in the writ application being the decision dated 11th February, 1998 cannot be said as illegal and or perverse.
Since the respondent No. 6 got no chance to appear in the interview despite his name was sponsored and inspite of the order of this Court passed by SR Mishra, J (as His Lordship then was) on 5th December, 1996 entertaining his writ application the order impugned was justified for directing fresh interview to the candidates in terms of the sponsored list dated 26th July, 1996.
The writ petitioner got no right to remain in the post as his selection was de hors of the Rule namely, recruitment process in view of the fact that the School authority did not call the candidates mentioned in the final list of the Employment Exchange dated 26th July, 1996.
17.
The respondents are directed to call the interview of the candidates in terms of the Employment Exchange list dated 26th July, 1996 and to complete the entire process of selection within three months from this date.
Since the petitioner's service was not at all approved, and his appointment in the post in question also was carrying the mandate of the order dated 5th December, 1996 passed by SR Mishra, J (as His Lordship then was) in CO No. 18351 (W) of 1996 whereby the Court directed that appointment against the post would be subject to the decision of District Inspector of Schools concerned, the petitioner is not legally entitled to get any salary, allowances and benefits as prayed for as there was a basic illegality in preparing a panel and according approval of such panel, which has been now finally decided by District Inspector of Schools following order of SR Mishra, J  18.
Having regard to such state of affairs, the writ application fails and it stands dismissed.
There will be no order as to costs.
Interim orders, if any, stand vacated.
Stay as prayed for is refused.
Order accordingly.
