Jawahar Lal Gupta, J - The petitioners in these six cases are members of the Educational Service in the State of Haryana.
They complain that the letter dated April 25, 1980 issued by the State of Haryana by which it was decided that 50 per cent posts of Headmasters/Headmistresses created with effect from October 12, 1979, or thereafter were to be reserved for the Masters/Mistresses of the Provincialised Cadre is illegal and unconstitutional as it violates the rights of the members of the service guaranteed under Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
The petitioners also allege that the action of the State Government in denying the benefit of the higher pay scale as sanctioned for the Masters/Mistresses belonging to the Provincialised Cadre to the members of the State Cadre is violative of the rule of equal pay for equal work.
The petitioners consequently pray for the issue of a writ in the nature of mandamus to grant the "Headmasters Grade -- from the date of completion of 22 years of service ...".
Counsel for the parties have referred to the facts as averred in Civil Writ Petition No. 6319 of 1993.
These may be briefly noticed.
2.
The petitioners are working as Masters.
They belong to the State Cadre.
They joined service on different dates during the years 1962 to 1970.
The petitioners were appointed to their respective posts after regular selections.
The posts of Masters were initially in the pay scale of Rs. 110- 250 with selection grade of Rs. 250-300 to the extent of 15 per cent of the cadre strength.
The pay scale was periodically revised.
In the erstwhile State of Punjab, there were various schools which were being run by the Municipal and the District Boards.
By an executive order dated September 27, 1957, the schools run by the Municipal Boards and the District Boards in Ambala and Jalandhar Divisions were taken over by the Education Department with effect from October 1, 1957.
The services of the teachers who were working in these schools were also taken over by the State Government.
As a result, they became employees of the State Government.
The employees of the schools which were taken over by the State Government were called `provincialised' teachers.
With effect from October 1, 1957, they were given the same grades of pay and other allowances as were admissible to their counterparts who had initially joined service in Government schools.
This parity was maintained at the time of revision of scale of pay up to April 1, 1979.
3.
On April 25, 1980, the State of Haryana granted the pay scale of Rs. 700- 1,250 to the persons who were working as Masters/Mistresses in the Provincialised Cadre with effect from October 12, 1979.
The persons like the petitioners who were members of the State Cadre were not given this scale of pay.
The petitioners further allege that as a result of implementing the report of the Fourth Pay Commission, the Masters and Mistresses belonging to the Provincialised Cadre were given the scale of Rs. 1,600-2,660 with effect from January 1, 1986.
With effect from May 1, 1990, this scale was further revised to Rs. 2,000-3,500.
As against this, the Masters and Mistresses in the State Cadre to which the petitioners belong were given the scale of Rs. 1,400-2,600 with effect from January 1, 1986.
The petitioners allege that the Masters and Mistresses working in the two cadres (the State Cadre and the Provincialised Cadre) possess the same qualifications and discharge identical duties.
The action of the State Government in granting different pay scales to the members of the two cadres is violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution as well as the principle of equal pay for equal work.
In Civil Writ Petition No. 3953 of 1980, it has been also alleged that the members of the State Cadre and the Provincialised Cadre were eligible for promotion to the posts of Headmasters in the ratio of 13:1.
By the order dated April 25, 1980, the State Government has decided to change the ratio to 50:50.
This action, the petitioners' counsel allege, is violative of Article 16.
It is further alleged that it amounts to change in conditions of service.
The State of Haryana could not have brought about this change without the prior approval of the Central Government under Section 82(6) of the Punjab Reorganisation Act, 1966.
The petitioners consequently pray that a writ in the nature of mandamus be issued directing the respondents to grant the pay scale of Rs. 700-1,250 to the Masters/Mistresses in the State Cadre in the same manner as it has been granted to the members of the Provincialised Cadre.
They further pray that the notification dated April 25, 1980, be quashed to the extent it provides for the grant of promotion to the cadre of Headmasters on 50:50 basis.
4.
The respondents contest the petitioners' claim.
In the written statement filed on behalf of the respondents, it has been stated by way of a preliminary objection that the writ petition has been filed after an inordinate long delay inasmuch as the order passed on April 25, 1980, has been challenged in the year 1993.
It has been further stated that the Provincialised Cadre "was a diminishing/dying cadre whereas the State Cadre to which the petitioners belong became an expanding cadre.
The ratio of promotion to the posts of Headmasters/Headmistresses was very heavily loaded in favour of the State Cadre Masters/Mistresses than those of the Provincialised Cadre ------- if a post of provincialised master/mistress is vacated due to retirement or for any other reason, this very post goes to the State Cadre ---.
In view of the hardship in the case of promotion etc. the masters/mistresses of the provincialised cadre pressed their claim for relief in terms of higher quota in promotion and removal of stagnation in terms of pecuniary benefits."
The case was examined.
It was found that the persons recruited in the State Cadre get their promotion to the posts of Headmasters/Headmistress in early 1970 whereas those "from the Provincialised Cadre who were in service as on 1101957 or earlier not only continued to remain masters/mistresses but were also stagnating at the maximum of their pay scales".
In view of this hardship, it was decided to increase the promotion quota to 50 per cent with effect from October 12, 1979.
It was also simultaneously decided to grant a special grade of Rs. 300-600 (revised Rs. 700-1,250) "as a measure personal to such masters/mistresses of provincialised cadre who had reached the maximum of the selection grade of Rs. 400-500 in view of the fact that it was a diminishing cadre".
It has been further stated that the masters/mistresses of the provincialised cadre form a separate class and the State Government was competent to grant the benefit to them as a personal measure.
It has been further pointed out that the masters/mistresses in the State Cadre were placed in the scale of Rs. 1,400-2,600 which was equal to that of the trained graduate teachers in the Central Government.
However, only such masters/mistresses of the provincialised cadre were given the pay scale of Rs. 1,600-2,660 who were earlier placed in the scale of Rs. 700-1,250 with effect from October 12, 1979.
The masters/mistresses who had not reached the maximum of the selection grade of Rs. 400-500 were not entitled to the grade of Rs. 700-1,250 irrespective of the length of service.
Only such teachers as had reached the maximum of the selection grade were entitled to the special grade which was revised to Rs. 1,600-2,660 with effect from January 1, 1986.
The averment that this scale had been further revised to Rs. 2,000-3,500 with effect from May 1, 1990, has been specifically denied.
The scale of Rs. 2,000-3,500 has only been given to the Headmasters/Headmistresses in the provincialised cadre.
This position has been reiterated in the reply on merits.
5.
Counsel for the parties have been heard.
On behalf of the petitioners, the arguments were addressed by Mr. PS Patwalia, Mr. RK Chopra, Mr. SP Laler and Mr. Ravi Verma.
The learned counsel contended that the teachers working in the two cadres were performing identical duties and were thus entitled to parity of treatment in the matter of pay scales.
The action of the State Government in not granting the scale of pay sanctioned for the posts of masters/mistresses in the provincialised cadre to the persons belonging to the State cadre is discriminatory and violative of the principle of equal pay for equal work as enshrined in Article 39(d) of the Constitution.
It was further contended that the action in changing the quota for promotion from 13:1 to 50:50 was violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
According to the counsel, it was even without jurisdiction as the conditions of service governing the teachers have been changed without the prior approval of the Central Government.
The claim on behalf of the petitioners was controverted by Mr. Jaswant Singh, Deputy Advocate General, Haryana, who appeared on behalf of the respondents.
The questions that arise for consideration are :-  (1) Is the action of the respondents violative of Articles 14, 16 and 39(d) of the Constitution ?
(2) Is the action violative of the provisions of Section 82(6) of the Punjab Reorganisation Act, 1966 ?
Regarding (1)  6.
Admittedly, the members of the State cadre including the petitioners are governed by the conditions of service laid down in the rules called the Punjab Educational Service Class III School Cadre Rules, 1955.
These rules were promulgated on May 13, 1957.
The rules lay down the qualifications for appointment, the recruiting authority, the conditions of service and the method of determination of inter se seniority of members of the service.
The scales of pay admissible to teachers falling in different categories are laid down in the appendix.
7.
As against this, the members of the Provincialised Cadre were initially recruited in schools which were being run by the Municipal and District Boards.
By an executive order dated September 27, 1957, the schools as well as the staff were taken over by the State Government with effect from October 1, 1957.
The teachers working in these schools were described as belonging to the Provincialised Cadre.
Their conditions of service are governed by the rules called the Punjab Educational Service (Provincialised Cadre) Class III Rules, 1961.
These rules were made effective from October 1, 1957.
It is further clear that the Provincialised Cadre was a "diminishing class to become extinct in the course of time".
Whenever a post became available on account of promotion, death or retirement of a member of the Provincialised Cadre, it was added to the State Cadre.
Consequently, a member of the State Cadre "stood a better chance of promotion than the teachers belonging to the Provincialised Cadre".
8.
The validity of some of the provisions of 1961 Rules was challenged before the High Court.
It was held that "the 1961 Rules insofar as the same create two cadres of persons in the same service and insofar as the same create unequal opportunity for promotion in between the two cadres are void rules and in particular those rules are No. 2 insofar as it relates to the definition of the two cadres and No. 3 insofar it provides for the effect of two cadres in the matter of promotion in the same service".
The State filed an appeal.
Their Lordships of the Supreme Court reversed the decision of the High Court.
The decision is reported as The State of Punjab v. Joginder Singh, AIR 1963 SC 913.
Their Lordships were pleased to hold as under:-  "By the order dated September 27, 1957 which came into effect from October 1, 1957 teachers in the erstwhile Board Schools became employees of Government and were given the same scales and grades of pay as were applicable to their counterparts in the State cadre, but except this equality of grade and pay there was nothing more that was contemplated or provided for by that order.
There was no integration of the two cadres either expressly or by necessary implication.
It would follow that it was not the Punjab Educational Service (Provincialised Cadre) Class III Rules that created the two distinct cadres but that they existed independently of the rules.
The two services started as independent services.
The qualifications prescribed for entry into each were different; the method of recruitment and the machinery for the same were also different and the general qualifications possessed by and large by the members of each class being different, they started as two distinct classes.
If they were distinct services, there was no question of inter se seniority between members of the two services, nor of any comparison between the two in the matter of promotion for founding an argument based upon Article 14 or Article 16(1).
They started dis-similarly and they continued dis-similarly and any dis-similarity in their treatment would not be a denial of equal opportunity."
9.
Even the argument that the State cannot grant two different scales of pay to employees doing the same work based on Article 14 of the Constitution was rejected with the following observations :-  "It cannot be contended that the State cannot constitute two services consisting of employees doing the same work but with different scales of pay or subject to different conditions of service.
Constitution of such services is not violative of Article 14.
The proposition that Article 14 requires that equal work must receive equal pay or that if there is equality in pay and work there have to be equal conditions of service is untenable.
The Government which is carrying on the administration has necessarily to have a choice in the constitution of the services to man the administration and the limitations imposed by the Constitution are not such as to preclude the creation of such services.
Besides, there might for instance, be a temporary recruitment to meet an exigency or an emergency which is not expected to last for any appreciable period of time.
To deny to the government the power to recruit temporary staff drawing the same pay and doing the same work as other permanent incumbents within the cadre strength but governed by different rules and conditions of service, it might be including promotions, would be to impose restraints on the manner of administration which was not intended by the Constitution."
10.
A perusal of the above clearly shows that the petitioners and the members of the Provincialised Cadre belong to two different services.
Their conditions of service are governed by different rules.
They had "started dissimilarly ----- and any dissimilarity in their treatment would not be a denial of equal opportunity".
What is the position in the present case ?

With effect from October 1, 1974, the selection grade of Rs. 400-500 was admissible to the Masters in the State Cadre to the extent of 15 per cent of the posts.
However, the members of the Provincialised Cadre could get the selection grade only on completion of 18 years of service.
This position was marginally altered in the case of the members of the State Cadre with effect from April 1, 1979, inasmuch as the selection grade was granted to the extent of 20 per cent of the cadre posts.
However, the position which existed on October 1, 1974, was maintained in respect of the members of the Provincialised Cadre.
With effect from October 12, 1979, even though the position with regard to the members of the State Cadre continued to be the same, there was a marginal change in the case of persons belonging to the Provincialised Cadre.
It was provided that such members as had reached the maximum of Rs. 500/- in the selection grade of Rs. 400-500 would be placed in the scale of Rs. 700-1,250 as a measure personal to them.
The persons who were placed in the scale of Rs. 700-1,250 were placed in the scale of Rs. 1,600-2,660 with effect from January 1, 1986.
Those in the time scale were placed at par with the members of the State Cadre.
This position has been brought out by the respondents in the written statement filed in Civil Writ Petition No. 6103 of 1993 which has not been controverted by the petitioners.
11.
Since the teachers working in the two cadres belong to two different services and their conditions of service are governed by separate rules, it cannot be said that the mere grant of different scales of pay is violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
It is the admitted position that the members of the State Cadre had better chances of promotion than those belonging to the Provincialised Cadre.
If in view of this position the State Government has chosen to give a marginally higher scale of pay to the persons who were not likely to get promotion to the higher post, it cannot be said that the State has treated equals as unequals.
In fact, as observed by their Lordships of the Supreme Court, the two classes were never equal.
They had started dis-similarly and continued to be dis-similar.
Thus, the grant of different scales of pay is not violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
12.
On behalf of the petitioners, it was submitted that the higher scale of pay viz. Rs. 700-1,250 was sanctioned for the posts of Headmasters and had been granted to the Provincialised Cadre on completion of 22 years of service.
It was claimed that even the members of the State Cadre should have been treated similarly and granted the Headmasters' scale of pay on completion of 22 years of service.
This contention is wholly misconceived.
Factually, the members of the Provincialised Cadre had worked for some years before the schools were taken over by the State Government on October 1, 1957.
These teachers were given no benefit of their service which they had rendered up to September 30, 1957.
Still further, the mere fact that a marginally higher scale of pay was given to them in the year 1979 does not mean that the State Government had laid down a rule that a member of the service shall be placed in the Headmasters' scale on completion of 22 years of service.
In fact, it appears that the teachers in the State Cadre were granted selection grade of Rs. 400-500.
This scale was admissible to the teachers irrespective of the number of years for which they had served.
The members of the Provincialised Cadre became eligible for this scale on completion of 18 years of service.
In the year 1979, the members of the State Cadre were entitled to be placed in the selection grade of Rs. 700-1,150 to the extent of 20 per cent of the cadre posts.
However, in the case of members of the Provincialised Cadre a marginal increase of Rs. 100/- was allowed and it was provided that such teachers who had reached the stage of Rs. 500/- in the scale of Rs. 400-500 shall be placed in the scale of Rs. 700-1,250 as a measure personal to them.
It was for the purposes of compensating them for the denial in the chances of promotion.
The scale was not being given on completion of a particular period of service.
It deserves notice that the selection grade of Rs. 400-500 was sanctioned in both the cadres in the year 1967.
Consequently, it is not unlikely that a person would have remained stuck at Rs. 500/- without earning any increment for a sufficiently long time.
To undo this hardship, the State Government had provided that such persons shall be placed in the scale of Rs. 700-1,250 while those in the State Cadre were to get the selection grade of Rs. 700-1,150.
This marginal difference did not imply that the teachers in the State Cadre were being automatically granted promotion to the rank of Headmaster on completion of 22 years of service.
13.
The learned counsel for the petitioners have placed strong reliance on the decision of their Lordships of the Supreme Court in JBT Rajkiya Adhyapak Sangh and others v. State of Haryana and others, 1991(3) R.S.J.
111.
In this case, the controversy related to the grant of selection grade to the JBT Teachers in the State Cadre.
The issue was thus totally different from the one that arises in the present case.
Even otherwise, their Lordships of the Supreme Court have vast powers under Article 142 of the Constitution which are not available to this Court under Article 226.
In any event, it has not been held as a matter of law that the State Government is not competent to treat the members of the two cadres differently.
The ratio of the decision in Joginder Singh's case (supra) remains unaffected.
Consequently, the petitioners can derive no advantage from this decision.
14.
It was also contended by the learned counsel for the petitioners that the action of the State Government was violative of the principle of equal pay for equal work as enshrined in Article 39(d) of the Constitution.
Even this contention cannot be sustained.
The policy behind Article 39(d) is primatively to ensure that men and women are not treated differently in the matter of pay.
They should get equal pay for equal work.
It is not the policy that persons working in different services who are governed by separate rules cannot be treated differently in the matter of pay.
As already observed, the persons working in the Provincialised Cadre did not have adequate chances of promotion.
They were stagnating.
In order to reduce the hardship a marginally different scale of pay was granted to them.
This does not amount to treating equals unequally.
It does not violate the principle of equal pay for equal work.
A small compensation for a person who is stagnating at a particular level in the service for long years, does not amount to either discrimination or violation of the principle of equal pay for equal work vis-a-vis a person who is entitled to be considered for promotion to a higher post and has actually better avenues of promotion.
The contention is consequently rejected.
15.
In view of the above, it is held that the action of the respondent State of Haryana is not violative of the provisions of Articles 14, 16 and 39(d) of the Constitution.
Regarding (2)  Mr. PS Patwalia, learned counsel in Civil Writ Petition No. 3953 of 1980, complains that the order of the State Government that the posts of Headmasters shall be filled up on 50:50 basis from amongst the members of the State and Provincialised Cadres is violative of Articles 14 and 16.
He further submitted that the State Government had violated the conditions of service governing the petitioners by issuing letter dated April 25, 1980.
It could not have done so without the prior approval of the Central Government.
16.
A perusal of the letter dated April 25, 1980, shows that initially the posts of Headmasters/Headmistresses had to be filled up by promotion from amongst the members of the State and Provincialised Cadres in the ratio of 13:1.
This ratio had been fixed in the year 1957.
It was observed in the letter that "the Provincialised Cadre being a diminishing one the Masters and Mistresses in the Provincialised Cadre have to wait for their promotion for a pretty long period whereas their counterparts ----- are promoted as Headmasters/Headmistresses much earlier ------".
It was also observed that "the Masters/Mistresses of this cadre placed in the selection grade of Rs. 400-500 (provincialised) are getting maximum of the grade but there is no likelihood of their being promoted as Headmasters and Headmistresses in the near future".
In the background of this position and in order to remove the hardship to Masters/Mistresses of the Provincialised Cadre, the Government had decided that "50 per cent of posts of Headmasters/Headmistresses created with effect from 12101979 and to be created onwards be reserved for Masters/Mistresses of the Provincialised Cadre."
The other part of the order related to the grant of special grade of Rs. 700-1,250 with effect from July 12, 1979.
It was observed by their Lordships of the Supreme Court in Joginder Singh's case (supra) that the services of 27,709 teachers had been taken over at the time of Provincialisation of the schools.
As against that, according to the petitioners themselves (in Civil Writ Petition No. 3953 of 1980) the number of Masters "in the State Cadre is about 7,000 whereas the total strength of Masters under provincialised cadre is 463 only".
This was apparently the effect of the policy that a post when vacated by a member of the Provincialised Cadre had to be added to the State Cadre.
Similar policy was followed in respect of the posts of Headmasters.
Thus prior to the issue of letter dated April 25, 1980, whenever a Headmaster belonging to the Provincialised Cadre retired or died, the resultant post/vacancy was added to the State Cadre.
Still further, out of a total 14 posts of Headmasters only one was filled up by promotion from amongst the members of the Provincialised Cadre.
This apparently resulted in injustice.
In order to do away with this hardship, the State Government took the policy decision to grant a marginally higher scale and promotion on 50:50 basis against posts "created w.e.f.
121079 ----".
In other words, out of two vacancies only one shall go to the State Cadre and the other one to a member of the Provincialised Cadre.
This benefit is admissible only in respect of the posts which have been created with effect from October 12, 1979, or thereafter.
The decision is apparently fair and in the facts and circumstances of the case, it calls for no interference.
17.
Even the contention that this decision varies the conditions of service of the persons belonging to the State Cadre is totally misconceived.
Firstly, it must be remembered that the State Government which creates a post has also the right to determine the sources and the manner of its being filled up.
Secondly, the quota has been prescribed only in respect of the posts which are created with effect from October 12, 1979.
It does not affect the position as it existed prior to November 1, 1966, when the State of Punjab was reorganised.
Thirdly, chances of promotion are not a condition of service.
Consequently, the fixation of the quota does not require the prior approval of the Central Government.
Lastly, it may also be mentioned that in view of the objection of the apex Court in JK Vasavada and others v. Chandrakanta and others, 1975(2) S.L.R.
522, the general approval granted by the Central Government vide letter dated May 11, 1957, was `law in force' and would operate as a prior approval under Section 82(6) of the Punjab Reorganisation Act, 1966.
In view of the above, the contention raised by the learned counsel for the petitioners is rejected.
Accordingly, both the questions as posed above are answered against the petitioners.
In view of this conclusion, it is not necessary to consider the preliminary objection of delay raised in the written statement filed on behalf of the respondents.
Consequently, there is no merit in these petitions which are dismissed.
Since the petitioners are only teachers, there will be no order as to costs.
Petitions dismissed.
