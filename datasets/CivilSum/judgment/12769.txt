NK Agrawal, J - This is a petition by two persons, Smt. Chanchal Vohra and Smt. Pushpa Arora, under Articles 226 and 227 of the Constitution for quashing the order dated June 16, 1994 whereby the pay scale of Rs. 1400-2600 was ordered to be given only to those Hindi teachers who possessed the qualification of Prabhakar with OT (Oriental Training) and JBT  2.
The petitioners possessed the qualifications of MA in Hindi and B.Ed.
Petitioner No. 1 was appointed as a Hindi Teacher, Classical and Vernacular (C&V) in the pay scale of Rs. 480-760/- by order dated July 5, 1984.
Petitioner No. 2 was also appointed as Hindi Teacher in the aforesaid pay scale by order dated September 4, 1987.
Both of them had been appointed by the Managing Committee of Shri Viveka Nand High School (for short, "the School") after seeking permission from Director, Secondary Education, Haryana Government.
Qualifications for the post of Hindi Teacher at that time were matriculation and Prabhakar with OT Both the petitioners possessed qualifications which were much higher than the prescribed qualifications.
Rule 45 of the Haryana Aided Schools (Security of Service) Rules, 1974 permitted relaxation in qualification if eligible persons were not available for appointment as teachers.
Both the petitioners were appointed under that Rule on having been selected after advertisement by a Selection Committee of the School.
They were confirmed in the service after probation period.
3.
The pay scale of Rs. 480-760 payable to the C&V teachers was revised to Rs. 1200-2040 with effect from January 1, 1986.
Petitioners were granted the revised scale with effect from January 1, 1986.
The pay scale of Rs. 1200- 2040 was further modified to Rs. 1400-2600 with effect from May 1, 1990.
This scale was also granted to the petitioners but with effect from May 1, 1991.
4.
District Education Officer, Yamuna Nagar, informed the School, by letter dated June 16, 1994 (Annexure P-8) that the pay scale of Rs. 1400-2600/- is to be given to those Hindi Teachers only who possessed OT Prabhakar and JBT qualifications and not to those who did not possess those qualifications but possessed higher qualifications.
Consequently, pay scale of Rs. 1200-2040 was given to the petitioners after withdrawing the pay scale of Rs. 1400-2600.
5.
The petitioners' case is that the pre-revised pay scale had been granted to them at the time of their appointment and that scale, after having been revised twice and also granted to them, could not be withdrawn.
Further, no valid and plausible reason was given for withdrawing the modified pay scale.
No opportunity of hearing was given to them before withdrawing the pay scale.
When the petitioners were initially appointed the regular pay scale of Rs. 480-760, the scale, after having been revised, could not be denied after 10 years of appointment.
They had been found eligible for the pay scale meant for C&V grade teachers.
The respondents had no valid reason to deny the revised pay scale on the ground that the petitioners did not possess the minimum qualifications for the post, though they possessed higher qualifications.
The pay scale was revised twice.
It was given to the petitioners after both the revisions.
6.
Respondents No. 1 to 3 have in Para 1 of their reply (Preliminary Objections) admitted that the petitioners were appointed as Hindi Teachers by the School in relaxation of the prescribed qualifications under Rule 45.
The pay scale of Rs. 1400-2600 was, however, applicable to the cases of those teachers who possessed the qualifications of Prabhakar and OT The petitioners did not possess those qualifications.
It is also admitted by the respondents that the pay scale of Rs. 480-760 meant for C&V teachers was revised to Rs. 1200-2040 with effect from January 1, 1986.
The pay scale was further modified to Rs. 1400-2600 with effect from May 1, 1990.
The initial scale of Rs. 480-760 as well as the revised scales were granted to the petitioners.
However, the modified scale of Rs. 1400-2600 was later on withdrawn after noting that the scale was meant for those teachers only who possessed qualifications of Prabhakar, OT and JBT It is further explained that the qualification of Prabhakar is a specialised qualification in Hindi.
7.
Learned counsel for the petitioners has argued that once the pay scale meant for Hindi Teachers had been given to the petitioners at the time of their initial appointment after relaxing the prescribed minimum qualification, that pay scale even if revised in future could not be withdrawn.
Qualification of Prabhakar means a Graduate in Hindi (Honours).
The petitioners are, on the other hand, Post Graduates in Hindi.
They are, thus, better and highly qualified.
Further, the question regarding qualification had been considered and the requirement of qualification was relaxed at the time of their appointment.
8.
Reliance is placed by the learned counsel for the petitioners on a decision of this Court in Manvinder Kaur v. The State of Punjab and others, 1991(1) RSJ 716 : 1991(2) SCT 364 (P&H).
A similar question regarding qualification of BA, Prabhakar and B.Ed.
was considered.
The petitioner possessing the aforesaid qualification was not treated to be eligible for the post of Hindi Teacher for which post the required qualifications were Matric, Prabhakar and OT/STC.
It was held that the object behind prescribing minimum academic and professional qualifications is not that the candidate with higher or better qualification should be rendered ineligible on hyper- technical objection.
9.
A question regarding qualification was also examined in Dr. Braham Dutt v. The State of Haryana and another, 1991(3) RSJ 643 : 1991(3) SCT 127 (P&H).
It was noticed that the qualification which was considered equivalent for the purpose of initial appointment was treated differently by the Department while granting advance increments and while making promotions in future.
It was held that it was arbitrary and violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
10.
In Bimla Devi v. State of Haryana and others, 1994(6) SLR 158 : 1994(2) SCT 87 (P&H), it was found that the qualification of JBT had been considered equivalent to another qualification at the time of initial recruitment.
It was held that the equivalence would enure the entire service including promotion and not for initial recruitment only.
The ratio of this judgment helps the petitioners who were possessing the higher qualifications and were found eligible for appointment as Hindi Teachers.
They could not, therefore, be denied the pay scale on the ground that they did not possess the requisite qualification.
11.
In Smt. Amarjit Kaur v. State of Haryana and others, 1994(8) SLR 672 : 1994(3) SCT 521 (P&H), a similar question again came up for consideration before this Court.
The petitioner was MA and B.Ed.
The minimum qualification for appointment as Teacher was Matric and Proficiency in Language.
The respondents took the plea that the petitioner was not qualified for the post of Hindi Teacher.
The plea was rejected.
It was held that there was nothing on record to show that a person who qualifies the course of Prabhakar and Oriental Training learns something more which a person who passes MA and B.Ed.
does not possess.
This decision helps the petitioners herein on all fours.
12.
On a consideration of the matter, it is found that the respondents cannot deny higher scale to the petitioners inasmuch as the higher scale was granted to them twice after revision of the pay scales.
They were appointed after relaxing the rule regarding the minimum qualifications.
They, undisputedly, possessed higher and better qualification.
Both are Post Graduates in Hindi.
I am inclined to take the some view as in the case of Smt. Amarjit Kaur (supra)  13.
In the result, the writ petition is allowed.
Order, withdrawing the pay scale of Rs. 1400-2600 from the petitioners, is quashed.
The respondents are directed to restore the revised pay scale of Rs. 1400-2600 to the petitioners from the date from which it was withdrawn.
Arrears shall be paid within three months from the date of this order.
The petitioners shall also be entitled to costs which are assessed at Rs. 4,000/-.
Petition allowed.
