HL Dattu, J - Petitioner now retired from service, is before this court in a petition filed under Art. 226 of the Constitution for a direction to the respondents, in particular, to Accountant General in Karnataka, Bangalore, who is arrayed as 3rd respondent to properly and rightly fix the terminal benefits that he is entitled to after his retirement on completion of 58 years and also requests this court to quash the negative thinking of the respondent reflected in his orders dated 19101988, 15111988 and 21.6.1989, Annexures E.G. and H respectively, annexed to the writ petition docket.
2.
The facts in nutshell are, petitioner retired from service on 3171984 as Asst.
Professor Civil Engineering from 4th respondent institution, which is a Government aided College, on attaining the age of superannuation, i.e. after completing 55 years of age.
In view of the Circular No. ED 137 TGL 83 dated 10111983 issued by Education Department circulated through its Director of Technical Education, Bangalore, the management of 4th respondent institution reappointed the petitioner w.e.f.
1st August, 1984 in the same post and on the same salary and allowances that he was drawing at the time of retirement from service subject to the approval of Director of Collegiate Education, till the end of `Even Semester' of 1983-84.
In fact, the action of the management in re-employing the petitioner till 19.8.1984 had been approved by the Director of Technical Education in his order dated 24111984.
By virtue of these orders, petitioner was continued in service without any break even beyond 19.8.1984 till 3171987.
3.
By an order dated 24.8.1984, the State Government raised the age of retirement of Govt. servants from 55 years to 58 years with immediate effect.
In para 4 of its order, it was also stated that the extent of application of the decision enhancing the age of superannuation to the retired Govt. servants re-employed or re-employed on contract basis in posts under the Government without break in service after their retirement would be indicated separately.
4.
Pursuant to the indication given in Govt. Order dated 24.8.1984, the State Govt. issued yet another order dated 17th September, 1984 indicating the extent of application of the enhanced age of retirement of those who are re- employed etc.  5.
Petitioner after his retirement from service, after completing 58 years of age, went before the authorities requesting them to quantify the pension amount payable to him taking into account the last drawn salary as on 3171987.
This request and the claim of the petitioner is rejected by Accountant General even though it was recommended by Director of Technical Education, Bangalore only on the ground that the petitioner does not fulfil all the conditions prescribed in Govt. Order No. DPAR 18 SDE 84 dated 1791984.
Its learned counsel also reiterates the same reasoning at the time of hearing of the petition and further submits that at the most, petitioner is entitled for salary for the extended period of service viz., 3171984 till 3171987 and that service cannot be counted for quantifying the terminal benefits including pensionary benefits.
In my view, the said thinking of the respondent and the contention of its learned counsel is too hyper-technical which is not well sustained on the scheme of the Govt. Order dated 17th September, 1984.
6.
Let me now consider the scheme of the Govt. Order which governs the controversy between the parties.
The benefit of the enhanced age of superannuation was extended in the order dated 1791984 to the re-employed persons fulfilling the following three conditions.
They are :  "I.
They should have retired from service on attaining the age of 55 years on or before Ist August, 1984; II.
They should have been continued in service on re-employment or on re- employment on contract basis or on extension of service immediately on retirement without there being any break in service; and III.
They should have been in service on 2nd August, 1984."
7.
Keeping in view the above three important conditions, let me examine whether petitioner fulfils all the conditions envisaged therein.
The management of 4th respondent institution by its memo dated 31st July, 1984 informs the petitioner that he would retire on 31st July, 1984 after attaining the age of superannuation and in the same memo, he is also informed that he is reappointed with effect from Ist August, 1984 in the same post which he was holding on the date of retirement and he would be paid the same salary and other allowances which he was drawing on the date of retirement and his services are continued in the same department till 19.8.1984.
The above details clearly suggest that petitioner had retired from service on attaining the age of 55 years before Ist August, 1984.
Thus he fulfils the first condition.
8.
Petitioner was reappointed in the same post with the same pay scale immediately on retirement viz., 181984 without any break in service.
Thus, he answers the second condition prescribed in the Government Order.
9.
Lastly, on 2nd August, 1984, petitioner was in service as Asst.
Professor in 4th respondent aided institution and thus, he satisfies the third and the last stipulation in the Govt. Order.
Consequently, in my view, the 3rd respondent could not have rejected the reasonable claim of the retired person and the impugned orders and endorsement cannot be sustained.
10.
I may also mention here that the scheme envisaged by the State Govt. in its order dated 19th September, 1984 is to give benefit to the retired Govt. servants re-employed in any post under Government without any break in service after their retirement.
The object is to encourage the Govt. servants and in such cases, I am of the view that the liberal construction must be given to the scheme so that the beneficial scheme reaches all those Govt. servants for whose sake such a scheme is evolved by the State Govt. By giving narrow and superficial construction and interpretation to the scheme, the respondent should not have whittled down the benefit of the Govt. Order to the petitioner.
In my view, petitioner is not only entitled for the salary benefits for the period 3171984 till 3171987 and also entitled for counting that period for pensionary benefits.
11.
In the result, this petition succeeds and is allowed.
Rule made absolute.
The impugned order and endorsements dated 19.1.1988, 15111988 and 21.6.1989 (Annexures E, G and H) are quashed.
Further, a direction is issued to the 3rd respondent to calculate the pensionary benefits payable to the petitioner on the last drawn salary as on 3171987.
This exercise respondent shall complete as expeditiously as possible, at any rate, within three months from the date of receipt of a copy of this court's order.
No order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
