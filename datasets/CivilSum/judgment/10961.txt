RL Anand, J - Santosh Aggarwal, petitioner, has filed the present writ petition under Article 226/227 of the Constitution of India praying for the issuance of a writ in the nature of certiorari for quashing of the order.
Annexure P-2, with further directions to the respondents to treat the petitioner in service with all service benefits.
2.
The case set up by the petitioner is that she had been serving the department as Teacher with due diligency, honesty and with full devotion.
She was never communicated any adverse report except for the year 1982-83 when her class results were not found to be satisfactory and after the said period and to the best of her knowledge she had been earning good, very good and excellent reports.
Her results for the years 1990-91 were 8780% for 10th class, 100% for IX Class and also 100% for VIIth class.
On 591991, the District Level Teachers Day Function was held in which she was awarded with an Appreciation Letter.
Whenever, the Headmistress remained on leave or otherwise the post remained vacant, the petitioner used to officiate as Headmistress of the School, the said school was upgraded to the Senior Secondary School in the year 1990 and the petitioner, in the absence of the Principal, used to officiate as the Principal of the said School.
She had been training the students for declamation contests etc. She attained the age of 55 years on 4.9.1991 and inspite of the fact that she had earned good, very good and excellent reports, she was served with a notice on 5.8.1992 ordering her premature retirement from service after the expiry of three months from the date of the receipt of the notice.
As per the petitioner, this notice of premature retirement, annexure P-2, has been issued by respondent No. 2 under Rule 326 of the Civil Services Rules, Vol.
I, read with Rule 5.32.
According to the petitioner, she could only be retired prematurely in public interest but the competent authority did not consider the record of the petitioner.
There was no basis for the respondents to pass the impugned order, Annexure P-2, in public interest.
Earlier, the State Govt. had issued instructions dated 1681983 and had decided that after the age of 55 years, the extension in service to the officials would be given if they obtained 70% or more good reports in the last 10 years.
The respondents have not complied with these instructions before passing the impugned order, Annexure P-2, which according to the petitioner is illegal and invalid.
The average reports which were given to the petitioner could not be considered as adverse.
The petitioner has earned 70% good reports and, therefore, she is entitled to be retained in service till the age of her superannuation.
Even as per the latest position, only 5 years service record is required to be seen while considering the case of a Govt. employee for retention in service or otherwise.
During the last 5 years, the petitioner has not earned even a single adverse report.
Respondent No. 2 while passing the order, Annexure P-2, did not apply his mind to the facts in hand.
With the above broad allegations, the petitioner has given challenge to the order, Annexure P-2, vide which she was retired prematurely from service.
3.
Notice of the petition was given to the respondents, who filed the written statement and denied the allegations.
According to the respondents, the case of the petitioner for retention in service beyond the age of 55 years was considered by the competent authority as per the instructions dated 1681983 and 19111991.
According to these instructions, only such employees whose 70% service record for the last 10 years is in good or better category, are to be allowed to continue in service beyond the age of 55 years.
For retention in service beyond 55 years, the service record for the last 10 years from 1981-82 to 1990-91 was considered and the 6 A.C.Rs.
of last 10 years of the petitioner were found to be in good or better category.
On merits, the stand of the respondents is that no legal right of the petitioner has been infringed.
The ACRs of the teaching staff are to be evaluated on the basis of their results.
In case, the results of below the pass percentage of the Board/Departmental Examination, the grading of the report is down graded by one step and in case of plus results, the report is ungraded by one step.
The ACRs of the petitioner were assessed on the basis of the results shown by the petitioner in the annual examinations.
The ACR's for the years 1982-83 and 1985-86 were recorded as average by the Reporting Officer on the performance of the petitioner.
The ACR for the year 1986-87 was down graded from good to average on the basis of the results shown by the petitioner.
Moreover, the petitioner concealed her results for the year 1990-91, which is (-) 4735% for the 8th class.
As the service record of the petitioner was not found upto mark, as such, she was not found fit for retention in service beyond the age of 55 years.
The respondents denied that the petitioner had been serving the department with due diligency, honesty and devotion.
With the issuance of the appreciation letter to the petitioner, she does not become entitled to continue in service beyond the age of 55 years.
Being the senior-most teacher in the school, the petitioner had to officiate as Incharge of the School.
This has no relevancy with the case of the petitioner for retention in service beyond the age of 55 years.
The petitioner attained the age of 55 years on 4.9.1991 and was served 3 months notice cum order of retirement under the provisions of Rule 326 of the Punjab Civil Services Rules, Volume I Part I read with Rule 5.32, according to which the appointing authority retains an absolute right to retire any employee on or after the age of 55 years.
In short, the stand of the respondents was that since the ACRs of the petitioner did not meet the requirement of 70% good or better reports, therefore, her inefficiency was apparent and it had become necessary to retire her from service on attaining the age of 55 years in public interest.
4.
The petitioner has also placed on record certain documents; such as the impugned order, Annexure P-2, the Appreciation Letter issued to the petitioner, Annexure P-1, the orders of crossing of the efficient bar of the petitioner, Annexures P-3 and P-4.
Similarly, the respondents have placed on record the Govt. instructions issued from time to time, including the past record of 10 years of the petitioner starting from 1981-82 to 1990-91.
5.
I have heard Mr. SK Bansal, Advocate, on behalf of the petitioner and Mr. Ashok Chaudhary, AAG, Haryana, for the respondents and with their assistance have gone through the record of this case.
6.
Section 326 (d) of the Punjab Civil Services Rules, Vol.
I Part I, which deals with compulsory retirement, lays down as follows :-  "(d) The appointing authority shall, if it is of the opinion that it is in the public interest so to do, have the absolute right to retire any Government employee, other than Class IV Government employee by giving him notice of not less than three months in writing or three months pay and allowances in lieu of such notice:- (i) If he is in class I or class II Service or post and had entered Government service, before attaining the age of thirty five years, after he has attained the age of fifty years; and (ii) (a) If he is in class II service or post, or (b) If he is class I or class II service or post and entered Government service after attaining the age of thirty five years; after he has attained the age of fifty-five years.
The Government employee would stand retired immediately on payment of three months pay and allowances in lieu of the notice period and will not be in service thereafter."
7.
A reading of the above would show that it is the absolute right of the appointing authority if he comes to the opinion and in public interest that a particular employee should be compulsorily retired, such competent authority will give a notice of not less than 3 months in writing or 3 months pay and allowances in lieu of such notice upon the employee who is going to be retired compulsorily with the passing of the order.
8.
What is the public interest will be the crucial point for determination.
9.
Public interest has not been defined in the Punjab Civil Services Rules.
As a lay man, one can say what is in the interest of public.
Public interest will always be a question of fact.
When this rule has been incorporated in the Punjab Civil Services Rules, it must have been incorporated with the object and idea to ensure that an inefficient employee has no place to serve in the administration.
The Govt. requires efficiency on the part of a public servant on every step.
10 This rule was interpreted from time to time by the various law courts.
In Bal Krishan Hooda v. The Secretary-cum-Commissioner, Haryana, 1992(2) RSJ 372 : 1992(2) SCT 397 (P&H), it was held that if the record of the public servant shows improvement from state to stage, it would be too harsh on behalf of the appointing authority to pass an order of premature retirement of an employee, if such employee had started showing improvement in the last five years before the impugned action is taken, such an employee cannot be held to be a dead wood and it will be unfair on the part of the competent authority to give compulsory retirement to such a public servant.
In the cited case, the Govt. took the decision for the compulsory retiement of an employee on the basis of the instructions dated 1681983 which have already been struck down in KK Vaid v. State of Haryana, 1990(1) RSJ 193.
11.
In Chander Singh Negi v. State of Punjab, 1992(2) RSJ 367 : 1992(2) SCT 501 (P&H)(DB), a Hon'ble Division Bench of this court held that before invoking the provisions of Rule 326(d), the appropriate authority has to exercise his its own judgment and if it has relied upon totally on the instructions of the State Govt. while considering the record of the public servant, such an order will not be able to stand judicial scrutiny.
It was further observed that the instructions issued by the State Govt. leave no discretion to the appropriate authority and the appointing authority has been left with no option but to ignore release or waive the Govt. policy instructions.
The stress of the Hon'ble D.B.
was that the appointing authority must apply its independent mind to formulate and opinion and such opinion must be based on the material record.
In the absence of any material, the order will be termed as arbitrary and colourable exercise of power.
12.
The object of Rule 326 has also been enunciated by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in K Kandaswamy v. Union of India and another, 1995(4) SCT 567, and it was held that the object is to accelerate public interest and weeding out dead wood, inefficient, corrupt and people of doubtful integrity.
Once the competent authority has formed opinion to this effect on the basis of the material on record, in that eventuality, the court will not interfere.
Also, it was observed that before passing such type of orders, the entire record more particularly, the latest, would form the foundation for the opinion and furnish the base to exercise the power under the relevant rule to compulsory retire a Govt. servant.
13.
This court also in Dharam Paul v. State of Haryana, 1996(3) AIJ 580 : 1996(3) SCT 354 (P&H)(DB), took the following view :-  "8.
A perusal of the above would show that an absolute right has been given to the appointing authority to retire a Government employee by giving him a notice of not less than three months in writing provided the action is taken in the public interest.
Though the order of compulsory retirement is not a punishment and the Government employee is entitled to draw all retirement benefits including pension, but the Government must exercise its powers only in the public interest to effectuate the efficiency of the service.
The idea behind this rule is that to remove the dead wood from the public administration so that the efficiency of the department be increased.
However, the underlying intention of the framers of this rule is that integrity in the public service must be maintained.
Also it is the object of the law that while exercising the powers of compulsory retirement, it must not be with an object to haunt a public servant rather an action should be taken as a check and reasonable measure to ensure efficiency of service and free of corruption and incompetence.
The case in hand will be examined in the light of above principles."
14.
More reliance can also be placed on Darshan Singh, Deputy Superintendent of Police v. The State of Punjab and others, 1989(1) RSJ 303, PK Rai v. The State of Haryana and another, 1991(3) RSJ 661, Mohmood Khan v Collector, Gurgaon, 1991(1) RSJ 267 : 1991(1) SCT 542 (P&H) and Maha Singh v. The State of Haryana, 1992(1) RSJ 170 : 1994(1) SCT 154 (P&H).
15.
A reading of these judgments would show that if the court comes to the conclusion that the petitioner was not a dead wood which had to be chopped off, in such a situation, the impugned order cannot sustain in the eyes of law and has to be held that such an order has been passed without due application of mind and not in public interest.
16.
I have already stated above the object and intent of Rule.
326 and, now, it is to be seen in the light of the ACRs earned by the petitioner whether it can be said that she was a dead wood which requires to be chopped off from the public administration.

It will show that in the year 1981-82, the petitioner's overall rating was B + (Good).
She was below average in 1982-83.
Again, she started improving in the year 1983-84 and arrived at the level of average.
She put more labour in the year 1984-85 and secured a good report.
For 1985-86 and 1986-87, she fumbled and showed downward trend and secured average reports.
However, from 1987-88 onwards the petitioner started putting labour in her work and conduct and she got A (very good reports) as against two average reports for the years 1985-86 and 1986-87, which will show that the petitioner took a lot of interest in the year 1987-88 by giving good results of the Wards.

She repeated the same performance in 1989-90 and for the year 1990-91 she got outstanding report.
Thus, the trend from 1987 onwards upto 1991 was upwards.
Either the petitioner got very good reports or good reports or outstanding reports.
In the first six years starting from 1981-82 to 1986-87, she got two good reports also.
19.
The criteria of 70% good or better reports in the last 10 years, in the opinion of this court is not a rational one.
Rather, the criteria to retain an employee after the age of 55 years should be whether such public servant has the aptitude to put hard work in his service career or not.
supposing an employee in the first six years earns good, very good or excellent results and, subsequently, he totally losses his interest in his service career and earns below average reports, whether such an employee should be retained into service after the age of 55 years.
The rational answer of this proposition would be in the negative because it is true that initially the employee showed good performance but for one reason or the other his performance started dwindling for lack of interest continuously and the wood started becoming dead wood losing all hope of sprouting again.
Thus, the criteria before the appointing authority should be to see from the entire service record and particularly of last 10 years and, again, the last 5 years should be given more importance which would give a clear picture about the interest of such public servant in his duty.
20.
The learned counsel for the respondents submitted that the petitioner has concealed her results for the year 1990-91 of the 8th class.
It was (-) 4735% than the result of the Board and, therefore, it cannot be said that the petitioner's ACR for the year 1990-91 was A (Outstanding).
21.
I do not accept this contention.
Firstly, the grading was done by the department as A + (Excellent).
Further, a reading of Annexure R-5 would show that the results have been shown as +4.49% in one category.
This report was never downgraded and, therefore, it cannot be made the basis for the compulsory/premature retirement of the petitioner.
Further, a reading of Annexure P-4 would show that the petitioner was permitted to cross the efficiency bar prescribed at the stage of Rs. 2,300/- in the grade of Rs. 1400-2600 with effect from 141992, raising her pay to Rs. 2360/-.
This order was passed on 1491992.
While passing the order of crossing of efficiency bar, the department must have taken note of the ACRs of the petitioner especially for the years 1990-91 and 1991-92.
If the petitioner was found fit to cross the efficiency bar w.e.f.
141992, it suggests that she was not a dead wood so as to necessitate her premature retirement.
22.
In the light of the above, this court is of the opinion that the order, Annexure P-2, has been passed in irrational basis and not in the larger interest of public and, therefore, cannot be sustained in the eyes of law.
The impugned order shows non-application of mind on the part of the competent authority who has not assessed the service record of the petitioner in the right perspective.
Therefore, the order, Annexure P-2, is set aside and stands quashed.
23.
The writ petition is hereby allowed and it is hereby declared that the petitioner shall be deemed to be in service for all intents and purposes till she attained the age of superannuation as per the rules of the department and she would also be entitled to all the benefits of service till that age, in the shape of pay, allowances, etc.
The benefits which have accrued to the petitioner with the passing of this order shall be paid by the respondent- authorities within 3 months from the receipt of the copy of the order.
However, there shall be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
