The case of the petitioner is that with the division of cadre strength of Senior Translators in the ratio of 50:50 and their redesignation as Senior Translators Grade-II and Senior Translators Grade-I did not amount to creation of new cadres.
The seniority list of the Senior Translators Grade II and Senior Translators Grade I remains one and the placement of Senior Translators Grade-II in the scale of Senior Translators Grade-I would not involve any promotion in the conventional sense from post of lower responsibility to a posts of distinct higher responsibility or from one cadre to another or from a lower class to a higher class of service.
The same was true with regard to the Assistants and Senior Assistants in the Civil Secretariat.
In the case of division of cadre strength of Junior Translators in the ratio of 50:50 and their re-designation as Junior Translators Grade-II and Junior Translators Grade-I did not amount to creation of any promotional post in the cadre.
Same is the case of Clerks and Senior Clerks.
With the bifurcation of cadre strength of Clerks in the ratio of 20:40:40 and their redesignation as Clerks, Senior Clerks and Junior Assistants in different pay scales does not amount to creation of new cadres.

The case of the petitioners is that the Government further revised pay scales of Assistants and clerks in April, 1990.
They moved a representation on 2041990 wherein petitioners No. to 4 who are Senior Translators Grade II claim any parity in the pay scales with that of the Assistants in the offices of Punjab Civil Secretariat and Punjab Vidhan Sabha Secretariat.
It is stated that the representation was forwarded by the Speaker alongwith the case of revision of pay scales of some other cadres of Punjab Vidhan Sabha Secretariat with the recommendation that parity in the Senior Assistants and Clerks may be maintained in the categories of Senior Translators and Junior Translators in the offices of Punjab Vidhan Sabha but it is stated that without going into merits of the case, the claim was refused.
The petitioners moved yet another representation to the Secretary, Punjab Vidhan Sabha, dated 2411991.
The same was considered by the Speaker favourably and the case of the petitioners was again sent to Respondent No. 1 for concurrence.
But it is stated that no action was taken by the respondents in the matter.
On the aforesaid facts, action of the respondents in not granting the pay scales to the Senior Translators and Junior Translators of the Panjab Vidhan Sabha at par with the Assistants and Clerks is stated to be illegal, arbitrary, discriminatory and violative of Article 14 of the Constitution of India.
It is mainly pleaded as also has been canvassed by the learned counsel for the petitioners that from the very beginning till June, 1990, the pay scales of Senior Translators and Junior Translators had remained at par with the Assistants and Clerks respectively in the offices of the Punjab Vidhan Sabha and other offices of the Civil Secretariat and therefore denial of parity of pay scales after June 1990 is violative of Article 14 of the Constitution of India.
It is also made out that once the competent authority had equated posts of Senior Translators and Junior Translators with that of the Assistants and Clerks by following proper procedure, as long as the equation continued, the revision in pay scales of one post necessarily requires commensurate hike in the equated post.
The principle of equal pay for equal work is also pressed into service.

An interim direction was issued to the respondents to dispose of the representation within one month.
The representation was considered and rejected.
6.
This petition has been opposed.
In the Written Statement filed on behalf of the respondents No. 1 and 2, it has been pleaded that in the Punjab Civil Secretariat, posts of Superintendents Press, Senior Translators and Junior Translators were merged with the corresponding posts on the general side of Superintendents, Assistants and Clerks respectively not on the ground that they had similar duties as those of the posts in which they were merged but this was done on the recommendation of the Second Pay Commission keeping in view the fact that they did not have adequate chances of promotion in their own small cadre.
Therefore, no separate entity of Senior Translators and Junior Translators existed in the Punjab Civil Secretariat.
By virtue of the merger of the posts of Translation Wing of the Secretariat in the general wing of the Secretariat, the persons so merged could have the right to claim the scales of the Superintendents, Assistants and Clerks of the Secretariat.
Otherwise the posts of Translation Wing and General Wing of the Department have altogether different duties and responsibilities, and cannot in any manner be equated for the purpose of grant of equal pay scales.
It is also pleaded that equation of posts and equation of pay are matters primarily in the discretion of the Government.
In so far as the kind of duties that are involved in the two posts are concerned, the case of the respondents is that the Translation Wing and the General Wing of the respondents have altogether different duties and responsibilities and therefore, cannot be equated for grant of equal pay scales.
It is further pleaded that placement of Senior Translators Grade II in Senior Translators Grade I and Junior Translators Grade II in Junior Translators Grade I, Assistants as Senior Assistants, Clerks as Senior Clerks involve no promotion in the conventional sense from the post of lower responsibility to that of distinctly higher responsibility or from one cadre to another or from lower class to the higher class service.
Similar is the position in the case of bifurcation of cadre of clerks in the ratio of 40:60 and their redesignation as Junior Assistants, Senior Assistants and Clerks.
But it is also pleaded that in the case of Assistants, this bifurcation has been abolished and all Assistants have been redesignated as Senior Assistants.
In brief, the main defence put in the written statement is that duties and responsibilities of Junior and Senior Translators are altogether different from those of the Assistants, Senior Assistants and Clerks in the Punjab Civil Secretariat and as such question of equation of these two categories of posts of Translation Wing and General Wing of Punjab Vidhan Sabha does not arise and also that the equation of pay scales of Senior Translators and Junior Translators with those of Assistants, Senior Assistants and Clerks cannot be claimed as a matter of right and action of the Government is in no way discriminatory and violative of principle enshrined in Article 14 of the Constitution  7.
I have heard the learned counsel for the parties and am of the considered view that the case of the petitioners is meritorious and therefore, this petition must succeed.
8.
Vide application filed under Section 151 of the Code of Civil Procedure, extract of Government notification dated 2111991 has been produced on record as Annexure A-1 and a perusal whereof shows that the Punjab Government Finance Department issued notification dated 2111969 in exercise of the powers conferred by proviso to Article 309 read with clause 3 of Article 187 of the Constitution of India, after consultation with the Chairman of the Punjab Legislative Council and Speaker of the Punjab Legislative Assembly by virtue of which Punjab Civil Services (Revised Scales of Pay) 1969 came into force on the 1st day of February, 1968.
Revised pay, revised scale and Schedule have been defined in Rule 3 which are reproduced as under :  (e) "revised pay" means basic pay of a Government servant in the revised scale appropriate to the basic pay in the existing scale;  (f) "revised scale" means basic pay specified for a post as revised scale in Schedule `B' or `C' and as spelt out in detail in Schedule `A'.
(g) "Schedule" means a Schedule annexed to these rules."
9.
Schedule-C clearly makes out that the post of Senior Translator has been equated to that of Assistant Grade-A whereas the post of Junior Translator to that of Clerk plus Rs. 20/- as Special Pay.
Graduates were to be given two advance increments.
It is thus proved that the Government did equate the posts of Senior Translator to that of Assistant Grade A and that of Junior Translator to that of Clerk.
The First question that therefore, arises is as to whether the equation of aforesaid posts continues or has been discontinued.
The case of the respondents is that this equation is no more available as vide notification dated 1221971 wherever the word equation had been mentioned in the Schedule `C' the same was omitted.
The extract of the notification has been placed on record Annexure R-2 and a bare reading whereof shows that the notification came into existence in exercise of the powers conferred by the proviso to Article 309 of the Constitution or India and all other powers enabling him in this behalf by virtue of which Punjab Civil Services (Revised Scales of Pay) Rules, 1969 were amended.
It is, however, apt to mention immediately that the amendment of the Rules in the manner aforesaid was not done after consultation with the Speaker as was necessary as per provisions contained in Article 187 of the Constitution of India which runs thus :  "187.
(1) The House or each House of the Legislature of a State shall have a separate secretarial staff :- Provided that nothing in this clause shall, in the case of the Legislature of a State having a Legislative Council, be construed as preventing the creation of posts common to both Houses of such Legislature.
(2) The Legislature of a State by law regulate the recruitment, and the conditions of service of persons appointed to the secretarial staff of the House or Houses of the Legislature of the State.
(3) Until provision is made by the legislature of the State under clause (2), the Governor may, after consultation with the Speaker of the Legislative Assembly or the Chairman of the Legislative Council, as the case may be, make rules regulating the recruitment, and the conditions of service of persons appointed, to the secretarial staff of the Assembly or the Council and any rules so made shall have effect subject to the provisions of any law made under the said clause."
It is admitted case between the parties that the legislature of the State has not by law regulated the recruitment and the conditions of service of persons appointed to the secretarial staff of the House and it is only through rules laid by the Governor after consultation with the Speaker of the Legislative Assembly that govern the recruitment and the conditions of service of persons appointed to the secretarial staff of the Assembly.
Annexure A-1 came into being in exercise of the powers conferred by the proviso to Article 309 read with clause 3 of Article 187 of the Constitution of India whereas Annexure R-2 dated 1221971 was without any such consideration.
In my considered view, therefore, Annexure R-2 cannot replace or amend the notification dated 2111969 Annexed A-1 with the petition' and therefore, the relevant Punjab Civil Services (Revised Scales of Pay) Rules 1969 would govern the field.
As mentioned above, the post of Senior Translator has been equated to that of Assistant Grade A whereas post of Junior Translator has been equated to that of Clerk vide Schedule `C' attached to Rules of 1969.
It may be mentioned in passing at this stage itself that so far as Speaker is concerned he recommended revision of pay scales of Translators so as to put them at par with their counterparts but as mentioned above the representation of the petitioners on which the aforesaid recommendation was made has since been rejected.
The comparative table reproduced above would thus demonstrate that in the year 1968 the pay of Assistants was equal to that of Senior Translators i.e. Rs. 225-500.
From 111978 where the Assistants were made Senior Assistants and Assistants, accordingly the Senior Translators were made Senior Translators Grade I and Senior Translators Grade II and the pay scale of Assistant was kept at par with Senior Translator Grade II.
In 1986 as well when there was hike in pay scales, the pay parity was maintained.
It is only by virtue of Punjab Civil Services (Revised Pay) (IIIrd amendment) Rules, 1990 that all Assistants and Senior Assistants were put in pay scale of Rs. 1800-3200 with effect from 111986 whereas no decision was taken with regard to Senior Translators Grade II.
The case of the petitioners is thus on solid wicket and their claim that they should be put into one scale, may be known as Senior Translators Grade-I or Senior Translators Grade-II or simply Translators in one pay scale which may be equivalent to the Senior Assistants in the pay scale of Rs. 1800-3200 and that too from 111986 is justified.
It is settled by this court in a number of judgments delivered from time to time that once the Government equates one post to that of another then the hike in pay scale of one necessarily entail commensurate escalation in the other.
Learned counsel appearing for the petitioners squarely relies upon KK Mehta and another v. State of Punjab and another, 1983(3) SLR 732; Harsaran Singh v. State of Punjab and others, 1984(2) SLR 384 and Kipaljit v. State of Punjab, 1987(4) SLR 594.
10.
The contention of the learned counsel appearing for the State that the equation as earlier done is no more in existence by virtue of the notification Annexure R-2 or that it was the discretion of the Government to give rise in pay scale to one post and not to other and also that all the Senior Assistants and Assistants have been redesignated as Assistants whereas Senior Translators Grade I and Senior Translators Grade II were not redesignated, needs to be repelled.
The other matters have been discussed earlier and the point with regard to Senior Assistants and Assistants now getting the post of Assistant is concerned suffice it to say that whenever there was redesignation of the posts of Assistant a corresponding redesignation in the post of Senior Translator was also there and on that account no distinction can be made if by now designating Senior Assistants and Assistants as Senior Assistants, the Senior Translators Grade I and Senior Translators Grade II have not been designated as Senior Translators.
It has also been demonstrated that this bifurcation or redesignation as the case may be, is not on account of any promotion or higher responsibility.
However, it shall be open for the respondents to redesignate Senior Translators Grade I and Senior Translators Grade II as Senior Translators or simply Translators but they shall have pay scales admissible to Senior Assistants.
In the case of Junior Translators Grade I and Junior Translators Grade II there is no difference at all and therefore mentioning of facts with regard to these posts will be only repetition.
Suffice is to say that by virtue of amendment brought about in the Rules of 1990, clerks have been redesignated as Junior Assistants 40%, Senior Clerks 40% and Clerks 20% whereas no such redesignation in the posts of Junior Translators Grade I and Junior Translators Grade II has been brought about.
Therefore, Junior Translators Grade I and Junior Translators Grade II shall be considered as one cadre and first 40% of the Junior Translators will get pay scale of Rs. 1500-2640 whereas next 40% would get any pay scale of Rs. 1200-2100 and the last 20% would get pay scale of Rs. 950-1800 with an initial start of Rs. 1000/-.
If petitioner Nos. 5 and 6, who are Junior Translators Grade II, would get benefit of this judgment in accordance with their fitment i.e. on finding out as to whether they come in first 40% or second 40% or last 20%, that pay scale shall be allowed to them.
For the reasons aforesaid, this petition is allowed.
It is directed that petitioner Nos. 1 to 4, who are Senior Translators Grade II be given same pay scale as is admissible to Senior Assistants i.e. viz. Rs. 1800-3200 with effect from 1.
11986.
Petitioner Nos. 5 and 6 would get enhancement in their pay scales as per observations made above.
The petitioners will get rise in pay scales with effect from 111986.
The writ is allowed in the manner indicated above with costs, which are quantified at Rs. 1000/- (Rupees one thousand).
The relief granted to the petitioners would be accorded within three months from today.
