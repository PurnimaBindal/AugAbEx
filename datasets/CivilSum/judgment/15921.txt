YV Narayan, J - In this batch of writ petitions, the petitioners challenge the re-fixation of their salaries by the respondent - Life Insurance Corporation and the consequential recovery of excess amount paid to them on the ground that the re-fixation by the respondent-corporation is illegal, arbitrary, discriminatory, violative of principles of natural justice and Article 14 of the Constitution of India.
2.
The brief facts of the cases are : The writ petitioners, who were discharged ex-servicemen, are the employees of the respondent-corporation.
Their pay was fixed on a mistaken construction of the Instructions dated 261989, which govern the fixation of the salaries of Ex-servicemen after re-employment and discovering that mistake, the respondent-corporation re- fixed their salaries reducing their basic pay and directed recovery of the excess amount paid to them in easy monthly instalments of Rs. 200/-.
Assailing this fixation of salaries of the petitioners, these writ petitioners are filed.
3.
It is contended by the learned counsel for the petitioners that before re-fixation of the salaries of the petitioners herein, no notice was given, and no opportunity was given to the petitioners to put forth their objections.
In support of this contention the learned counsel relied on the decisions reported in Bhagwan Shukla v Union of India, AIR 1994 SC 2480 : 1994(4) SCT 406(SC) and Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation, AIR 1986 SC 180.
On the other hand the learned counsel for the respondent-Corporation contended that there is no need to give notice while re-fixing the salaries of the employees of the Corporation, as their rights are not affected and no purpose will be served even if the notices are served.
In support of this contention he relied on the decisions reported in Shiba Prasad v. Srish Chandra, AIR 1949 PC 297 and Sales Tax Officer v. Kanhaiya Lal, AIR 1959 SC 135.
4.
A perusal of the record shows that after re-fixing the salaries of he petitioners, they were informed of the same and some of the writ petitioners filed representations making their objections for the re-fixing of their salaries and after considering those representations also, the recovery of excess amount paid to the petitioners was ordered at easy monthly instalments of Rs. 200/-.
Hence there is no force in the contention of the learned counsel for the petitioners that no notice was issued to the writ petitioners.
Moreover when the petitioners were paid money by mistake which was not due to them, the same has to be recovered.
The excess money was paid to the writ petitioners in thinking by the respondent-Corporation that it was due to the petitioners, but in fact it was not due and when that mistake is established, the excess amount paid to the petitioners has necessarily to be recovered from the petitioners who received the same.
In similar circumstances in UK Udayasankaran v.
U.O.I., 1996(3) SCALE 383, the Supreme court considered the similar issues involved in these writ petitions and observed that Paragraph 5 of the Instructions dated 261989 clearly sets out that the ex- servicemen who are appointed after 111988 shall be fitted as per formula given in Paragraph 31 either from 111988 or the date of appointment whichever is later; that Paragraph 7 clarifies that position by saying that the notice of salary of all ex-servicemen appointed in the Corporation henceforth shall be governed by these instructions and that the respondent- corporation sought to correct its mistake by recalculating the salaries of its employees from 1991 and the respondent-corporation is entitled to reduce the pay of the appellants therein on the basis of the correct fitment to be given to the appellant in the light of the instructions of 261989.
This decision squarely applies to the facts of these writ petitions as in these cases the respondent-corporation after recognising the mistake committed by it in fixing the salaries of the writ petitioners, ordered recovery of the excess amounts paid to be petitioners, after following the instructions dated 261989.
Hence these batch of writ petitions are liable to be dismissed.
W.P. Nos. 20042 of 1993; 7522, 20715 and 21392 of 1994 and 5243 of 1995  5.
In this batch of writ petitions, the petitioners sought re-fixation of their pay taking into consideration their service in the Military in terms for the Govt.
Memo No. 3/1/85-Estt-(P-11) dated 3171986 or at least on equal par with their junior Ex-servicemen re-employed in the respondent-corporation from the dates of their eligibility with all other consequential benefits.
6.
The respondent-corporation filed counter stating that the petitioner pay will be fixed as per the Corporation Rules and Regulations; that the respondent-Corporation did not adopt the Central Civil Services (fixation of pay of re-employed persons Order dated 3171986, but had taken note of the same in issuing the Revised Instructions to all its offices on 261989 and hence no relief can be granted to the petitioners in these writ petitions.
7.
Admittedly the petitioners in these writ petitions were appointed by the respondent-corporation on or after 111988 and hence they are governed by the Instructions dated 261989 only.
In view of the decision of the Supreme Court in Udayasankaran's case, 1990 (3) SCALE 383 (supra) which clearly held that the ex-servicemen who are appointed after 111988 shall be fitted as per formula given in Paragraph No. 31 either from 111988 or the date of appointment whichever is later and that Paragraph 7 again clarifies this position by saying that the fitment of salary of all ex-servicemen appointed in the respondent-corporation henceforth shall be governed by the instructions dated 261989.
As the pay of the writ petitioners was fixed following the formula laid down in the instructions dated 261989, no relief can be granted to the writ petitioners and the writ petitions are liable to be dismissed.
8.
In view of the above, all the above writ petitioners are dismissed, but without costs.
However it is open to the respondent-corporation to consider the representation to be filed by the petitioners regarding recovery of the amounts in easy monthly instalments.
