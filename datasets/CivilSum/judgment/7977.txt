Swatanter Kumar, J - The petitioner in this writ petition under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India challenges the order dated 2122000, Annexure P/10 to the writ petition, pre-maturely retiring the petitioner under Rule 3(1)(a) of Punjab Civil Services (Pre-mature Retirement) Rules, 1975.
The petitioner joined the Home Guards service of Punjab as a Clerk on 5121966.
According to the petitioner his service record was good and because of his hard-work the petitioner was ultimately promoted as Platoon Commander on 521976.
On 16101995 a raid was conducted by the Vigilance Bureau in the office of the petitioner.
On 921996 an FIR was registered under Sections 420, 419, 467, 409, 468, 471, 120-B of the Indian Penal Code and Sections 13(1) and 13(2) of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988.
On 23.2.1996 the petitioner was placed under suspension.
The investigating agency filed the challan in the Court of Special Judge, Ludhiana on 2581999.
On 2122000, the petitioner was reinstated with a clear stipulation that the vigilance enquiry against him would no way be adversely affected.
Vide order of the same date the petitioner was pre-maturely retired from service,.
2.
Upon notice, the respondents filed a detailed reply in which it was stated that besides the above criminal case and the vigilance enquiry pending against the petitioner, the entire service record of the petitioner was such that would render him unworthy of retention in Home Guards service.
3.
The learned counsel for the petitioner in view of the above undisputed facts argued that the adverse entries in the confidential record of the petitioner are so old that no note can be taken thereon while taking conscious decision as to whether the petitioner should be retained in service beyond the age of 55 years.
This contention is without any merit.
The Hon'ble Apex Court in the case of Partap Narain and another v. District Judge, Azamgarh and another, AIR 1996 Supreme Court 111 held that the competent authority is fully justified in considering the entire service record of an employee while taking decision in regard to retention of the employee beyond the period of 55 years.
Such a decision is objective one and must be based upon the material available before the authority.
4.

5.
From the above narrated facts it is clear that the concerned authorities have truly applied their mind to the entire service record of the petitioner and had then taken an objective assessment in regard to utility of the petitioner for the department.
This Court cannot sit as a Court of appeal over the decision rendered.
So far the process of decision making is in consonance with the settled canons of law.
A Division Bench of this Court in the case of ASI Sunehra Singh v. The State of Haryana and others, CWP No. 9857 of 1995, decided on 211997, in somewhat similar circumstances, held as under :-  "In the facts and circumstances of the case we are of the view that the authorities have not erred in considering the entire service record of the petitioner which was available to the authorities immediately prior to the point of time when the case of the petitioner was to be considered by the authorities for crossing the efficiency bar.
The consistent adverse record of the petitioner right from 1983-84 to 1989-90 was certainly of relevance for the authorities concerned.
The mere fact that the petitioner was promoted as Assistant Sub-Inspector in the year 1988 by itself would not have the effect of wiping out the previous adverse entries so as to eliminate the same from the purview of consideration of the competent authorities."
"The competency of the authorities to look into the entire service record of the petitioner and to pass appropriate orders of the present kind cannot be held to be without jurisdiction so as to result in vitiating the orders itself.
In this regard it may be appropriate to make a reference to the judgments of the Supreme Court in the cases of S Ramachandra Raju v. State of Orissa, A.I.R. 1995 Supreme Court 111 : 1994(4) SCT 430 (SC) and State of Orissa and others v. Ram Chander Dass, 1996(4) SCT 288 (SC) : A.I.R. 1996 Supreme Court 2436.
6.
Keeping in view the pleadings of the parties and the extracts of the service record of the petitioner as afore-noticed, we are not in a position to find any error in the decision taken by the respondents which prima-facie is a record based decision.
We find no reason to set aside the impugned order dated 2122001.
Accordingly, this writ petition is dismissed, leaving the parties to bear their own costs.
Petition dismissed.
