THB Chalapathi, J - The petitioners filed this writ petition for issuance of a writ of mandamus directing the respondents to regularise the service of the petitioners as per the decision of the Supreme Court and also the instructions of the State Government.
2.
The petitioners have been appointed on daily wages in the office of respondent No. 3 on muster rolls without issuing any order of appointment during the years 1983 to 1994.
According to them, they remained in service till 30th of May, 1994 and thereafter they have not been given any muster roll.
Some other similarly situated persons approached this Court and their Writ Petitions were allowed.
The services of the petitioners were not regularised, but the services of the persons junior to them have been regularised.
In order to seek regularisation of their services, the petitioners filed a writ petition bearing No. 10890 of 1988.
By an order dated 2nd June, 1993 this Court passed the following order :-  "Learned Counsel for petitioners stated that in so far as petitioners Nos. 1, 9, 15, 21 and 22 are concerned they, have not completed two years service prior to the filing of the present writ petition and they would like to withdraw their petition to as to approach the appropriate authority for the desired relief.
Petition on their behalf is dismissed as withdrawn."
In so far as other petitioners are concerned it has been pleaded that they have completed more than two years' service prior to the filing of this writ petition.
If the averment to the effect aforesaid is correct, the respondents would consider their case for regularisation in view of the judgment passed by the Supreme Court in State of Haryana v. Piara Singh, 1992(5) SPJ 1 : 1992(3) SCT 201 (SC), and the instructions issued by the Government on that behalf, if they are found to have requisites for regularisation, their services shall be regularised.
However, till such time final orders with regard to regularisation of their services are passed, one way or the other, their services shall not be terminated."
After passing the said order, the services of the petitioners were not regularised.
The petitioners also served a legal notice to implement the order of this Court.
In spite of that, the services of the petitioners were not regularised.
According to the petitioners, their services are to be regularised in view of the order of this Court.
Since no order has been passed by the respondents, the petitioners filed this writ petition.
3.
On behalf of the respondents, a written statement is filed admitting that the petitioners were employed as casual labourers purely on daily wage basis and it is also contended that the petitioners are paid for the days they are present on duty.
Their services are not regular and since they are only casual labourers on daily wages, they are not entitled for regularisation.
They further stated that in compliance with the order of this Court referred to above, the case of the petitioners was considered for regularisation in the light of the decision of the Supreme Court in State of Haryana v. Piara Singh and the representations of the petitioners were rejected vide orders Annexures, R-3A, R-3B, R-4A and R-4/B.
According to the respondents they have not completed 240 days in a year during the period of five years from 1988 to 1993 and therefore they are not entitled to have their services regularised.
Therefore, their writ petition is liable to be dismissed.
4.
In their replication, the petitioners reiterated their pleas taken in the writ petition.
There is no dispute of the fact that the petitioners have been working as Casual Labourers on daily wages.
The Government of Haryana on the basis of the decision of the Supreme Court in State of Haryana v. Piara Singh, 1992(3) RSJ 235 issued instructions on 2751993 vide Annexure R-1 in regard to regularisation of work charge/casual/daily rated employees.
According to those instructions, the casual and daily rated employees who have completed 5 years of continuous service on 31st March, 1993 and were in service on that date shall be regularised.
On regularisation, they shall be put in the time scale of pay applicable to the lowest Group `D' cadre in the Government and they would be entitled to all other allowances and benefits available to regular Govt. servants of the corresponding grade.
Subsequently, a clarification was issued by the Chief Secretary to Government, Haryana in his circular letter dated 18.3.1994 which is marked as Annexure R-2.
According to this letter, the Government has decided that those daily wages employees who have worked for 240 days in a year should be regularised, but there should not be a break in service for more than 30 days at a time in a year.
Thus it is clear that for getting the benefit of regularisation of services in the Policy of the Government, the employee must have worked for a period of 240 days in a year without any break of more than 30 days.
5.

This it is clear that the petitioners have not fulfilled the conditions imposed by the Government in its circular letter dated 18.3.1994 (Annexure R- 2).
6.
The learned Counsel for the petitioners placed reliance on a decision of this Court in Giri Raj and others v. State of Haryana and others, reported in 1997(2) RSJ 506, to which I am a party.
In that case the petitioners have fulfilled the conditions of working 240 days in a year.
The break in service for more than 30 days was held not to come in the way of regularisation of the petitioners and in that case the break was not on account of the fault of petitioners therein, but due to banning on muster rolls for that month.
In the decision it has also not been held that those petitioners who have not completed for 240 days are entitled to regularisation.
The learned counsel for the petitioner further relied upon a decision of this Court in Ram Dhari v. The Chief Wild Life Warden, Haryana and others, 1996(1) RSJ 246 : 1996(1) SCT 615 (P&H)(DB).
In that case, the petitioner completed more than 240 days during the period of five years from 1988 to 1993 and that too without any break in service.
Since the petitioner in that case worked for more than 240 days and there was no break in service during the period of five years, his writ petition was rightly allowed by this Court directing the respondents to consider his case for regularisation.
Since in the present case, the petitioners have not completed 240 days, they cannot take advantage of the above decision.
The other decision relied upon by the petitioners in Anand Kumar v. Haryana Urban Development Authority etc. reported in 1995(1) R.S.J.
230 : 1995(1) SCT 677 (P&H)(DB), also cannot be pressed into service since in that case the break in service more than one month occasioned due to the circumstances beyond the control of the daily wagers/casual employee.
7.
In this case, the petitioners have not fulfilled the primary condition that they should be in service for 240 days or more in a year.
Since that condition is not fulfilled, they cannot take any benefit of the instructions issued by the Haryana Government vide Annexure R-2.
Since they have not completed 240 days service during the period of five years prior to 3131993 they are not entitled to have their services regularised.
I do not, therefore, see any merit in this writ petition.
8.
The result is, the writ petition fails and is, accordingly, dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
