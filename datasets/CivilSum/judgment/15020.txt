SD Anand, J - Both the Courts (learned Trial Court and also the learned First Appellate Court) held the appellants accountable for the delay in the disbursement of the relevant pensionary benefits to the plaintiff-respondent and burdened them with the payment of interest @9% per annum.
2.
Learned counsel for the appellants argues that the impugned findings deserve to be reversed inasmuch as it was a case of voluntary retirement and not a retirement on superannuation.
3.
The plea is plainly illogical.
Concededly, the plaintiff-respondent had initially issued a notice dated 2412001 announcing his intention to obtain voluntary retirement w.e.f.
3152001.
He issued another notice pre-poning the proposed date of voluntary retirement.
Whether an employee retires on superannuation or on voluntary retirement, the Competent Authority is charged with the responsibility of ensuring the payment of retiral benefits to the employee within a reasonable time.
In the present case, all the retiral benefits (GP Fund, leave, encashment, gratuity and commuted pension) were paid much after the date of retirement.
For example, the plaintiff respondent retired on 3152001.
GP Fund, leave encashment, gratuity and commuted pension were paid to him on 1682001, 16102001, 14.12.2001 and 1332002.
Both the Courts have recorded justified reasons for the award of interest payable by the appellants to the respondent in the context.
Dismissed.
Appeal dismissed.
