Ashok Bhan, J - This petition has been filed under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India for issuance of a writ of certiorari quashing order, Annexure P-3 dated 471996, retiring the petitioner compulsorily on attaining the age of 55 years by giving him three months notice.
2.
Briefly stated, the facts are:-  Petitioner was appointed as a Clerk in the office of the Deputy Commissioner, Gurgaon, on 2731961.
He was allowed to cross the efficiency bar on 131988.
He was promoted as Assistant on 24101989 and was posted as District Nazar in the Nazarat Branch, which duty he joined on 30101989.
He was also given the higher standard scale of pay of Rs. 1600-2600 and Rs. 1640-2900, in terms of the Haryana Government Instructions contained in letter No. 1/34/93-4FR(FD) dated 821994 as modified vide letter No. 1/34/93 PR(FD) dated 951995, as he had completed regular satisfactory service of 20 years.
3.
Impugned order, Annexure P-3, compulsorily retiring the petitioner at the age of 55 years was passed on 471996 making it operative with effect from 2061996.
Petitioner had earned two doubtful integrity reports for the years 1967-68 and 1977-78.
Work and conduct of the petitioner during the last 10 years immediately preceding the passing of order Annexure P-3, compulsory retiring him, was good or above and no adverse remarks were conveyed to him during the last 10 years.
State Government issued instructions from time to time to be followed by the competent authority while retiring a government employee compulsorily under Rule 326(d) of the Punjab Civil Services Rules Volume I Part I State Government issued instructions dated 2231990, Annexure P-4, superseding the earlier instructions dated 981975, Annexure R-3, providing as under:-  ``The matter has further been considered by the State Govt. and it has been decided that while considering the cases of retention in service beyond the age of 50/55 years, the reports of doubtful integrity of an employee pertaining to the period beyond the last ten years, may be ignored.
However, the instructions regarding placing the cases of those employees whose integrity has been suspect within this period will continue to be in force and such cases would be placed before the Officers Committee consisting of the Chief Secretary to Govt. Haryana, the Financial Commissioner, the Administrative Secretary and Head of Department concerned.''
4.
Petitioner has impugned the order, Annexure P-3, compulsorily retiring him at the age of 55 years in the ground that the reports of doubtful integrity earned by him, which pertain to the period beyond the last 10 years, were to be ignored and he could not be compulsorily retired at the age of 55 years as he had earned good or above reports in the 10 years immediately preceding the order of compulsory retirement.
It has also been submitted that the petitioner having been promoted as an Assistant in the year 1989 and the higher standard scale of pay having been given to him in the year 1994, adverse reports prior to the date of promotion could not form the basis of passing of the impugned order, Annexure P-3.
Another ground taken is that the petitioner could not be compulsorily retired with retrospective effect, making the order, Annexure P- 3, passed on 471996 operative with effect from 2061996, being contrary to Rule 326 of the Punjab Civil Services Rules.
5.
In the written statement filed, the stand taken by the respondents is that the petitioner had earned two doubtful integrity reports for the years 1967-68 and 1977-78 and in view of the Haryana Government instructions dated 981975, Annexure R-3, petitioner could be retired compulsorily at the age of 55 years.
Counsel for the parties have been heard.
6.
Haryana Government instructions dated 981975, Annexure R-3, stood superseded by the subsequent instructions dated 2231990, Annexure P-4, which provides that the reports of doubtful integrity of an employee pertaining to the period beyond the last 10 years, may be ignored.
The two doubtful integrity reports earned by the petitioner are, admittedly, beyond the period of last 10 years, which could not be taken into consideration while considering the case of the petitioner for retaining him in service beyond 55 years.
Petitioner had also been promoted as an Assistant in the year 1989 and was also allowed higher standard scale of pay vide order, Annexure P-2, with allowed higher standard scale of pay vide order, Annexure P-2, with effect from 131994, by the authorities after satisfying itself that more than 70% of his service record during the last 10 years was good or above.
Adverse record prior to the promotion and a grant of higher standard scale of pay lost much of their sting and could not be taken into consideration while considering the case of the petitioner for retention in service beyond the age of 55 years, especially in view of the instructions, Annexure P-4.
7.
Counsel for the petitioner next contended that the order, Annexure P-3, compulsorily retiring the petitioner from service, having been passed with retrospective effect, is illegal and inoperative.
8.
Counsel for the respondents did not seriously dispute the proposition that the order, Annexure P-3, could not be made retrospective but contended that the order of compulsory retirement and making it operative retrospectively are severable and the petitioner could be retired with effect from the date of the issuance of the order Annexure P-3.
Reliance for this was placed upon a judgment of the Apex Court in R Jeearatnam v. The State of Madras, 1967 SLR 657, where in the case of a Government employee who had been dismissed from service with retrospective effect, it was held that the order of dismissal and making it operative retrospectively consisted to two severable parts and while upholding the first part of the order regarding dismissal, the second part of the order which made it retrospective was set aside.
It was held as under:-  ``Assuming that the second part, giving retrospective effect to the order, is invalid on the ground that an order of dismissal cannot be passed with retrospective effect, there is no reason why the first part of the order, namely, dismissal as from the date when such order is made, should not be given the fullest effect.
The court, no doubt, cannot pass a new order of dismissal but surely it can give effect to the valid severable part of the order.''
Impugned order, Annexure P-3, is also held to be unsustainable to the extent it has been made operative retrospectively.
An employee can be compulsory retired by either giving him three months notice or three months salary in lieu thereof.
He cannot be deprived of the salary for the period for which he has worked.
If the order of compulsory retirement is made operative retrospectively, the resultant effect would be to deprive the employee of the salary for the period for which he had worked and a notice of less than three months before retiring him from the service.
Order, Annexure P-3, to the extent it has been given retrospective effect is held to be bad in law.
For the reasons stated above, we hold that the order, Annexure P-3, cannot be sustained and the same is quashed.
This petition is accepted.
Respondents are directed to take the petitioner back in service with all consequential benefits.
No costs.
