GS Singhvi, J - Though learned counsel for the petitioners has not appeared, keeping in view the nature of the claim made by the petitioners, I am of the view that there is no justification to postpone decision of the writ petition.
2.
Petitioners are working as Teachers in various schools of the Government of Punjab.
They have made a claim for grant of selection grade in accordance with the policy decision of the Government of Punjab as contained in Circular dated 23.7.1957.
Petitioners have stated that the claim made by similarly situated persons was accepted by this Court in Letters Patent Appeal No.135 of 1986, decided on 1511990.
However, the Government confined the benefit of selection grade only to those who had approached the Court.
Claiming that they have been subjected to discrimination, the petitioners have stated that when the persons similarly situated qua them have been given the benefit of selection grade, there is no reason or justification to deny that benefit to the petitioners.
3.
No reply to the writ petition has been filed and therefore, the averments made in the writ petition have remained uncontroverted.
4.
I have heard Ms. Tuli and have perused the record to the case.
5.
It is an admitted position that the writ petition filed by similarly situated persons was allowed by the learned Single Judge on 15111985 and Letters Patent Appeal No.135 of 1986 (State of Punjab v. Hira Lal and others) was dismissed on 1511990.
Civil Writ Petition No13504 of 1993 filed by Prem Parkash and three others was disposed of by a Division Bench on 331994 in view of the fact that petitioners in that writ petition were granted the benefit of selection grade.
6.
Learned counsel for the respondents has fairly conceded that the petitioners in this petition are senior to the persons who were petitioners in Civil Writ Petition No13504 of 1993.
That apart, learned Assistant Advocate General has not been able to point out as to how the case of the petitioners is distinguishable from the case of Hira Lal and others, which was decided on 15111985.
She has also not been able to show as to how the Government can deny the benefit of selection grade to the petitioners when other similarly situated persons have been given that benefit.
7.
In the absence of any explanation by the respondents and their failure to give selection grade to the petitioners even though their juniors have been given that benefit, there remains little doubt that the petitioners have been discriminated and their right of equality before law has been violated.
8.
Consequently, the writ petition is allowed.
The respondents are directed to give benefit of selection grade to the petitioners in accordance with the judgment of this Court dated 15111985 (Single Bench) and 1511990 in Letters Patent Appeal No.135 of 1986.
Necessary action should be taken by the respondents for grant of selection grade to the petitioners within a period of four months, failing which the respondents shall have to pay interest to the petitioners at the rate of 18 per cent per annum on the amount of arrears which may become payable to the petitioners in accordance with this order.
