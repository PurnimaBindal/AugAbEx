TS Doabia, J - Counsels heard.
2.
With the consent of the parties, the petitions are taken up for disposal.
3.
This order shall dispose of the writ petitions mentioned above.
These petitions have come to be filed in this court under following circumstances:  4.
All the writ petitioners in the above mentioned writ petitions were working as Naib Tehsildars.
They were seeking promotion to the post of Tehsildars.
With a view to qualify the promotion to the post of Tehsildar, they had to pass a departmental examination.
The requirement of law as per SRO 571/79 was that a candidate must obtain 50% marks in the departmental examination.
The further fact is that a departmental examination was held.
The petitioners in writ petitions 273/1983, 724/1983 and 429/94 were not able to have the requisite grading.
They were, however, made eligible as later on SRO 571/79 came to be amended.
This amendment was enforced vide SRO 57/82.
This was issued on 26th Feb, 82.
The requirement of obtaining 50% marks was altered.
This was fixed at 45%.
Not only the requirement was brought down to 45% but this was sought to be given retrospective effect w.e.f.
01.06.1981.
It is this reduction of eligibility criteria which enabled the writ petitioners of the aforementioned three petitions to compete with those candidates who obtained more than 50% marks, is the contentious issue which has been raised in this writ petition.
To sum up the facts are as under:  i) That all the petitioners were working as Naib Tehsildars; ii) That they had to qualify the examination in terms of SRO 571/79 known as `Jammu and Kashmir Departmental Examination (Tehsildar) Rules, 1979'; iii) That the qualifying marks as fixed in R 5(4) were 50%; iv) That later on vide SRO 57/82 issued on 26th Feb, 82, Rule 5(4) was amended and instead of 50%, the pass marks were fixed at 45%; v) That the SRO issued on 26th Feb, '98 was given retrospective effect to from Ist of June, 1981.
5.
The learned counsel appearing in writ petition 623/82 submits that the course adopted by the respondent-authorities is not in accordance with the law.
According to the petitioner, a right had vested in the petitioner and this could not be taken away by making amendment.
What is sought to be argued is that the State Government could amend the Rules by giving prospective effect and not by giving retrospective effect.
6.
Thus, the only question which is required to be gone into in this petition is as to whether the State Government was well within its rights to bring about an amendment in 1979 Rules whereby the grade for passing the examination could be brought down from 50% to 45% and as to whether this could be given a retrospective effect.
7.
I am of the opinion that no fault can be found with the course adopted by the State Government.
8.
It is settled law that the Government while framing the Rules under Article 309 of the Constitution of India, exercises legislative powers.
This power is thus exercised in the same manner as is exercised by State Legislature while enacting a Law.
This could well be done by the Rule-making Authority by exercising powers under Article 309 of the Constitution of India.
Independently of what has been stated above, the amendment giving retrospective effect has been held to be valid in a number of judicial pronouncements.
One such decision is BS Vadera v. Union of India, AIR 1969 SC 118.
The aforementioned decision was noticed by this Court in Triloki Nath Khosa and others v. The State of J&K, 1971 LAB.
IC 1565 (V 4 C 3883).
Paras 11,12 & 13 are relevant and be noticed as under:  Para 11.
In BS Vadera v. Union of India, AIR 11969 SC 118, their Lordships of the Supreme Court while approving the decision of the Full Bench of the Allahabad High Court in AIR 1962 All 328 (FB) (supra) held that the Governor under the proviso the Article 309 of the Constitution of India (which corresponds to the proviso to Section 124 of the Constitution of Jammu and Kashmir) could frame a rule having a prospective or retrospective operation and the rule so made has to be given full effect.
Para 12.
Following the abovementioned dictum of their Lordships of the Supreme Court in AIR 1969 SC 118 (supra) a Full Bench of the Patna High Court in Madan Mohan Parshad v. Govt. of Bihar, AIR 1970 Pat 432 (FB) held that the power of the authority under the proviso to Art. 309 can be exercised both prospectively and retrospectively in the absence of appropriate legislation.
Para 13.
In Man Mohan Kaushik v. State of Rajasthan, AIR 1971 Raj 60, Tyagi J relying on the observations made by the Supreme Court in AIR 1969 SC 118 (supra) said:-  "The rule-making authority under Article 309 of the Constitution can make the rules, which may be brought into operation with retrospective effect and the employee cannot claim, that rights vested in him prior to coming into force of certain rules should be allowed to be enjoyed by him in spite of the rules having come into operation with retrospective effect.
In view of this state of law, the petitioners cannot claim that their seniority must now be determined in accordance with the unamended proviso to Rule 33, which then stood when they were appointed as members of the Rajasthan Police Service.
If the rule-making authority has a power to amend the rules with retrospective effect then it would be deemed that the proviso (vi) to Rule 33, Rajasthan Police Service Rules (1954) was in existence at the time when the petitioners had entered into police service."
Keeping in view the above-mentioned pronouncements I have no manner of doubt that the Governor can under the proviso the Section 124 of the Constitution of Jammu and Kashmir amend the Service Rules and give them prospective as well as retrospective effect.
9.
In view of the judicial pronouncement, it cannot be said that the State Government acted contrary to any canons dealing with powers of Rule Making Authority in making the amendment in the rules by which retrospective effect was given.
10.
There is another aspect of the matter.
Only a vested right is protected from retrospective operation of a Rule Making Authority.
In the present case, merely because the petitioners passed the examination, it cannot be said that any right had come to vest in them.
A right would vest in them when they were actually given appointment.
The fact that some candidates had passed the examination, would not confer any vested right on them.
Reliance in this regard has been rightly placed in AIR 1991 SC 1612.
The relevant para is as under:-  "It is not correct to say that if a number of vacancies are notified for appointment and adequate number of candidates are found fit, the successful candidates acquire an indefeasible right to be appointed which cannot be legitimately denied."
11.
In view of the above, there is no merit in the writ petition 623/82.
This petition is dismissed.
So far as remaining writ petitions are concerned i.e. SWPs No. 724/83, 273/83 and 429/94, these petitions are allowed.
It is accordingly held that the State Government was well within its rights to amend the Rules.
Whatever benefits which are likely to ensue on account of aforementioned amendment by now given effect to in the case of above writ petitioners.
12.
The petitions are accordingly disposed of.
Petition dismissed.
