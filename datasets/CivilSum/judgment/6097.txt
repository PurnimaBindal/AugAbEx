A. K. Pattanaik, J - The Petitioner was appointed as Lecturer in English with effect from 21-11-1983 in Khaira College, Khaira in the district of Balasore and the petitioner joined as Lecturer in English in the said college on 21-11-1983.
The College became eligible to receive grant-in-aid with effect from academic session 1985-86.
The Principal of Khaira College submitted the records of the college for verification of staff of the college for creation of additional posts including that of the petitioner by letter dated 22-7-1992 but the Director of Higher Education did not pass any order.
The petitioner filed a Writ Petition (O.J.C.
No. 2251 of 1989) which was disposed of in terms of the judgment of this Court in O.J.C.
Nos. 358 and 359 of 1985.
Thereafter, the Director of Higher Education rejected the proposal for approval of the appointment of the petitioner on the ground that he had not secured 54 per cent in MA in English which was the minimum marks for being appointed as Lecturer.
The petitioner again moved this Court in O.J.C.
No. 6796 of 1995 which was disposed of with a direction to the Director to consider the proposal in the light of the judgment dated 5-2-1992 in O.J.C.
No. 2255 of 1989 (Laxmikanta Padhi v. State of Orissa).
Thereafter, the Director of Higher Education passed orders on 24-8-1996 holding that the petitioner was not entitled to approval of his appointment as the third post of English Lecturer held by him was not admissible to the college on the basis of the workload for +2 classes.
Aggrieved by the said order, the petitioner filed a Writ Petition (O.J.C.
No. 13371 of 1996) and on 28-10-1998, this Court disposed of the said writ petition by calling upon the Director of Higher Education, Orissa, to re-consider the matter for which the Principal of the college will submit fresh materials justifying approval of a third post in English in the college within two weeks and on receipt of the said particulars, the Director will act in accordance with the circulars and orders referred to in paragraph 4 of the said order dated 28-10-1998 of the Court.
Para 4 of the said order dated 28-1-1998 in O.J.C.
No. 13371 of 1996 is quoted hereinbelow :  "4.
It may be noted that Government of Orissa in Education and Youth Services Department Letter No. 37172/EYS dated 8-8-1983 has fixed the yardstick for teaching posts in Government and non-Government colleges of the State.
The said yardstick would govern the case of the petitioner.
One of the conditions is that the workload for a teacher should be 25 periods a week for 6 working days and if the total workload computed on the aforesaid basis exceeds any multiple of 25 per week by a fraction up to 3, then no additional post will be sanctioned for the fractional excess.
This test is with regard to general classes.
So far as tutorial classes are concerned, we may refer to Director's Memo No. 12389, dated 11-3-1981 which prescribes, so far as English is concerned, two tutorial periods per group, each group consisting of 16 students (now enhanced to 24 students).
According to learned counsel, while making calculation on the basis of the particulars submitted by the Principal as per Annexure-3 an arithmetical mistake has crept in, inasmuch as instead of taking "two" tutorial classes for each group, only 'one' tutorial class was taken into account and on account of this mistake the final decision taken in the matter is vitiated."
Thereafter, the Director of Higher Education, Orissa, re-considered the matter and again rejected the claim of the petitioner by order dated 6-1-1999 on the grounds that the workload of the college did not justify the post of the petitioner and that the petitioner did not possess the requisite qualification to hold the post.
Aggrieved by the said order dated 6-1-1999, the petitioner has filed this writ petition with a prayer to quash the same and for a direction to the opposite parties to approve the appointment of the petitioner with effect from 21-11-1983 and to release the salary components as per the prevailing grant-in-aid principles.
2.
Mr. B Routray, learned counsel for the petitioner, submitted that it will be clear from the impugned order that the Director of Higher Education, Orissa, has taken into consideration the Government Letter No. 37172/EYS, dated 8-8-1983 in which yardstick for teaching posts in Government and non-Government colleges with effect from the session 1983-84 has been laid down.
But, as per the said Government latter dated 8-8-1983, the post of the petitioner as a Lecturer in Khaira College was justified.
He referred to the chart enclosed along with the proposal of the Principal of the college dated 14-11-1998 sent to the Deputy Director, NGCI, Directorate of Higher Education, Orissa, to show that if the +3 Ist year Arts Stream is excluded, the total number of classes in the subject per week will work out to 66 and as per the letter dated 8-8-1983 of the Government, 3 Lecturers of English would be justified for the college.
Mr. Routray also placed before us the chart annexed to the counter-affidavit filed on behalf of opposite party No. 2 as Annexure-A/2 to show that there was no difference in the said two charts except that two tutorial classes in the chart enclosed along with the proposal of the Principal submitted to the Directorate of Higher Education, Orissa, have been taken as one tutorial class in the chart annexed to the counter- affidavit as Annexure-A/2.
He submitted that it will be clear from the letter dated 1-5-1984 of the Directorate of Higher Education in Annexure-9 to the writ petition, that two tutorial periods per week for all subjects except Commerce for +2 Course is to be taken and in the letter dated 8-8-1983 each tutorial group was to comprise of 24 students.
Hence, the Principal has taken the correct number of tutorial classes in the proposal.
Mr. Das, Additional Government Advocate for the State, however, relied on the averments in the counter-affidavit filed on behalf of opposite party No. 2 and submitted that the calculation given in Annexure-A/2 in respect of the workload for English subject in the college, was correct.

The result is, whereas as per the assessment of the Principal of the college the number of periods works out of 66, as per the assessment of the Directorate of Higher Education the number of periods works out to 43.
We have perused the letter dated 8-8-1983 of the Government of Orissa in the Education and Youth Services Department, which provides for fixation of yardstick for teaching posts in Government and non-Government colleges and we do not find anywhere in the said letter dated 8-8-1983 that only one tutorial class or period per week for a group of 24 students is to be taken for calculation of workload.
On the other hand, it appears from the letter dated 1-5-1984 of the Directorate of Higher Education in Annexure-9 that two tutorial classes or periods for +2 course in different subjects except in Commerce faculty is to be taken for calculation of workload as on 31-12-1983.
We are thus of the opinion that the workload calculated by the Principal of the College as 66 classes/periods is in accord with the letter dated 8-8-1983 of the Government.
The said letter dated 8-8- 1983 of the Government further provides that the workload for a teacher would be 25 periods a week and when the total workload computed on the aforesaid basis exceeds any multiple of 25 per week by a fraction upto 3 (three), then no additional post will be sanctioned for the fractional excess meaning thereby that if the workload computed exceeds any multiple of 25 per week by a fraction more than 3 (three), an additional post will be sanctioned for the fractional excess.
A total of 66 (sixty six) periods a week for English will mean 2 (two) Lecturers in English taking 25 periods per week per Lecturer a fraction of 16 justifying a third post of Lecturer in English in the College.
The reason given in the impugned order that the workload did not justify the third post of Lecturer in English held by the petitioner in the College is, therefore, contrary to the yardstick in the letter dated 8-8-1983 of the Government.
4.
The other reason given in the impugned order is that the petitioner was not eligible for being appointed as Lecturer in English as he had secured only 46 per cent of marks in MA in English whereas the minimum qualification for being appointed as Lecturer in English in affiliated colleges was 54 per cent at the relevant time.
5.
Mr. Routray, learned counsel for the petitioner vehemently argued that in the case of Ghana Shyam Jena v. State of Orissa (O.J.C.
No. 2560 of 1990 decided on 8-3-1991), this Court has already held that the qualification of 54 per cent as prescribed in the resolution dated 5th of September, 1978 of the Government of Orissa, Education and Youth Services Department could be relaxed by the Selection Committee in the circumstances indicated in the said resolution and that if the Governing Body of the college had appointed the candidate even though he did not have the requisite marks of 54 per cent in MA in the concerned subject, it should be presumed that Selection Committee and the Governing Body had relaxed the said minimum qualification and had appointed the candidate in question.
Mr. Routray pointed out that this view has also been taken by a Division Bench of this Court in case of Laxmikanta Padhi v. State of Orissa (O.J.C.
No. 2255 of 1989, decided on 5-2-1992).
Mr. Routray argued that the petitioner's case is covered by the said two Division Bench judgments of this Court in the case of Ghana Shyam Jena and in the case of Laxmikanta Padhi (supra).
Mr. Das, learned Additional Government Advocate, on the other hand, submitted that the petitioner did not have the qualification as prescribed in the resolution dated the 13th of September, 1983 of the Government of Orissa, Education and Youth Services Department, which provided that a candidate not holdIng an M Phil.
degree should possess a higher second class Master's degree, i.e. 54 per cent of marks for recruitment as Lecturer in affiliated Colleges in the State of Orissa.
To a query made by the Court as to how the said minimum qualifying mark of 54 per cent was relevant for the purpose of deciding as to whether the appointment of the petitioner and the post held by the petitioner should be approved for the purpose of grant-in-aid, Mr. Das, learned Additional Government Advocate referred to the provisions of Clause 9-E of the Grant-in-aid Order, 1994 which provides that a post would be deemed to have completed the qualifying period if it is filled up at all times during the qualifying period by a person who is qualified to hold such a post.
6.
We fail to see as to how the provisions of the Grant-in-aid Order, 1994 would apply to the case of the petitioner.
The Grant-in-aid Order, 1994 was made under Section 7-C of the Orissa Education Act which was introduced with effect from 1-6-1994 and cannot possibly be applied retrospectively to the case of the petitioner.
The petitioner was appointed in November, 1983 and the proposal for his approval and grant-in-aid was submitted on 22-7-1992 when the principles of grant-in-aid laid down in the Circular No. 11131, dated 17-3- 1983 of the Government of Orissa, Education and Youth Services Department were in force.
The said Circular No. 11131, dated 17-3-1983 is extracted hereinbelow :  "GOVERNMENT OF ORISSA EDUCATION AND YS DEPARTMENT No. VE/G5/82, 11131/EYS 17-3-1983 From : Sri D D Jena, O.E.S.
(I) Deputy Secretary to Government  To  The Director of Public Instruction (HE) Orissa, Bhubaneswar.
Sub.
:- Direct payment of full salary cost to the staff of non-Government Colleges in the State maturing for grant.
Sir, In continuation of G O No. 19462-EYS, dated 27-5-1978, 1 am directed to say that direct payment of full salary cost of the teaching and non-teaching staff of non-Government colleges as admissible according to the prescribed yardsticks shall be payable to such colleges which have been accorded Government concurrence prior to the academic session 1979-80.
The colleges who have received Government concurrence with effect from the academic session 1979-80 or thereafter, until further orders shall be governed by the new grant-in-aid principles as detailed below :- (i) Colleges in advanced areas will receive 1/3rd of the deficit as grant-in- aid after 5 years from the date of Government concurrence (recognition), 2/3rd after 7 years and full deficit after 9 years.
(ii) In backward areas these periods will be 3, 5 and 7 years respectively.
In the context the criteria for determining an area to be backward will be the same as is applicable to High Schools approved by Government.
For the purpose of computing qualifying of 5 years for a college to receive grant-in-aid, the date with effect from which the college has been accorded Government concurrence shall be taken into account.
Yours faithfully,  Sd/- D. D. JENA 17-3-1983  Deputy Secretary to Government" 7.
A bare reading of the aforesaid circular would show that there is no provision therein which provides that the person holding the post must have been qualified to be appointed and to hold the post for the purpose of receiving grant-in-aid.
That apart, as has been held by this Court in the case of Ghana Shyam Jena and in the case of Laxmikanta Padhi (supra), even if the petitioner did not have the minimum marks of 54 per cent at the time of his appointment, the Governing Body of the College having appointed him way back in November, 1983, at this stage the authorities cannot conclude that the petitioner was not qualified to be appointed in the post of Lecturer in English and was thus not eligible to grant-in-aid.
On the contrary, the appointment of the petitioner by the Governing Body has to be treated as a relaxation of his qualification for the purpose of appointment as a Lecturer.
8.
For the aforesaid reasons, we allow the writ petition, quash the impugned order dated 6-1-1999 of the Director of Higher Education, Orissa in Annexure-6 to the writ petition and direct the opposite parties to approve the appointment of the petitioner with effect from 21-11-1983 and allow him grant-in-aid on completion of five years, seven years and nine years at one- third, two-third and full components respectively.
This direction will be complied with within a period of three months from the date of receipt of a certified copy of this order by the Commissioner-cum-Secretary to the Government of Orissa, Higher Education Department and the Director of Higher Education, Orissa.
In the circumstances of the case, the parties are to bear their own costs.
Petition allowed.
