Harjit Singh Bedi, J - The petitionr, who belongs to the Scheduled Castes applied for admission to the Multi Purpose Health Worker (Male) course, to be conducted at Kharar, District Rupnagar, against one of a total number of sixty seats.
On a scrutiny of the petitioner's application he was found to have the requisite qualification and vide letter dated January 22, 1990, he was called for interview to be held on February 6, 1998.
The petitioner, however, received another letter dated February 3, 1998 informing him that the interview would now be held on February 20, 1998.
The petitioner appeared for interview on the stipulated date but was informed that it had been postponed once again.
He was subsequently informed through letter dated March 12, 1998, Annexure P-2 to the petition, that the interview would now be held on March 27, 1998.
The petitioner appeared before the interview Board on the said date along with the relevant documents and was told that the information with regard to his merit would be intimated later.
The petitioner received a letter dated April 29, 1998, Annexure P-3 to the writ petition, on May 20, 1998 informing him that he had been selected for the course and that he was called upon to join the course on May 11, 1998 by 400 pm On receiving the letter, the petitioner went post-haste and met the Principal and informed him that the letter dated April 29, 1998 had been received by him on May 20, 1998 and as such, he had no knowledge of the fact that he had been asked to join the course on May 11, 1998.
The petitioner's request was, however, not entertained as a presumption had been drawn as per Annexure P-3 that as he had not appeared in time, he did not want to take admission and as such a candidate from the waiting list, one Jaspal Kaler, another Scheduled Caste Category Candidate, who was lower in merit, had been granted admission on May 28, 1998.
2.
The present writ petition came up for preliminary hearing on May 26, 1998 and notice of motion was issued and accepted by the State counsel and an interim order was passed to the effect that one seat in the Course, if available, would be kept vacant for the petitioner till further orders.
The case was thereafter adjourned repeatedly and admitted to a final hearing on October 14, 1998 and ordered to be listed for today.
3.
A reply has been filed on behalf of the respondents and the broad facts as pleaded have not been denied.
It has been stated that the letter dated April 29, 1998, Annexure P-3, had been despatched well within time and as the petitioner had not appeared for joining the course on May 11, 1998, admission could not be granted to him thereafter.
The fact that the petitioner had approached the Principal on May 21, 1998 and that Jaspal Kaler, who was lower in merit, had been granted admission, has not been denied but it has been explained that as the petitioner had not joined the course on May 11, 1998, the seat had been offered to him as per Annexure P-3 and that he had in consequence thereto joined the course on May 29, 1998.
4.
Mr. RS Bajaj, the learned counsel for the petitioner, has raised only one argument before me.
He has pointed out that the letter dated April 29, 1998, Annexure P-3, had been received by the petitioner on May 20, 1998, as would be evident from Annexure P-4 dated May 20, 1998, a certificate issued by the Sub Post Master of Village Rurka Kalan and as he had approached the respondents on May 21, 1998, one seat, reserved for the Scheduled Castes was yet available as Jaspal Kaler had been admitted against that seat on May 29, 1998 and in this situation, the petitioner could be denied his claim more particularly in the light of the judgment in V Ramesh v. Convenor, EAMCET- 1995, Jawahar Lal Nehru Technological University, Hyderabad, AIR 1997 Andhra Pradesh 79 and Tejmohan Singh v. Panjab University, 1992(1) SCT 451.
5.
Mr. Anil Sharma, the learned Deputy Advocate General, Punjab, appearing for the respondents, has, however, pointed out that it was the petitioner's responsibility to join the course on May 11, 1998 and as he had not done so, the respondents had absolutely no option but to grant admission to the wait listed candidate, namely, Jaspal Kaler.
He has also urged that it was not clear from the facts on record as to when the communication, Annexure P-3, had been received by the petitioner and as disputed questions of fact had been raised in the petition, no relief could be granted to him.
He further urged that the Multi Purpose Health Worker Course was of 18 months duration and as the course had started on May 15, 1998 and the trainees had already undergone field training the petitioner could not now be accommodated.
6.
I have heard the learned counsel for the parties and have gone through the record very carefully.
It is evident from Ananexure P-4 that the registered letter Annexure P-3 had been received by the petitioner on May 20, 1998, though it had been despatched on May 1, 1998.
No fault, therefore, lay with the petitioner.
It bears notice that he had admittedly approached respondent No. 3 on May 21, 1998 when one seat for the Scheduled Caste Category Candidate was yet available as Jaspal Kaler had been granted admission on May 28, 1998.
It was in this situation that this Court had, in Tejmohan's case (supra), observed that if a candidate had approached the respondent authority after the completion of the admission process, a question could be raised as to his admission at that stage but if the admission process was still going on and candidate, who was higher in merit had appeared for interview though belatedly, his candidature could not be ignored on the ground that he had not appeared for the interview on the date earlier fixed.
As already mentioned above, it is the admitted position that the seat which the petitioners would have occupied had been given to Jaspal Kaler on May 29, 1998.
To my mind, the petitioner was entitled to the admission against that seat as he had approached the authority on May 21, 1998.
7.
Mr. Sharma, the learned Deputy Advocate General, Haryana, has informed me that one seat though in the general category, still remains vacant as one of the candidates has left the Course midway.
It is, therefore, evident that the petitioner can be admitted to the course without difficulty.
8.
This petition is accordingly allowed and a direction is issued to the respondents to give admission to the petitioner forthwith in the Multi Purpose Health Worker (Male) Course being run at Kharar.
It is further directed that the petitioner's lectures will be computed from the date when he joins the Course and that the respondents shall make arrangements so that he gets his field training in time.
There shall be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
