Jayanta Kumar Biswas, J - The petitioner is questioning validity of the West Bengal Schools (Recruitment of Non-teaching Staff) Rules, 2005 on the ground that the rules had been made and published even before the enabling law made by the West Bengal Schools (Control of Expenditure) Act, 2005, Section 22 came into force.
2.
Counsel says that provisions of Section 22 came into force on December 27th, 2005, as will appear from the notification published in the official gazette.
He further says that the rules were directed to come into force on December 26th, 2005.
His contention is that the state government made the rules when it possessed no power to make them.
3.
Mr. Saikat Banerjee present in Court (he is one of the State government counsel), appears for assisting the Court, since none appears for the state.
I hope the government pleader shall regularize his appointment in the matter.
He gives me a list of dates showing that the rules were actually published in the official gazette on December 27th, 2005.
4.
He is absolutely right in his submission that the state government was empowered to frame and notify the rules the moment provisions of Section 22 came into force.
Those provisions came into force also on December 27th, 2005.
It seems to me that the petitioner challenged the validity of the rules without ascertaining the correct position.
5.
He threw the challenge on the basis of the date mentioned by the state government in the relevant memorandum.
It was dated December 26th, 2005.
That date is irrelevant, since the rules were to come into force only after they were published in the official gazette.
I therefore do not find any merit in the challenge thrown by the petitioner.
6.
Counsel argues that as will appear from the order of the division bench dated September 21st, 2006 made in MAT 3778 of 2006, their Lordships observed that the 2005 recruitment rules needed examination for ascertaining whether they would offend provisions in arts.
14 and 16 of the Constitution.
In my opinion, the observations made by their Lordships are of no assistance in so far as this case is concerned.
7.
I have already delivered judgment dated July 18th, 2006 in Smt. Smritikana Roy (Halder) v. State of West Bengal & Ors, WP No. 16785 (W) of 2006, holding that the provisions in the recruitment rules did not offend provisions in arts.
14 and 16 of the Constitution.
For that I relied on the apex Court decision in Union of India v. N Hargopal and Ors, (1987)3 SCC 308.
In that judgment I also considered the subsequent apex Court decisions and the five judge bench decision of this Court in Rabindra Nath Mahata v. State of West Bengal and others, 2005(3) CHN 337.
8.
For these reasons the writ petition is dismissed.
There shall be no order for costs in it.
9.
Urgent certified xerox copy of this order, if applied for, shall be supplied to the parties within three days from the date of receipt of the file by the Section concerned.
Petition dismissed.
