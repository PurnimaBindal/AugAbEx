VK Jhanji, J - Both the writ petition No. 2184 of 1987 and No. 3031 of 1987 have been directed against the Award dated 5111986 of the Labour Court, Rohtak, whereby it has been held that termination of the services of the workman was illegal and unlawful.
The management was directed to pay a sum of Rs. 45,000/- as compensation in lieu of reinstatement.
Civil Writ Petition No. 2184 of 1987 has been filed by the Management whereas Civil Writ Petition No. 3031 of 1987 has been filed by the workman.
When Civil Writ Petition No. 2184 of 1987 came up for motion hearing, the only challenge made against the Award was that the petitioner bank is registered as a commercial establishment under the Punjab Shops and Commercial Establishments Act, 1958 (hereinafter referred to as the "1958 Act") and thus the workman was not entitled to invoke the jurisdiction of the Labour Court.
In any case, he could not be awarded back wages as envisaged by the Industrial Disputes Act and his only remedy was to make a grievance under the Act, whereby he could be awarded compensation for two months as provided under Section 22 of the 1958 Act.
2.
The workman in his writ petition has claimed that the Labour Court having found that the order of termination being illegal, he was entitled to all the back wages instead of Rs. 45,000/- which was awarded by the Labour Court as compensation in lieu of reinstatement.
3.
Learned counsel for the management has frankly conceded that the challenge to the locus standi of the workman to invoke jurisdiction of the Labour Court is no more available in view of the decision of the Supreme Court in OP Bhandari v. India Tourism Development Corporation Limited, A.I.R. 1987 SC 111.
4.
The workman had been employed as Chowkidar by the Bank and was allotted a room in the bank premises for watch and ward duties of the guest home located in the same building.
The case of the workman is that on the night intervening 15/16 July, 1982, Shri Desh Bandhu Gupta, a friend of Shri Deputy Parshad who was administrator of the Bank at that time, entered the bank in a drunken condition and started talking with his wife in an insulting manner which attracted his attention and in the meantime Shri Deputy Parshad also reached there and they both gave beatings to him and the commotion so caused attracted his two brothers.
Shri Desh Bandhu Gupta as well as Shri Deputy Parshad tried to slip away and in the process Shri Desh Bandhu Gupta fell down and received certain injuries.
5.
On the other hand, the case of the management is that on the night intervening 15/16 July, 1982, the workman alongwith his two brothers and three other persons, without any provocation and out of personal vendetta, picked up a quarrel with Shri Deputy Parshad, Administrator of the Bank who was staying in the guest house along with his friend Shri Desh Bandhu Gupta, inflicted multiple injuries upon them regarding which a First Information Report was lodged with the Police Station (City) Sonepat on the following morning.
Shri Deputy Parshad made a written complaint to the Assistant Registrar, Co-operative Societies, Sonepat, who was the Chairman of the Board of Administrators of the Bank and the Board of Administrators, in its meeting held on 24111982 resolved that the workman be ordered to vacate the bank premises and he be also relieved of duties of watch and ward of the guest house.
This order was stated to have been duly served on the workman who refused to abide by the order.
The Board of Administrators in its meeting held on 15.10.1982 authorised the Manager of the Bank of serve charge-sheet upon the workman which was served upon him on 29.10.1982.
After considering the reply dated 9111982 of the workman, the Manager of the Bank submitted his report before the Board of Administrators on 23.11.1982 and after considering the report and the previous record of the workman, the Board resolved to terminate the services of the workman and thus his services were terminated on 24111982.
The workman as well as the management led evidence in support of their case before the Labour Court.
The Labour Court vide impugned order found that the order of termination was patently illegal because the same was passed without holding any enquiry into the allegations of misconduct against the workman.
However the Labour Court awarded a sum of Rs. 30,000/- being the back wages for the last four years from the date of termination at the rate of Rs. 600/- per month.
A sum of Rs. 15,000/- was awarded to the workman as compensation in lieu of reinstatement.
Thus, the management was directed to pay a total sum of Rs. 45,000/- to the workman.
6.
Mr.
US Sahni, Advocate, learned counsel for the workman has contended that once the Labour Court having found that the order of termination of the workman was patently illegal, it acted illegally in not ordering reinstatement in service with full back wages to the workman.
His precise submission is that when the termination of the services of the workman is bad, reinstatement with full back wages is the rule.
In support of his submission, he has relied upon :-  i) Punjab National Bank Ltd v. AIPNEE Federation, AIR 1960 SC 160. ii) Assam Oil Co v. Its Workmen, AIR 1960 SC 1264. iii) Hindustan Tin Works v. Its Employees, AIR 1979 SC 75.
7.
On the other hand, learned counsel for the management has submitted that the workman was not entitled to a sum of Rs. 45,000/- in view of his admission in his statement before the Labour Court that he was drawing a sum of Rs. 340/- per month.
8.
As far as the contention of learned counsel for the workman is concerned there is no dispute about the proposition that once the termination is found to be bad, then the normal rule is reinstatement.
However, in the facts and circumstances of each case, it is open to the Labour Court to consider whether or not reinstatement should be ordered in case of wrongful or illegal termination.
This is always a question of fact depending upon facts and circumstances of each case.
It is not in dispute that the workman was employed as Chowkidar and was put on watch and ward duty which duty is very responsible and in view of the allegations levelled against each other, the Labour Court rightly awarded compensation in lieu of reinstatement.
We do not propose to pronounce on the validity or otherwise of the allegations and counter allegations made by the parties.
It has also come on record that a criminal case under Sections 325/506/149 of Indian Penal Code is pending trial in the Court of Chief Judicial Magistrate, Sonepat.
Relations between the parties appear to have been strained and reinstatement is also not in the interest of the workman.
Therefore, we find no ground to interfere in the discretion exercised by the Labour Court.
9.
When the writ petition preferred by the management was admitted, compensation amount beyond Rs. 25,000/- was stayed by this Court.
The said amount of Rs. 25,000/- has already been paid to the workman.
Taking into consideration that the management has still to pay balance sum of Rs. 20,000/- to the workman which was stayed by this Court in 1987, it would meet the ends of justice if this amount is enhanced by another sum of Rs. 10,000/- over and above the amount which is payable to the workman.
Thus, a total sum of Rs. 30,000/- shall be paid to the workman within a period of two months from today failing which the workman shall be paid interest at the rate of 15 per cent per annum on the said amount till payment.
The workman shall also be entitled to the costs which are quantified at Rs. 2,000/-.
10.
Resultantly, Civil Writ Petition No. 3031 of 1987 of the workman is allowed in the aforesaid terms and Civil Writ Petition No. 2184 of 1987 of the management is dismissed.
Orders accordingly.
