GC Garg, J - The petitioner herein has impugned the order dated January 7, 1986, Annexure P-5 whereby his claim for grant of pay scale of Rs. 200-250 (Central Scale-unrevised) now revised to Rs. 775-1025 (Punjab scale) has been rejected by the respondent.
2.
The petitioner was appointed as a Cook by the respondent vide appointment letter dated May 25, 1982, Annexure P-1 in the pay scale of Rs. 196-3-220/3- 232 plus usual allowances as are sanctioned by the Central Government from time to time, on the terms and conditions enumerated therein including the one that he will be governed by the rules and regulations and such other conditions of service as are applicable and may be framed for the employees of the respondent-Institute from time to time.
Accordingly, the services of the petitioner are governed by the Postgraduate Institute of Medical Education and Research Chandigarh Rules, 1967.
Services of the petitioner were regularised by the respondent by order dated August 26, 1982, Annexure P-2.
The petitioner submitted a representation dated July 5, 1982, Annexure P-3 to the respondent stating therein that all the Cooks employed in the Postgraduate Institute were drawing their salary in the pay scale of Rs. 325- 495 (Punjab scale)/Rs.
200-250 (Central scale) and, therefore, he was also entitled to the said pay scale and that the pay scale of Rs. 196-232 was wrongly given to him.
By another representation dated January 30, 1985, Annexure P-4, the petitioner prayed for grant of pay scale of Rs. 200-250 specifically mentioning therein that there was no pay scale of Rs. 196-232 given to a Cook by the Central Government.
His request for correction of the pay scale was ultimately declined by the respondent by letter dated January 7, 1986, Annexure P-5.
Even after rejection of his claim, the petitioner went on making representations, the last one being dated July 3, 1991, Annexure P-9 and ultimately filed the present petition.
3.
Learned counsel for the petitioner vehemently contended that since the Cooks and other similarly situated employees working in the respondent- Institute and performing the same duties are being paid the pay scale of Rs. 775-1025 (Rs. 200-250 Central Scale), there is no justification to keep the petitioner in the pay scale of Rs. 196-232 especially when he was appointed as a Cook and is now a regular employee of the respondent-Institute.
Learned counsel further submitted that the impugned order has been passed without taking into consideration the statutory Rules framed by the respondent- Institute and the denial of the said pay scale by the respondent amounts to violation of the principle "equal pay for equal work."
4.
The facts themselves are not in dispute here.
However, all that has been explained by the respondent by filing a written statement is that though it is correct that the services of the petitioner are governed by the Rules and Regulations of the respondent-Institute yet the petitioner could not have been given the pay scale of Rs. 200-250 as at the relevant time the post of a Cook against which the petitioner was appointed, was created in the pay scale of Rs. 196-232.
While admitting that the petitioner was performing the duties identical to other Cooks of the Institute, it was clarified by the respondent that the employees who joined the Institute before April 1, 1967 had been given the Punjab Pay Scale whereas those who were appointed after the said date, were drawing the Central Pay Scale.
However, it has been categorically admitted by the respondent that at the time of appointment of the petitioner, the pay scale prescribed in the recruitment rules of the Institute for the post of a Cook was Rs. 200-250.
5.
After considering the submissions of the respective parties and hearing learned counsel for the parties, I am of the view that this petition must succeed.
The petitioner has sought a writ of mandamus to the respondent to give him the pay scale of Rs. 200-250 which has been denied to him because the post against which he was appointed was created in the pay scale of Rs. 196-232.
I do not think that the stand taken by the respondent is well- founded.
The claim of the petitioner for grant of pay scale of Rs. 200-250, as is being given to the other Cooks working in the respondent-Institute, is based on the well recognised principle in service jurisprudence that "equal pay must be paid for equal work."
In the same cadre there cannot be a distinction made to award a lower pay scale to a person simply because the post against which he was appointed, was created in the lower pay scale, especially when the other Cooks are getting the pay scale of Rs. 200-250, now revised to 775-1025 (Punjab Scale).
As a matter of fact the admission of the respondent in the written statement that at the time of appointment of the petitioner, obviously as a Cook, the pay scale prescribed for the post of Cook as per the relevant rules of the Institute was Rs. 200-250, clinches the whole dispute.
As per respondent's own showing, the Cooks appointed after April 1, 1967 by the respondent-Institute are drawing Central Pay Scale.
Admittedly, the petitioner having been appointed on May 25, 1982, was appointed after the aforesaid date.
He, therefore, was clearly entitled to the Central Pay Scale meant for the post of Cook which at the time of appointment of the petitioner was admittedly, Rs. 200-250.
The stand of the respondent for not accepting the petitioner's claim does not appeal to any reason and thus, has to be clothed as discriminatory and is liable to be struck down as violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
Consequently, the order dated January 7, 1986, Annexure P-5 passed by the respondent rejecting the claim of the petitioner is quashed.
For the reasons stated above, the writ petition is allowed and a direction is issued to the respondent to fix the pay of the petitioner in the pay scale of Rs. 200-250 (Central Scale-unrevised) from the date of his appointment and also grant any other corresponding pay scale revised thenceforth.
But since the petitioner after rejection of his claim by the respondent in January 1986, procrastinated to approach the Court, for about five years, the respondent- Institute is directed to pay to him the arrears of pay which might accrue on his being granted the pay scale allowed in this petition, with effect from the date of filing of the present writ petition.
The arrears of pay be paid within four months from today.
No costs.
