GS Singhvi J - The petitioners, who were initially appointed as Clerks on ad hoc basis by the Deputy Commissioner, Amritsar, between 18-1-1972 and 1181980, and who were sub-regularised in terms of the instructions issued by the Government of Punjab vide circular letter dated 29.1.1973] (Annexure P2), have filed this writ petition for quashing the order dated 781992 (Annexure P.5) issued by the Deputy Commissioner, Amritsar, rejecting their claim for assignment of seniority on the basis of total length of service.
2.
Petitioners had earlier filed C.W.P.
No. 2088 of 1992 for which a direction was given by the Court on 11.5.1992 that the representations made to the petitioners may be decided within a period of three months.
After hearing the petitioners on 23.7.1992 the Deputy Commissioner has passed the impugned order and rejected the representations of the petitioners.
3.
In assailing the impugned order, learned counsel for the petitioners as argued that once they have been regularised in service, the petitioners, have acquired a legal right to be assigned seniority on the basis of their total length of service.
Learned counsel argued that all the petitioners had been appointed in the service by a competent authority after it had called the names of the petitioners from the Employment Exchange and, therefore, the initial appointments of the petitioners cannot be treated as against the rule.
Learned counsel argued that having been appointed in accordance with the provision of the rules, the petitioners have acquired a legal as well as constitutional right to have their seniority determined on the basis of total length of service.
Learned counsel relied on a decision of this Court in Malook Singh and others v. The State of Punjab and others, 1992(2) SCT 133 (P&H) and Direct Recruit Class II Engineering Officers, Association v. State of Maharashtra and others, (1990)2 SCC 715.
4.
While opposing the writ petition, respondents have stated that the services of the petitioners had been regularised in terms of the Government letter dated 29.1.1973 and in that very letter it has been made clear that the petitioners will not be assigned seniority on the basis of their ad hoc service.
Respondents have pleaded that under Rule 11 of the Punjab District Services Class III Rules, 1976, the seniority inter se of members of service is required to be determined by their continuous appointment to the cadre of the service and since the petitioners became the members of the service only on regularisation of their service they cannot claim benefit of ad hoc service for the purpose of determination of their seniority.
5.
We have heard the learned counsel for the parties and carefully perused the record of the case.
It is an admitted fact that all the petitioners had been initially appointed on ad hoc basis.
None of them had been selected by a competent selecting body.
Persons who have been placed senior to the petitioners were recruited as Clerks on the recommendations of the Subordinate Services Selection Board.
Normally the petitioners would have continued in service as ad hoc employees.
However, in view of the instructions issued by the Government of Punjab vide Annexure P2 dated 29.1.1973, the petitioners became entitled to be regularised in service and in accordance with these instructions the services of the petitioners have been regularised.
Some of the petitioners have been regularised in service as late as in the year 1991.
A look at Annexure P.1 shows that some others were regularised from 1977, 1980 and 1991.
It can thus be said that the petitioners have become members of the service between 1977 and 1991.
Till the date of regularisation of their service the petitioners did not even become members of service.
Therefore, the claim made by the petitioners that they should be placed above those who have been appointed in service after regular selection by a competent body cannot but be held to be misconceived.
A look at paragraph 2(b) of the circular letter dated 29.1.1973, on the basis of which the petitioners got the benefit of regularisation of their service, itself shows that the Government had made it clear to the petitioners that the service rendered by them as ad hoc appointees shall not be taken into account for the purposes of seniority except to determine the inter se seniority amongst the ad hoc employees.
The petitioners accepted these conditions while taking advantage of the circular letter issued by the Government.
Therefore, they cannot now turn round and claim that they should be given the benefit of their ad hoc service for the purpose of determination of their seniority.
6.
In C.W.P.
No. 9200 of 1993, Gurmail Singh and others v. The State of Punjab and others, 1992(3) SCT 335 (P&H) decided on 2171991 this Court has examined in detail the claim of ad hoc employees to be assigned seniority on the basis of total length of service and has negatived it.
In its order dated 2171994 the court held, that persons who are appointed on ad hoc basis and who are subsequently regularised in service cannot make a claim for assignment of seniority on the basis of total length of service.
In that judgment, this Court has taken note of the decision of the Supreme court in Direct Recruit Class II Engineering Officers Association's case (supra) Masood Akhtar Khan and others v. State of Madhya Pradesh and others, 1990(4) SCC 24; Keshav Chandra Joshi and others v. Union of India and others, 1990 Lab IC 216; Dr. MA Haque and others v. Union of India and others, 1993(2) SCT 618 (SC) : JT 1993(2) SC 265; The Excise Commissioner, Karnataka and another v. V Sreekanta, 1993(3) SCT 186 (SC) : JT 1993(2) SC 384; State of West Bengal and others v. Aghoye Nath Dev and others, 1993(2) SCT 734 (SC) : JT 1993(2) SC 598; Sohan Lal v. State of Haryana and another, 1992(3) SCT 424 (P&H) and Malook Singh and others v. The State of Punjab and others, 1992(2) SCT 133 (P&H).
The Division Bench has held that the decision of the Supreme Court in Direct Recruit Class I Engineering Officers Association's case (supra) has been explained in the subsequent judgments of the Supreme Court and in view of the subsequent decision of the apex Court the principle laid down in Malook Singh's case (supra), namely, that ad hoc appointees are entitled to seniority on the basis of total length of service, cannot be treated as correct.
The Court has also held that in view of the conditions incorporated in the letter dated 29.1.1973, ad hoc appointees cannot claim benefit of seniority on the basis of total length of service.
In our considered opinion, the claim of the petitioners for assignment of seniority on the basis of total length of service does not merit acceptance.
Judgment in Gurmail Singh's case (supra) is fully applicable to the facts of this case.
Consequently, the writ petition fails and it is hereby dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
