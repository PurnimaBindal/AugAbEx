Jawahar Lal Gupta, J - The petitioner, a Sub Inspector of Police, is aggrieved by the action of the respondents in not promoting him to the rank of Inspector while persons junior to him viz. respondent Nos. 3 to 5 were promoted vide orders dated March 16, 1989.
His representation having borne no fruitful results, he has approached this Court through the present writ petition.
2.
The petitioner was recruited as a Constable on April 4, 1963.
He was promoted as Head Constable in the year 1971 and as Assistant Sub Inspector in the year 1976.
He was promoted as Sub Inspector in 1984.
During his career, the petitioner claims to have been awarded 24 commendation certificates with cash awards.
Two of the Class-I commendation certificates awarded to him in the years 1985 and 1986 carried cash awards of Rs. 500/- and Rs. 5000/- respectively.
The certificate at Annexure P-1 was awarded to the petitioner on January 15, 1985 in recognition of liquidation of one dangerous extremist.
The certificate at Annexure P-2 was awarded to the petitioner on November 26, 1986 with a cash award of Rs. 5000/- for showing extraordinary devotion to duty.
The petitioner had passed the Upper School Course in the term ending August 1983.
In spite of the fact that respondent Nos. 3 to 5 were junior to the.
petitioner, they were promoted as Inspectors vide order dated March 16, 1989 while the petitioner's claim was ignored.
The petitioner has further averred that his supersession was totally illegal as he had not been conveyed any adverse report ever.
The petitioner had filed a representation to the Director General-cum-Inspector General of Police on March 27, 1989 vide Annexure P-7.
When nothing was heard in reply, he filed the present petition challenging the action of the respondents in ignoring his claim for promotion to the rank of Inspector.
3.
A written statement was filed on behalf of respondent Nos. 1 and 2 in which it has been averred that the petitioner's case was duly considered for promotion to the rank of Inspector but he was not found suitable for promotion in view of the adverse remarks conveyed to him, with regard to his working for the period from April 1, 1985 to August 17, 1985, July 1, 1986 to September 30, 1986 and July 1, 1986 to March 31, 1987.
In paragraph 8 of the written statement it has been averred that vide TPM dated March 16, 1989, 41 Sub Inspectors were approved and allotted to various Ranges for promotion to the rank of Inspector.
It has been further averred that list was framed on March 8, 1989.
The petitioner's name was also considered along with his juniors but his case was kept pending as the record of the petitioner was not complete.
The petitioner's claim, it is alleged, was again considered on November 30, 1989 and he was ignored for list F due to the above-mentioned adverse remarks.
On this basis, it has been alleged that there was no violation of any law.
The petitioner filed a replication.
It has been averred that in the report for the period from April 1, 1985 to August 17, 1985 it has been reported that the petitioner was a "brave officer but his investigation capability needed improvement."
These remarks, the petitioner submits, were communicated as an advice.
As for the remarks for the period from July 1, 1986 to September 30, 1986 and July 1, 1986 to March 31, 1987, it has been averred that the remarks were communicated to the petitioner only on December 20, 1989 after an inordinate delay of about two years and nine months vide confidential Memo, dated November 18, 1989.
The petitioner made a representation against these remarks promptly within two weeks on January 12, 1990.
Since the remarks had been conveyed after an inordinately long delay, the petitioner claims that these could have not been taken into consideration while considering his case for promotion.
A rejoinder was filed in which it was stated that the remarks for the period from July 1, 1986 to September 30, 1986 were contained in the remarks for the period from July 1, 1986 to March 31, 1987 and consequently the remarks for the three months were not separately communicated to the petitioner.
It has been further averred that the remarks may have been received by the petitioner after some time, but the fact remained that on November 30, 1989, when the case of the petitioner was considered, adverse remarks for the period from April 1, 1985 to August 17, 1985 and July 1, 1986 to March 31, 1987 stood conveyed to him.
On this basis, it has been prayed that the writ petition has no merit and may be dismissed.
4.
The petitioner has thereafter filed Civil Misc.
Application No. 3103 of 1991.
A copy of this Misc.
Application appears to have been delivered in the office of the Advocate General, Punjab, on April 20, 1991.
By this application, the petitioner has produced a copy of the order dated March 25, 1991 by which the adverse remarks for the period from July 1, 1986 to March 31, 1987 have been ordered to be expunged.
The petitioner has also produced a copy of the instructions issued by the Govt. vide letter dated June 7, 1982 on the subject of recording of remarks in the annual confidential reports.
5.
I have heard Mr. SS Bajwa learned counsel for the petitioner and Mr. TS Dhindsa for respondent Nos. 1 to 3.
Mr. Bajwa has contended that the petitioner was fully qualified for promotion to the rank of Inspector.
Upto the date of consideration of his claim viz. March, 1989 and November 30, 1989, no adverse remarks had been communicated to the petitioner.
In any case, the remarks for the period from July 1, 1986 to March 31, 1987 were conveyed to the petitioner only on December 20, 1989.
According to the learned counsel, the petitioner had a right to make representation against the adverse remarks.
However, the remarks could not have been taken into consideration before the representation was decided.
In any case, the learned counsel submits that the remarks having been expunged, the action in superseding the petitioner is vitiated.
6.
Mr. TS Dhindsa, on the other hand, contended that at the time of consideration of the claim of the petitioner the adverse remarks were in existence and could not have been excluded from consideration.
7.
After hearing the learned counsel for the parties, I find that the record of the petitioner has undergone a sea-change after November 30, 1989.
The Deputy Inspector General of Police, Border Range, Amritsar has, inter alia, vide orders dated March 25, 1991 found that "the reporting officer had recorded these adverse remarks due to misunderstanding with biased mind.
He has further found that the adverse remarks were without any substance.
Accordingly, the adverse remarks were expunged.
In view of this changed position, it would be incumbent upon the respondents to consider the petitioner's claim for being brought up on list F with effect from March 8, 1989 when the names of his juniors were included in the said list and for promotion to the rank of Inspector with effect from March 16, 1989 when his juniors were actually promoted.
If on consideration, the petitioner is found suitable for promotion, he will be promoted with effect from the date(s) when persons junior to him were promoted.
He will also be entitled to the refixation of his pay and arrears of salary along with interest at the rate of 12 per cent per annum with effect from the date of his promotion till the arrears are actually paid.
His seniority in the rank of Inspectors will also be accordingly fixed.
The petitioner is also entitled to his costs which are assessed at Rs. 3,000/-.
Petition allowed.
