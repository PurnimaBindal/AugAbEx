RP Sethi, J - On demand being raised, an industrial dispute was referred to the Labour Court for adjudication, which was disposed of vide award Annexure P/3 holding that the workman was entitled to reinstatement with continuity of service and full back wages.
The main grievance of the petitioner herein is that the workman had been granted relief despite the fact that he had not completed 240 days of employment before his services were allegedly terminated.
It is further contended that as the respondent preferred his demand notice after a period of 4-1/2 years from the date of termination, he could not be granted back wages for the period beyond the period for demand notice.
2.
After perusing the documents produced before it, the Labour Court had come to the positive conclusion on facts that the workman had put in service of more than 240 days.
The contention of the representative of the management is that the workman was being appointed for 25 days on various date, he was not entitled to the grant of relief and was rightly rejected by the Labour Court holding it to be an unfair labour practice.
The reinstatement of the respondent-workman was, therefore, legal, valid and justified.
3.
We find force in the submission of Mr. SS Shergill that the workman could not be granted full back wages as he had admittedly not preferred any claim or served a demand notice for a period of 4-1/2 years from the date of his termination, the respondent was entitled to the grant of back wages only from the date of demand notice and not from the date of termination of his service in the instant case.
4.
Accordingly, the writ petition is disposed of by modifying the award to the extent that upon his reinstatement with continuity of service, the respondent-workman would be entitled to back wages only with effect from the date of demand notice, i.e., 27121989.
Ordered accordingly.
