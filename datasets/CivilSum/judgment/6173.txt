GS Singhvi, J - This petition has been placed before us for rehearing in view of the order dated 3181995 passed by the Supreme Court in SLP No. 20281 of 1995 (Haryana Urban Development Authority v. Rajbir Singh).
Counsel for the petitioner as well as counsel for the respondents have been informed but no one has appeared to argue the case.
2.
The petitioner was appointed as Assistant Draftman on daily wages with effect from 24.2.1987 in the newly created Electrical Division of the Haryana Urban Development Authority (for short, HUDA).
The petitioner was posted under respondent No. 3.
He worked till 3151987.
Subsequently he was given daily wage appointments w.e.f 261987, 191987 and 141988.
As on the date of the filing of the writ petition i.e. 691988, the petitioner had completed about one year and six months of service.
The petitioner claimed that after completion of 240 days of service he has acquired a right to be regularised in service and in any case he has become entitled to be paid salary in the regular pay scale.
3.
Notice of the writ petition was ordered to be issued on 7.9.1988 and at the same time, the High Court granted an order of status quo.
The writ petition was finally decided on 1111988 by passing the following order :-  "The learned Advocate General, Haryana, states at the Bar that in the cases which are covered by Division Bench judgment of this court in CWP No. 72 of 1988 (Piara Singh v. State of Haryana) decided on 2691988, the petitioner will be given the relief according to that judgment, subject to the right of the State to appeal to the Supreme Court.
The stand of the learned Advocate General, Haryana is fair.
In view of his statement the writ petition is disposed of in terms of Piara Singh's case (supra).
The petitioner, however, will have costs of the petition which are quantified at Rs.
500/-."
4.
The HUDA appealed against the order dated November 1, 1988 and by its order dated 3181995, the Apex Court has disposed of the SLP in accordance with its decision in State of Haryana v. Piara Singh, 1992(3) SCT 201 (SC) : 1992(4) SCC 118 and has further directed that the directions issued in State of Haryana and others v. Sunita Kumari etc. etc. (SLP(C) No........../90(CC 2402) etc. etc.) to govern the fate of the respondent.
5.
In view of the fact that the judgment of this Court in Piara Singh v. State of Haryana, 1988(4) SLR 739 has been reversed by the Supreme Court, the petitioner is not entitled to get any benefit like regularisation of service on the basis of that decision.
He is also not entitled to any relief in the form of regularisation of service on completion of 240 days of service.
6.
Before us, no rule or order has been placed under which the petitioner can claim regularisation of service and, therefore, on the basis of the pleadings as they stand, no relief can be given to the petitioner.
However, keeping in view the fact that after filing of the writ petition, a number of policy circulars have been issued by the Government for regularisation of service of ad hoc and daily wage employees and such policy circulars have been taken note of by the Supreme Court in State of Haryana v. Piara Singh (supra), we dispose of this writ petition by directing the respondents to consider the case of the petitioner for regularisation of his service in accordance with the policy instructions of which reference has been made in the decision in State of Haryana v. Piara Singh (supra).
If the competent authority finds that the petitioner fulfils the conditions of eligibility under any of the policy circulars, then, his suitability should be adjudged and appropriate order by passed within a period of four months of the submission of a certified copy of this order.
Parties are left to bear their own costs.
