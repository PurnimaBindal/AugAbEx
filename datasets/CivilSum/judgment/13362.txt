Jawahar Lal Gupta, J - The petitioner who was working as an Armed Guard with the Bank of Baroda, claims that he had retired from service in October, 1996 when he had attained the age of superannuation viz. 60 years.
He is, thus, entitled to the grant of retiral benefits.
The Respondent-Bank controverts this claim.
It alleges that the petitioner had tendered his resignation on November 18, 1996 as he wanted to contest election to the Municipal Council.
Having resigned, the petitioner had forfeited his right to the retiral benefits.
Is it so ?
A few facts may be noticed.
2.
The petitioner was born on October 15, 1936.
On July 18, 1969, the petitioner had joined service as an Armed Guard with the Respondent-Bank.
On November 18, 1996, the petitioner requested the Bank to permit him to contest election to the Municipal Council.
He stated that in the event of his election, he "shall resign from the Bank's service forthwith...failing which.... shall be liable to be discharged from the Bank's service/or the Bank shall be free to treat this letter as the letter of resignation ....." On November 27, 1996, the petitioner was informed that he "cannot be granted permission to contest MC elections."
On December 2, 1996, the petitioner requested the Bank to grant him "voluntary retirement with immediate effect."
In this letter, the petitioner also requested that the requirement of "the notice of three months for voluntary retirement may be waived" as he was going to retire on December 31, 1996.
By an undated letter, a copy of which has been produced as Annexure P.5, the petitioner requested that his "resignation may be accepted from 16.11.1996 AN."
By a letter of November 18, 1996, the Bank informed the petitioner that his request for resignation w.e.f.
November 16, 1996 had been accepted subject to his depositing one month's pay and allowances in lieu of notice and clearing of the dues of the Bank.
He was also informed that "in terms of Clause 22 of Bank of Baroda Employees' Pension Regulations, 1995" he "may have to forfeit entire past service and consequently will not qualify for pensionary benefits."
This letter is at Annexure P.6 with the writ petition.
Despite this letter, on January 8, 1997 the Bank informed the petitioner that calculations had been made inter alia with regard to the commutation amount and the fixation of monthly pension.
The petitioner was asked to furnish certain information so that the pension payment order may be forwarded to him.
The details worked out by the Bank were also furnished to the petitioner.
On February 24, 1997, the petitioner represented to the Bank alleging inter alia that he had superannuated on 15101996.
According to Regulation 28, he was entitled to the superannuation pension.
The letter regarding acceptance of resignation w.e.f.
November 16, 1996 was "issued ...... on 4121996 though ante-dated 18111996."
This representation was followed by another request made by letter dated May 18, 1997.
Having failed to get a favourable reply, the petitioner has approached his court through the present writ petition.
3.
The petitioner maintains that he had completed the age of superannuation on October 15, 1996 and that the service of 27 years and three months rendered by him could not be washed away.
He prays that the respondents be directed to grant pensionary benefits alongwith interest.
4.
The respondents contest the petitioner's claim.
A written statement has been filed by Assistant General Manager.
It has been admitted that the petitioner was born on October 15, 1936.
However, the petitioner's claim that he had superannuated on October 15, 1996 has been controverted.
It is alleged that "the date of retirement according to the award applicable to the petitioner was 31121996."
It has been further alleged that before seeking permission to contest the election, the petitioner had "already submitted his resignation on 16.11.1996" vide Annexure R1.
This letter was forwarded to the Assistant General Manager on the same day.
It has also been alleged that even according to the request made by the petitioner, his resignation was due to be accepted on 8.12.1996 when he was duly elected to the Municipal Council.
By the operation of Regulation 22, the petitioner had forfeited his rights to the retiral benefits.
In para 8 of the written statement, it has been averred that "the resignation letter was accepted on 18111996."
With regard to the communication regarding determination of pension etc.
It has been averred that the letter was issued "after 3121996 by the Pensionary Department on the presumption of the petitioner having retired from service without the knowledge of the resignation of the petitioner which was duly accepted and that the petitioner was not entitled to the pensionary benefits under Regulation 22 of the Pension Regulations."
On these premises, the respondents maintain that the petitioner had not attained the age of superannuation on the date he had resigned.
The resignation had been duly accepted.
Thus, by the operation of Regulation 22, he had forfeited his rights to the retiral benefits.
5.
The petitioner has filed a replication.
He has categorically averred that there is no settlement or award whereunder an employee may be continued in service even after attaining the age of superannuation.
The petitioner has averred that no application was made by him prior to 18111996.
The application dated 18111996 was declined on 27111996 (Annexure P.2).
There has not been any written communication between 18111996 and 27111996.
It is wrong and denied that the petitioner submitted his application for resignation on 16.11.1996.
There is fabrication and tampering with the application (Annexure R1).
Petitioner has annexed the application as Annexure P.5.
The respondents have put the date as 16.11.1996 at the bottom of the application which is not there in the application made by the petitioner.
This application was obtained from the petitioner by misrepresentation on 4121996.
Annexure R.2 and R.3 are all ante-dated and were in fact, written on December 4, 1996.
There is a clear fabrication and distortion of the record.
Even the other averments in the writ petition have been reiterated and those in the written statement controverted.
Counsel for the parties have been heard.
6.
Mr. OP Goyal, learned counsel appearing for the petitioner contended that he had attained the age of 60 years on October 14, 1996.
By the operation of the Regulations, the petitioner shall be deemed to have retired on October 31, 1996.
His continuance in service thereafter by the Bank could be in the nature of re-employment.
The petitioner having retired from service shall be entitled to all the retiral benefits.
He further contended that there is no award by any court whereby it may have been directed that an employee shall be kept in service even after attaining the age of superannuation till the end of the year.
Still further, the counsel submitted that the plea of the respondents that the petitioner had submitted his resignation on November 16, 1996, and that it was accepted on November 18, 1996, is totally wrong.
The claim made on behalf of the petitioner was controverted by Mr. Vinod Sharma, counsel for the respondents.
7.
After hearing counsel for the parties, the short question that arises for consideration is - Are the respondents right in rejecting the petitioner's claim for the grant of retiral benefits ?
8.
It is the admitted position that the Bank has framed Regulations under Section 19 of the Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertakings) Act, 1970.
These Regulations are called the 'Bank of Baroda (Employees) Pension Regulations, 1995'.
The following provisions are relevant :-  "2(k) Date of retirement means that last date of the month in which an employee attains the age of superannuation or the date on which he is retired by the Bank or the date on which the employee voluntarily retires or the date on which the officer is deemed to have retired.
14.
Qualifying service.
Subject to the other conditions contained in these regulations, an employee who has rendered a minimum of ten years of service in the Bank on the date of his retirement or the date on which he is deemed to have retired shall qualify for pension.
28.
Superannuation Pension.
9.
A perusal of the above provisions shows that an employee retires on the last day of the month and not the last day of the year in which he attains the age of superannuation.
Under Regulation 14, a person who has rendered 10 years of service, is entitled to claim pension.
By virtue of Regulation 28, an employee who retires on his attaining the age of superannuation becomes entitled to the grant of superannuation pension.
10.
It is the admitted position that the age of superannuation is 60 years.
Reading Regulations 2(k) and 28 together, it is clear that the petitioner shall be deemed to have retired on October 31, 1996 which was the last day of the month in which he had attained the age of 60 years.
This having happened, the right to claim superannuation pension had matured on October 31, 1996.
11.
The respondents have claimed that the date of retirement "according to the award applicable to the petitioner was 31121996....." This averment has been made in paragraphs 2, 3 and 14 of the written statement.
The petitioner contested this claim.
Ultimately, learned counsel for the respondents had to concede that there was no award.
However, the tenuous plea raised on behalf of the respondents was that it is a practice.
No basis even for this practice was disclosed.
In fact, Mr. Vinod Sharma, counsel for the respondents had stated that the practice has been in vogue for the last about 50 years.
This claim, however, cannot be sustained in view of the fact that the pension regulations were framed in the year 1995.
In Regulation 2(k), while defining the date of retirement, it has been clearly provided that it is "the last date of the month in which the employee attains the age of superannuation."
Had it been the last date of the year, the regulations would have so provided.
It is clear that the plea taken in the written statement is factually incorrect.
In fact, there is an inaccurate averment in the written statement filed on behalf of the respondents.
12.
On a perusal of the regulations and the factual position, it is clear that the petitioner had attained the age of superannuation on October 14, 1996 and the last day of the month would be the date of his retirement.
On that date, the petitioner had rendered more than 27 years of service.
He would be entitled to the grant of superannuation pension by virtue of Regulation 28.
13.
Mr. Vinod Sharma, counsel for the respondents contended that the petitioner had resigned from service.
Thus, he has forfeited his right to the grant of retiral benefits.
14.
We are unable to accept this contention.
Firstly, as already observed, the petitioner's right to receive retiral benefits had matured on October 31, 1996.
Secondly, his continuance in service thereafter would be in the nature of re-employment with effect from November 1, 1996.
This view is in consonance with the observations of their Lordships of the Supreme Court in Ramswaroop Masawan v. Municipal Council and another, AIR 1999 SC 705 : 1999(2) SCT 299 (SC).
Still further, we are also satisfied that the petitioner's resignation had not been accepted on November 18, 1996 as averred in paragraph 8 of the written statement.
It was categorically asserted on behalf of the petitioner that the respondents had raised a false plea.
He had continued in service even after November 18, 1996.
Counsel for the petitioner had asserted that his name had continued to appear in the Attendance Register even after the alleged date of the acceptance of his resignation.
Mr. Sharma appearing for the respondents was unable to deny this allegation.
Thus, the plea of the respondents that "the resignation letter was accepted on 18111996" is not correct.
In this behalf, it deserves mention that vide letter dated November 27, 1996, the Bank had declined the petitioner's request for permission to contest the elections to the Municipal Council.
It was also observed in this letter that in case, it was found that he had contested the elections, the Bank may take action against him.
Surely, if the petitioner's resignation had been accepted on November 18, 1996 with effect from November 16, 1996, there would have been no occasion for the Bank to write the letter of November 27, 1996.
15.
On December 2, 1996, the petitioner had admittedly applied for permission for voluntary retirement.
He had also requested that the requirement of three months notice may be waived as he was in any case going to retire on December 31, 1996.
No order was passed on this application.
The petitioner alleges that on December 3, 1996, he was made to write a request for acceptance of his resignation with effect from November 16, 1996.
Thereupon, the letter was issued to him on December 4, 196.
It was ante-dated and shown to have been issued on November 18, 1996.
A copy of this letter has been produced as Annexure P.6 with the writ petition.
The petitioner's contention that the letter was ante-dated, appears to be correct.
16.
In view of the above, we hold that the petitioner had retired from service on October 31, 1966.
He had earned pension and other retiral benefits on that date.
The respondents have falsely averred that the date of retirement was to be the last day of the year under the award.
There is no award.
The respondents have also falsely alleged that the petitioner's request for resignation was accepted on November 18, 1996.
Even the date on the letter, a copy of which is at Annexure P.6 with the writ petition is not correct.
17.
Counsel for the parties had even raised the plea with regard to the validity of Regulation 22.
Certain decisions had also been cited to contend that pension is not a bounty.
In view of our above conclusion, we do not consider it necessary to go into these questions.
18.
As a result, the writ petition is allowed.
The petitioner shall be paid all the retiral benefits within two months.
He will also be entitled to interest @ 12% per annum.
Costs are assessed at Rs. 5,000/-.
Petition allowed.
