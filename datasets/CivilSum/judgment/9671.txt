SS Nijjar, J. (Oral) - By this common order, we propose to decide CWP No. 17349 of 2004 (Yog Raj and others v. The State of Punjab and another), CWP No. 4279 of 2005 (Jagdish Kaur and others v. State of Punjab and another), CWP No. 7196 of 2005 (Kaur Singh and others v. State of Punjab and another), CWP No. 7870 of 2005 (Krishna Nand Sharma v. The State of Punjab and another), CWP No. 8042 of 2005 (Harbajan Kaur and others v. State of Punjab and another), CWP No. 8168 of 2005 (Krishan Chand Gupta and others v. State of Punjab and another), CWP No. 14948 of 2005 (Bihari Lal and others v. The State of Punjab and another), CWP No. 14950 of 2005 (Surinder Singh and others v. The State of Punjab and another), CWP No. 14976 of 2005 (Subash Kumar Dhir and others v. State of Punjab and another) as common questions of law and fact are involved.
For the purposes of the order, we shall make reference to the facts as narrated in CWP No. 11553 of 2004.
2.
It may be noticed, at the outset, that all the petitioners have retired on or after 3152003 as depicted in the Chart (Annexure P-1).
All the petitioners were working in the Punjab Privately Managed Recognised Schools which were governed by the Punjab Privately Managed Recognised Schools Employees (Security of Service) Act, 1979 (hereinafter referred to as `the 1979 Act').
Their service conditions are governed by the Punjab Privately Managed Recognized Schools Employees (Security of Service).
Rules, 1981 (hereinafter referred to as `the 1981 Rules').
This group of Teachers has been continuously agitating and litigating for being granted the same conditions of service as the Teachers working in the Government schools.
From time to time, their demands have either been met by the Government on their own or through the orders of the Court.
In the matter of pension, ultimately, the Government agreed to grant pensionary benefits to the Teachers and the aided staff of the aided schools on Punjab Government pattern with effect from 521987.
The pensionary benefits were introduced by notification No. GSR.3/P.A.18/79/S.15/ Amd.
(1)/91 dated 1511991 by amending the 1981 Rules.
By notification No. GSR/10/PPM RSE(SS)R81/R 22-A/92 dated 1021992, the Government framed a scheme called "The Punjab Privately Managed Recognized Aided Schools Retirement Benefit Scheme (hereinafter referred to as `the 1992 Scheme').
By virtue of the 1992 scheme, it was decided that the Teachers or the employees who are in service on 521987 or who are appointed on or after 521987 against aided posts, would be entitled to get pensionary benefits.
The Teachers were required to exercise their options as provided in paragraph-4 of the 1992 Scheme.
All the petitioners submitted the options within the requisite time period.
As a consequence, the petitioners have lost the benefits under the Contributory Provident Fund Scheme.
Those employees who wished to continue with the Contributory Provident Fund Scheme, were also required to give their options in the negative.
It was also mentioned in the 1992 Scheme that in case, any employee fails to give his option upto 1261992, it would be presumed that he/she does not want to opt for the pension and he/she wants to opt for the Contributory Provident Fund Scheme.
The amount of Provident Fund which was deposited with the Managing Committee or with the Regional Provident Fund Commissioner was directed to be deposited in the Government Treasury before 1261992.
In case it was not so deposited, the Managing Committee or the Regional Provident Fund Commissioner would be required to pay interest on the delayed payments.
The 1992 Scheme was again amended by notification No. GSR.94/PPM RSE(SS) R.18/R.22A/Amd.
(2)93 dated 23.11.1993.
In this amendment, it was provided that all these employees who were working on 521987 and who had opted for the pension scheme would deposit the amount received by them on account of employee's contribution towards Provident Fund including the interest thereon.
It was also decided that the employees joining after 521987 will have no option but to be governed by the pension scheme alone.
After the establishment of the Retirement Benefit Fund, the retired Teachers and employees of the aided schools who retired on or after 521987 were granted the benefits of - (a) Superannuation Pension; (b) Death-cum-Retirement Gratuity; (c) Family Pension; (d) Invalid Pension; (e) Compensation Pension; (f) Compassionate Allowance and (g) Retiring Pension at par with the Government employees.
The parity so established was maintained by the Punjab Government with regard to the grant of benefit of Dearness Allowance and Interim Relief from time to time also.
Since then all the pensioners have been granted the pension at par with their counter-parts i.e. with the Government Schools Teachers/Pensioners.
3.
Subsequently, the pay scales of the Teachers working in aided schools were brought at par with the Teachers working in Government schools.
The pension was also revised from time to time at par with the Teachers working in Government schools.
Some time in the year 1999, the Government failed to release the benefit of enhancement of pension/D.A.
to the Teachers of the aided schools, compelling them to file Civil Writ Petition No._____ Ujagar Singh and others v. State of Punjab and others.
The writ petition was disposed of, on the statement made by the State counsel that the Government would pay enhanced pension and other pensionary benefits to the retirees within a period of two months of the issue of the notification.
In spite of the abovesaid undertaking, the retiral benefits were not paid.
Some Teachers, therefore, again approached this Court by filing Civil Writ Petition No. 17153 of 2000 titled Harbhajan Singh and others v. State of Punjab and others.
Again, a statement was made by the counsel for the State of Punjab that the Government vide Memo No. 8/79/92-E-3/22904 dated 7112004 had given its approval for grant of pensionary benefits with effect from 111996 to the employees of the aided schools at par with the employees of Government school.
The writ petition was disposed of with a direction to the State Government to grant all the benefits to the employees within a period of six months from the date of the orders of this Court i.e. 23.1.2002.
This Court also directed that the benefits should not only be restricted to the petitioners but should be granted to all the similarly situated retired Teachers.
The department again failed to honour the undertaking leading the petitioners to file Contempt Petition No. 1215 of 2002 in Civil Writ Petition No. 17153 of 2000.
In these contempt proceedings, the State Government again gave an undertaking on 2842003.
It was stated that before 882003 revised pensionary benefits will be released to all the retirees.
The matter was adjourned to 882003.
It was observed that if the relief is not granted by that time, the Chief Secretary, Punjab will appear in person.
Again, the revised benefits were not paid.
Thereafter, on orders of the Court, the Chief Secretary of the State of Punjab appeared before this Court on 1282003.
It was only then, that the retirement benefits were paid to those petitioners.
Thereafter, a proposal has been mooted to amend the Pension Scheme, 1992.
A bill has been moved in the Punjab State Legislative Assembly to amend Section 15 of the 1979 Act.
It is now provided that the State Government may by notification and subject to the conditions of previous publication, make rules with prospective effect and with retrospective effect for carrying out the purposes of this Act.
But there is no amendment in the 1992 Scheme till date.
The State Government has, however, issued executive instructions through letter No. 315-2003 Pen (1) dated 1672003 (Annexure P-9) regarding grant of pension to the retired employees of private aided schools.
In this, it has been provided that the pension cases of employees retiring from the private aided school after 162003 be not sent to the concerned authorities for further action till further orders.
As a result of the aforesaid communication, none of the petitioners who have retired after 3152003, have been released the pensionary benefits.
Consequently, the petitioners have been constrained to file these writ petitions seeking a writ in the nature of certiorari quashing letter dated 1672003 and for issuance of a writ in the nature of mandamus directing the respondents to release the pensionary benefits to the petitioners under the 1992 Scheme.
4.
The respondents have filed a short affidavit on 752005.
It has been stated that matter with regard to the pensionary benefits to the employees of the aided schools at par with the Government employees from 111996 was placed before the Council of Ministers and it has been decided on 2252003, that the employees who had retired before 3152003 or were retiring on 3152003 would continue to get pension under the existing rules/schemes.
It has also been decided that a revised contributed pension scheme would be prepared for the employees who retire after 162003.
In view of the above, the pensionary benefits have not been released to the retirees after 3152003.
Today, another short affidavit has been filed by the respondents.
It has been stated that the respondents will require another 4 months' time to formulate a suitable scheme in consultation with the Department of Finance.
Annexure-A attached to the short affidavit is a notification dated 1292003 issued under Rule 22-A of the 1981 rules.
Under this notification, the Governor of Punjab has been pleased to repeal the 1992 Scheme with effect from 1st day of June, 2003.
On this basis, the respondents claim to deny the benefit of any pension whatsoever to the pensioners who have retired from the aided schools.
5.
We have considered the entire matter.
From a perusal of the facts narrated above, it becomes apparent that the Teachers working in the aided schools have been granted security of tenure and payment of pay scales at par with their counterparts in Government schools, after engaging in continuous agitation and litigation; before this Court or Hon'ble the Supreme Court.
Ultimately, the Government had voluntarily formulated even the 1992 Pension Scheme.
It is, undoubtedly, applicable to all the employees who were in service on 521987.
All the petitioners fall within the purview of the 1992 Scheme.
They are denied the benefit of pension on the sole ground that matter with regard to amendment or formulation of a fresh scheme is under active consideration of the Government.
The 1992 Scheme is said to have been repealed by the notification dated 1292003.
But it would continue to apply to the employees who have retired on or before 31st May, 2003.
The end result is that the petitioners are not granted any retirement benefits under the 1992 Scheme only on the ground that they have retired after 3152003.
The 1992 Scheme is applicable to all employees who were in service on 521987, irrespective of their date of retirement.
All employees covered under the 1992 Scheme form one homoegenous class.
The petitioners, therefore, cannot be denied the benefit of pension under the 1992 Scheme.
On the one hand the petitioners have been denied the benefit under the 1992 Scheme, and at the same time, the respondents do not have a new scheme under which their pension rights could be regulated.
In our opinion, the action of the respondents is wholly arbitrary and indefensible under Article 14 of the Constitution of India.
It has been stated and reiterated by the Supreme Court on numerous occasions that pension is not a bounty, which is granted to a Government employee, out of any benevolent of humanitarian grounds.
Pension is a valuable right which is earned by the retiring official after rendering long years or meritorious service.
We find absolutely no justification in the respondents denying the retiral benefits to the petitioners only on the ground that new pension scheme is in the process of being formulated.
6.
In view of the above, we allow these writ petitions.
The letter dated 1672003 (Annexure P-9) is hereby quashed.
All the petitioners who fall within the erstwhile 1992 Scheme i.e. they were in service on 521987 and had opted for the Pension Scheme are held entitled to the grant of pension under the aforesaid Scheme.
Let the pensionary benefits be released to the petitioners and all the other similarly situated employees of the aided schools irrespective of whether they have retired before or after 3152003.
Let the benefits be released within a period of two months from today.
Petitions allowed.
