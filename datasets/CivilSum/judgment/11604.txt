Swatanter Kumar, J - The prayer in this petition under Article 226 of the Constitution of India is for issuance of a writ of mandamus or any other appropriate writ directing the respondents to regularise the services of the petitioner in terms of the State policy dated 731996, Annexure P/2 to the writ petition.
2.
Petitioner was appointed on 2831989 on daily wages in polythene bag factory, owned and run by the Government of Haryana.
The petitioner worked in the factory upto 1371991 regularly on which date the services of the petitioner were terminated by the employer.
A reference was made by the competent authority under Section 10 of the Industrial Disputes Act, hereinafter referred to as the Act.
The learned Presiding Officer, Labour Court, Ambala vide reference No. 112 of 1992 answered the reference after a settlement was effected between the parties vide order dated 10.6.1996.
After recording statements of the parties appearing for the respective sides, granted the relief of reinstatement.
The reinstatement was granted with continuity of service but without back wages.
The petitioner was reinstated but again his services were terminated on 2861996 for which the petitioner again raised a dispute and the matter was referred to the learned Labour Court, Ambala.
The State of Haryana framed a policy for regularisation of daily wages employees, but have completed three years of service as on 3131996 and had worked for 240 days in each calendar year.
This policy was framed on 3111996 and was amended on 1831996.
The petitioner claims to have competed 7 years of service as a result of whcih he is entitled to regularisation of service.
As the services of the petitioner were terminated the respondent did not regularise his services.
According to the petitioner.
Similarly situated employees, who were working with the petitioner, were taken back in service and after the judgment of this Court in CWP No. 311 of 1998.
Copy of which is annexed to the petition as Annexure P/3, the services were regularised and case of the petitioner is squarely covered by the said judgment.
3.
Upon notice, the respondents filed detailed reply where it is admitted that the petitioner was engaged as a casual labourer and the services were never terminated and in fact the petitioner left the work of his own.
The records have already been weeded out upto 3131993 in accordance with Rule 1524 of the Haryana Forest Manual Volume-II.
The settlement before the Lok Adalat is not disputed.
The petitioner failed to join duties as per notice served by the respondents upon him and as such he is not entitled to the relief of regularisation.
4.
Additional affidavit was filed on behalf of the respondent on 1612002 wherein it was stated that in compliance to the notice dated 1432000, the petitioner has joined his duties as a daily wages labourer at Pehowa and the petitioner is working in the said department.
It is also stated that the demand notice has already been withdrawn by the petitioner.
5.
In view of the above pleadings of the parties it is clear that the matter had been settled before the Lok Adalat where on 10.6.1996 the department had granted relief of the petitioner of reinstatement with continuity of service, but without back wages.
Petitioner has also been permitted to join and is presently working in the department as per the affidavit of the respondents.
No dispute in that regard survives as the petitioner has already withdrawn the demand notice.
The only question that survives for consideration of the Court is whether the petitioner is entitled to grant of relief of regularisation or not.
6.
The petitioner was admittedly appointed on daily wages on 2831989 and had worked till 13.7.1996 when his services were terminated.
The petitioner was reinstated with the consent of the parties with continuity of service vide award dated 10.6.1996 and he did join the department thereafter.
There is serious doubt as to whether the petitioner continued to work from 2861996 till 1432000 because according to the respondents the petitioner did not join despite service of notices upon him for that purpose while according to the petitioner he was not permitted to function after 2961996.
7.
Be that as it may, the case of the petitioner has to be considered for regularisation as on 3131996, the date on which he would be deemed to be in employment because of the award which grants him continuity of service.
The wages were denied to the petitioner by consent of the parties and continuity of service with reinstatement was granted again with the consent of the parties.
As such, both the parties are bound by the terms of the consented award.
8.
Furthermore, the decision of a Division Bench of this Court in CWP No. 311 of 1998, titled as Dalip Singh and others v. State of Haryana and others, 1999(3) SCT 385 (P&H)(DB) : decided on 30111998, squarely is applicable to the facts and circumstances of the present case.
With respect we follow the view expressed by the other Division Bench and would grant somewhat similar relief to the petitioner in the present case as well.
9.
The contention of the respondents that petitioner was not physically present and, thus, cannot be considered in employment as on 3131996 is without any substance.
In this regard we may refer to the judgment of this Court in the case of Deep Chand v. State of Haryana and others, CWP No. 14165 of 2001, decided on 1012002.
In view of the above principle, the respondents are obliged to treat the petitioner on duty for all purposes and intents and cannot take up the plea that the petitioner was not present, thus, cannot be granted the benefit.
10.
For the reasons afore-stated we partly allow this writ petition with the direction to the respondents to consider the case of the petitioner for regularisation in the light of the observations made in this judgment as on 1831996.
However, with regard to any other relief, the petitioner is at liberty to take steps under Section 33-C(2) of the Industrial Disputes Act.
The parties are, however, left to bear their own costs.
Appeal partly allowed.
