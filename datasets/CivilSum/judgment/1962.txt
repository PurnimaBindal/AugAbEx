DV Sehgal, President.
- This Order shall dispose of CWP No. 2450 of 1991 and CWP No. 3093 of 1991, by which the validity of the impugned order dated 17101990 passed by the Chief Labour Commissioner (Central) under Rule 25(2)(v)(b) of the Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Central Rules, 1971 has been questioned.
The undisputed position is that by a notification dated 27th September, 1985 (Annexure P-1), the Government of India exercised its powers under Clause (b) of Sub-section (1) Section 5 of the Minimum Wages Act, 1948 (for short the Act) and fixed the minimum wages for the employees for piece work done by them in the stone mines in Faridabad District at Rs. 71/- per truck load of 150 cft., provided the broken stone is between 5'-8".
A memorandum of settlement was also entered into between Khan Mazdoor Union and others on 1491987, as a result of which, it was agreed that rate of wages for the size of stone below 5" will be paid @ Rs. 160/- per 200 cft.
measurement and that the rate of wages for size of stone above 8"-12" will be paid @ Rs. 55/- + Rs. 10/-, if the workman put the hole for blasting by themselves for 200 cft.
of measurement.
The notification issued by the Government of India on 27th September, 1985 (Annexure P-1) became the subject matter of the litigation.
Writ petitions were filed challenging its validity in Delhi High Court as also in Punjab and Haryana High Court and stay of operation of the notification was ordered by the courts.
2.
Since the operation of the stay was quite protracted the Chief Labour Commissioner (Central) exercised his jurisdiction under Rule 25(2)(v)(b) of the Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Central Rules, 1971 and passed the order annexure P-5.
He clearly mentioned therein that the notification annexure P-1 having been stayed and because of prolongation of the stay by the Courts, there is no order fixing minimum wages for the workman working on stone mines, and, therefore, he was exercising his jurisdiction under the aforesaid provisions.
It is this order of the Chief Labour Commissioner (Central) which is the subject matter of these two writ petitions.
3.
Sh. Vivek Singhal, learned AAG, Haryana, has placed before us photocopy of the judgment of the Supreme Court in MC Mehta v. Union of India and others, IA No. 29-A in W.P. (C) No. 4077 of 1985.
A Division Bench of the Supreme Court vide judgment dated November 19, 1996 has declared the notification dated 27th September, 1985 issued by the Government of India to be valid and has set aside the judgment of the High Court of Delhi and the Punjab and Haryana High Court.
A further direction has been made by the Supreme Court that the award of the Regional Labour Commissioner dated 19th August, 1986 be enforced by the authorities under the Act in accordance with law and payment shall be made to the workers after recovering the same from the quary-owners- respondents and that this should be done within two months.
The order also provides that the workman shall be entitled to their wages with interest @ 12% and that they shall be entitled to the wages as fixed under the said notification till the new notification was issued by the Government of India.
4.
Rule 25(iv) of Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Central Rules, 1971 provides that the rate of wages payable to the workman shall be not less than the rates prescribed under the Minimum Wages Act, 1948.
Sub-clause (v)(b) of Rule 25 ibid, however, further provides that in other cases the wages holidays, hours of work and conditions of service of the workmen shall be such as may be specified in this behalf by the Chief Labour Commissioner (Central).
It is clear that clause (v)(b) of the said Rules comes into play only in the absence of the rates of wages prescribed under the Minimum Wages Act, 1948.
Since, however, notification dated 27th September, 1985 in respect of the wages prescribed for the workman working on the stone mines has since been upheld by the Supreme Court in the aforesaid judgment dated November 19, 1996, it is clear that the sub-clause (v)(b) of Rule 25 ibid, would not come into play and the order passed by the Chief Labour Commissioner (Central) dated 17101990, which is the subject matter of these two writ petition is rendered inoperative.
In this view of the matter and keeping in view the judgment of the Supreme Court dated November 19, 1996 referred to above, both these writ petitions are disposed of accordingly.
