ML Singhal, J - By means of this judgment, RSA No. 4226 of 2001 and 4302 of 2001 shall be disposed as in these appeals, same question of law and facts is involved.
Facts are being taken from the case of Kesar Singh.
2.
Kesar Singh rendered military service during the operation of emergency which was proclaimed in the year 1982 on account of external aggression.
He rendered military service during 2641963 to 9.1.1968.
He rendered military service during the proclamation of second emergency which remained in operation from 3.12.1971 to 2731977.
At the time of discharge from the Indian Army, he was holding the rank of Sepoy No. 1524497 in unit of Bombay Engineering Group.
After he was discharged from the army, he joined Municipal Fire Brigade, Municipal Corporation, Ludhiana as Fireman.
He was given this post in the capacity of an ex-serviceman.
First emergency was proclaimed on account of external aggression committed by China on India.
The Government of India was in great need of young men to join the military service.
So that young men felt encouraged to join the military, some benefits were assured to them.
First National Emergency was proclaimed on 26101962.
It remained in operation till 1011968.
Second emergency was imposed on 3.12.1971 and was lifted on 2731977.
In July, 1963, a circular was issued by the Financial Commissioner, Punjab with regard to the concession to civilian employees and others who joined the military service during emergency which will account for increments, seniority, promotion and pension etc. in civil employment later on.
On the instructions of the Central Govt., concessions as well as promises through circular and by other means were incorporated in rules framed by the composite State of Punjab under Article 309 of the Constitution of India.
Govt. of Punjab, prior to the carving out of the State of Haryana out of its territory, made statutory rules under Article 309 of the Constitution of India which are called "The Punjab Govt. National Emergency Concession Rules, 1965".
These rules were adopted by the State of Haryana also.
Benefit of military service rendered during the proclamation of national emergency had to be allowed to all ex-servicemen on their appointment in the government departments, local bodies, corporations, etc.
Thus on his appointment in a civil post under the State of Punjab/Municipal Corporation, Ludhiana, he became entitled to the benefits of military service towards increments, promotion and pension, etc.
He is entitled to these benefits with retrospective effect and all consequential benefits.
1965 rules cast a statutory obligation on the State of Punjab/local bodies, Corporations, etc. to grant the benefit of military service to him.
3.
On these allegations, Kesar Singh filed suit for declaration against the State of Punjab/Municipal Corporation, Ludhiana to the effect that he is entitled to the benefit of military service rendered by him during the operation of the proclamation of the first and second national emergency for the period of 2641963 to 911965 and 3.12.1971 to 2731977 towards increments, seniority, pension, promotion, etc. in his post of Fireman in Municipal Fire Brigade, Municipal Corporation, Ludhiana and also to the consequential benefits from retrospective dates emerging from the fixation of seniority, promotion, etc.  4.
Defendants contested the suit of the plaintiff.
It was urged that the Punjab Govt. National Emergency Concession Rules, 1965 are not applicable to the plaintiff.
It was urged that these rules were repealed vide notification No. G.S.R.11/Const./Art.
309, 234 and 318/82 dated 221982 while he was regularised on 26.2.1982.
These rules were repealed before the regularisation of his services.
Since the benefit of these rules could only be given if the appointment was on regular basis, no benefit of these rules could be given to him.
He was appointed on ad hoc basis on 3171981.
5.
On the pleadings of the parties, the following issues were framed by the learned trial court :-  1.
Whether the plaintiff is entitled to the declaration prayed for ?
OPP 2.
Relief.
6.
Vide order dated 2812000, Civil Judge (Senior Division), Ludhiana decreed the plaintiff's suit declaring that the was entitled to all benefits prescribed in the Punjab Govt. National Emergency (Concession), Rules 1965 of military service rendered by him during the operation of emergency from 2641963 to 9.1.1968 and 3.12.1971 to 2731977, in view of his finding, that he was entitled to the benefits of these rules as he joined the service of Municipal Corporation, Ludhiana on 3171981.
Fact that he was regularised with effect from 26.2.1982 is of no consequence so far as the grant of benefit of these rules is concerned.
7.
Defendants went in appeal which was dismissed by learned District Judge, Ludhiana vide order dated 462001.
Still not satisfied, defendants have come up in further appeal to this Court.
8.
In these RSAs, the following question of law arises :  "Whether the benefit of 1965 Rules could be given to those who had been recruited on ad hoc basis but become regular after the repeal of those rules which took place on 221982 ?"
9.
It was submitted by the learned Additional Advocate General, Punjab, assisted by Shri Rupinder Khosla, Advocate appearing for Municipal Corporation, Ludhiana that the benefit of the 1965 rules could not be available to the respondent plaintiff as benefit of these rules was available only to the employees recruited on regular basis.
In support of this submission, he drew my attention to Jacob v. State of Punjab, 1984(4) (Sic) 591 where it was held that after the repeal of 1965 rules, no declaration could be made under the power granted by the 1965 rules to declare any service to be "military service" under rule 2 of the 1965 rules.
The rules of 1965 came to an end by repeal in 1982.
The question of declaring thereafter any other service to be military service including the service during second emergency did not arise.
1965 rules stood repealed by 1982 rules which came into force on February 2, 1982.
Otherwise also, as per the definition of the "military service" given in 1965 rules, the military service was only for the emergency period which was declared on 26101962 and not the one which was declared on 3.12.1971.
In Ex-Capt.
AS Parmar and another v. State of Haryana and others, (1950- 1988)(2) RSJ 558, the Apex Court observed that the benefit of military service is only to the extent "rendered by a person during the period of operation of the proclamation of emergency made by the President under Article 352 of the Constitution of India on October 26, 1962.
Apart from that any other service declared by the State Government to be military service under Rule 2 of 1965 Rules.
The said Rules does not envisage the counting of the military service rendered during the second emergency.
Similar view was expressed by a Full Bench of this Court in Rajinder Kumar v. State of Haryana, 1992(3) SCT 20 : 1992(3) RSJ 302.
10.
It was submitted that the respondent-plaintiff was not at all entitled to the benefit of military service rendered by him during the operation of the second emergency.
He was not entitled to the benefit of military service rendered by him during the period when the proclamation of the first national emergency was in operation because of the repeal of the 1965 Rules with effect from 221982 when his services were regularised with effect from 26.2.1982.
Benefit of "military service" was admissible only to regular incumbents and not to ad hoc appointees.
11.
Faced with this position, learned counsel for the respondent-plaintiff submitted that he joined the service of Municipal Corporation, Ludhiana on 3171981 on adhoc basis.
His services were regularised on 26.2.1982.
After his services had been regularised, it should be taken as if he was regularised since the day one.
In support of this submission, he drew my attention to Dev Dutt, ASI v. State of Punjab and Others, 1996(3) RSJ 852 where the benefit of military service rendered by Dev Dutt, ASI was given to him for the period during which he remained in military during the operation of the proclamation of the first emergency.
He joined military service on 391963 and was discharged on 2651970.
On 2781975, he joined Police Telecommunication, Punjab, as a Constable.
On 16121984, he applied to the Director, Police Telecommunication, Punjab for giving him the benefit of military service in terms of the Punjab Government National Emergency (Concession) Rules, 1965.
He claimed that he be given increments for the period spent by him in military service as well as extend the said benefit towards seniority and pension.
His prayer was allowed by this Court.
12.
In Dev Dutt's case (supra), the question of the repeal of 1965 Rules did not come in.
In the case in hand, Kesar Singh was regularised in the service of Municipal Corporation, Ludhiana on 26.2.1982 while 1965 Rules were repealed on 221982.
As on 2621982, 1965, Rules ceased to have any application.
13.
For the reasons given above, I am of the opinion that both the courts below were in error while granting relief prayed for to the respondent plaintiff.
So, both these appeals are allowed.
In consequence, suit of the respondent-plaintiff (in both the appeal) are dismissed.
No costs.
Appeals allowed.
