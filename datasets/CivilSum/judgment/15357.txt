GS Singhvi, J - Whether the decision of Kurukshetra University, Kurukshetra (for short, `the University') to decline approval to the petitioner's appointment as part-time Lecturer in Sanskrit in the service of Indira Gandhi Mahila Mahavidyala, Kaithal (hereinafter described as `the College') is violative of University Statutes and Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India is the short question which arises for determination in this petition filed by Smt. Achla Malik for quashing letter Annexure P4 dated 24.9.1993 sent by Assistant Registrar (Colleges) to the Principal of the College declaring that the revised qualification contained in Apendix IX-A under ordinance XVI of the University cannot be invoked for determining her eligibility.
2.
For deciding the aforementioned question, I may briefly notice the facts.
3.
The petitioner joined service in the College as a part-time Lecturer in Sanskrit in September, 1974.
She served in that capacity for different periods from 291974 to 28.2.1979, the details of which are given below :-  1.
291974 to 31.03.1975 2.
16.10.1975 to 29.11.1975 3.
03.01.1977 to 28.03.1977 4.
11101977 to 31.03.1978 5.
05.08.1978 to 28021979.
Thereafter, she joined as Lecturer in Sanskrit in RKSD College, Kaithal under the Scheme sponsored by the University Grants Commission (for short, the Commission).
She served under the scheme from 1979-80 to 1981-82.
From April 1983 to 1988, she worked as part-time Lecturer in Sanskrit in RKSD College.
In July, 1988, she again joined as part-time lecturer in the College and served for various periods detailed below :-  1.
26.7.1988 to 28.02.1989 2.
10081989 to 28021990 3.
26.07.1990 to 28021991 4.
03081991 to 29.2.1992 5.
31.08.1992 to 31031993 6.
Present appointment is till 31031994.
4.
In 1983, the Vice Chancellor of the University approved the petitioner's appointment as Lecturer in Sanskrit up to degree classes.
This was conveyed by Deputy Registrar (Colleges) to the Principal of the College vide letter Annexure P1.
In 1988, Assistant Registrar (Colleges) sent letter Annexure P3 dated 25111988 to the Principal of the College conveying the University's approval to the petitioner's appointment as part-time lecturer up to 28.2.1989.
In September, 1993, the Principal of the college sent letter Annexure P5 dated 2891993 seeking approval to the petitioner's appointment.
This time, the university declined approval on the ground that the petitioner does not possess the revised qualifications.
5.
The petitioner has challenged the decision of the University mainly on the ground that it is violative of her fundamental right to equality guaranteed under Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India.
She has averred that those who were appointed as Lecturers on regular basis in the year 1974 and thereafter on the basis of the qualifications similar to those possessed by her and are performing similar duties are continuing in service but, the University has arbitrarily declined to approve her part-time appointment on the spacious ground that she does not possess the revised qualifications and in this manner, she has been discriminated.
She has further averred that the revised qualifications laid down by the Commission, which were adopted by the University in 1993 cannot be applied to her case because her appointment had been approved as early as in 1983.
6.
In the reply filed on behalf of the University, it has been averred that the petitioner cannot compare her case with those appointed on regular basis because she had never faced the regular selection.
It has been further averred that the revised qualifications laid down by the Commission, which have been incorporated in Appendix IX-A under ordinance XVI, are binding on all affiliated colleges and as the petitioner does not possess the revised qualifications, she could not have been appointed as part-time Lecturer in the year 1994.
7.
The stand taken by the management of the College is that the petitioner cannot continue in service because her appointment was not approved by the University.
8.
Shri HS Gill, learned Senior Advocate appearing for the petitioner argued that the approval granted by the University to the petitioner's appointment vide Annexure P1 dated 10101983 was final and she is entitled to continue in service as part-time lecturer de hors the decision contained in Annexure P4.
He submitted that the management of the college was not required to seek fresh approval for the petitioner's appointment as part-time lecturer and, therefore, the rejection of its proposal is liable to be ignored and the petitioner is entitled to continue in service because she has already served for about 20 years.
Learned counsel then argued that the revised qualifications contained in the University Ordinance cannot be invoked for determining the petitioner's eligibility because she was appointed in 1974 after due selection.
Shri Gill further argued that the decision of the University to decline approval to the petitioner's appointment should be declared discriminatory and quashed because it has not taken any action qua those who possess identical qualifications and were appointed on regular basis in 1974 and thereafter.
In support of his arguments, Shri Gill relied on the following decisions :-  (1) Bhagwati Parsad v. Delhi State Minerals Development Corporation, AIR 1990 SC 371.
(2) Mrs. Neera Mathur v. Life Insurance Corporation of India and anr., 1992(1) SCT 624 (SC) : 1992(1) SLR 473.
(3) Mrs. Satwant Khera v. State of Haryana and others, 1991(2) SCT 458 (P&H) : 1991(2) RSJ 66.
9.
Shri Jatinder Chauhan defended the decision of the University to decline the approval to the appointment of the petitioner as part-time lecturer for the year 1993-94 by arguing that she could not have been appointed by the management of the College ignoring the fact that she does not possess the revised qualifications.
He further argued that even though the petitioner was appointed as part-time Lecturer from time to time, she did not acquire a right to hold the post because she had never faced selection.
Shri Chauhan countered the submission of Shri Gill that the petitioner is entitled to be treated at par with regularly selected Lecturers by pointing out that the procedure for part-time appointment is altogether different than the one laid down for regular appointments and the petitioner had never competed for regular selection.
10.
Shri JK Goel adopted the arguments of Shri Chauhan and submitted that in the face of the University's decision not to approve her appointment as part- time Lecturer in Sanskrit, the petitioner cannot seek a direction for being allowed to continue in the service of the college.
11.
I have given serious thought to the respective arguments.
In my opinion, the impugned decision of the University does not suffer from any legal or constitutional infirmity requiring interference by this Court under Article 226 of the Constitution of India.
12.
The argument of Shri Gill that the approval granted vide letter Annexure P1 was final and should be treated as operative for all times to come is based on a misconceived assumption that the petitioner's appointment was regular in nature.
As per her own showing, the petitioner was initially appointed in 1974 as part-time Lecturer.
All her subsequent appointments were also on part-time basis.
Therefore, the approval granted by the University will be deemed to have terminated when the tenure of her appointment came to an end and the petitioner cannot claim that the approval granted in 1983 should be treated as final and valid for all times to come.
13.
I am further of the view that reason contained in communication Annexure P4, namely, the petitioner does not possess the revised qualifications prescribed for the post of Lecturer cannot be termed as erroneous.
The tenure of the petitioner's appointment as part-time Lecturer in 1992 came to an end in March, 1993.
Thereafter, the management of the College again appointed her as part-time lecturer some time in August/September, 1993.
By that time, the University had adopted the revised qualifications laid down by the Commission.
Admittedly, the petitioner did not possess the revised qualifications.
Therefore, she was not entitled to be considered for appointment even as a part-time lecturer in the services of the college, which is affiliated with the University and is bound to follow the norms prescribed by the University for appointment of teachers.
Thus, no illegality was committed by the University when it declined to approve the petitioner's appointment as part- time lecturer on the ground that she does not possess the prescribed qualifications.
14.
The petitioner's claim for parity with regular Lecturers merits summary rejection because she had never competed for selection and the nature of her appointment was totally different than those appointed after regular selection.
15.
At this stage, it would be appropriate to mention that the part-time appointments given to the petitioner from time to time did not create any right in her favour so as to entitle her to claim continuance in service.
In its very nature, part-time appointment as a teacher has the fixed tenure with a limited liability to teach students for a fixed period.
Such appointments cannot confer enforceable legal right upon the incumbent to hold the post.
Therefore, there is no valid ground to accept the petitioner's prayer that she should be allowed to continue in service de hors the University's decision to decline approval to her appointment.
16.
The judgments relied upon by Shri Gill do not help the cause of the petitioner because in none of those cases, the Court had considered the claim of the part-time Lecturer to be continued in service in the back-drop of the University's refusal to approve such appointment on the ground that the candidate was not having the prescribed qualifications.
No other point has been argued.
For the reasons mentioned above, the writ petition is dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
