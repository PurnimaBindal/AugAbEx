VS Aggarwal, J - Ram Kumar petitioner is invoking the extraordinary jurisdiction of this Court under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India.
He seeks quashing of the order passed by the Presiding Officer, Labour Court, Panipat dated 9111995 and for a writ of mandamus to direct respondent No. 2 to reinstate the petitioner with continuity of service and with full back wages from the date of termination of his services.
2.
The petitioner alleged that he was appointed as Sweeper-cum-Chowkidar on 20121985 as per the rates fixed by the Deputy Commissioner.
He continued to work without any break with respondent No. 2 upto 4.10.1988.
There was no complaint against him.
The services of the petitioner were terminated illegally and without any reason.
The petitioner raised on industrial dispute.
A reply was filed.
The matter was referred to the Labour Court.
Respondent No. 2 had taken the stand that petitioner did not work continuously from 20121985 to 4.10.1988 and also he did not work for 240 days in any of the calendar years during this period.
He alleged that services of the petitioner were never terminated and that he left job of his own accord.
The learned Presiding Officer, Labour Court recorded the statements of the parties.
It was held that petitioner worked from 20121985 to 4.10.1988 and that his services were terminated without following the procedure prescribed under Section 25-F of the Industrial Disputes Act.
The termination order was held to be illegal.
The Presiding Officer, Labour Court declined the reference only on the ground that the petitioner raised the industrial dispute after 5 years of the termination of his services.
3.
By virtue of the present writ petition the petitioner assails the said order alleging that one Ramesh Kumari had filed a writ petition alongwith the petitioner.
The said matter was heard by a learned Single Judge of this Court.
The writ petition was dismissed as withdrawn on 4.11.1992 with liberty to approach the Labour Court.
It is in this process that delay had occurred.
The said Ramesh Kumari had raised the industrial dispute and in her case the reference was not declined on the ground of delay.
Her case is stated to be identical to that of the petitioner.
4.
Notice of the petition had been issued and respondent No. 2 contested the same.
It was reiterated that petitioner was appointed as Sweeper-cum-Chowkidar on 20121985 on daily wages.
It was denied that petitioner had worked with respondent No. 2 upto 4.10.1988.
The petitioner's services were never terminated.
He did not work for 240 days in a year before the said date of 4.10.1988.
He wilfully abstained himself.
5.
On behalf of the respondent it had been urged that petitioner had voluntarily abandoned the job from 4.10.1988.
His services had never been terminated.
In any case he did not work for 240 days.
In this regard one can conveniently refer to the award of the Labour Court and the findings returned are :-  "Ram Kumar (WW-1) had stated on oath that he was appointed as Sweeper-cum- Chowkidar on daily wages on 20121985.
He was paid wages as per the rate fixed by the Deputy Commissioner.
He has further stated that he worked as such upto 4.10.1988 when his services were terminated without following the procedure laid down in Section 25-F of the Industrial Disputes Act, in order to accommodate some other person.
On the other hand, Inder Pal (MW-1) has stated on oath that Ram Kumar worked from 3111985 to 3131988.
He has admitted that he worked during this period continuously and he worked for more than 500 days during this period.
He has admitted that procedure laid down in section 25-F was not followed while terminating the services of Ram Kumar.
He has stated that workman left the services at his own accord and did not turn up to join the duties thereafter.
It is pertinent to mention here that in the written statement it has been averred that the workman did not attend the duties from 4.10.1988 whereas Inder Pal has stated that he did not attend the duties w.e.f.
3181988.
So, there is discrepancy in the statement made by Inder Pal and the averments made in written statement.
In these circumstances, it is not proved that workman left the job at his own accord.
So, the statement of workman has to be accepted."
6.
These are findings of fact arrived at by the learned Labour Court.
These are based on evidence.
The Presiding Officer, Labour Court had noted the inconsistent statements that had been made on behalf of respondent No. 2 and, therefore, this Court will not interfere in the said findings of fact.
7.
As pointed out above the sole ground which prevailed with the learned Presiding Officer, Labour Court in refusing the reference was that there has been inordinate delay in raising the industrial dispute because the services are stated to have been terminated in 1988.
But the petitioner has filed Civil Writ Petition No. 1172 of 1989 alongwith one Ramesh Kumari.
The said writ petition was dismissed as withdrawn with liberty to approach the Labour Court on 4.11.1992.
It was thereafter that industrial dispute was raised.
Significantly Ramesh Kumari had been granted the relief by the Labour Court.
This not only shows the reason for delay that petitioner was litigating with respondent No. 2 in this Court, but this case cannot be equated over with other cases where there is inordinate delay in raising the industrial dispute.
To this extent the findings of the Labour Court are to be set aside and the award in this regard cannot be sustained.
8.
For these reasons, the writ petition is allowed and the impugned order/award is set aside whereby on account of delay the relief was refused.
The Presiding Officer, Labour Court would be competent to consider as to what relief, if any, is to be granted.
The parties are directed to appear before the learned Presiding Officer, Labour Court, Panipat on 811999.
Petition allowed.
