Syed Bashir-ud-Din, J - Petitioner a Chowkidar in Education Department was to retire on 31.3.1990, but was actually made to retire on 3131992.
The reason being that the Education Department treated his case as covered by Article 226(1) read with Schedule II (inferior service) of CSR.
This provision would have applied only to those cases where the employees of said inferior service joined such service after 10101966, while petitioner joined the service on 11.8.1979.
Petitioner has been paid for the period of overstay and has rendered duties and performed work.
Respondent No. 2 Accountant General, refused to treat two years period beyond 31.3.1990 towards petitioner's qualifying service for pension and instead treated him as having retired on 31.3.1990 (Annexure P1).
This annexure is challenged in this petition with prayer of finalising the pension case of the petitioner and release of his other pensionary benefits and for regularisation of petitioner's overstay period.
2.
Reply has been filed by respondents.
The respondent's case is that the petitioner, a member of inferior service, being covered by Article 226(1) of CSR, read with Schedule II of CSR Vol. II, had to retire on attaining the age of 58 years as he has joined the duty on 11.8.1979, much beyond the cut-off date provided for retirement of persons of inferior service at 60 years.
The cut-off date is 10101966 and therefore, the petitioner shall be deemed to have been retired on 31.3.1990.
Regularisation and counting of these two years towards qualifying service is stated to be not permissible on the plain language of the Article 226(1) read with Vol. (II) of CSR (inferior service).
3.
Heard.
It is conceded by the parties that in terms of Article 226(1) read with Schedule II Volume (2) of CSR as applicable to inferior service, the petitioner had to retire on 31.3.1990 after having attained the age of 58 years.
His case is not covered by 60 years retirement slab in so far as he has joined service much beyond the cut-off date of 10101966.
The question to be determined is how to treat two years period of overstay of petitioner and what about the pay, other dues paid to him for this period.
4.
Admittedly, petitioner is not at fault.
The petitioner continued till 3131992, in the Education Department due to the fault of the concerned officials and authorities.
He has discharged duties and worked for the period.
There is no complaint of non-performance or misconduct on his part.
In such circumstances, the employment of petitioner after 31.3.1990, would be obviously deemed under law as re-employment and therefore, the benefits which he has drawn beyond the retirement age cannot be recovered or taken back from him.
5.
In AIR 1994 SC 599 (State of Bihar v. Narasimha Sundram), where for failure to hold enquiry by the employer, the employee was allowed to continue beyond the retirement age, it was observed :-  "3.
So far the question of payment of arrears of salary is concerned, we do not find any merit in the contention of learned counsel for the appellant that the respondent can be refused his emoluments for the period in question as no proceedings were ever initiated for inquiry as to the alleged fraud played by the respondent on the department.
It is not denied that the respondent worked till 30th September, 1989 and in that view we confirm that part of the impugned judgment which refers to the salary.
The respondent should be paid his arrears of salary, if not already paid, within two months from today."
In Ali Mohd.
v. State and others, 1989 KLJ 8, a Division Bench of this Court held :-  "4........The period of overstay which occurred not due to any mala fide intention of the petitioner, but on account of negligence of the competent authority, has, therefore, to be treated as his re-employment.
He has already put in the labour for such period and also received his wages for the same which were due to him.
That being so, there is no administrative hitch in treating the period of overstay of the petitioner as re-employment.
For financial convenience, the competent authority shall have to pass an order accordingly."
In result, the payment of salary/emoluments paid to petitioner for the period of overstayal from 141990 to 3131992 shall not be recovered, after all he has rendered duty and performed work during the period and his case for the period shall be treated as re-employment.
The case of petitioner for pensionary and other retiral benefits including pension, leave encashment, salary, gratuity etc., consequent on his retirement on his superannuation on 31.3.1990, shall be processed and released to petitioner within a period of two months unless for cause shown period extended.
Disposed of.
Petition allowed.
