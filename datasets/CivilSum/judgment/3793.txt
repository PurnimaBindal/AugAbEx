GS Singhvi, J - The petitioners have sought intervention of this Court for protection of house rent allowance drawn by them as on 3081988.
At the relevant time, they were posted in District Ferozepur and drawing house rent allowance as per the instructions contained in circular No. 10/7/88/88- FPI/8014 dated 3081988 issued by the Department of Finance (Finance Personnel-I Branch), Government of Punjab.
Their grievance is that on account of fixation of their pay in the revised scales, the State Government is seeking to reduce the house rent allowance payable to them.
2.
Shri SC Chhabra relied on order dated 21.4.1995 passed by the Supreme Court in 1996(1) SCT 586 (P&H) : Civil Appeal No .........of 1995 (arising out of SLP (C) No. 9149 of 1992) - Mohinder Singh and others v. State of Punjab, order dated 9.3.1999 passed by a Division Bench of this Court in C.W.P.
No. 10243 of 1998 - Hem Raj Gupta and others v. State of Punjab and others and the judgment of the Single Bench in Pal Singh v. State of Punjab, 2001(2) SCT 778 and submitted that relief in similar terms may be given to the petitioners.
3.
Smt. Charu Tuli, Senior Deputy Advocate General, Punjab conceded that the case of the petitioners is similar to those of Hem Raj Gupta and others and Pal Singh, but argued that the judgments of the Division Bench and the Single Bench relied upon by Shri Chhabra require re-consideration because after having exercised option for higher pay scales, the petitioners cannot seek protection of house rent allowance paid to them in terms of the instructions issued by the State Government vide circular dated 3081988.
4.
I have given serious thought to the respective submissions.
In Mohinder Singh's case, the Supreme Court accepted the plea of the appellants for protection of house rent allowance payable in terms of circular dated 3081988 by recording the following order :-  "These appeals impugn a common judgment and order of the High Court of Punjab and Haryana.
The only question urged in these appeals is in relation to the house rent allowance (HRA) payable by the State of Punjab to its employees classified in the A, B, C, D categories of a circular dated 30th August, 1988.
The rates of HRA were thereby revised.
A proviso stated that HRA being drawn by employees at rates higher than those specified in the circular shall be protected, till their rate of HRA gets adjusted in these revised rates, it is not in dispute that this proviso protects the HRA that is being drawn if it is at a rate higher than that prescribed by the circular.
The protection ensures until the rate specified in the circular is enhanced to a rate higher than that presently drawn.
In view of this undisputed position, the appeals are allowed and the judgment and order of the High Court is set aside to the extent aforesaid.
The State Government shall give such protection to its appellant employees."
5.
In Hem Raj Gupta's case (supra), the Division Bench referred to the order of the Supreme Court in Mohinder Singh's case, as also order dated 2581998 passed in C.W.P.
No. 15315 of 1995 - Pawan Kumar Goyal and others v. State of Punjab and others and order dated 781998 passed in 2000(4) SCT 790 (P&H) : C.W.P.
No. 10211 of 1991 - Sadhu Ram and others v. State of Punjab and others and granted relief to the petitioners by making the following observations :-  "We have gone through the orders passed by the Supreme Court in Mohinder Singh's case (supra) and by this Court in Pawan Kumar Goyal's case (supra).
We have also gone through the order dated 781998 passed in C.W.P.
No. 10211 of 1991, Sadhu Ram and others v. State of Punjab and others and are of the opinion that the petitioners are entitled to relief in terms of the orders passed by the Apex Court and by this Court.
The learned Deputy Advocate General could not draw our attention to anything which may justify passing of a different order in the case of the petitioners."
6.
In my opinion, order dated 21.4.1995 passed by the Supreme Court in Mohinder Singh's case (supra), which is also the foundation of order dated 9.3.1999 passed by the Division Bench in the case of Hem Raj Gupta and others (supra) is binding on me.
Therefore, it is not possible to entertain the submission of the learned Senior Deputy Advocate General that the issue raised by the petitioners should be re-examined/re-considered.
Rather, I am convinced that the writ petition deserves to be disposed of in terms of that order.
7.
Hence, the writ petition is allowed with the direction that the petitioners shall be given the benefit of protection of house rent allowance in terms of the orders passed by the Supreme Court in Mohinder Singh's case (supra) and by the Division Bench in Hem Raj Gupta's case (supra).
Petition allowed.
