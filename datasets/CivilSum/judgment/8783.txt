NK Sodhi, J - This order will dispose of Letters Patent Appeal No. 222 of 1984 and Regular Second Appeal 1607 of 1991 which were ordered to be heard together by the Bench admitting the second appeal.
Since we are of the opinion that the respondent in these appeal is trying to recover his salary for the same period from two different institutions which he is not entitled to, it is necessary to state the facts in detail.
2.
Shri B Dutt (referred to hereinafter as 'the respondent') joined IB College, Panipat as a Senior Lecturer on 171972.
His appointment was on probation for 3 months which was letter extended.
By a letter dated 2321973 he was informed by the Management of the College that he had ceased to be a member of the staff of the collage on account of his having remained absent without leave.
It is alleged that he applied for leave through telegrams but the same was declined.
He challenged the letter dated 2321973 as also the extension of his probationary period before a civil court on May 14, 1975.
He also claimed a sum of Rs. 25,000/- as salary from 1111972 till the date of institution of the suit as, according to him, the salary had not been paid to him.
The suit was dismissed by the trial court and an appeal filed by him in the court of Additional District Judge, Karnal also met with the same fait.
He filed RSA 1046 of 1984 in this court in which the parties arrived at a settlement.
In terms of the compromise the respondent joined duties as a Senior Lecturer on 1731986 w.e.f.
1101972.
As regards the salary for the period he remained under termination (from 2321973 to 16.3.1986) the Management agreed to forward his claim to the Education Department of the State Government because 95% of the salaries are paid by the Government.
It was agreed between the parties that in case the Government accepted the claim of the respondent the management would also pay its share of 5% towards his salary.
In case the Government was not to accept his claim then the management too would not be liable to pay its share of 5%.
The regular second appeal was disposed of by a learned Single Judge on 1221986 in terms of the compromise arrived at between the parties.
The respondent is said to have submitted his joining report to the Managing Committee of IB College, Panipat on March 17, 1986 and soon thereafter he represented to the college authorities as also to the Director, Higher Education, Haryana for the fixation of his salary for the period 2321973 to 16.3.1986 during which period the remained under termination and pay the same to him.
Since the representation was not disposed of by the Director he filed C.W.P.
3721 of 1987 in this court which is pending.
The prayer made in this writ petition is for the issuance of a mandamus directing the Director and the Managing Committee of the College, respondents in the writ petition, to fix his salary in terms of the decree passed by this court in RSA 1046 of 1984 and pay the same to him.
3.
Now coming to the dispute in the present appeals.
After his services were terminated by the IB College, Panipat, the respondent joined on 2471975 Amar Shahid Baba Ajit Singh Jujhar singh Memorial College, Bela, District Ropar as Principal having been appointed by its Managing Committee.
The respondent did not disclose to the appellant that his services were terminated by the IB College, Panipat and that his litigation with that institution was pending.
Here also, the respondent was appointed on probation for a period of one year.
This period of probation was yet to expire when his services were terminated w.e.f.
621976 and a communication dated March 15, 1976 to this effect was sent to him informing him that his services stood terminated as he had absented himself from college since 621976 without obtaining any prior leave from the appellant.
Feeling dissatisfied with the order of termination the respondent moved the Director of Public Instructions, Punjab under Sections 3 and 4 of the Punjab Affiliated Colleges (Security of Service) Act, 1974 (for short, 'the Act') claiming that the order impugned by him was not an order of discharge simpliciter and that he had been removed from service on account of misconduct without holding any inquiry.
His petition was dismissed by the Director holding that the same was not maintainable as according to the Director only the management could approach him under Sections 3 and 4 of the Act for getting its action approved.
4.
Being aggrieved by the order of the Director, the respondent filed an appeal to the District Judge, Ropar under Section 4(4) of the Act who as per his order dated 2411977 allowed the same it being held that the order of termination amounted to removal of the respondent from the post of Principal and that his case fell within the ambit of Section 4 of the Act.
The learned District Judge further held that under Section 3 of the Act it was incumbent for the appellant to hold an inquiry in regard to allegations of absence of the respondent without leave after affording him a reasonable opportunity of being heard.
This not having been done, the order of termination was set aside and it was declared that the respondent shall be deemed to be continuing in service and would be entitled to the payment of his salary and other emoluments as Principal.
The respondent then resigned from the post of Principal w.e.f.
24.11.1977.
5.
It is against the aforesaid order of the District Judge that the appellant filed CWP 397 of 1977 in this Court.
The order was challenged on two grounds : firstly, that the respondent could not approach the Director under any provision of the Act and, secondly, on the ground that the services of the respondent, who was on probation, could be terminated during the period of his probation and when it was so done the provisions of the Act were not applicable in terms of Section 6(ii) thereof.
Both the contentions were negatived by a learned Single Judge and the writ petition was dismissed, Hence, this Letters Patent Appeal.
6.
Having succeeded before the District Judge in getting the order of termination set aside the respondent also filed a Civil suit in the Court of Sub Judge Ist Class, Ropar for the recovery of Rs. 45132/- as salary for the period 121976 to 31101977.
As already observed, the respondent had resigned as Principal w.e.f.
24.11.1977.
This suit was decreed for Rs. 4507760 on 3171987 and since the aforesaid letters patent appeal was pending in this court the decree was made subject to the decision in that appeal.
An appeal filed by the appellant against the decree of the trial court was also dismissed by the Additional District Judge, Ropar on 241991.
Against the judgment and decree of the lower appellate court the appellant filed RSA 1607 of 1991 which was admitted and ordered to be heard with the letters patent Appeal.
This is how both the appeals have come up for hearing before us.
7.
Mr. PS Patwalia, learned counsel for the appellant strenuously urged before us that the respondent did not disclose to the appellant the fact of his earlier employment with IB College, Panipat and the termination of his services from that institution and that the respondent by prosecuting the present litigation is trying to recover his salary for the same period both from the appellant as also from the IB College, Panipat, which he is not entitled to under the law.
The learned counsel referred to CWP 3721 of 1987 filed by the respondent in which he has kept back the factum of present litigation and equally in this litigation he did not disclose the pendency of CWP 3721 of 1987 in which he has already made a prayer for the payment of salary to him for the period 2321973 to 16.3.1986 which covers the period for which he is asking for salary in the present Regular Second Appeal.
We find merit in this contention.
There is no gain saying the fact that the respondent has filed CWP 3721 of 1987 in which the prayer made is for a direction to the State of Haryana and the Management of IB College, Panipat to fix and pay the salary to him for the period 2321973 to 16.3.1986.
Even though the respondent had succeeded in getting his order of termination set aside by the District Judge which order has been upheld by the learned Single Judge, the respondent cannot claim reinstatment with the appellant because he has already resigned from the post of Principal w.e.f.
24.11.1977.
By prosecuting the present R.S.A.
he is trying to recover from the appellant his salary for the period 121976 to 31101977 for which period he is also demanding salary from the State of Haryana and the Management of IB College, Panipat in CWP 3721 of 1987.
He cannot be allowed to do this.
Since his claim for the salary is pending in CWP 3721 of 1987 his claim in the suit out of which RSA 1607 of 1991 has arisen is wholly misconceived.
Consequently, we have no hesitation is allowing the appeal.
The suit filed by the respondent which led to this appeal, consequently stands dismissed.
8.
In view of the aforesaid finding of ours, it is not necessary for us to examine the other contentions advanced by Mr. Patwalia to the effect that the order of termination was one of the discharge simpliciter during the probationary period of the respondent and, therefore, the provisions of the Act were not attracted.
It is also not necessary for us to examine another contention of the appellant that it was not open to the respondent to approach the Director under the provisions of Sections 3 and 4 of the Act.
9.
In the result, RSA 1607 of 1991 is allowed and LPA 222 of 1984 having become infructuous is dismissed as such.
We may again make it clear that the respondent is not entitled to claim any salary from the appellant as he has already made a claim for salary for the same period from the State of Haryana and the Management of IB College, Panipat.
There is no order as to costs.
