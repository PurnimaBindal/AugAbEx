HS Bedi, J. (Oral) - Petitioner No. 1 was appointed as an Electrician on 2691969 in the Public Works Department (Buildings and Roads) Public Health Department whereas petitioner No. 2 was appointed as a Fitter Kulli on 7111971 in the same Department.
As both the petitioners were possessing the qualification of matriculation with ITI certificate of two years in the Electrical trade, petitioner No. 1 was promoted as Electrical Chargeman in the pay scale of Rs. 120-250 vide order dated 811973, Annexure P-1 to the petition; whereas petitioner No. 2 was promoted as Electrician vide order dated 931972, Annexure P-2 to the petition.
As the petitioners had the requisite qualifications to be treated as technicians in one of the six categories determined by the Punjab Pay Commission (popularly known as the Kothari Commission), certain pay scales were suggested for the categories to which the petitioners belonged.
The Punjab Government thereafter issued a circular, Annexure P-3 dated 421969, whereby qualified technicians in category IV (i.e., the category to which the petitioners claimed to belong), were given the pay scale of Rs. 140-300.
The petitioners, however, were granted the pay scale of Rs. 100-160 for the period upto 111978.
This pay scale was further revised to Rs. 400-600 w.e.f.
111978 and then to Rs. 950- 1800 w.e.f.
111986.
Petitioner No. 2 made a representation dated 2331988, Annexure P-4 to the petition, claiming that he was entitled to the pay scale of Rs. 140-300 which through subsequent revisions was now Rs. 1200-2100.
The representation was duly recommended by the department and forwarded to the State Government which accepted the same vide Annexure P-7 dated 2181990 and the concerned Executive Engineer was directed to make immediate compliance therewith.
Vide Annexure P.8 dated 1791990, petitioner No. 2 was granted the pay scale of Rs. 140-300 w.e.f.
931972 with subsequent revisions as and when they were made.
A similar representation was filed by petitioner No. 1 on 15111990 and he too was granted the revised pay scale w.e.f.
25.9.1969.
2.
The petitioners, however, received a notice dated 13.5.1994, Annexure P-9, wherein it was proposed that the revised pay scale given to them as a consequence of the order, Annexure P-8, was proposed to be withdrawn.
In this notice, it was, inter alia, mentioned that the pay scale of Rs. 140-300 was to be given to the Electricians working in the P.W.D.
(B&R) and Irrigation departments and not to those working in the P.W.D.
Public Health Department.
Petitioner No. 2 filed reply to the notice and in addition to various facts mentioned above, it was pointed out that a large number of employees of the P.W.D.
Public Health department had got the pay scale of Rs. 140-300 as revised from time to time and, as such, the stand of the department that the benefit of Annexure P-3 could not be given to the petitioners was not well founded.
This representation was, however, rejected vide order dated 381995, Annexure P-16, and it was ordered that in supersession of the earlier orders, the pay scales of Rs. 140-300 etc. granted to the petitioners w.e.f.
111978 was being withdrawn and that the excess payment be recovered.
The petitioners, thereafter filed CWP Nos. 13217 and 14016 of 1995 impugning the order, Annexure P-16.
In the reply filed by the respondents, an objection was taken that as the petitioners had been appointed initially on work-charged basis, they could not be given the benefit of the pay scales as revised and duly notified under the Punjab Civil Services (Revised Scales of Pay) Rules, 1969 and thereafter the higher scale in the subsequent revisions.
The aforesaid writ petitions were accordingly dismissed as withdrawn on 2721996 at the instance of the learned counsel for the petitioners so as to challenge the vires of the rules.
The present writ petition has been filed challenging the vires as also seeking the quashing of the order, Annexure P- 16, on various other points as well.
3.
The petition was admitted on 21.3.1996 and notice regarding stay was ordered to be issued for 1651996.
On May 29, 1996, however, the prayer for stay was declined by this Court but it was ordered that in case the petitioners succeed, they would be entitled to not only the repayment of money but also interest thereon.
4.
In the reply filed by the respondents, a new case has been set out and it has been averred that there were two different scales of pay for Electrician Grade-I and Electrician Grade-II and that Electricians Grade-I were entitled to a pay scale of Rs. 140-300; whereas Electricians Grade-II (such as the petitioners) were entitled to a pay scale of Rs. 100-160 w.e.f.
1.2.1968.
It has also been averred that the petitioners had been on the work-charged establishment till about 1977 and as such they could not be given the benefit of the pay scale of Rs. 140-300.
It has also been pointed out that as the duties of Electrician Grade-I and that of Electrician Grade-II were substantially different, no parity with regard to pay scales was admissible with respect to these two categories.
The plea of the petitioners that the Rules afore-mentioned were also ultra vires was denied.
5.
Mr. Surya Kant, the learned counsel for the petitioners, has not agitated the question as to the vires of the Rules but has argued that once the State Government had taken a decision vide Annexure P-7 that the pay scale of Rs. 140-300 duly revised from time to time was to be made available to the petitioners, the said pay scale could not be withdrawn by the Superintending Engineer at his level vide Annexure P-16.
He has also urged that the respondents' contention that the petitioners were Electricians Grade-II was untenable on account of various documents put on record with the replication.
6.
I have heard learned counsel for the parties and have also gone through the record.
It will be evident from Annexure P-7 that the State Government had accepted the recommendation made by the department that the benefit of the pay scale of Rs. 140-300 was to be given to the petitioners and consequently the said benefit had been conferred on the petitioners vide Annexure P-8 dated 1791990 issued by the Executive Engineer, Mechanical Public Health Circle, Patiala.
To my mind, therefore, issuance of a show-cause notice, Annexure P- 9, by the Superintending Engineer, Mechanical Public Health, Patiala, and the subsequent order, Annexure P-16, withdrawing the benefit of the pay scale of Rs. 140-300 made by the aforesaid officer under the authority of the Chief Engineer, Punjab, was not justified and legally unsustainable.
There seems to be equal merit in Mr. Surya Kant's assertion that the stand taken by the respondents in the present writ petition was at variance with that taken in the previous writ petition and the argument that petitioner No. 2 in particular was an Electrician Grade-II and, therefore, not entitled to the higher pay scale was erroneous.
In this connection, Mr. Surya Kant has referred to the documents, Annexure P-21 dated 931972; Annexure P-23 dated 171981 (regularising his services w.e.f.
141977); Annexure P-22 (copy of the extract of the gradation list showing petitioner No. 2 to be an Electrician Grade-I); and Annexure P-26 dated 12101993 (confirming him as such in the department).
To my mind, in the light of the documents on record, it is evident that petitioner No. 2 was an Electrician Grade-I No reliance whatsoever could be placed by the respondents on Annexure P-25 which identifies petitioner No. 2 as Electrician Grade-II as the same came into existence during the pendency of the present litigation and the petitioner's entry at serial No. 8-A appears to have been made as an interpolation.
I am, therefore, of the opinion that for this additional reason as well, this writ petition deserves to be allowed qua petitioner No. 2.
7.
The writ petition is accordingly allowed, the order, Annexure P-16, is quashed with consequential benefits to flow to both the petitioners in the light of the interim order dated 2951996, I accordingly grant interest to the petitioners at the rate of 12% per annum from the date of withdrawal of the pay scale to the date of payment.
It is further made clear that arrears would be paid to the petitioners within three months from the date a certified copy of the order is made available to the respondents.
No costs.
Petition allowed.
