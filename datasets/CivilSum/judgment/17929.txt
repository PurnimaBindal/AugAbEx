Jawahar Lal Gupta, J - These three writ petitions viz. CWP Nos. 10894 of 1990 and 363 and 4016 of 1991, challenge the action of the State Government in not taking into consideration Special Pay while determining the amount of Dearness Allowance admissible to the officer/officials serving in different departments.
CWP No. 10894 of 1990 has been filed by a former Assistant District Attorney and now an Advance of this Court.
All the three petitions were argued by him on behalf of the petitioners.
The facts as stated in this petition may be briefly noticed.
2.
The petitioner was working as an Assistant District Attorney under Department of Prosecution in the State of Haryana.
This post originally carried the scale of Rs. 350-650 with a special pay of Rs. 75/- Vide orders dated March 30, 1982, a photocopy of which is placed on record as "Mark-A", the existing pay scales of various posts were revised.
The pay scale of the post of Assistant District Attorney was raised to Rs. 700-1250 and Rs. 750- 1450 w.e.f.
April 1, 1979.
Both the scales carried a special pay of Rs. 75/- with effect from February 1, 1981, the special pay was raised to Rs. 100/-.
This pay scale was further revised and raised to Rs. 1640-2000 w.e.f.
January 1, 1986.
The special pay of Rs. 100/- was raised to Rs. 200/-.
It is the claim of the petitioner that the special pay attached to the post of Assistant District Attorney was not that as defined under Rule 252 of the Punjab Civil Services Rules Volume-I, Part-1, but had been granted in lieu of a higher time scale and thus forms a part of the scale itself.
3.
Prior to the revision of the scales w.e.f.
January 1, 1986, while paying Dearness Allowance, special pay was treated as part of pay.
By way of illustration, it may be mentioned that the scale of Rs. 700-1250 with special pay of Rs. 100/- was treated as a scale of Rs. 800-1350 and the dearness allowance was accordingly determined by the respondents.
However, after the revision of the pay scales, the dearness allowance is being paid only on the basic pay and the element of special pay is not being taken into consideration.
This has been challenged by the petitioner as being arbitrary, unfair and violative of Article 14 of the Constitution.
4.

It is claimed that the special pay granted to the cadre of Assistant District Attorney was not in lieu of a higher scale, but had been granted on account of the arduous nature of duties.
A copy of the letter, dated March 30, 1982 ("Marks-A") was produced to show that wherever the Government had granted special pay in lieu of higher time scale a specific mention was made.

A perusal of the above shows that wherever the Government had granted special pay in lieu of a higher time scale, a specific mention to that effect was made.
In the case of an Assistant District Attorney, it was not mentioned that the special pay had been granted in lieu of the higher time scale.
In the written statement it has been further mentioned that special pay has been specifically defined under Rule 252 and did not form a part of pay as defined under Rule 244.
It is averred that the pay scales of the Haryana Govt. employees were revised w.e.f.
January 1, 1986 on the pattern of the Government of India on the basis of the report submitted by the Fourth Pay Commission.
In the Government of India, special pay was not treated as part of pay for any purpose including the grant of Dearness Allowance etc.
The State of Haryana following the same pattern decided not to treat special pay as part of pay for the purpose of grant of Dearness Allowance.
According to the respondent, similar position existed in the neighbouring States of Punjab and Himachal Pradesh also.
Accordigly, it was maintained that the writ petition deserved to be dismissed.
6.
I have heard Mr. JS Yadav, the petitioner and Mt.
Jaswant Singh, Assistant Advocate General, Haryana for the respondents.
The relevant provisions of the rules provide as under :  Rule 244 defines "Pay" as under :-  244.
(a) Pay means the amount drawn monthly by a government employee as :  (i) the pay, other than special pay or pay granted in view of his personal qualifications, which has been sanctioned for a post held by him substantively or in an officiating capacity or to which he is entitled by reason of his position in a cadre; and (ii) Overseas pay, special pay and personal pay; and (iii) any other emoluments which may be specially classed as pay by the competent authority.
(b) ............. (i) ............. (ii) .............
Note 1 : Judicial pay has been classed as pay under rule 244(a)(iii).
Note 2 : ..........
Note 3 : ..........  Rule 252, which defines "Special Pay" reads as under :-  "252.
Special Pay means an addition, of nature of pay to the emoluments of a post or of a Government employee granted in consideration of :-  (a) the specially arduous nature of the duties; or (b) the Specific addition to the work or responsibility and includes non-practising allowance granted to doctors in lieu of private practice."
7.
The petitioner claims that the special pay granted to him was in lieu of the higher time scale and, therefore, falls within the definition of pay as given in Rule 244.
The respondents claim that the special pay had been granted to the petitioner in lieu is specifically excluded from pay.
8.
A perusal of the letter dated March 30, 1982, the relevant portion of which has already been extracted, shows that special pay had been granted in respect of various posts.
However, wherever the special pay was granted in lieu of the higher time scale, a specific entry to that effect was made.
So far as the post of Assistant District Attorney is concerned, there is no mention that the special pay of Rs. 100/-, which had been specifically raised to Rs. 200/- vide order dated January 11, 1986 (Annexure P.3), had been granted in lieu of a higher time scale.
It is thus clear that the special pay granted to the petitioner was not in lieu of a higher time scale.
It thus fell within the definition of special pay as mentioned above.
`Special Pay' has been specifically excluded from the definition of 'Pay'.
It is clear from the perusal of the rules that pay does not include special pay which was being drawn by the petitioner.
9.
The State of Haryana has granted dearness allowance.
The rate of dearness allowance has been revised from time to time.
Vide letter dated September 27, 1985 (Annexure P.5) while granting dearness allowance, it was specifically mentioned that the terms 'Pay' would include basic pay, personal pay, special pay, overseas pay and deputation allowance where admissible as defined in Rule 244 of CSP Volume-I, Part-I.
Thereafter, the dearness allowance was granted vide letter dated April 29, 1987.
The rates of dearness allowance were fixed with reference to the basic pay.
In this order, it was specifically mentioned that basic pay for the purpose of calculation of dearness allowance shall be as defined in Rule 244(a)(i).
A copy of this letter is at Annexure P.6.
In all the subsequent orders, viz., Annexures P.7 to P11, the dearness allowance was granted with reference to pay only on the same pattern as was done by the order, dated April 29, 1987.
The Government is.
the best judge of the needs of the employees.
In its wisdom it has chosen to grant to benefit of dearness allowance at different rates as fixed from time to time on the basis of pay as defined in Rule 244.
Special Pay has not been treated as part of pay.
In the process it does not appear to have violated any provision of the rules governing the petitioner.
In the absence of any violation of law, the claim made by the petitioner cannot be sustained.
10.
The petitioner claims that the action is arbitrary.
He states that special pay is taken into consideration while determining the rate of pension.
According to the petitioner, it cannot be excluded while determining the benefit of dearness allowance.
I regret my inability to accept the contention.
Pay, pension, gratuity are various conditions of service, which have been determined by rules.
If special pay has been included for the computation of pension, it does not necessarily follow that it has to be included even for the purpose of computing the amount of dearness allowance.
The Government had granted the benefit by its order, dated September 27, 1985, Subsequently, it has chosen to deny the benefit by its orders, dated April 29, 1987 etc. As already observed, the action of the Government did not violate any provision of the rules.
That being so, the claim made on behalf of the petitioner that special pay having been included for the purpose of computation of pension, the Government was bound to include it even while computing the amount of dearness allowance cannot be accepted.
11.
Nor I am satisfied that the action of the Government is arbitrary.
On an enquiry from me, the petitioner stated that prior to the revision of pay scales w.e.f.
111986, he was drawing total emoluments of Rs. 2,25950 in the pay scale of Rs. 700-1250.
After revision of pay scales w.e.f.
January 1, 1986, his emoluments had risen to Rs. 2620/-.
There was thus an increase of Rs. 26050.
The petitioner has gained by the revision.
He has not been put to any loss.
I cannot find any element of arbitrariness in the action of the respondents.
12.
In view of the above, the writ petition is dismissed with costs.
The costs are assessed at Rs. 1,000/- in each case.
Petition dismissed.
