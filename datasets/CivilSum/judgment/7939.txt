Amar Bir Singh Gill, J - This bunch of Civil Writ Petition Nos. 6234, 7057, 7130, 7160, 7297, 7458, 7781, 7797, 7798, 7800, 7825, 8208, and 8523 of 2001 on identical issues of law and fact are being disposed of by this common judgment and learned counsel for the parties concede that this judgment will govern them all.
The facts of these writ petitions are not in dispute and as such for the purpose of disposal of these writ petitions, facts from Civil Writ Petition No. 6234 of 2001 are being referred to.
2.
Petitioner Satnam Kaur being victim of 1984 Anti-Sikh riots was appointed on the post of Peon against the vacant post in the Primary Health Centre, Balachaur, in the year 1992 vide appointment letter copy of which is Annexure P-1.
The term of appointment of the petitioner for 89 days was successively extended vide different orders, copies of which were Annexure P-2 to P-8.
Government of Punjab in its Department of Personnel and Administrative Reforms issued instructions dated 18011995, Annexure P-9, for regularisation of Class III and Class IV employees who have served the department for 240 days upto 31121994.
The petitioner was directed to appear for interview before the Selection Committee on 16051995 for regularisation of her service.
Her case on being recommended for regularisation of service, Civil Surgeon, Nawanshahar, issued appointment letter on June 4, 1997, copy of the which is Annexure P-13.
A number of candidates, who had appeared before the Selection Committee and could not be regularised, filed Civil Writ Petition Nos. 8753 and 11116 of 1997 and these writ petitions were disposed of by this Court by a common judgment dated 10112000 in Civil Writ Petition No. 11116 of 1997 vide which the appointments of the respondents in the writ petition against Class IV posts were quashed.
On the basis of the aforesaid judgment of this Court, the respondents passed the impugned order Annexure P-15 on 23.04.2001 dispensing with the services of the petitioner.
The petitioner has approached this Court in its writ jurisdiction under Article 226 of the Constitution of India for quashing of the impugned order Annexure P-15 on various grounds, and inter alia, on the ground that the petitioner was never issued any show cause notice before passing of the impugned order.
The petitioner was never a party to the decision of this Court on the basis of which the services of the petitioner are sought to be dispensed with.
The petitioner relied upon the ratio of decision of this Court in Civil Writ Petition No. 6975 of 1976, titled Baldev Singh and others v. The State of Punjab and others, decided on 01-11- 1979.
The petitioner has further prayed for issuance of writ of mandamus directing the respondent authorities to allow the petitioner to rejoin duty forthwith.
3.
The case of the respondents in the written statement is that the services of the petitioner have been terminated w.e.f.
23-4-2001 in compliance with the decision of this Court in Civil Writ Petition No. 11116 of 1997.
4.
We have heard learned counsel for the parties.
5.
The petitioner of this writ petition as well as other petitioners in the connected writ petitions, admittedly, were working on ad hoc basis and their cases for regularisation on the basis of government instructions, copy of which is Annexure P-9, were considered.
These instructions were issued on 1811995.
The condition for regularisation of the services of ad hoc class IV employees was if they had completed minimum period of 240 days on 31121994 and were in service on that date.
The notional break in service not exceeding 30 days in that year was to be ignored.
It is not disputed that the petitioner of this writ petition and other petitioners in the connected writ petitions fulfilled the eligibility criteria and their cases were covered under the Government instructions Annexure P-9.
In fact, under the government instructions, the departments were to consider the cases of such employees who fulfil the criteria for regularisation.
Thee was no question of any selection to be made by any Selection Committee.
Rather the cases were to be considered and recommended for regularisation.
The services of these petitioners were, admittedly, regularised after considering their cases individually.
One Balvir Chand in Civil Writ Petition No. 11116 of 1997 had challenged the selection of 18 employees whose services were regularised on the basis of bias, favour and mala fides which was quashed by this Court.
A copy of the said decision in Annexure P-14.
The challenge to the regularisation of the services of the respondents in Balvir Chand's case (supra) was on the basis of specific allegations against each of the respondents which have been duly discussed in the judgment of this Court Annexure P-14.
Admittedly, the petitioners of all present writ petitions were not impleaded as respondents in this case nor their selection was challenged specifically in any manner.
The petitioners, in fact, were the necessary parties as the decision was likely to effect the regularisation of their services.
6.
The petitioners relied upon the ratio of decision of this Court in Baldev Singh's case (supra), copy of which has been placed on the record.
The issue involved in the aforesaid writ petition was similar to the one in hand.
It pertained to selection of Constables for the Cadre School Course on the basis of existing instructions.
Some of the failed Constables challenged the government instructions on the basis of which selection for the Cadre School Course was made, however, impleading some of the selectees.
The Court disposed of the writ petition and the instructions were held to be invalid and on the basis of the decision, the selectees were sought to be reverted.
A Division Bench of this Court considered the applicability of the decision of the Court on the Constables promoted as Head Constables who were not arrayed as respondents in the writ petition.
Relying upon the decision of the Supreme Court in case reported as Devadasan v Union of India and another, AIR 1964 SC 179, Apex Court held that the petitioners who were neither party or privy to the decision cannot be adversely affected by the decision therein and quashed the reversion of the petitioners.
Admittedly, the petitioners of these writ petitions are stated to be adversely affected by the judgment of this Court in Balvir Chand's case (supra) when the petitioners were not impleaded as party.
As such, the ratio of decision of this Court in Baldev Singh's case (supra) is equally applicable in this case and the services of the petitioners cannot be dispensed with on the basis of the decision in Balvir Chand's case (supra).
Otherwise, it is not disputed that the case of the petitioners is, admittedly, covered under the government instructions for regularisation of service.
7.
In view of what has been stated above, these writ petitions are allowed.
The impugned order dispensing with the services of the petitioners are quashed.
The respondents are further directed to allow the petitioners to rejoin their duties.
Needful shall be done within two months.
Petitions allowed.
