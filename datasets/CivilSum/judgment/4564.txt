Amarjeet Chaudhary, J - The State of Punjab in term of provisions of rule 3(1) of Punjab Civil Services (Premature Retirement) Rules, 1975, (hereinafter referred to as 'the Rules') prematurely retired Shri Manohar Lal, Executive Engineer, Public Health, from Government service with effect from 23rd June, 1989, in public interest, consequent upon premature retirement, Shri Manohar Lal was paid three months' pay and allowances at the same rate at which he was drawing immediately before the date of retirement in lieu of 3 months notice.
A copy of the said order of premature retirement is Annexure P-7 to the writ petition.
2.
The relevant facts of the case are that the petitioner was appointed as Sub-Divisional Engineer Public Health Department through Punjab Public Service Commission who joined as such on 9th February, 1971.
The petitioner was promoted as Executive Engineer on ad hoc basis vide order dated 2nd February, 1977 and order for regular appointment as Executive Engineer in PES Class-I was issued, on 27th April, 1981.
3.
The challenge to the premature retirement order is that there was no reason with the authorities to pass the impugned order and the petitioner had during the last 10 years carried up to 85 per cent good/very good reports.
The adverse remarks conveyed to the petitioner are in fact of very short duration.
The other submission is that the petitioner was allowed to cross efficiency bar and according to the counsel only good officer is allowed to cross the efficiency bar.
In support of his contention the learned counsel for the petitioner has relied upon Punjab State and others v. Kulwantbir Singh, 1993(2) SCT 567 (P&H) (FB) : 1993(2) RSJ 307.
The counsel further contended that action of the respondents is contrary to the decision of the Supreme Court in Brij Mohan Singh Chopra v. State of Punjab, AIR 1987 SC 948 and KK Vaid v. State of Haryana, 1990(1) SLR 1.
4.
The respondent-State has filed a detailed written statement and has controverted the stand of the petitioner.

The plea of the learned counsel for the petitioner that he was allowed to cross the efficiency bar does not hold good in view of the Full Bench decision in Kulwinder Singh's case (supra) in which reference to Brij Mohan's case (supra) KK Vaid v. State of Haryana, 1990(1) SLR 1 and latest Supreme Court decision in Balkuntha Nath Dass v. Chief District Medical Officer, 1992(2) SCT 92 : AIR 1992 SC 1020 was made.
The Supreme Court had laid the following principles :  (i) An order of compulsory retirement is not a punishment.
It implies no stigma nor any suggestion of misbehaviour.
(ii) The order has to be passed by the Government on forming the opinion that it is in the public interest to retire a government servant compulsorily.
The order is passed on the subjective satisfaction of the Government.
(iii) Principles of natural justice have no place in the context of an order of compulsory retirement.
This does not mean that judicial scrutiny is excluded altogether.
While the High Court or this Court would not examine the matter as an appellate Court, they may interfere if they are satisfied that the order is passed (a) mala fide, or (b) that it is based on no evidence or (c) that it is arbitrary in the sense that no reasonable person would form the requisite opinion on the given material in short, if it is found to be perverse order.
xx xx xx xx  Crossing of efficiency bar in view of the abovesaid principles, loses its significance and the plea of the learned counsel for the petitioner that only latest record is to be seen does not hold good in view of Baikunath Nath Dass's case (supra) in which it was held that even uncommunicated remarks can also be taken into consideration and then the matter of compulsory retirement is the subjective satisfaction of the Government and the Government should consider the entire service record.
10.
The High Level Screening Committee examined the case of the petitioner.
I do not find any irregularity in the proceedings nor there is any allegation of mala fide against any of the members of the Committee.
As such no interference in the findings of the Committee by way of judicial review is called for.
The age of' retirement of a Govt. employee is 58 years but by invoking the provisions of Rule 3(1) (a) of Punjab Civil Service (Premature Retirement) Rules, 1975, the date is preponed on the appraisal of the service record of a Govt. employee.
The premature retirement is not a punishment.
The Govt. employee is given all the retiral benefits.
As a consequence, the writ petition is dismissed.
However, there will be no order as to cost.
Petition dismissed.
