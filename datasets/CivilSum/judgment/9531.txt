RL Anand, J - Rajender Singh, ex-Junior Engineer, working in the office of Block Development and Panchayat Officer, Bhattu Kalan has filed the present writ under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India for the issuance of a writ of Certiorari for the quashment of the impugned orders Annexures P3, P6 and P8 and also prayed for a writ of Mandamus directing the respondents to regularise his services from the date of his appointment.
2.
The case set up by the petitioner is that he was appointed as Junior Engineer on ad hoc basis through employment exchange on 361980 in the office of Block Development and Panchayat Officer, Mohindergarh.
The Govt. of Haryana issued notification dated 1591982 to the effect that all the ad hoc government employees having two years service at their credit will be regularised.
In the year 1984 and 1987 similar type of notifications were issued by the Haryana Government.
The notification dated 1521987 stipulates that he ad hoc government employee who had completed two years service on 1111986 will be regularised.
From time to time the State of Haryana had been issuing the various notifications by way of policy decisions to regularise the services of the ad hoc employees.
The latest notification which was issued by the State Government was dated 2821991 wherein Class-III employees who had worked for two years as on 31121990 were regularised.
The petitioner further alleges that earlier he was appointed in the pay scale of Rs. 600-1100 which was revised to Rs. 1400-2300 on 141989 w.e.f.
111986.
The petitioner used to contribute to General Provident Fund but his seniority and pay was never fixed by the respondents.
He alleges that he was conveyed the adverse remarks in his ACR pertaining to the year 1981-82 vide letter dated 1.7.1986 by the respondent No. 3.
He filed representation dated 2771986 against the remarks explaining that as per Haryana Govt. instructions on the subject, the adverse remarks should be conveyed promptly and that there was no truth in the said remarks and the same be expunged.
He further alleges that during his entire service career he was being treated as a regular employee for all intents and purposes.
He came to know for the first time that he was still being treated as an ad hoc employee when he received the written statement filed in C.W.P.
No. 5418 of 1991 filed by him for seeking direction to the respondents for fixation of seniority and pay.
The petitioner further alleges that the respondents rejected his representation dated 2771986 on 1551992 illegally and arbitrarily without giving him any opportunity to explain his position.
The order rejecting the representation is a non-speaking one.
He was never made aware the basis on which his ACR for the year 1981-82 was recorded adversely.
It was conveyed to him after a lapse of more than five years.
He reiterates that in the year 1981-82 he worked honestly and sincerely and he did not give any cause of complaint.
The said ACR has been recorded against the instructions dated 151975 issued by the Govt. of Haryana in which there is a clear reference that adverse remarks should be conveyed to the affected employee promptly and that adverse remarks beyond three years should not be conveyed to the official concerned.
But inspite of the instructions issued by the Government, the respondent No. 2 did not accept the representation of the petitioner.
This was the sole adverse entry on the basis of which the termination order dated 19.5.1992 was passed.
The petitioner further alleges that the impugned order is stigmatic.
With above broad allegations, the petitioner has prayed that the adverse orders Annexure P3 and the order dated 19.5.1992 vide which his service were terminated be set aside.
Notice of the writ was given to the State which filed the reply and denied the allegations.
The State tried to justify its stand by the stating that the integrity of the petitioner was found doubtful by his superiors during the year 1981-82 and in these circumstances the impugned order dated 19.5.1992 was passed and the said order is not stigmatic in nature.
However, some delay has been caused because of the official procedure which was supposed to be adopted by the State Government.
3.
I have heard Mr. RK Malik, Advocate, on behalf of the petitioner, Mr. Sultan Singh, AAG, Haryana, for the respondents and with their assistance I have gone through the records of this case.
4.
The challenge in this case has been given to the orders Annexures P3, P6 and P8.
Annexure P3 is letter dated 1.6.1986 vide which adverse ACR was communicated to the petitioner pertaining to the year 1981-82 and the integrity of the petitioner was declared doubtful.
Further it was mentioned in this letter that petitioner does not respect his boss; he is not faithful; he leaves his headquarters without permission and he is a habitual late comer.
Finally, he was graded below average.
Annexure P6 is the letter dated 1551992 vide which the petitioner was informed that the competent authority after going through his representation dated 2771986 has rejected the same.
Annexure P8 is the order of termination dated 19.5.1992 and the operative part of this order can be referred as follows :-  "The representation against the adverse remarks appearing in the ACR of 1981- 82 has been considered and rejected and Sh. Rajinder Singh has been informed of this vide registered letter No. 3689, dated 1551992.
According to one of the conditions of Haryana Govt.
Notification dated 1911984 for regularisation, the work and conduct of employees should be over-all of good category and no disciplinary proceedings are pending against them.
As he has been adjudged as dishonest person in the ACR for the year 1981-82 and his representation against these adverse remarks has been considered and rejected by the competent authority, the work and conduct of Sh. Rajinder Singh is not found that of good category and he does not fulfil the condition of regularisation, therefore, the services of Sh. Rajinder Singh ad hoc JE is dispensed with."
5.
The above summary of facts would show that the ACR for the year 1981-82 became the foundation of the adverse action which was taken against the petitioner vide order dated 19.5.1992.
There is no plausible explanation coming on the record why the ACR of 1981-82 was conveyed to the petitioner as on 1.7.1986.
The petitioner was not negligent and he made a prompt representation on 2771986 and tried to dispute the ACR.
Still the State took six years practically in disposing of that representation when it was rejected on 1551992 and within five days of the rejection order the impugned order Annexure P8 was passed.
The conveying of the adverse ACR and the decision of the representation on the part of the State-authorities is not a formulated.
A public servant has the right to know if something is working against him in the department.
If his superior is not satisfied with his work and conduct and if the superior of a particular public servant has formulated some adverse opinion about the work and conduct of a public servant, such a public servant has the right to know not only the ultimate decision but also the basis formulating that decision.
In the absence of such communication, a public servant can always take into his head that his work and conduct in the department is satisfactory.
In the present case, firstly the department has conveyed the adverse ACR after a lapse of four years and then rejected the same after a lapse of six years.
In such circumstances, it can be held that the ACR which was relied upon by the department has become stale and had lost its sting especially when the petitioner had not received any adverse communication or adverse remarks from 1982 upto the date of his termination.
6.
In Baidyanath Mahapatra v. State of Orissa and anr., 1989(4) SLR 220, the Hon'ble Supreme Court has held that where the adverse remarks are communicated to the servant after several years, such belated communication of the entries results into the denial of reasonable opportunity to improve his performance and such belated communication of adverse entries are not proper.
The Hon'ble Supreme Court has deprecated the practice in sending adverse ACRs after years together.
Reliance can also be placed on Himangsu Sekhar Jha v. State of West Bengal and ors., 1979(1) SLR 837, where it was observed by the Calcutta High Court that if there is a delay in communication of the adverse remarks, such adverse remarks should no longer weigh with the authorities in considering the prospective career of the public servant.
In this view of the matter, this Court is of the opinion that the impugned order dated 19.5.1992 which is solely based on the adverse entry of 1981-82 cannot be upheld especially when the Government has not been able to place on record any other additional circumstances from which an opinion can be formulated regarding the integrity of the petitioner.
It need not be mentioned here that the petitioner served for more than 10 years w.e.f.
141982 to 2051992 and he did not earn any displeasure, whatsoever, of any of his superiors.
There can be some honest mistake on the part of a supervisory officer, who wrote the ACR for the year 1981-82.
Even in the year 1980-81 when the petitioner was on ad hoc basis, he did not earn any bad report of his superior during the relevant year.
Thus the impugned order dated 19.5.1992 cannot stand in the eyes of law being stigmatic in nature and the same is hereby set aside.
7.
The ancillary point which arises for consideration is what further relief the petitioner is entitled.
The learned counsel for the petitioner concedes and states at the bar that the petitioner is not entitled to any back wages w.e.f.
2051992 till the date he joins the duty in pursuance of this judgment.
Otherwise also, this Court is of the opinion that the petitioner is not entitled to any back wages because he has not worked against this post.
However, he shall be entitled to regularisation of his services as per the policy of the State Government.
Resultantly, the writ is partly allowed, the orders Annexures P3, P6 and P8 are hereby set aside and directions are given to the State to allow the petitioner to join the duty against the post of Junior Engineer.
The petitioner shall not be entitled to any back wages.
However, he will be entitled to the continuity of service and his service are liable to be regularised as per the policy of the State Government.
No order as to costs.
Petition partly allowed.
