MM Kumar, J - The petitioner was a member of disciplined force being Assistant Sub-Inspector of Police.
She has filed the instant petition under Article 226 of the Constitution praying for quashing order dated 1852005 (P-3), passed by the Senior Superintendent of Police, Rupnagar-respondent No. 5 dismissing her from service in pursuance to a departmental inquiry in which she has been found guilty of the charge that she had taken Rs. 8,00,000/- from Gurjit Singh son of Bahadur Singh, resident of Boodri, District Ludhiana and gave that amount to Ravinder Singh Gulati, for sending Gurjit Singh to America in connivance with her husband Sukhdev Singh alias Sukha, Ravinder Singh Gulati, Preeti Gulati and Manjit Singh.
Order dated 15122005 (P-5), passed by the Deputy Inspector General of Police, Ludhiana Range, Ludhiana-respondent No. 4 as well as order dated 852006, passed by the Inspector General of Police, Jalandhar Zone, Jalandhar-respondent No. 3 (P-7) are also subject matter of challenge in the instant petition, wherein the order dated 1852005 (P-)) has been upheld maintaining the dismissal of the petitioner.
A further prayer has been made for quashing orders dated 288200 7 (P-9) and 952008 (P-12) whereby mercy petition and subsequent representation made by the petitioner have been dismissed being not maintainable under the Punjab Police Rules, 1934.
Still further, a prayer has been made for directing the respondents to reinstate the petitioner with all consequential benefits.
2.
Brief facts may first be noticed.
On 1061985, the petitioner was appointed as Lady Constable in Punjab Police.
She was promoted as ASI (ORP) in the year 1989 and promoted as such on regular basis in 2002 after having passed Intermediate School Course in October, 2001.
3.
An FIR No. 81, dated 16122003, under Sections 406/420/120B IPC read with 13(i)(d) and Section 13(2) of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, was registered at Police Station, Vigilance Bureau, Ludhiana, against the petitioner, her husband and four others, with the allegation that they took Rs. 8,00,000/- from Gurjit Singh for sending him to United States of America.
On 1032004, departmental proceedings were initiated against the petitioner and a charge-sheet on the same allegation was issued to her.
4.
After holding regular departmental inquiry and recording statements of various departmental and defence witnesses, the Enquiry Officer submitted his report on 892004 and reached the conclusion that the petitioner was guilty of charge (P-2).
Agreeing with the findings recorded by the Enquiry Officer, the Senior Superintendent of Police, Rupnagar-respondent No. 5, who is the punishing authority of the petitioner, issued a show cause notice to the petitioner, which was duly handed over to the father-in-law of the petitioner, on 2932005.
The petitioner furnished written reply to the show cause notice.
She was also afforded an opportunity of personal hearing, which she did not avail.
After considering the written reply to the show cause notice, the punishing authority-respondent No. 5 dismissed the petitioner from service, vide order dated 1852005.
The punishing authority after perusing the past service record of the petitioner also specifically noticed in the order of dismissal that she had remained absent for 1 days from 1672001 to 1772001 and was censured for that lapse.
5.
The aforementioned order was challenged in appeal (P-4) and the Deputy Inspector General of Police, Ludhiana Range, Ludhiana-respondent No. 4, dismissed the appeal on 15122005 (P-5).
The petitioner then availed the remedy of revision (P-6).
But the same was also dismissed by the Inspector General of Police, Jalandhar Zone, Jalandhar-respondent No. 3, vide order dated 852006 (P-7).
6.
The petitioner also made an un-successful attempt by filing a mercy petition (P-8) before the Director General of Police, Punjab-respondent No. 2, which was dismissed on 2882007 (P-9).
The petitioner again made a representation to the Principal Secretary, Department of Home Affairs, Punjab.
Chandigarh-respondent No. 1 (P-10).
It has been claimed by the petitioner that in the meanwhile, the learned Judge, Special Court, Ludhiana, acquitted the petitioner in the criminal case arising out of FIR No. 81, dated 1612003, vide order dated 23 22007 (P-11) i.e. prior to rejection of mercy petition of the petitioner.
Despite supply of copy of order dated 23.2.2007, passed by the Special Judge, Ludhiana, the representation of the petitioner has been rejected vide order dated 952008 (P-12).
7.
Having heard learned counsel for the petitioner and perusing the paper book, we are of the view that there is no merit in the instant petition and the same deserves to be dismissed.
The petitioner has been dismissed from service after conducting departmental proceedings against her and due procedure in accordance with rules has been followed.
Agreeing with the findings recorded by the Enquiry Officer, the punishing authority has passed the order of dismissal after issuance of show cause notice to the petitioner to which she has filed a written reply.
Even an opportunity of personal hearing was also afforded to her, which she failed to avail.
8.
It is undisputed that the punishing authority-respondent No. 5 passed the order of punishment on 1852005 (P-3), which was challenged in appeal.
The appeal was dismissed on 15122005 by the Deputy Inspector General of Police, Ludhiana Range, Ludhiana-respondent No. 4 (P-5).
Even the revision petition filed by the petitioner was dismissed on 852006 (P-7).
The instant petition has been filed on 17.7.2008 by claiming that subsequent mercy petition and representation (P-8 & P-10 respectively) filed by the petitioner were dismissed on 2882007 (P-9) and 952008 (P-12).
It is appropriate to mention that after availing all statutory remedies in the form of appeal and revision, no remedy of filing mercy petition or representation is available to the petitioner under the Punjab Police Rules, 1934.
The petitioner has filed the mercy petition and representation inviting orders dated 2882007 and 952008.
It is, thus, apparent that there is inordinate and unexplained delay in challenging orders dated 1852005 (P-3), 15122005 (P-5) and 852006 (P-7).
It is well settled that sending of representations repeatedly would not extend the period of limitation provided for challenging the orders passed by the Government.
In that regard, reliance may be placed on a Constitutional Bench judgment of Hon'ble the Supreme Court in the case of SS Rathore v. State of MP, (1989)4 SCC 582.
Therefore, the writ petition would not be maintainable on account of delay and laches on the part of the petitioner.
9.
The legal position can be viewed from another angle also.
Hon'ble the Supreme Court has crystallised the `Wednesbury' principle in a Seven-Judges Bench judgment in the case of Rameshwar Prasad (VI) v. Union of India, (2006)2 SCC 1, by bserving as under :  "242.
The Wednesbury [Associated Provincial Picture Houses Ltd v. Wednesbury Corpn., (1948) 1 KB 223] principle is often misunderstood to mean that any administrative decision which is regarded by the Court to be unreasonable must be struck down.
The correct understanding of the Wednesbury principle is that a decision will be said to be unreasonable in the Wednesbury sense if (i) it is based on wholly irrelevant material or wholly irrelevant consideration, (ii) it has ignored a very relevant material which it should have taken into consideration, or (iii) it is so absurd that no sensible person could ever have reached it."
10.
The findings in the instant case are based on relevant material, inasmuch as, statements of various witnesses have been taken into account.
After recording the formal statements of departmental witness Gurcharan Singh, No.
327/Ldh., and Gurnam Singh, No.
1685/Ldh., the Enquiry Officer has recorded the statements of Gurjit Singh and also Narender Pal Chhinna.
The statement of Head Constable/Head Munshi Ashwini Kumar has also been taken into account with regard to registration of an FIR.
The statement of Gurjit Singh and Narender Pal Chhinna substantially support the allegation that the petitioner was paid Rs. 8,00,000/-.
The Enquiry Officer also duly considered the statements of defence witnesses and thereafter he had recorded the following conclusion :  "I have carefully perused the relevant records of inquiry file, statements of the prosecution witnesses, statements of defence witnesses and written reply of charged officer Lady ASI Balwinder Kaur and it transpires that charged officer alongwith her husband Sukhdev Singh alias Sukha, Manjit Singh resident of Toose, Ravinder Singh Gulati and Preeti Gulati residents of Mohali had taken Rs. 800 lacs from Gurjit Singh, resident of Village Bhoodri for sending him to America.
During inquiry, she would proved her to be innocent because she has produced only one witness i.e. Ujagar Singh, Ex. Sarpanch, Boodri to whom I put a question as to whether you given some statement against ASI Balwinder Kaur in this connection or not, in reply to which Ujagar Singh aforesaid defence witness answered in negative.
From this it is also apparent that the statement made by Gurjit Singh before me is correct because had Gurjit Singh given a wrong statement against Lady ASI Balwinder Kaur, he would have contacted Ujagar Singh and to make statement in a particular manner, but the witness has said that Gurjit Singh had not said anything to him.
It is apparent from this that Gurjit Singh having told the truth was confident that Ujagar Singh would also make the same statement.
The charged officer Lady ASI Balwinder Kaur had in some manner contacted Ujagar Singh and make him to agree to make a statement in her favour because if the statement of Ujagar Singh is correct, she could have produce the other witnesses whose names to whom have been mentioned by Gurjit Singh was like Amandeep Singh, Bahadur Singh.
But she could not produce them from which it is apparent that she could get only one witness resiled.
Thus from my entire enquiry the charges leveled against Lady ASI Balwinder Kaur, No. 1361/Ldh.
stand proved as leveled in the charge- sheet."
11.
On the basis of the enquiry report and after giving due opportunity of hearing, the petitioner was dismissed from service by taking into account his right to pension.
The aforementioned order has been upheld in appeal as well as in revision, vide orders dated 15122005 and 852006 (P-5 & P-7).
We do not find that the respondents have taken into consideration any material which is irrelevant or have ignored the relevant material.
We are further of the view that it cannot be concluded that impugned decision is so absurd that no sensible person could have ever reached it.
12.
The only argument raised by the learned counsel for the petitioner is that the petitioner has not been acquitted in the criminal case and her case deserves reconsideration in view of the order of acquittal.
A perusal of the judgment of learned Special Judge, dated 23.2.2007 (P-11) shows that the prosecution witnesses were declared hostile, who were subjected to lengthy cross-examination by the prosecution.
Obviously, considerations in the departmental inquiry are entirely different than in the criminal prosecution.
The departmental inquiry aims at finding out the misconduct committed by an employee and to assess as to whether such a person could be retained in service, whereas criminal prosecution aims at finding the criminal intent which may require a criminal to be segregated from the law abiding society.
In the present case, disciplinary proceedings were initiated on 1032004, which stands concluded on 892004 whereas the criminal prosecution was prolonged resulting in passing of the judgment came to an end on 23.2.2007.
It shows that statements of the witnesses in the departmental inquiry were recorded earlier.
Therefore, merely because the petitioner has been acquitted in a criminal case registered under the provisions of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 would not ipso facto result into her exoneration in the departmental proceedings.
13.
For the reasons aforementioned, this petition fails and the same is dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
