KC Gupta, J - This Criminal Writ Petition has been directed by the petitioner, Birender Kumar Sharma, for issuance of a writ in the nature of certiorari for quashing the order dated 2311999, Annexure P-7 and order dated 761999, Annexure P-8 vide which the D.I.G.
(H.P.
), I.T.B.P., respondent No. 3, dismissed his appeal and respondent No. 4 had illegally and arbitrarily ordered forfeiture of his five years without any reason.
2.
Briefly stated, the facts are that the petitioner joined as a Constable in I.T.B.P.
Force in the year 1990 and in the year 1993, he was selected as Head Constable General Duty.
He was transferred from 21st Battalion Leh to 9th of ITBP Ramgarh, District Panchkula in the year 1995.
Since then, he was working there.
3.
The petitioner along with one Constable Naresh Kumar was nominated to undergo V.I.P.
Security Course in December 1997 which they successfully completed.
Thereafter, they were selected to undergo V.I.P.
Security Course at NSG, Manesar, Gurgaon from 511998 to 3111998.
4.
It was further averred that the petitioner was Group Writer of Group No. 3 comprising of 70-80 trainees and being Incharge of the Group, he was to discharge certain multifarious duties such as deputing the trainees for security duty at night time etc. After the completion of the Course, he remained busy in discharging his duties in depositing the D.P.Riles, digging tools and other allied material/articles.
5.
It was next averred that on 3111998, on the day of completion of the Course, separate movement orders were issued to all the Trainees individually for going back to their respective Units and similarly, Naresh Kumar, Constable, was issued individual movement order bearing No. 101 and left the Training Centre on 3111998 without informing him and he tried his best to trace Naresh Kumar but in vain.
He searched for Naresh Kumar, Constable, at ISBT, Delhi on 121998 but could not trace him and then on 2.2.1998, he started from ISBT, Delhi and reached his Battalion Head-quarter Ramgarh on 321998.
On reaching at the Battalion Head-quarter, he came to know that Constable Naresh Kumar had lost his weapon and ammunition etc.  6.
It was further averred that on 1421998, the petitioner was straightaway placed under suspension vide order Annexure P-6.
Thereafter, vide order dated 2311999, Annexure P-7, respondent No. 4 forfeited the five years service of the petitioner.
The petitioner filed an appeal, which was also rejected by respondent No. 3 vide order, Annexure P-8 dated 761999.
With these allegations, the above-mentioned Criminal Writ Petition was filed.
7.
On notice of motion having been issued, respondent Nos. 1 to 4 filed written statement on 492001, denying the allegations of the petitioner.
8.
I have heard Shri SS Dinarpur, counsel for the petitioner, Shri CM Sharma, Advocate, Additional Standing Counsel for Union of India and carefully gone through the record.
9.
It is an admitted fact that the petitioner along with Constable Naresh Kumar was detailed for main V.I.P.
Security Course at Manesar, Gurgaon from 511998 to 3111998.
It is further an admitted fact that both of them completed the said Course on 3111998.
The movement order, Ann.
P-5, a photostat copy of which has been placed on file, shows that the departure date and time was 3111998/121998 afternoon.
It is further mentioned in it that the destination is known to the individuals.
A list of the Trainees is attached with the said Annexure.
The name of Naresh Kumar is mentioned at Sr. No. 101 while that of Birender Kumar Sharma, petitioner, is mentioned at Sr. No. 104.
It is not mentioned in the said movement order that Naresh Kumar, Constable would be under the command of Birender Kumar Sharma, petitioner, or Birender Kumar Sharma, Head Constable, will be responsible for the arms and ammunition carried by them.
The allegation of Naresh Kumar is that on 30.1.1998, he had met Birender Kumar Sharma and he had told him that they would leave for ISBT, Delhi in the first bus for onward journey to Ramgarh, Haryana.
This has been so stated in Annexure P-4.
However, Birender Kumar Sharma was not found there.
In such circumstances, since Naresh Kumar was without escort and was carrying arms and ammunition, so, he should not have boarded the bus when he did not find Birender Kumar Sharma in the bus or he should have contacted him at the premises known as NSG Training Centre, Manesar before leaving from there.
If he had taken these precautions, then the arms and ammunition would not have been lost.
However, there does not appear to be any fault on behalf of Birender Kumar Sharma.
The movement order does not show that Naresh Kumar was placed under the command of Birender Kumar Sharma.
Even if it is presumed that according to some Rule or Regulation of the I.T.B.P.
Force, the petitioner being senior was to act as command qua Naresh Kumar, then also there is no evidence that this fact was brought to the notice of the petitioner.
The orders indicate that they were to return individually.
In the way if Naresh Kumar had lost his arms and ammunition, then the petitioner is not liable for the same.
Vide Annexure P-9 dated 10111998, the petitioner was charged as under :-  "FIRST CHARGE: ITBPF ACT, 92 SEC. 21(a) :- ABSENTING HIMSELF WITHOUT LEAVE: is that he after having been relieved of his duties on completion of VIP Security course at NSG Training Centre, Manesar (Gurgaon) on 31.1.98 (FN) violated the direction to report HQRs 9th Bn ITBP Ramgarh along with No. 910090271 Ct/GD Naresh Kumar `A' Coy of 9th Bn ITBP and absented himself enroute without leave/permission upto 3.2.98 (AN).
SECOND CHARGE: ITBPF ACT, 92 SEC. 43 :- VIOLATING GOOD ORDER  AND DISCIPLINE: in that he was given departure from NSG Trg.
Centre Manesar (Gurgaon) after completion of VIP Security Course on 31.1.98 (FN) along with No. 91009271 Ct/GD Naresh Kumar of `C' Copy 9th Bn to 9th Bn HQRs Ramgarh.
But he did not accompany and escort him causing the loss of one 9mm Carbine Machine and other Govt. property by Ct/GD Naresh Kumar."
10.
Therefore, no duty was cast upon the petitioner to report for duty immediately after 3111998 along with Naresh Kumar and further if Naresh Kumar had lost arms and ammunition, then the petitioner is not liable.
Of course, if the petitioner is absented for one or two days without any reason, then he can be proceeded separately but not that he has violated any direction that he must return along with Naresh Kumar.
Hence, charges under Section 21 (a) and Section 43 of the ITBPF Act, 1942 are baseless and the same cannot be sustained.
Therefore, the five years service of the petitioner has been wrongly forfeited.
In view of the above discussion, the criminal Writ Petition is allowed and the order dated 2311999, Annexure P-7, vide which the service of the petitioner to the extent of five years was forfeited and the order dated 761999, Annexure P-8, vide which his appeal was dismissed, are quashed being illegal and arbitrary.
Petition allowed.
