Ashok Bhan, J - (Oral) Petitioner raised an industrial dispute including the point as to whether the Octroi Moharir and Sweepers should be allowed all gazetted holidays ?
If so, with what details ?
Vide an award of the Industrial Tribunal published in Punjab Govt. Gazette dated 1191970, the petitioners were allowed all gazetted holidays as in the case of other employees employed in the office of Municipal Committee.
Instead of filing an application under Section 33(C)(2) of the Industrial Disputes Act claiming wages in terms of the award dated 1191970, immediately the petitioner filed the application at a belated stage under Section 33(C)(2) of the said Act in the year 1979.
Presiding Officer of the Labour Court, vide its order dated 1511980 (Annexure P.2) held that the petitioners filed the claim at a belated stage and are thus not entitled to the wages upto 3131976.
Respondent Municipal Committee was directed to prepare and file a correct statement of account showing the amount due to each of the petitioner on that score from 111976 to 3131979.
Case was further adjourned to 421980.
2.
Petitioners filed an application before the Labour Court stating therein that they proposed to file a writ in the High Court against the order of the Labour Court dated 1511980 whereby their claim for wages was restricted to the period from 141976 to 3131979 claiming therein that they were entitled to the wages for the entire period, i.e., from the year 1970 till 1979.
Case was adjourned twice for this purpose and ultimately, vide order Annexure P/5, the applications of the petitioners were dismissed for non-prosecution as the petitioners failed to lead any evidence in support of their claim.
Petitioners have filed the present petition impugning the orders Annexures P/2 and P/5.
3.
I have perused the record and heard the learned counsel for the parties.
4.
So far as the order Annexure P/5 is concerned, the same is liable to be quashed on the ground that the Labour Court had directed the respondents, vide its order dated 1511980 (Annexure P-2) to prepare and file a correct statement of account showing the amount due to each of the petitioner for the period from 141976 to 3131979.
The respondents failed to file such statement of account.
Under the circumstances, the claim applications of the petitioners should not have been dismissed by the Labour Court.
The petitioners should have been given an opportunity to lead evidence in support of their claim.
The dismissal of the application filed by the petitioners for want of prosecution is, therefore, not justified.
5.
So far as the order Annexure P/2 dated 1511980 is concerned, the Labour Court restricted the claim of the petitioners for a period of three years ie 141976 to 3131979 as if Article 137 of the Limitation Act was applicable to the claims under Section 33(C)(2) of the Industrial Disputes Act.
It was argued that Article 137 of the Limitation Act, would apply to the applications being moved in the Court but not before any Tribunal like the Labour Court constituted under the Industrial Disputes Act.
Reliance was placed on a judgment of Delhi High Court in M/s Cooperative Store Ltd v. KS Khurana and others, 1990(1) SLR 631, in which it has been held that the provisions of Limitation Act are not applicable to the application filed under Section 33(C)(2) of the Industrial Disputes Act.
Para 6 of this judgment is reproduced below :-  "1.
It has been further argued by the learned counsel for the petitioner that the claims made by the respondents were barred by limitation inasmuch as under the Delhi Shops Act the claims should have been brought in within one year of the accrual of the dues whereas the claims in the present case relate to periods varying between 10 years or less.
It is now settled law that the provisions of Limitation Act, 1963, are not applicable to the claims brought under Section 33(C)(2) of the Act, A similar contention was raised in the case of Bharat Steel Tubes Ltd, (supra) but the same was negative.
The Supreme Court has consistently taken the view that there is no period of Limitation for bringing a claim under Section 33(C)(2) of the Act.
(See the Bombay GS Company Ltd v. Gopal Bhiva and others, AIR 1964 SC 752, Chief Mining Engineer, M/s East India Coal Co Ltd v. Rameshwar and others, AIR 1968 SC 218, Town Municipal Council v. Presiding Officer, Labour, Hubli and others, AIR 1969 SC 1335, Management of the State Bank of Hyderabad v. Vasudev Anant Bhide and others, AIR 1970 SC 196 and Nityanand M Joshi and another v. The Life Insurance Corporation of India and others, AIR 1970 SC 209).
It has been held that the provisions of Article 137 of the Limitation Act would apply to the applications being made before a Labour Court under the Industrial Disputes Act.
There was a conflict of law with regard to the interpretation of Article 137 of the Limitation Act, 1963, as to whether that would apply only to the applications being made under the provisions of the Code of Civil Procedure or would also apply to the applications to be made under any other law.
That has been now set at rest by the Supreme Court in the judgment reported as The Kerala State Electricity Board, Trivandrum v. TP Kunhaliumma, AIR 1977 SC 282, by laying down authoritatively that Article 137 of the Limitation Act, 1963, would apply to all petitions or applications made under any statute and not merely to the applications arising under the Code of Civil Procedure.
But it is made clear in the aforesaid judgment of the Supreme Court that Article 137 would apply only to the applications being moved in Court and not before any Tribunal like the Labour Court constituted under the Industrial Disputes Act.
So, the claims of respondents 2 to 67 cannot be held to be time-barred."
On the basis of this authority it was argued that the Labour Court could not restrict the claim of the petitioners for a period of three years and further that they were entitled to the wages for the holidays for the entire period from 1970 to 1979.
Labour Court denied the wages for the gazetted holidays from 1970 till 3131976 on the ground that the respondents were not expected to preserve the relevant record of holidays, leave and attendance of the staff for such a long time and that it would be difficult for the respondents to answer or defend the claim of the petitioners for want of such record.
In my opinion, the Labour Court was not justified in restricting the claims of the petitioners for three years immediately preceding the filing of the application under Section 33(C) (2) of the Act on the ground that the respondents may not have in their possession the relevant record of holidays, leave and attendance of the staff.
A duty could be cast on the petitioners to prove their case and bring on record the list of holidays, leave and their attendance for the period 1970 to 1976 but on this account their claims could not be restricted for a period of three years only, i.e., 141976 to 3131979.
6.
For the reasons aforementioned, this writ petition is allowed, the impugned orders Annexures P/2 and P/5 are quashed.
Labour Court is directed to proceed in accordance with law after affording full opportunity of hearing to the petitioners to prove their claims for wages for the entire period, i.e., from 1970 till the filing of the application.
The Municipal Committee shall make the relevant record available with it to the Labour Court with regard to the number of holidays, leave and attendance of the workmen.
No costs.
Parties through their counsel are directed to appear before the Labour Court on 2321993.
