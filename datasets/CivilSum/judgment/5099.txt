MM Kumar, J - This petition filed under Article 226 of the Constitution prays for issuance of a writ in the nature of Mandamus directing the official respondents to place the petitioner above the private respondent Nos. 6 and 7 in District Bhiwani, which is the original cadre of the petitioner.
It has further been prayed that a writ in the nature of Certiorari be issued quashing memo No. 8181/8-3 1661994 addressed by the Director General of Police, Haryana to the Additional Director General Armed Police and Training, Madhuban (Annexure P-3).
It has been observed in the memo that 16 Head Constables including Head Constable Rajiv Kumar No. 160/SPT (2/110) and Attar Singh No. 531/BWN (2/44) were transferred from Haryana Armed Police (for short `the H.A.P.')
to district police out of turn at various intervals and these transfers were irregular.
As a consequence on 571991 those Head Constables were ordered to be transferred back to H.A.P.
but on account of filing of a writ petition in the High Court, the orders of transfer were withdrawn.
It is pertinent to mention that respondent No. 7 Attar Singh also belonged originally to district Bhiwani.
A further prayer has been made to depute the petitioner for Intermediate School Course over and above respondent Nos. 6 and 7 and his seniority be fixed in his original/parent cadre i.e. District Bhiwani as per rules.
2.
Brief facts of the case are that the petitioner was enrolled as a Constable in the Haryana Police on 27111979 in District Bhiwani.
In February, 1980, he was sent to Police Training College, Madhuban for `Recruit Basic Training Course' which he qualified.
Thereafter, the petitioner was transferred to Haryana Armed Police alongwith respondent Nos. 6 and 7 without his consent.
It is also admitted position that Haryana Armed Police constitute a separate cadre and it is a separate range than the District Cadre Bhiwani.
Although a number of Constables were transferred back to their parent cadre after three years but the petitioner was not transferred on the plea of non-availability of a vacancy in District Bhiwani.
The petitioner had competed and was selected for Lower School Course, which he joined on 141983.
He passed the course on 24101983 and was enlisted on promotion list `C' with effect from 25101983.
He was further promoted as Head Constable with effect from 25.11.1983 under Rule 131 read with Rule 138 of the Rules and was confirmed as Head Constable on 31.1.1988.
3.
Respondent No. 6 was enrolled as Constable in District Bhiwani on 2691980.
He was also transferred to HAP and was deputed to Lower School Course in September, 1984.
He was promoted as Head Constable in 1986, on which basis, he was confirmed on 31.1.1988 alongwith the petitioner.
Respondent No. 6 was transferred to District Gurgaon vide order No. 4474-77 dated 28.2.1990 by the D.I.G.
H.A.P., Madhuban and later on he was transferred to District Bhiwani from Gurgaon.
Thereafter, he was deputed to undergo Intermediate School Course in October, 1993, which he underwent at P.A.C.
Madhuban.
4.
Respondent No. 7 was enrolled on 2.3.1981 as Constable in District Sonepat and was promoted as Head Constable in 1986.
He was confirmed as officiating Head Constable on 3111989.
On 2511991, he was transferred to District Rohtak where the petitioner was as his Head Constable in the office of D.I.G.
Rohtak.
Respondent No. 7 had been deputed to the Intermediate School Course from October, 1993.
In para 11 of the writ petition, the names of various Head Constables have been given claiming that all of them were junior to the petitioner and were transferred to various Districts.
It has further been claimed that the name of the petitioner has been at Sr. No. 297 in the combined seniority list of Head Constables in H.A.P.
and those of respondents No. 6 and 7 at Sr. Nos. 338 and 518.
It has been alleged that respondent Nos. 6 and 7 have been transferred to District Police Cadre with an oblique motive to grant them advantage of quick promotion by ignoring the superior claim of the petitioner.
The petitioner claimed that a representation was submitted by him on 24.2.1994 (Annexure P-2).
The petitioner has also asserted that he filed C.W.P.No.
5485 of 1994, which was disposed of by a Division Bench of this Court directing the official respondents to consider and decide the representation made by the petitioner.
The representation of the petitioner was decided and memo dated 7.5.1994 has been issued taking the stand that 16 Head Constables including above named Rajiv Kumar and Attar Singh were transferred from H.A.P.
to District Police out of turn at various intervals.
They were ordered to be transferred back to H.A.P.
vide order dated 571991.
The orders of transfer were challenged by some of the Head Constables by filing a writ petition and this Court had stayed their reversion from the District Police to H.A.P.
Thereafter, the orders of transfer were withdrawn and the writ petition was rendered infructuous by this Court.
It has been observed in the memo that reviewing the order would result into Contempt of Court.
5.
The principal stand taken in the written-statement by Respondent Nos. 1 to 5 is that transfer of lower subordinates from H.A.P.
to district police or from district police to H.A.P.
are made under Rule 1226 with the concurrence of the DIG of Police concerned.
The allegations of pick and choose method for transferring Head Constables respondent Nos. 6 and 7 to Gurgaon, Bhiwani and Sonepat Districts have been denied claiming that the enrolled police officers are liable to serve anywhere in the State.
It has further been claimed that the Head Constables are deputed for Intermediate School Course in accordance with their seniority in their respective cadre and promotion is granted to them according to Rule 139.
The seniority position of the petitioner has been denied by asserting that after transfer from H.A.P., their seniority of the District Police Cadre has to be seen.
6.
Mr. IS Balhara, learned counsel for the petitioner has argued that the petitioner has always been senior to respondent Nos. 6 and 7 in the seniority list of Head Constables maintained by H.A.P.
The name of the petitioner stands at Sr. No. 297 and those of respondent Nos. 6 and 7 at Sr. No. 338 and 518 respectively.
According to the learned counsel, the official respondents acted in violation of Articles 14 and 16 by transferring respondent Nos. 6 and 7 to the district police so as to occupy higher seniority and promotion on the post of A.S.I.
Learned counsel has also challenged the action of the respondents of transferring the petitioner from the District Cadre of Bhiwani to H.A.P.
without his consent.
7.
Mr. SS Brar, learned State counsel has argued that in the administrative exigencies, respondents No. 6 and 7 were transferred to District Police, which did not emanate from any malice or favouritism.
According to the learned counsel, respondent Nos. 6 and 7 alongwith other 16 Head Constables were sought to be brought back to H.A.P.
vide office order No. 7894/B-1 dated 571991.
The afore-mentioned order was challenged before this Court and repatriation of the petitioner therein was stayed.
Thereafter, the department withdrew the whole order and the writ petitions were rendered infructuous.
8.
No one has put in appearance on behalf of respondent Nos. 6 and 7 despite service.
9.
Having heard the learned counsel for the parties at a considerable length, I am of the considered opinion that this petition deserves to be allowed.
In the memo dated 2361994/2171994, the official respondents have accepted that 16 Head Constables including respondent Nos. 6 and 7 were transferred from H.A.P.
to District Police out of turn.
These transfers were irregular and they were ordered to be transferred back to H.A.P.
vide officer order No. 7894/B-1 dated 571991.
Once this factual position is accepted and conceded in Annexure P-3, then there was no warrant for the official respondents to withdraw the orders merely because a writ was filed in this Court and ad- interim stay was granted.
There was no question of violating the orders of the Court once it is accepted that out of turn transfer of 16 Head Constables including respondent Nos. 6 and 7 was irregular and not in order.
Merely because order dated 571991 was withdrawn before this Court would not provide a valid excuse as no directions were issued by this Court.
I am further of the view that even otherwise if transfer from one cadre to another is to be made then the procedure consistent with Articles 14 and 16(1) of the Constitution has to be followed.
One possible way could have been to invite option in order of seniority equivalent to the number of constables sought to be transferred from HAP to other cadre.
In the event of refusal to exercise the option by the petitioner he could have been validly deprived of his right vis-a-vis his juniors like respondent Nos. 6 and 7 to be transferred outside the cadre.
If the transfers are made from one cadre to another on request then seniority of the transferred employee is to be fixed at the bottom.
Therefore, the order.
Annexure P-3 is violative of Articles 14 and 16 is liable to be quashed qua respondent Nos. 6 and 7.
10.
The argument of the learned counsel for the petitioner that the petitioner be sent back to his original cadre of Constable in District Bhiwani cannot be accepted as the petitioner has acquiesced and accepted H.A.P.
as his cadre.
No grievance with regard to transfer of cadre from District Police Bhiwani to H.A.P.
was raised.
The petitioner has been deputed for B-I test in the year 1983 and passed the Lower School Course on 24101983 from H.A.P.
He was enlisted on the promotion list `C' with effect from 25101983 and he was also confirmed as Head Constable under Rule 138 on 31.1.1988.
Therefore, in a writ petition filed in 1995, it cannot be claimed that his encadrement to H.A.P.
was without his consent as the claim made by the petitioner suffers from delay and laches.
Even a civil suit for declaration would not be within limitation after the expiry of three years.
The prescribed period of limitation provided for filing a civil suit has to be read as for filing a writ petition.
For the afore-mentioned proposition of law reliance could be placed on a judgment of the Supreme Court in the case of State of M.P. v. Bhai Lal Bhai, A.I.R. 1964 SC 1006.
11.
In view of the above, this petition is allowed and the memo dated 2361994/2171994 (Annexure P-3) is quashed.
As a consequence the office order No. 7894/B1 dated 571991 is revived in respect of respondent Nos. 6 and 7.
The official respondents are directed to consider the petitioner for deputing him to intermediate school course in accordance with the rules and if the petitioner has already qualified the course then to grant him deemed date of promotion which has to be earlier to respondent Nos. 6 and 7.
The petitioner shall be entitled to all other consequential benefits with regard to salary, increment and seniority etc.
The needful shall be done within a period of three months from the date of receipt of copy of this order.
The petitioner shall be entitled to his costs which are assessed at Rs. 5,000/-.
Petition allowed.
