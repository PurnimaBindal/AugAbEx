VS Aggarwal, J - Petitioner Manohar Lal Juneja joined service on 25.6.1954.
He retired as Head Master on attaining the age of superannuation on 3041994.
In accordance with the rules, he was entitled for payment of cash equal to leave salary.
It was granted for 87 days in lieu of the earned leave.
An order to this effect was passed on 1021994 which reads:-  "In terms of Haryana Government letter No. 11/5/78-FE II dated 13278 read with letter of even number dated 22279 and letter No. 11/50-87 sanction is hereby accorded to the payment of case equivalent leave salary for 87 days in lieu of earned leave to the credit of Sh. Manohar Lal Juneja retired Head Master, Government High School, Kalanaur (Rohtak).
This is further subject to the condition that Drawing & Disbursing Officer concerned will ensure that the amount of Honorarium if any paid to this officer for attending training camps etc. during summer vacations is adjusted out of the amount of leave encashment to be paid to him."
By virtue of the present petition, the petitioner seeks a writ of mandamus to direct the respondents to credit the petitioner's account with maximum period of earned leave admissible in case of an employee and release the amount due to the petitioner on account of 240 days unutilized earned leave forthwith.
Penal interested has also been claimed on the said amount.
2.
Petitioner's claim is that he was entitled to full earned leave and 219 days of earned leave even was verified by the District Education Officer, Rohtak.
This is for the reason that petitioner's claim that he was assigned duty during summer vacation and was prevented from enjoying 219 days of vacation.
The clerk in the school was absent during summer vacation.
The head of the institution had passed an order for the petitioner to perform the duties as incharge, though the petitioner was working in the vacation department.
He contends that in term of the Punjab Civil Services Rules, Volume 1, Rule 8.117(a) the petitioner is entitled to earned leave which should be credited to his account in accordance with the above said rules keeping in view that he had put in more then 20 years of service.
It has further been contended that the State Government decided that teachers working under the Government will be entitled to 10 days earned leave on full pay in lieu of 20 days half pay leave and encashment of the leave would be admissible.
Copy of the said circular has been appended which reads:-  "I am directed to address you on the subject noted above and to say that grant of earned leave instead of half pay leave to teachers working in Haryana Govt. has been engaging the attention of Government.
After careful consideration it has been decided that the teachers working under the Govt. except where otherwise provided will henceforth be permitted to 10 days earned leave on full pay during the leave in lieu of 20 days half pay leave, as admissible at present.
2 - It has also been clarified that half pay leave at the credit of teachers as on the date of issue of the instruction is to be shown separately in the leave account and may be granted as half pay leave/commuted leave on the same terms and conditions as applicable to them prior to date of issue of these instructions.
Teachers appointed after the date of these instructions will not be entitled to any half pay leave and there is, therefore, no question of granting commuted leave on medical grounds.
It is further clarified that encashment of leave would be admissible to teachers suo moto as to other Government servants subject to the fulfilment of the prescribed condition.
3 - Necessary amendment in the Rules will be made in due course."
In accordance with the said circular it is contended that petitioner is entitled to earned leave from 1351986 to 12.5.1993.
Since the earned leave had not been properly credited as mentioned above, the present petition has been filed.

In accordance with the rules, the earned leave was rightly calculated and there is no mistake therein.
Besides the duty period as mentioned above, the respondent's claim is that petitioner has been allowed the benefit of conversion of half pay leave into full pay leave from 1351986 to 12.5.1993.
In this way leave encashment for 178 days has become due.

5.
The relevant rule on the subject is the Punjab Civil Services Rules, Volume 1, Chapter 8, Rule 8.117.
It reads :-  "8.117.
(a) Earned leave is not admissible to a Government employee serving in a vacation department in respect of duty performed in any year in which he avails himself of the full vacation.
(b) The earned leave admissible to such Government employee in respect of any year in which he is prevented from availing himself of the full vacation is such proportion of the following periods as the number of days of vacation not taken bears the full vacation :-  (Foot-note :- Rule 8117(b) shall be deemed to have come into force from 1st July, 1959).
(i) to a Government employee with 10 year service or less : 15 days  (ii) to a Government employee with more than ten years service but not exceeding 20 years service : 20 days (iii) to a Government employee with over 20 years service : 20 years service.
If in any year he does not avail himself of the vacation, earned leave will be admissible in respect of that year in accordance with the provisions of Rule 8116.
(c) Vacation may be taken in combination with or in continuation of any kind of leave under the rules in this section; provided that the total duration of vacation and earned leave taken in conjunction, whether the earned leave is taken in combination with or in continuation of other leave or not, shall not exceed the amount of earned leave due and admissible to the Government employee at a time under rule 8116."
6.
The said rule shows as to when an employee is prevented from availing of the full vacation in a vacation department; how the earned leave had to be credited to him.
In accordance with Rule 8117(b) the earned leave admissible to such Government employee in respect of a year is in which he is prevented from availing himself is such proportion of the following periods as the number of days of the vacation not taken bears the full vacation.
Consequently, if the petitioner worked for few days, the earned leave would be credited in accordance with the said formula.
If the petitioner works for full 60 days i.e for two months, he would earn leave for 30 days otherwise, he would earn less leave as per the above formula.
In accordance with the said formula the respondents had calculated the earned leave which is credited to the petitioner and is given above.
The same is correct.
The petitioner, therefore, cannot claim that earned leave should be credited to him as calculated by him.
The petition can, therefore, only be allowed as has now been conceded by the respondents.
7.
Learned counsel for the petitioner relied upon the decision of this court in the case of BK Bhalla v. State of Punjab and others, 1992(4) SLR 629.
In the cited case Shri Bhalla was working as a Lecturer in a Government College.
The post of lecturer is also in a vacation department.
In the year 1980 the Principal of the College was absence from 30.5.1980 to 15.7.1980.
During this period the petitioner was looking after the affairs of the college.
He was appointed as Drawing and Disbursing Officer.
When the dispute arose, it was held that he was entitled to 30 days earned leave to be credited for the year 1980.
But it is obvious that he has worked for full two months during the entire vacation.
It is not so in the case of the petitioner and, therefore, the petitioner cannot take advantage of the facts of the cited decision.
No other argument was raised.
8.
For this reasons, the petition is only allowed in part.
It is directed that the petitioner is entitled to salary for 178 days earned leave as calculated by the respondent and admitted in the written statement.
The petitioner should be paid the arrears within a month from the date of the present order.
In default he would be entitled to interest at the rate of 12 per cent per annum on the balance amount till the date of actual payment.
No order as to costs.
