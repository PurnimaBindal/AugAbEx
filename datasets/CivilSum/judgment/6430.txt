Jawahar Lal Gupta, J - The petitioners who are working as Sanitary Inspectors in the Health Department complain that the order dated April 27, 1993, passed in pursuance to directions given by this Court in C.W.P.
No. 1920 of 1987 by which their claim for parity of pay scale with the Sanitary Inspectors working in the Local Bodies has been declined, is illegal.
The challenge is two-fold.
Firstly, it is alleged that the factual premises on which the order has been passed are non-existent.
Secondly, the petitioners urge that the order is arbitrary, unfair, discriminatory and thus violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
The respondents have controverted this claim.
2.
It is the pleaded case of the petitioners that prior to January 1, 1968, the Sanitary Inspector working in the Health Department as well as the Local Bodies were in the pay scale of Rs. 80-150.
With effect from January 1, 1968, both the categories of employees were placed in the scale of Rs. 120-250.
The scale was further revised with effect from January 1, 1978 and raised to Rs. 510-940 in respect of the petitioners as well as their counter- parts in the Local Bodies.
However, vide order dated March 24, 1983, the Sanitary Inspectors working in the Local Bodies were placed in the scale of Rs. 570- 1080 with effect from January 1, 1978.
Thus, a disparity was created for the first time in the year 1983.
This disparity was made to continue at the time of the subsequent revision of pay scale with effect from January 1, 1986 when the Sanitary Inspectors in the Health Department viz. the petitioners were placed in the scale of Rs. 1410-2460, while those in the Local Bodies were placed in the scale of Rs. 1500-2640.
3.
The petitioners had earlier approached this Court through C.W.P.
No. 1920 of 1987 as also a number of other petitions.
They had made a claim for parity of pay scales.
It was also alleged that they were entitled to the grant of selection grade as had been given to the persons working in the Local Bodies.
This writ petition was disposed of vide order dated August 12, 1992.
It was directed that the respondents should decide the whole matter expeditiously by passing a speaking order.
It is in pursuance to this direction that the impugned order declining the claim was passed by the respondents.
4.
A perusal of the impugned order shows that the claim of the petitioners has been declined on the basis of the advice given by the Finance Department vide its letter dated January 6, 1993.
The advice as reproduced in the order is as under :-  "In the light of judgment dated 12892, of the Hon'ble Punjab & Haryana High Court case was resubmitted to the Finance Department.
Finance Department has given advice as under : 1.
That the pay grade of Multipurpose Supervisor in Health Department in 1968 was Rs. 510-940.
On the other hand the pay scale of Sanitary Inspectors in the Local Bodies in 1968 was Rs. 160-400 and in 1978 was Rs. 570-1080.
Similarly, in 1986 although Multipurpose Supervisor of Health Department's grade was required to be of Rs. 1350-2400 after conversion, but the Pay Commission had recommended the grade of Rs. 1410-2460 for this post.
Contrary to it Sanitary Inspector of the Local Bodies was given the pay grade of Rs. 1500-2640 after conversion of Rs. 570-1080 in 1986.
2.
Though the pay scale of both these posts were different from 1968 and pay scale of Multipurpose Supervisor (Male) of the Health Department remained always lower than Sanitary Inspectors of Local Self/Bodies, even then it is appropriate to say that the pay scales of both the posts are equal.
Regarding parity in pay scale, the recommendations or decision by the Pay Commission Anomaly Committee or Govt. must be there, which is not available in this case.
Therefore, there is no justification in view of this for the grant of pay scale to Multipurpose Supervisor of the Health Department equal of the pay scale of the Sanitary Inspector of the Local Bodies.
Keeping in view the above advice, self-speaking order should immediately be issued according to the orders of the Hon'ble High Court and the Govt. be informed immediately."
5.
Mr. Sandhu, learned counsel for the petitioners, submits that the above advice/decision is based on a totally wrong assumption of facts.
He appears to be right.
It has been observed that the pay scale of the Multipurpose Supervisor in the Health Department (which appears to be the designation of the posts held by the petitioners) in 1968 was Rs. 510-940.
It was not so.
In fact the pay scale was Rs. 120-250.
Similarly, the observation that the Sanitary Inspectors in the Local Bodies were in the pay scale of Rs. 160-400 in 1968 is incorrect.
In fact, they were also in the pay scale of Rs. 120- 250.
It is the admitted position that till 1983, both the categories of employees had remained in the same scale of pay.
In spite of this, in para 2 of the above advice it has been observed that "the pay scale of both these posts were different from 1968...." This was not so.
In view of the above, it is clear that the impugned order is based on a totally wrong assumption of facts.
It cannot thus be sustained.
6.
Mr. DS Dhillon, appearing for the respondent has submitted that the action of the respondents in placing the two categories of employees in different scales of pay is justified in view of the reasons mentioned in the written statement.
This contention cannot be accepted.
The validity of the order passed by the respondents in pursuance to the judgment of this Court has to be examined on the grounds stated in the order.
The reasons cannot be supplemented by subsequent affidavits.
In any event, in the circumstances of this case, I do not consider it appropriate to go into this matter at this stage.
7.
Accordingly, the impugned order is set aside on the ground that it is based on wrong assumption of facts.
The respondents are directed to decide the matter afresh in accordance with law.
It appears to appropriate to further direct the respondents to hear a representative of the petitioners before passing the final order.
The needful shall be done within three months from the date of receipt of a copy of this order.
The writ petition is allowed in the above terms.
The impugned order is quashed.
The respondents are directed to decide the case afresh.
In the circumstances of the case, there will be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
