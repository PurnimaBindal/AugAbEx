ML Singhal, J - Neel Kanth was appointed as peon/chowkidar on 2591991 in the Haryana State Federation Cooperative Sugar Mills Limited, Chandigarh.
He worked as daily wager up to 22.8.1992 at the rates fixed by the Deputy Commissioner, Chandigarh.
After 22.6.1992, he was given appointment letters from time to time for 89 days by giving one or two days notional breaks.
Latest appointment order was given to him on 24.9.1993 for a period of 89 days, which was to enure up to 18121993, but vide order dated 13.10.1993 his services were terminated.
Termination of his services was arbitrary, illegal and without complying with the provisions of Industrial disputes Act, 1947.
Neither was he given any notice nor any retrenchment compensation was paid to him before his services were terminated.
He raised industrial Dispute which was referred to the Labour Court under Section 10(1)(c) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.
Labour Court set aside the order of termination and ordered him to be reinstated with continuity of service.
In lieu of back wages, Labour Court thought it proper to award a lumpsum compensation of Rs. 10,000/-.
Labour Court's award is Annexure P-1.
After the award of the Labour Court, he was asked by the Managing Director of respondent No. 3 that if he gave in writing that he would not claim Rs. 10,000/- and also will not file any other case in Court, the Federation that he would not file any appeal in the High Curt against the award of the Labour Court and further he was ready to forego the compensation amount of Rs. 10,000/- as fixed by the labour Court.
In addition, he also agreed that he would not claim any back wages or seniority etc and he be kept in service.
Inspite of the said writing Annexure P-2 given by him, respondents did not regularise his services but gave an order dated 19111997 Annexure P-3 that he was neither entitled to medical allowance nor entitled to lumpsum amount of Rs. 10,000/- as awarded by the Labour Court.
After the receipt of Annexure P-3, when he found that his services had not been regularised and compromise/agreement which was entered and given in writing by him on the asking of the Managing Director had not been complied with by the respondents, he withdrew his writing and stuck to his claim of Rs. 10,000/- and other consequential reliefs flowing from the order of the Labour Court.
He made application Annexure P-4 to the Managing Director.
After he had submitted application Annexure P-4, the Managing Director issued a letter dated 2/8121997 and ordered the payment of Rs. 10,000/- to him but neither seniority was fixed nor his services were regularised in view of the instructions of the Government of Haryana which are applicable to the Federation also.
The Management Director issued another order vide which Ram Asray peon who was working on Tea/Coffee Machine to prepare Tea/Coffee for staff was asked to attend the work of ECT, Supdt./Staff and the petitioner was ordered to prepare Tea/Coffee and attend to the work of the staff.
In the order Annexure P-5 changing/assigning duties to him and Ram Asray-peon, there is no mention that nay extra payment will be made to him which was being made for the last so many years to Ram Asray-Peon who was working on this machine and was getting Rs. 250/- per month as additional payment.
Though he was assured that he will be given next order of regularisation, he felt shocked and surprised to know from the office order dated 26.7.1998 annexure P-6 in which condition was imposed that he (Neel Kanth) was being appointed on adhoc basis stop-gap arrangement for 89 days.
In order Annexure P-6, the post of peon-cum-chowkidar which he was holding had been changed to the post of peon-cum-cook.
With effect from 2621998 he was appointed as peon-cum-cook in the pay scale of Rs. 750/940 plus usual allowances on purely adhoc basis as stopgap arrangement for 89 days or till the joining of regular incumbent as per rules, whichever is earlier.
In order Annexure P-6, it was also stated that he would not be entitled to medical allowance.
Action of the respondents in passing order dated 2621998 (Annexure P-6) in which he was described as peon-cum-cook and further showing this appointment as stopgap arrangement is illegal and void as his service conditions could not be changed.
He was entitled to the regularisation of this service in view of the instructions of the Government of Haryana dated 731996, 1831996 and 1761997 (Annexures P-7 to P-9) respectively.
He was ordered to be reinstated into service with continuity of service by the Labour Court and had completed more than seven years service and more than three years service as daily wager as on 3111996 in the grade of Rs. 750-940.
From the order of the Labour Court he had already completed more than 3 years service without any break as on 3111996 and was also in service on that date.
Although he was entitled to be regularised in terms of the aforesaid policies/instructions, respondent No. 3 chose to fix interview for the post of peon-cum-cook at 1100 AM on 2041998, despite his representation to respondent No. 3 that no recruitment to the post of peon-cum-cook could be made as he was entitled to be regularised.
He appeared for the interview for the post of peon-cum-cook.
Respondent No. 5 who was working at the house of the Managing Director was appointed to the post of peon-cum-cook in preference to him, although he had put in seven years service and was entitled to be regularised.
His services were terminated vide order dated 151998 and in his place respondent No. 5 was appointed vide order Annexure P-13.
2.
Through this writ petition filed under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India, he has prayed for the issuance of a writ in the nature of certiorari quashing order Annexure P-13 dated 151998 of respondent No. 3 whereby his services were terminated and instead respondent No. 5 was appointed.
He has prayed that he be given all consequential reliefs after quashing Annexure P-13.
He has further prayed that operation of order Annexure P-13 be stayed and respondents No. 1 to 3 be commanded through a writ in the nature of mandamus to regularise his services.
3.
Respondents contested this writ petition.
It was urged that the appointment of the petitioner on daily wages was made de-hors the statutory service rules and on his own request and without consideration of the claim of any other eligible candidate.
Petitioner's candidature was not sponsored by the Employment Exchange.
Subsequently, the period of his appointment was extended from time to time with a stipulation that he will continue till the joining of the recommendee of the Employment Exchange.
His appointment was in the circumstances in patent violation of the statutory8 service rules which prescribe mode of selection and appointment.
When the very appointment of the petitioner was fortuitous and de-hors the statutory service rules, no right was conferred to him to claim regularisation.
Petitioner's claim to regularisation on the basis of instructions Annexures P-7 to P-9 is untenable as these instructions did not apply to respondent No. 3 i.e. the Haryana State Federation of Cooperative Sugar Mills Ltd which is a cooperative society.
These instructions have not been adopted by this Cooperative Society nor have they been made applicable.
Instructions Annexures P-7 to P-9 would have come in aid of the petitioner for regularisation if he had put in 5/3 years of service and also if he had been in service as on 3111996.
Petitioner remained on daily wages for a short period from 2591991 to 23.3.1992.
Instructions Annexures P-7 and P-8 cannot enure to his benefit.
Instructions Annexure P-9 also do not apply as they contemplate regularisation of such ad hoc employees who had been recruited through Employment Exchange.
His appointment was arbitrary and without consideration of the factor of merit in accordance with the Service Rules.
Termination of the services of such an appointee does not offend any provision of law so as to warrant interference of this Court in writ jurisdiction.
Even otherwise, a cooperative society is not amenable to the writ jurisdiction.
It was further urged that the petitioner had adequate and effective alternative remedy by way of proceedings before the Labour Court under the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 so far as order of retrenchment Annexure P-13 is concerned.
Order of retrenchment Annexure P-13 was passed by the competent authority after due compliance of provisions of the Industrial Disputes Act.
Petitioner could avail the remedy of statutory appeal before the Board of Directors of the Federation and second appeal before the Registrar, Cooperative Societies, Haryana, as provided under Rule 24 of the Haryana State Federation of Cooperative Sugar Mills Limited Service Rules 1988, statutorily framed under Section 131(xxxix) of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act, 1984 read with Rule 29 of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Rules, 1989.
He could not approached this Court without exhausting these statutory remedies of appeal and second appeal.
4.
I have heard the learned counsel for the petitioner, learned counsel for the respondents No. 1 to 3 and have gone through the record.
5.
Petitioner joined as peon-cum-chowkidar on 2591991.
He worked as such up to 13.10.1993.
With effect from 22.6.1992 he was in the pay scale of Rs. 750-940.
Prior to that, he was on wages fixed by the Deputy Commissioner.
He was issued appointment letters from time to time.
Last appointment order was given to him dated 24.9.1993, whereby his appointment was to enure upto 18121993.
His services were terminated vide order dated 13.10.1993.
He challenged the said order before the Labour Court, UT Chandigarh.
Labour Court, UT Chandigarh vide award Annexure P-1 ordered his reinstatement into service with continuity of service.
In lieu of back-wages, hw was ordered to be awarded a lumpsum compensation of Rs. 10,000/-.
Compromise (Annexure P-2) was reached between the petitioner and the respondent No. 3 whereby he undertook that he would not file appeal in the High court claiming back-wages or seniority etc. if respondent No. 3 regularised his services as peon-cum- cook.
He also undertook to forego the compensation amount of Rs. 10,000/-.
In the wake of this compromise, Annexure P-2, he has reinstated vide order Annexure P-3 on the post of peon-cum-cook with continuity of service on the same terms and conditions as they were before his termination dated 13.10.1993 i.e. he will continue to on adhoc basis on 89 days.
Since the appointment given to him in the wake of that compromise, Annexure P-2, did not conduce to that compromise.
he withdrew his compromise/writing.
He submitted joining report on 25111997.
Vide Annexure P-5, there was distribution of duties.
As he was given appointment as peon-cum-cook, he was given the duty of preparing tea/coffee and attend the staff.
Vide Annexure P-6 he was given appointment as peon-cum-cook on purely adhoc basis as stopgap arrangement for 89 days of till the joining of regular incumbent as per rules whichever is earlier in the pay scale of Rs. 750-940, plus usual allowances in the office of respondent No. 3 with effect from 2621998.
It was also mentioned in Annexure P-6 that he being adhoc employee will not be entitled to medical allowance.
The services of the petitioner were not extended and the post of peon-cum-cook was thrown open for recruitment.
On the appointment of duly selected regular incumbent and consequent to his joining of service on 3041998, Neel Kanth Sharma (petitioner) became surplus, as there was only one post of peon-cum-cook.
He was accordingly retrenched and he was given retrenchment compensation plus pay for the notice period in compliance of the requirement of Section 25(F)(a) and (b) of the Industrial Disputes Act.
He was also paid salary up to 3041998.
He was relieved on 151998 forenoon.
Hoshiar Singh-respondent No. 5 was selected for the post of peon-cum-cook.
6.
Learned counsel for the petitioner submitted that it was an act of grave high , handedness on the part of respondent No. 3 not to regularise the services of the petitioner, although he was in service since 2591991.
It was submitted that he was peon-cum-chowkidar since 2591991.
His appointment was continued till 18121993 but abruptly his services were terminated vide order dated 13.10.1993.
Petitioner challenged the termination of his service in the Labour Court.
UT Chandigarh.
Labour Court ordered his reinstatement into service with continuity of service.
He was reinstated with continuity of service on the post of peon-cum-chowkidar by the Labour Court.
Instead of the Labour Court's award/order, being complied with sportingly by respondent No. 3, he circumvented that order.
Respondent No. 3 reinstated him on the post of peon-cum-cook with continuity of service on the same terms and conditions as they were before on 13.10.1993.
He was to continue on adhoc basis on 89 days in the pay scale of Rs. 750-940 plus usual allowances.
He continued in service till 2621998.
7.
It was submitted that thereafter, he was malafide not allowed to continue and the post of peon-cum-cook was thrown open for recruitment.
Interview took place on 2041998.
He was not selected but Hoshiar Singh-respondent No. 5 was selected.
It was submitted that he become entitled to be regularised on or before February, 1998 in terms of instructions/policies Annexures P-7 and P-8.
As per policy Annexure P-7 dated 731996, he could ask for regularisation if he had put in 5 years of continuous service as on 3111996 and further was in service on 3111996 and had worked for a minimum period of 240 days in each year and the break in service was not more than one month at a time.
Vide Annexure P-8 dated 1831996 the period of five years was brought down to three years.
It was submitted that the petitioner was entitled to regularisation as he had put in more than three years as on 3111996 and was fulfilling the other conditions also regarding regularisation.
Instead of regularising him or taking him beyond 2621998, this post was thrown open for fresh recruitment.
In the fresh recruitment.
Hoshiar Singh-respondent No. 5 was selected.
It was submitted that he was selected because he was working at the house of the Managing Director of respondent No. 3.
8.
Learned counsel for respondents No. 1 to 3 submitted that petitioner could not ask for regularisation in terms of the aforesaid policies as these policies were never adopted by respondent No. 3.
9.
Suffice it to say, how could the petitioner be thrown out of gear when he had put in more than six years of service as on 262198, particularly when the post was there.
How could it be said that these policies were not binding on the respondents, when these policies had been framed by the Government with a view to dispel this impression that the Government was indulging in unfair labour practic., Assuming that these policies had not been adopted by the respondents, the fate of the petitioner could not be allowed to hang in the realm of uncertainties for all times to come.
Petitioner was armed with the award of the Labour Court.
Respondents did not honour this award of the Labour Court sportingly.
They did not show any grace.
Respondents should have given the post of peon-cum-chowkidar to the petitioner in terms of the award of the Labour Court.
Instead the respondents gave him post of peon-cum-cook.
10.
Learned counsel for the respondents No. 1 to 3 submitted that petitioner came to the post through back door.
He was appointed to this post on the basis of an application by him alone without there being any competition for the post.
It was submitted that there was nothing bad if selection took place afresh.
Candidates including the petitioner were interviewed.
Respondent No. 5 could make the grade vis-a-vis the petitioner and he was appointed.
11.
It was held in Jasbir Singh v. Punjab Backward Classes Land Development and Finance Corporation, Chandigarh, 1998(1) SLR 407 that there is nothing wrong if the petitioner who was refused extension and recruitment made afresh and on fresh recruitment he could not make the grade vis-a-vis others and the persons who were found to be more meritorious were appointed.
12.
In the case in hand, however, facts are quite different.
Respondents should have thought of first considering the desirability of regularising the services of the petitioner in terms of the aforesaid policies.
It was only if he had failed to be regularised that this post could be thrown open for selection afresh.
13.
In Arun Kumar Rout v. State of Bihar, 1998(1) S.C.T.
331 : 1998 SLR 61, the Hon'ble Supreme Court held that if the appellants possessed the requisite qualification and had rendered service more than 5 years, their cases for regularisation of services should be considered sympathetically.
14.
For the reasons given above, I am of the opinion that this writ petition should succeed.
This writ petition is accordingly allowed.
Respondent Nos. 1 to 3 are directed to absorb the petitioner in any class-IV post, may be on the post of peon-cum-chowkidar or peon-cum-cook or peon simplicitor etc. keeping in view his suitability.
He will always be deemed to be continuing on the job with all consequential benefits towards seniority and increments etc.
However, he will be paid 50% of the back-wages.
Respondent Nos. 1 to 3 shall consider the regularisation of his services in terms of policies/instructions Annexures P-7 and P-8.
Petition allowed.
