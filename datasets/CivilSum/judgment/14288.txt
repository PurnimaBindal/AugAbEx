RS Mongia, J (Oral) - Vide notification dated June 6, 1994 (copy Annexure P-3), the State Government had further revised the pay scales of various categories of posts under the Department of Planning (including that of the Economic and Statistical Organisation, Punjab) with effect from 111994.
The petitioners are working in the Economic and Statistical Organisation of State of Punjab as Research Assistants.
They pray that their pay scale should be revised with effect from 111986 as was done in case of certain other categories of posts under different departments on the recommendation of the Pay Commission.
Learned counsel for the petitioner brought to our notice a judgment of the Division Bench of this Court in Civil Writ Petition No. 1283 of 1996 - Swaran Singh and others v. State of Punjab and others decided on 711999 wherein the aforesaid notification was challenged as it fixed arbitrary date with effect from which the pay scale was to be revised.
Alongwith that aforesaid writ petition, certain other writ petitions were also decided.
It was specifically noticed while mentioning facts that in CWP No. 6423 of 1996 - Surinder Arora and others v. State of Punjab and others which was decided alongwith CWP No. 1283 of 1996, some of the petitioners were working as Research Assistants.
After noticing judgments of this Court and the Apex Court, the Division Bench concluded as under :-  "The doctrine of classification, which is recognised as an exception to the doctrine of equality does empower the government and public authority to classify similarly situated persons and things into different groups for the purpose of subjecting them to different treatment.
However, the burden to prove that the persons and goods belonging to one category have been rationally classified into two or more than two groups and such classification bears rational relation with the object sought to be achieved is always on the public authority.
In the case in hand the respondents have failed to prove that fixation of different dates for grant for revised pay scales has any rational relation with their decision to remove anomaly in the pay scales of the employees who were earlier put in the identical time scale.
Therefore, we are constrained to hold that the impugned notification does not satisfy the test of valid classification.
For the reasons mentioned above, the writ petitions are allowed.
The date 111994 specified in the notification dated 661994 is declared unconstitutional and the respondents are directed to amend the impugned notification so as to make the revision of pay scale effective from 111986.
The petitioners shall get all consequential benefits."
