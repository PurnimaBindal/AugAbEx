ML Singhal, J - By means of this order, I would dispose of CR No. 521 of 1993 and CR 520 of 1993 as their disposal hinges on the question of law and fact which is common to both of them.
Facts have been taken from Civil Suit No. 28/1 of 1990, date of institution 271090/1931991 decided on 321992 by Sub Judge Third Class, Ferozepur as these facts are the foundation of the litigation between Jasbir Singh and Prem Kumar, Steno-typists of the Office of Divisional Manager, Punjab Roadways, Ferozepur and the State of Punjab/Director, State Transport, Punjab, Chandigarh.
2.
Vide judgment and decree dated 321992 passed in civil suit No. 28/1 of 27.10.1990 by Shri Harjinder Pal Singh, Sub Judge Third Class, Ferozepur, Jasbir Singh Steno Typist of the office of Divisional Manager, Punjab Roadways, Ferozepur was held entitled to be considered for promotion with effect from 1.10.1990 and consequently to arrears admissible under the rules.
Facts :  3.
Jasbir Singh was appointed as Steno Typist in the Transport Department.
He joined on 21.12.1979.
He appeared in the Assistant grade examination on 2661988 and he was declared to have successfully passed that examination.
Intimation that he had successfully passed that examination was sent to the Director, State Transport, Punjab vide memo dated 2091988.
Despite the fact that he had passed that examination successfully, he was not promoted and posted as "Assistant" though the vacancy was existing on 2091988 in the cadre of "Assistants" in the Transport Department.
He made request to the Director, State Transport for promoting him to the post of Assistant and post him as such but to no effect.
While assigning the post of Assistant to him, seniority was to be fixed in the cadre of Assistants in accordance with the provisions of rule 10 of the Punjab State Assistant Grade Examinations Rules, 1984.
Delay on the part of the department in appoint him to the post of Assistant coupled with the holding and clearing of examination later on by any person senior to him is likely to affect his seniority.
Department was alleged to have been keeping the posts of Assistants vacant/unfilled although the examination had been held and the result declared.
Employees of the department who had cleared the Assistant grade examination held on 1891988 by Subordinate Services Selection Board, Punjab, Chandigarh have since been posted as Assistants whereas he who had cleared the said examination held on 2661988 has not been promoted so far.
There has, thus, been violation of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India and he has been discriminated vis-a-vis those who have been promoted.
4.
Defendant-State of Punjab contested the suit of the plaintiff urging that he was not entitled to promotion as "Assistant" with effect from the date of his passing the Assistant grade examination because promotion as Assistant could be made subject to availability of posts and seniority of the officials who qualify the examination.
Persons alleged to have been promoted on the basis of Assistant grade examination held in 8/88 were senior to the plaintiff in joint seniority list of Clerks.
All of them had been appointed as Clerks before 19121979 which is the date of appointment of the plaintiff.
Officials against whom the plaintiff was seeking relief had not been impleaded and, therefore, the suit was bad for non-joinder of necessary parties.
He was not the only official in the cadre who had cleared the Assistant grade examination held on 2661988.
There were as many as 11 officials who had cleared that examination.
As per rule 10(2) read with instructions issued by the govt. only those officials could take advantage of seniority who had passed the Assistant grade examination within first five chances when this test was introduced in April 1984.
Since the plaintiff passed this test in 8th chance, he could not take advantage of seniority over and above the other officials who had also cleared the test with him or at a later stage.
Promotion as Assistant from amongst the officials who had cleared the test after first five chances was to be made keeping in view their original seniority in the cadre and the plaintiff was junior in that respect.
Persons who had been promoted as Assistants were senior to the plaintiff because they had prior right to be promoted as Assistant.
5.
Plaintiff's suit was decreed for declaration that he was entitled to be considered for promotion in the Assistant grade of Rs. 1800-3200 with effect from 1.10.1990 and he was entitled to be considered for arrears as admissible in accordance with the departmental rules by Sub Judge Third Class, Ferozepur vide order dated 321992.
6.
Plaintiff sought to execute this decree.
State of Punjab put in objections assailing the executability of the decree urging that Director, State Transport, Punjab, Chandigarh vide his order bearing No. 24224 considered his case in the light of the decree of the Court dated 321992 and felt that he was unable to promote him which with effect from 1.10.1990 as on 1.10.1990 he was not fulfilling the seniority-cum-merit principle vis-a-vis other eligible employees.
No other employee junior to him has been promoted.
7.
Decree holder Jasbir Singh opposed these objections saying that the Director, State Transport, Punjab could not disregard the decree of the Court.
His duty was to implement the decree and not to question it as the Court had passed this decree in his favour after hearing both the parties that he was entitled to be promoted with effect from 1.10.1990 on the post of Assistant instead of from 2091988 as claimed by him.
It was also urged that the Court had found clearly in para 14 of the judgment that juniors to him had since been promoted.
It was further urged that S/Shri Gurdev Singh, Manohar Lal, Arjan Singh, Jagtar Singh, Joginder Singh, Ramesh Kumar shown to have been promoted as referred to in order dated 391992 cleared the Assistant Grade examination in the 11th, 9th, 11th, 10th, 12th and 12th batches respectively whereas decree holder Jasbir Singh had cleared the said examination in the 8th batch.
8.
Executing Court vide order dated 3.11.1992 dismissed these objections and held the decree to be executable and directed Director, State Transport, Punjab to promote the plaintiff with effect from 1.10.1990 in the scale of Rs. 1800-3200 and pay him arrears also consequential to this promotion.
State of Punjab has come up in this revision to this Court.
9.
In my opinion, the order passed by Director, State Transport, Punjab dated 391992 holding that Jasbir Singh and Prem Kumar Steno Typist of the office of Divisional Manager, Punjab Roadways, Ferozepur cannot be promoted with effect from 1.10.1990 in pursuance of the decree of the Court is quite explicit.
He has stated in this order that they were appointed on ad hoc basis on 20.12.1979 and 19121979 respectively and their services were regularised with effect from 26.10.1982.
They had passed the Assistant grade examination on 2091988 in the 8th batch.
They were considered along with other eligible officials who had qualified the Assistant grade examination for promotion as Assistant against the available pots as per departmental rules of seniority- cum-merit basis but they could not be promoted because they being junior-most.
As per departmental rules/govt.
instructions only those officials are to be considered for promotion as Assistant on seniority-cum-merit basis who fulfil the requisite conditions prescribed in departmental rules, govt. instructions and who have qualified the Assistant grade examination.
Accordingly, these officials who had qualified the Assistant grade examination who were eligible for promotion on seniority-cum-merit basis were promoted as Assistant with effect from 1.10.1990 and afterwards as under :  S. No. Name and designation S/Shri Date of appointment Date of order 1.

10.
In view of the position explained above, all the officials who were promoted as Assistants were senior to S/Shri Jasbir Singh and Prem Kumar, Steno typists.
Apart from this, it will be worthwhile to mention here that there are 39 officials who are senior to both of them and have also passed the Assistant grade examination, and they have not been promoted.
As Assistants because no post of Senior Assistant for promotion quota is available.
As such both these officials are not eligible for promotion as Senior Assistant as and when their turn will come, their case for promotion will be considered on seniority-cum-merit basis.
11.
Rule 10 of the rules lays down as follows :-  "10.
Saving of seniority - (i) Where a person who was promoted as Assistant before the commencement of these rules on provisional basis subject to his qualifying the test shall be liable to reversion to the post from which he was promoted if he fails to qualify the test within a period of three years as specified in the third proviso to rule 4 and in case such a person qualifies the test within that period his seniority shall be determined with reference to his date of promotion to the post of Assistant on provisional basis.
(2) Notwithstanding anything contained in these rules, if a person holding the post of a Clerk or any other post by whatever designation called from which he could be appointed by promotion to the post of an Assistant qualifies the test within the first two chances (first four chances).
(First five chances) available to him after his appointment to such post, he shall, on his promotion to the post of an Assistant, be assigned seniority in the cadre of Assistant in accordance with is seniority in the appointment from which he has been promoted to the post of Assistant.
Provided that if such a person fails to qualify the test within the aforesaid first five chances, he shall on his promotion to the post of Assistant be assigned seniority in the cadre of Assistant from the day he is promoted as such :" 12.
In my opinion, the Director, State Transport justifiably declined to implement the decree as the decree was passed by the Court in disregard of the rules and instructions on the subject.
Decree passed by the Court was nullity and executing Court cannot execute the decree which is nullity i.e. non est having no legal sanction behind it.
Decree was nullity on another score also; namely the persons against whom Jasbir Singh and Prem Kumar were claimed seniority had not been impleaded.
13.
These revisions succeed and are allowed.
Objection put in by the State of Punjab to the executability of the decree are allowed.
In consequence, execution petition has to be dismissed and is accordingly dismissed.
Revisions allowed.
