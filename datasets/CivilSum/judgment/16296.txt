NK Sodhi, J - The sole grievance of the petitioner in this petition under Article 226 of the Constitution of India is that his seniority as a Sub-Inspector in the seniority list prepared on 111970 had not been correctly shown and having been placed junior to many others who were initial junior to him as Assistant Sub-Inspectors, his future chances of promotion have been adversely affected.
2.
The brief facts in so far as they are relevant for appreciating the controversy may first be stated.
The petitioner joined the Police department as an Assistant Sub-Inspector on probation on April 14, 1960 and was later confirmed on this post on 14.4.1963.
He was selected for the upper school course in March 1966 and his name was brought on list 'E' on 1111966 in accordance with the provisions of Punjab Police Rules, 1934 (referred to hereinafter as the Rules) for promotion to the next higher; post of Sub- Inspector of Police.
He was actually promoted as a Sub-Inspector on 15.11.1966 and while he was so working in the year 1972, he was reverted on 25.5.1972 to the post of Assistant Sub-Inspector and the following order was passed by the Deputy Inspector General of Police, Ambala Range :-  "As ordered by the Deputy Inspector General of Police, Ambala Range, Ambala Cantt.
vide PRM No. 13947/A-1 dated 25th May, 1972 offg.
SI Avtar Krishan No. 245/A who has no right to this post of SI is reverted to the rank of ASI with effect from 25.5.1972 purely on administrative grounds.
This reversion does not involve any penal consequnces or stigma to him.
His further promotion depend on his work.
On reversion he will remain posted to this district."
The petitioner made a detailed representation against his reversion to the Inspector General of Police, Haryana under Rule 14.7 of the Rules.
After a year or so, the petitioner was again promoted as Sub-Inspector on 25.6.1973 whereafter he was further promoted to the post of Inspector on 15.7.1980 after his name had been brought on list 'F'.
He crossed his efficiency bar on 1.7.1986 and since some vacancies of Deputy Superintendents were available, the petitioner being eligible and fairly senior amongst the Inspectors claimed that he ought to have been promoted but when his claim was ignored and some persons junior to him were promoted.
he moved this Court by filing C.W.P.
No. 4232 of 1988.
He was, however, promoted as Deputy Superintendent during the pendency of that writ petition on 9.3.1989 on which post he is presently continuing.
The writ petition, thus became infructuous and was dismissed as such.
3.
Now coming to the question of seniority which is the subject matter of the present writ petition, it may be stated that the department prepared the gradation list of Sub-Inspectors and Assistant Sub-Inspectors on 111970 and the petitioner as an Assistant Sub-Inspector stood at Sr. No. 31 of the said list having been placed immediately below one Mr. Ram Chander, who was at Sr. No. 30.
The petitioner does not dispute the place of seniority assigned to him in this gradation list.
Since the petitioner had already been promoted as Sub-Inspector on 15.11.1966 his name also appeared in the seniority list of Sub-Inspectors as it stood on 111970.
In this seniority list, the petitioner was shown at Sr. No. 116 whereas Ram Chander who was immediately above him as Assistant Sub-Inspector was shown at Sr. No. 59.
The petitioner has alleged that this seniority list although prepared by the department was never circulated and the petitioner had no opportunity to make any representation against this list.
A large number of persons who were junior to the petitioner as Assistant Sub-Inspectors had been shown senior to him as Sub-Inspectors.
In the meantime the petitioner had been promoted as Inspector on 15.7.1980.
The seniority list of Inspectors as prepared by the Police department, Haryana as on 111981 showed Ram Chander at Sr. No. 30, whereas the petitioner was placed at Sr. No. 200.
The petitioner alleges that the same distortion which had taken place in the seniority list of Sub-Inspectors as on 111970 continued to exist and the petitioner was not assigned his due place in the seniority list as prepared by the department on 111987 and 141989.
It is the case of the petitioner that as and when these gradation lists were circulated he represented to the department and not having had any favourable response he approached this Court and filed the present writ petition.
4.
The case of the petitioner is that in the gradation list of Assistant Sub-Inspectors as it stood on 111970 he was assigned his seniority at Sr. No. 31.
His grievance is that persons who were junior to him as Assistant Sub-Inspectors had been shown senior in the gradation list of Sub-Inspectors and this is sought to be justified by respondents 1 to 3 on the ground that the petitioner had been reverted by an order dated 25.5.1972 and therefore the place presently assigned to him in the seniority list as Sub-Inspector is correct.
It is further stated that the reports earned by the petitioner during January 1966 upto May 1972 proved him unfit for promotion to the higher rank and it was for this reason that his representation was rejected.
The argument of the State in this regard cannot be sustained.
The petitioner was reverted in May, 1972 and even if this order of reversion was assumed to be legal, it could not affect his seniority as given to him earlier as Assistant Sub-Inspector on 111970.
The reversion took place on 25.5.1972 whereas the seniority of the petitioner was displaced to his detriment in 1970.
The respondents have not been able to furnish any justification for this action and the learned counsel appearing for the State could not point out anything from the record justifying the change made in the seniority list as it stood on 111970 wherein a number of Juniors were shown as senior to the petitioner as Sub-Inspectors.
The learned counsel for the respondents virtually conceded the case of the petitioner in this regard.
5.
For the reasons recorded above, I allow this writ petition, quash the seniority list of Sub-Inspector as on 111970 in so far as it pertains to the petitioner and direct respondents 1 to 3 to refix his seniority according to Rules keeping in view his seniority as Assistant Sub-Inspector and after giving him an opportunity to be heard.
Resultantly, the seniority assigned to the petitioner in the subsequent seniority lists prepared on 111981, 111987 and 141989 would also be corrected and his due place assigned to him with all consequential benefits, if any, to which he may be found entitled under the Rules.
There shall be no order as to costs.
