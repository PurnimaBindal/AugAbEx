Satyabrata Sinha, J - FMAT No. 3100 of 1996 is directed against an order dated 11996 passed by a learned single Judge of this court in CR No. 9635(W) of 1984 whereby and whereunder the said learned Court refused to review the order dated 31st July, 1996.
2.
FMAT No. 3114 of 1996 is directed against an order passed by the same learned Judge dated 22111985 in CR No. 9635(W) of 1984 whereby and whereunder the aforementioned order dated 11996 was corrected.
3.
An application under Section 5 of the Limitation Act has been filed in the later appeal as the said Appeal is barred by 300 days.
The appellants in both the appeals are said to be the Secretary, Managing Committee and the Headmaster of Vivekananda Pally Kishore Bharati High School (hereinafter referred to as the said school).
The said school is a X-Class High School and affiliated to the West Bengal Board of Secondary Education.
The respondent No. 1 is said to have been appointed on 2031975 as an assistant teacher of the said school.
Allegedly he was absent for 42 days in a year and his services were terminated purported to be in terms of letter of appointment dated 27376.
4.
Appeals were preferred against the said order before the Appeal Committee which were registered as Appeal No. 90/76 and Appeal No. 52/81.
The said Appeals were allegedly dismissed A writ petition was thereafter filed and by a judgment dated 29.8.89 Ajit Kumar Sengupta, J directed the authorities to appoint the respondent No. 1 on certain conditions and further directed the Director of School Education, West Bengal, to consider his case.
The said order was allegedly not complied with by the Director of School Education.
The Director of School Education filed an appeal which was registered as FMAT No. 75/90 but the said appeal was dismissed.
A review petition was also dismissed by a division bench of this court in the aforementioned appeal by an order dated 18490.
An application for special leave to file an appeal was filed before the Hon'ble Supreme Court of India but the same was not allowed.
It appears that in the meantime another writ application was filed for implementing the said order.
The matter was placed before several learned single Judges but they did not like to hear the appeal.
It is stated that before the learned Judge a prayer was made to refer the matter before the division bench which was refused.
By an order dated 23.11.1995 the said writ petition was disposed of with certain directions.
A contempt petition was filed in the meantime for non-compliance of the order dated 22111995 and a notice was directed to be issued as against the District Inspector of School Secondary Education, Calcutta and the respondent No. 1 Managing Committee as to why have they not complied with the order dated 22111995.
However, no contempt rule was issued on that date and 14th August 1996 was the date fixed therefor.
5.
An application for recalling the order and judgment dated 22111995 (wrongly written as 31.7.1996) was filed.
Before the learned Judge, it appears that prior to filing of the said review application, an application for correcting certain typographical mistakes had already been filed.
The main ground upon which the said application for modification was filed was that the learned trial Judge had in his judgment dated 1191996 stated:  "This may be treated to be a fresh appointment but age will be taken to be a bar in making such an appointment."
6.
The learned trial Judge in his impugned judgment dated 1191996 held that the entire order has to be read as a whole and the said order contains a typographical error inasmuch as the word, not, in between the words between the words `will' and `be' had not been typed.
7.
After the aforementioned order was passed, the aforementioned FMAT No. 3114/96 was filed questioning the order dated 22.11.95.
Evidently, the aforementioned order dated 22.11.95 was passed keeping in view the order passed by Ajit Kumar Sengupta, J  8.
Mr. Harasit Chakrabarty, the learned Counsel appearing on behalf of the petitioner, inter alia, submitted that in terms of the Recruitment Rules, age bar could not be directed to be condoned.
9.
We do not find any substance in the said submission.
The events referred to herein before clearly demonstrate that the judgment of AK Sengupta, J dated 28989 had been upheld finally and the same was binding on the appellants.
The learned single Judge has merely reiterated the orders passed by AK Sengupta, J, which had attained finally.
The appellants must be held to have filed FMAT No. 3114/96 by way of an after-thought.
If they were aggrieved by and dissatisfied with the judgment of the learned single Judge dated 22.11.95 an appeal could have been filed earlier.
We also do not find any substance in the application of condonation of delay.
10.
So far as the order dated 1191996 correcting the judgment is concerned, we agree with the learned single Judge that the mistake was apparent on the face of the record.
The learned trial Judge had the jurisdiction to correct a typographical mistake which was apparent on the face of the record.
In our opinion, the learned trial Judge has correctly held that the entire order has to be read as a whole.
Furthermore, the apex court and this court in similar cases had been directing condonation of age bar keeping in view the fact that the case of the teacher concerned is required to be taken into consideration as he had worked in the school for a long time.
11.
Mr. Chakrabarty has sought to point out various misconducts on the part of the respondent No. 1.
We are not concerned therewith as the services of the respondent No. 1 had not been terminated on that ground.
12.
For the reasons aforementioned we refuse to condone the delay in FMAT No. 3114/96 as we do not find that there was any sufficient cause therefor and thus the application under Section 5 of the Limitation Act and consequently the appeal stands dismissed.
We also find no merit in the appeal being FMAT No. 3100/96 for the reasons stated herein before.
13.
In the result, both appeals are dismissed but without any order as to costs.
Appeals dismissed.
