Surya Kant, J - Petitioner-Tarsem Lal has approached this court under Article 226 of the Constitution of India seeking issuance of a writ in the nature of certiorari for quashing of two memos dated 31101984 (Annexure P-8) and dated 411990 (Annexure P-11) with a further prayer to issue a writ in the nature of mandamus to command the respondents to confirm him w.e.f.
30101972, the date when his juniors were confirmed and to grant him all the consequential benefits like salary, increments as well as promotion with effect from the date his juniors were promoted and also to place him at Sr. No. 78 in the seniority list of Junior Engineers instead of at Sr. No. 154, as also to allow him to cross efficiency bar from the due date.
2.
As per averments made in the writ petition, the petitioner joined the Punjab Public Health Department as Junior Engineer on 21.12.1963 and successfully completed the period of probation; though the petitioner was due to be confirmed on completion of the maximum period of probation, yet he was confirmed with effect from 2891978 vide letter dated 491984 (Annexure P- 1); the petitioner was shown in the seniority list, circulated in the year 1987, at Sr. No. 154 whereas his juniors, S/Shri Darshan Singh, Bhajan Singh and Sham Lal Bansal were placed at Sr. No. 78, 79 and 80 respectively, during the years 1965 to 1976, because of some inimical relations and with a view to harass and victimize him, adverse comments were made in his annual confidential reports and some of them were never even conveyed to him whereas against the remaining adverse reports, he submitted his appeals which were not decided, the petitioner was not allowed to cross the efficiency bar in the year 1976 and it was after repealed representations that vide order dated April 8, 1982 (Annexure P-6) the petitioner was allowed to cross the efficiency bar w.e.f.
1.12.1977 only; that despite government circular dated 1891985 (Annexure P-7) providing that if junior official was confirmed from a specific date, then the case of his senior is liable to be considered from the same date, but no such relief was extended to the petitioner though one Mani Ram, JE was confirmed retrospectively from the date his junior was confirmed; the representation of the petitioner was rejected vide an order dated 31101984 (Annexure P-8) which the petitioner challenged in a civil suit before the Civil Court at Faridkot; the suit was decreed by the learned Civil Court vide judgment and decree dated 16101987 (Annexure P-9) holding the petitioner entitled for confirmation from 30101972 along with all consequential benefits; the State of Punjab filed an appeal against the decree dated 16101987 and the learned First Appellate Court at Faridkot, vide judgment and decree dated 10111989 (Annexure P-10), modified the decree to the extent that the petitioner was entitled to be considered for confirmation from 30101972 along with all consequential benefits; the petitioner filed an execution application before the Executing Court in which the respondents took the plea that the claim of the petitioner had already been considered in compliance of the judgment of the learned First Appellate Court and an order dated 411990 (Annexure P-11) was passed whereby his claim for confirmation w.e.f.
30101972 was reconsidered and rejected as well; the Executing Court dismissed the execution application vide an order dated 28.8.1990 on the ground that the order passed by the respondents dated 411990 could be challenged by the petitioner in a separate suit; the petitioner thereafter approached this Court in Civil Revision No. 1810 of 1991 but the same was also dismissed on 22101991; according to the petitioner, the adverse reports for the period from 1.3.1965 to 31.3.1966, 1966-67 and 1967-68 were never conveyed to him whereas the adverse entries relating to the period from 11.7.1975 to 3131977 were expugned; that the petitioner was served with a charge-sheet dated 3111979 (Annexure P-13) on the basis of the adverse entries and even an inquiry officer was appointed; however, when it was revealed before the inquiry officer that most of the adverse entries were either non-existent or had already been expugned or the appeals were pending against the same, the charge-sheet dated 3111979 was withdrawn vide order dated 19101981 (Annexure P-14); that in the Civil suit filed by the petitioner, one official of the respondent-Department, namely, Shri Babu Ram appeared as a defence witness and in his statement (Annexure P-15) he admitted that but for the charge-sheet dated 3111979, the petitioner would have been confirmed w.e.f.
30101972; that since the afore-mentioned charge-sheet was subsequently withdrawn by the respondents, there is no rhyme or reason in law to deny him confirmation from the due date and consequential seniority and other benefits; that the action of the respondents is violative of principles of natural justice inasmuch as, had any opportunity been given to the petitioner, he would have shown that the so-called adverse reports were not conveyed to him and the one which were conveyed, the adverse remarks of those reports had been expugned.

9.
I have heard Shri PS Patwalia, learned Senior Counsel for the petitioner and Shri KK Goel, learned Additional Advocate-General, Punjab for the respondents and have perused the record.
10.
From the above extracted pleadings of the parties, there hardly remains any doubt that adverse reports for the periods from (i) 13465 to 31366, (ii) 1466 to 30666, (iii) 1967-68, and (iv) 1967-68 were not conveyed to the petitioner and thus no opportunity was given to him to represent against the afore-mentioned adverse remarks.
Similarly, the adverse remarks for the period from 11775 to 31376 were expunged by the Chief Engineer vide his order dated January 23, 1981.
It has, however, clearly come on the record that the adverse reports for the periods (i) 1968-69, (ii) 9869 to 31370, (iii) 1470 to 31371, (iv) 1471 to 20.9.71, (v) 1972-73, and (vi) 11775 to 31376 were actually conveyed to the petitioner and adverse remarks in the afore-mentioned last report were expunged by the Chief Engineer after accepting the representation of the petitioner.
Be that as it may, there are five adverse reports duly conveyed to the petitioner coupled with two punishment orders dated 1781968 and 2581970 which form part of the petitioner's service record which was relevant for considering his case for confirmation w.e.f.
30101972.
However, as per the stand taken by the respondents in para 3 of the written statement even the adverse reports for the periods from 13465 to 31366, 1966-67 and 1967-68 were also taken into consideration while considering the case of the petitioner for confirmation w.e.f.
30101972.
From the adverments made in paras 11 and 12 of the written statement, it appears that while reconsidering the case of the petitioner for confirmation w.e.f.
30101972 in terms of the directions issued by the learned First Appellate Court at Faridkot, afore-mentioned uncommunicated reports were again taken into consideration.
Unfortunately, what was the criterion for confirmation in service at the relevant time has not been disclosed either by the petitioner or by the respondents.
In my view, this is a case where, after excluding the uncommunicated adverse reports, there is no other adverse material on the service record of the petitioner and thus a direction to confirm him from 30101972 can be issued.
Similarly, it cannot be overlooked that as per the case of the respondents themselves, even uncommunicated reports were also taken into account while considering the case of the petitioner for confirmation.
In view of the settled law that uncommunicated adverse reports cannot be relied upon to deny confirmation or promotion etc., the uncommunicated adverse reports against which the petitioner was denied an opportunity to represent, could not have been relied upon to hold him unsuitable for confirmation w.e.f.
30101972.
The claim of the petitioner regarding confirmation from 30101972, therefore, requires to be reconsidered to the limited extent that the respondents shall consider his case for confirmation from 30101972 afresh after ignoring the uncommunicated adverse reports and as per the criterion prevalent at the relevant time.
If the petitioner is found suitable for confirmation after excluding the uncommunicated reports, then he will be entitled for all the consequential benefits including seniority as well as consideration for promotion from the dates when his juniors were promoted.
Needless to say that if, on the basis of remaining communicated adverse reports coupled with two orders of punishments which form part of the service record at the relevant time, the competent authority finds that the petitioner is still unsuitable for confirmation from 30101972, it shall pass a speaking order and convey the same to the petitioner.
11.
Shri Patwalia, however, contended that since the departmental official who appeared as a defence witness in the civil suit has admitted in his statement (Annexure P-15) that the petitioner would have been confirmed from 30101972 but for the charge-sheet dated 3111979 (Annexure P-13) and the afore- mentioned charge-sheet having been withdrawn subsequently vide order dated 9101981 (Annexure P-14), it is imperative for the respondents to confirm the petitioner from 30101972.
I, however, find no merit in this argument.
Shri Babu Ram, SDO, who appeared as DW1 in the civil suit and made the statement Annexure P-15, was not the competent authority to consider the claim of the petitioner for confirmation from 30101972.
It was, at the best, an opinion or perception of an official, which had no legal or factual basis.
On the basis of such an opinion neither the petitioner can invoke the principle of estoppel nor it has any binding effect upon the competent authority.
As noticed earlier, even after ignoring the uncommunicated adverse reports, there still exists adverse material against the petitioner comprising of communicated adverse reports and the orders of punishment, therefore, it is not a case where the petitioner was denied confirmation from 30101972 merely on issuance of a charge-sheet to him.
In my considered view, the charge-sheet dated 3111979 (Annexure P-13) was a misconceived action as no charge-sheet could have been issued on the basis of adverse remarks or the orders of punishment already passed against the petitioner pursuant to some departmental actions.
On reconsideration of the matter, the authorities presumably realised their mistake and withdrew the charge-sheet.
It has no bearing, at all, on the claim of the petitioner for confirmation w.e.f.
30101972.
12.
Shri Patwalia then contended that in view of the findings recorded by the Civil Court and upheld by the First Appellate Court at Faridkot, the adverse reports, if any, lost their sting and the respondents were duty bound to consider the petitioner's claim for retrospective confirmation on the premise as if there existed no adverse material against the petitioner.
I have gone through the findings recorded by the learned Civil Court in its judgment dated 16101987 as also in the judgment dated 11101989 passed by the learned First Appellate Court at Faridkot (photostat copies of which were supplied by Shri Patwalia).
I gather from the afore-mentioned judgments that the civil suit was decreed primarily on the strength of the statement made by the defence witness (Annexure P-15) wherein it was stated that, but for issuance of the charge-sheet (Annexure P-13), the petitioner would have been confirmed w.e.f.
30101972.
I have already dealt with this aspect of the matter and in view thereof find no force in this submission as well.
13.
For the reasons stated above, the writ petition is disposed of with a direction to the respondents to reconsider the claim of the petitioner for confirmation w.e.f.
30101972 by ignoring the uncommunicated adverse reports and as per the criterion applicable at the relevant time.

In case, the petitioner's claim for confirmation w.e.f.
