Amarjeet Chaudhary, J - The petitioner while posted as Kanungo at village Jullana, Tehsil and District Jind was served with a three months' notice under the Punjab Civil Services Rules, Volume II, Rule 5.32 A (C) read with Rule 326(D) of Volume I, Part I of the said Rules, dated 2151987, Annexure P-7 prematurely retiring him from service after expiry of notice period.
This decision was taken in view of service record of the petitioner.
The petitioner has impugned the said order Annexure P-7 in the instant writ petition.
2.
The brief facts of the case are that the petitioner was appointed as Sub Inspector Consolidation of Holdings in the erstwhile State of Pepsu on 27.7.1954.
He was subsequently promoted as Patwari on which post he continued to work till 5.6.1984 when he was promoted as Kanungo.
A copy of the order of promotion as Kanungo is Annexure P-1 to the petition.
The petitioner was also allowed to cross efficiency bar with effect from 171982 vide order dated 28111983, a copy of which is Annexure P-3 to the petition.
The petitioner conveyed adverse remarks for the year 1963-64 and his honesty was judged doubtful.
These remarks were conveyed to the petitioner by the Settlement Officer, Consolidation of Holdings, Patiala.
However, the petitioner earned 12 confidential reports prior to the year 1971-72 and out of these 12 confidential reports the petitioner earned only three good reports and the remaining 9 were average/satisfactory.
3.
During the last 10 years, the petitioner had earned only 8 reports, out of which six are good, two average i.e. for the years 1977-78 and 1985-86 while two reports for the year 1981-82 and 1982-83 could not be recorded as the petitioner remained under suspension from 2971981 to 6.6.1983 on the charges of corruption and later on, the petitioner was reinstated because the criminal court acquitted him by giving benefit of doubt.
4.
The petitioner's case is that for retaining an employee into service beyond the age of 55 years, his last 10 years, confidential reports are to be taken into consideration.
Moreover, when the petitioner was allowed to cross efficiency bar and given promotion also, his old and stale adverse entries cannot be taken into consideration.
It was urged that out of the last 10 reports the petitioner has earned six good reports and two reports were not recorded because of petitioner's suspension.
On the contrary, the case of the respondents is that the entire service record of the petitioner is to be kept in view while retaining him into service beyond the age of 55 years.
Adverse remarks given to the petitioner during the year 1963-64 and 1971-72 were conveyed to him but the petitioner did not file any representation against both these adverse remarks for getting the said remarks expunged.
5.
I have considered the submissions made at the Bar and carefully perused the entire paper-book.
6.
This Court in various pronunciation has held that when an employee is permitted to cross efficiency bar and given promotion, old and stale adverse entries are not to be taken into consideration.
This view was taken by this Court in Dr. Om Parkash v. State of Haryana, 1988(6), S.L.R.
370 following the ratio of Baldev Raj Chadha v. Union of India and others, 1980(3) S.L.R.
1.
It is also the admitted case of the respondents that the petitioner during the last 10 years has earned 8 reports out of which six are good; two average and two reports for the years 1981-82 and 1982-83 were not recorded.
Even as per Government's own instructions, an employee who has earned 70 per cent good reports during the past 10 years, is to be retained into service.
In the present case, since only 8 reports were recorded during the last 10 years, out of which six were good and two average, the petitioner had, therefore, earned 75 per cent good reports.
Moreover, the average reports which were not communicated cannot be treated as adverse.
7.
Even in KK Vaid v. State of Haryana, 1990(1) S.L.R.
1, a Division Bench of this Court quashed the government policy framed vide letter No. 32/168/83-84/GSI dated 1981983 which envisages that every employee has to get at least seven annual confidential reports as Good/Very Good/Excellent out of the last ten reports for being retained in service beyond the age of 55 years.
It was held in that case that a government servant has a right in law to continue to serve till the age of superannuation i.e. 58 years, as provided in Rule 326 of the Rules and the tenure of the service cannot be brought to an end in a mechanical manner on the basis of adverse reports relating to the period of past service.
8.
For the foregoing reasons, this petition is allowed and the impugned order dated 2151987 Annexure P-7 retiring the petitioner from service is quashed.
The petitioner shall be entitled to all the consequential benefits.
In the peculiar facts and circumstances of the case, the parties are left to bear their own costs.
Petition allowed.
