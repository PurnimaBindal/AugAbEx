RS Mongia, J (Oral).
To decide the controversy in the present case, it would be necessary to give detailed factual matrix.
2.
On 8th April 1981, the petitioner was selected as a temporary Lecturer, after being interviewed by a properly constituted selection committee, in the Department of Zoology of the Panjab University, Chandigarh.
The said selection was approved by the Syndicate of the University on 18th April, 1981.
In pursuance of the letter of appointment dated 27th April, 1981, the petitioner had joined her duties on 28th April, 1981.
In the approval granted by the Syndicate on 18th April, 1981, it was mentioned that the petitioner was to report for duty on Ist July, 1981.
However, in the later meeting of the Syndicate held on 16th May, 1981, the approval regarding the appointment of the petitioner was given w.e.f.
28th April, 1981 (i.e. the date she had given) instead of Ist July, 1981.
The petitioner continued to work as such and she was also given increment in her pay w.e.f.
Ist April, 1982.
On 25th June 1982, the Chairman of the Department of Zoology wrote a letter to the Vice-Chancellor, which reads as under :-  "DEPARTMENT OF ZOOLOGY PANJAB UNIVERSITY CHANDIGARH.
No. 1155/Z/82 Dated 2561982  Dr (Mrs.) Paramjit Tewari and Dr. PK Mittal are working as temporary Lecturers in this Department against leave vacancies of the following persons :-  1.
Dr. Sudarshan Chaudhary, Lecturer.
2.
Dr. RC Sobti, Lecturer  Dr. Sudarshan Chaudhary has written to me that he will be resuming his duty in this department on 2561982.
One of the temporary Lectures will, thus be relieved as soon as Dr. Chaudhary joins the Department.
At that moment, the following vacancies of lecturers exist in the Department :-  1.
Lecturer in Zoology - 1 (vacancy caused by the Promotion of Dr SM Handa as Reader).
2.
Lecturer in Zoology-1 (Leave vacancy of Dr. SK Taneja) 3.
Lecturer in Zoology-1 (Leave vacancy of Dr. RC Sobti) I, therefore, recommend that Dr. (Mrs) Paramjit Tewari and Dr. PK Mittal may be allowed to continue to work as temporary lecturers against the three vacancies enumerated above in the Department.
Sd/-  (S.Khera)
Chairman, Department of Zoology
PU Chandigarh."
3.
The Senate of the respondent-University which is the appointing authority of a lecturer vide Resolution dated 19th December, 1982, approved the extension of appointment of the petitioner for one year or till the selection was made against the two vacant posts of lecturers lying in the Department.
The next increment had been given to the petitioner w.e.f.
Ist April, 1983.
The Chairman of the Department of Zoology was informed by the Vice Chancellor vide his letter dated 14th July, 1983 that the term of the petitioner's appointment had been extended for a period of three months more w.e.f.
Ist June, 1983.
It may be observed here that though there was no specific order for continuance in service for the period from 28th April, 1983 to 31st May, 1983, yet she was given pay for this period as she had actually worked during the said period.
Still further, vide letter of the Vice Chancellor dated 20th September, 1983, the Chairman of the Department of Zoology was informed that the period of petitioner's appointment had further been extended for three months upto 20th November, 1983.
However, when the matter was placed before the Syndicate on October 22, 1983 regarding the extension of petition's appointment, it was resolved as under :-  "The appointment in the Department of Zoology under Item No. 6 of Statement (b) would be upto the end of academic session 1983-84 instead of upto 30th November, 1983."
It may be observed here that Item No. 6 of Statement (B) related to the petitioner.
Later on petitioner was selected for regular appointment as a lecturer in the Department of Zoology and vide letter dated 27th March, 1984, the petitioner was informed that her selection as a regular lecturer in the Department had been approved by the Senate on 25th March, 1984.
Though the petitioner was already working as a temporary lecturer in the same Department She formerly joined as regular lecturer on 28th March, 1984.
It may further be observed that the petitioner's pay which she was drawing as temporary lecturer was protected.
4.
Though, according to the learned counsel for the petitioner, she had worked upto March 27, 1984 as temporary lecturer and thereafter as regular lecturer, her pay for the period from March 12, 1984 to March 27, 1984 was not released.
5.
The grievance of the petitioner is that in fact the petitioner had worked for the abovesaid period i.e. 12th March, 1984 to 27th March, 1984 as a temporary lecturer, but by not granting the pay for the said period and by treating the petitioner as a fresh appointee on regular basis, there would be a break in service, which would prejudicially affect her benefits like provident fund, gratuity and seniority for the purpose of house allotment.
6.
The case put forth by the respondent in the written statement is that the Vice Chancellor had extended the term of appointment of the petitioner as a temporary lecturer upto November 30, 1983 or till regular incumbent had joined, whichever was earlier, The appointment of the petitioner stood automatically terminated w.e.f.
March 11, 1984 on the joining of regular incumbent against whose vacancy the petitioner was working as a temporary lecturer.
7.
It may be observed that the Chairman of the Department of Zoology informed the University vide letter dated March 17, 1990 (Annexure P/14) that injustice had been done to the petitioner.
He had enclosed certain documents with that letter to show that in fact the petitioner had taught during the disputed period.
In response to the University Authorities query, the then Chairman of the Department, Sh. OP Mittal again wrote back to the University authorities on April 11, 1991 as under :-  "Kindly refer to letter No. 7477/Estt.
dated 2231991.
Evidently "the circumstances under which Dr. (Mrs.) Paramjit Kamal Tewari continued to work lecturer between 1231984 to 27.3.1984 without any appointment letter".
Could be because that since she had already been employed and there was work-load requiring her presence in the Department that she was not asked to discontinue teaching.
There is conclusive evidence of her having taught during the period in question, her salary bills having been signed and sent for payment by the then Chairman, and of another post having been lying vacant in the Department.
Whether it was inadvertence on the part of the then Chairman or he thought it fit to allow her to continue in the interest of the students in view of the fact that another post was lying vacant in the Department, the fact remains that Dr. Paramjit Tewari continued to teach without being informed of the decision mentioned in your letter.
In view of the above, it is clear that since there was no prior communication to Dr. Paramjit Kamal Tewari to discontinue teaching and she completed her assignment and did not let the teaching work in the Department suffer, she should be given her due."
From the above correspondence, learned counsel for the petitioner, submitted that it was beyond any pale of doubt that petitioner had in fact worked during the disputed period i.e. from March 12, 1984 to March 27, 1984 and, therefore, there was no question of putting any break in the service of the petitioner and she was entitled to salary for the said period as also to other benefits accruing to her from the continuous service i.e. temporary service followed by regular service without any break.
8.
Learned counsel for the respondent-University tried to support the action of the respondents on the basis of the stand already noticed above.
9.
After hearing the learned counsel for the parties I am of the view that this writ petition deserves to succeed.
10.
From the narration of the facts noticed above, it would be seen that the petitioner had been continuing in service in pursuance of the initial letter of appointment dated April 27, 1981 and no further letter of appointment appointing the petitioner on temporary basis was issued thereafter.
The approval of the University authorities regarding the extension of temporary appointment from time to time was given to the petitioner with retrospective effect.
The letter from the Chairman of the Department dated April 11, 1981 (already quoted above) clearly goes to show that factually the petitioner had worked as a temporary Lecturer in the Department during the disputed period i.e. from March 12, 1984 to March 27, 1984.
Coupled with this is the fact that though the Vice Chancellor had recommended the extension of the petitioner's temporary appointment till November 30, 1983, or till regular incumbent joined, whichever was earlier, the Syndicate vide its resolution dated October 22, 1983 had approved the temporary appointment of the petitioner upto the academic session 1983-84.
So the end result of all this is that the petitioner had actually worked during the disputed period i.e. from March 12, 1984 to March 27, 1984 as a temporary lecturer and the said appointment during that period stood already approved by the Syndicate on October 22, 1983 as the approval was for the session 1983-84 Mr. Majithia learned counsel for the respondent, on the written instructions from the University has informed me that the session 1983-1984 was from July 1983 to June 1984.
Consequently, it is held that the petitioner had worked as a temporary lecturer in the Department of Zoology from April 28, 1981 to March 27, 1984 when she was appointed on regular basis.
It goes without saying that on the basis of this finding the petitioner would be entitled to the benefits like provident fund, gratuity and allotment of house etc. as per the regulations of the University.
It is fairly conceded by the learned counsel for the petitioner that this period of temporary appointment is not to be counted for the purpose of seniority as a lecturer in the Department.
For the foregoing reasoning, this writ petition is allowed and it is held that the petitioner had worked as a temporary lecturer from April 28 1981 to March 27, 1984 to March 27, 1984 and other benefits flowing from such temporary continuous appointment followed by regular appointment.
I make no order as to costs.
Order accordingly
