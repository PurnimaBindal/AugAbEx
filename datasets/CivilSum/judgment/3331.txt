RL Anand, J - The petitioners have filed the present writ petition under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India for the issuance of the necessary directions and in particular for the quashing of para 28 of the Govt. of India, Ministry of Defence letter dated 2521994 wherein One Time Increase in Pension has been reduced by 10% for each year of re-employed service rendered after retirement.
As per the petitioners, this letter is arbitrary, discriminatory and against the principles of natural justice, equity and fair play.
2.
The case set up by the petitioners is that the petitioners 1 to 3 retired from Army after completing the pensionable service and were granted pension as per the rules.
The husband of petitioner No. 4 retired from Army after completing the pensionable service.
He was also granted pension as per the rules.
However, after his death, petitioner No. 4 being the widow, was granted family pension.
According to the petitioners, petitioners 1 to 3 and husband of petitioner No. 4 retired from Army at a young age and they got re- employed after earning pension.
Petitioner No. 1 was re-employed against a vacancy of Ex-serviceman in CISF w.e.f.
6.12.1972 and was discharged on 3171982.
Petitioner No. 2 was re-employed in Defence Security Corps on 12111986 and was discharged on 1421991.
Petitioner No. 3 was re-employed in N.C.C.
on 17101961 and was discharged on 17101967.
Husband of petitioner No. 4 was re-employed in General Reserve Engineer Force on 30.12.1960 and was discharged on 31.5.1967.
Petitioner No. 5 was re-employed in Defence Security Corps on 2041985 and discharged on 171992.
According to the petitioners, they did not get any benefit of re-employed service for enhancement of pension or payment of any retirement gratuity.
All the persons retired prior to 1986 and they got nominal pension and started facing financial hardship.
The Govt. of India in order to grant relief to the defence pensioners who retired prior to 1986, granted One Time Increase in Pension as per scales given vide Govt. of India letter dated 1631992.
According to the petitioners this letter was not made applicable to the Defence Forces Pensioners, who after retirement from Army and after earning pension got re-employed in Central/State services, including banks and public sector undertakings.
Furthermore, on 2521994, the Govt. of India issued yet another letter no. 1(3)/93/D (Pension/Services), granting One Time Increase in Pension in respect of Armed Forces Pensioners who were left over vide Govt. letter dated 1631992 but the scale of one time increase in pension was made applicable w.e.f.
111992.
The grouse of the petitioners is that para 28 of the letter dated 2521994 has reduced the pension by 10% for each year of re-employed service upto 10 years.
With these broad allegations, the petitioners have made the above prayers.
3.
Notice of the writ petition was given to the respondents who filed the written statement and denied the allegations and tried to justify para 28 of the letter by stating that this letter is not arbitrary and there is a reasonable classification because the petitioners when got re-employed in civil services, were benefited in their emoluments and got other benefits.
4.
I have heard the counsel for the parties and with their assistance have gone through the record of this case.
5.
Counsel for the petitioners tried to convince me that para 28 of the letter dated 2521994 is discriminatory.
According to the counsel for the petitioners, when the petitioners were re-employed, they were re-employed on account of their merit and not on account of their misconduct.
If the petitioners have benefited monetarily, the same is on account of their work which they had been discharging when they were in the civil services.
He further submitted that the petitioners had served the civil departments for less than 10 years and, therefore, para 28 is discriminatory in nature because the discharged personnel, who served the civil departments for more than 10 years, are not being affected.
6.
On the contrary, counsel for the respondents relies upon a judgment of the Delhi High Court dated 25.5.1995 passed in CWP 2995 of 1994 (Bhim Singh v.
U.O.I.)
and submitted that this very clause was challenged and the Delhi High Court held that this clause is a valid one and does not suffer from any constitutional vice.
7.
Counsel for the petitioners tried to distinguish this order by submitting that in the cited case, the petitioner has served for more than 12-1/2 years but in the present case, the re-employed period of service of the petitioners was less than 10 years, therefore, the ratio given by the Hon'ble Judges of the Delhi High Court is not applicable to the facts of this case.
8.
After giving due thought to the matter, I am of the considered opinion that the artificial distinction which is sought to be made by the counsel for the petitioners is of no help to the petitioners.
This very clause was upheld by the Delhi High Court.
This court follows the decision of the Delhi High Court and the present writ petition is hereby dismissed, being devoid of any merit, with no order as to costs.
Petition dismissed.
