Jawahar Lal Gupta, J - The petitioner is working as a Superintendent with the Haryana State Electricity Board.
He approached this Court through the present petition in which it was inter alia prayed that the order dated September 28, 1993, a copy of which was produced as Annexure P-13, by which respondents 3 to 5 had been promoted to the rank of Under Secretary/Aministrative Officer (HQ) and he had been 'ignored' be quashed.
2.
The Respondent-Board filed a written statement.
They produced a summary of his record of service from April 1, 1981 to November 30, 1992 as Annexure P-2.
The case had come upon for hearing on April 7, 1995.
On that date, Mr. Kamal Sharma, learned Counsel for the Respondent-Board stated that "on a perusal of Annexure P/2 produced with the written statement, it cannot be said that the petitioner's record was poor and that he was not suitable for promotion to the post of Under Secretary."
He prayed for an adjournment of the case so as to enable the Board to review the matter.
Accordingly the case was adjourned.
The Board passed an order dated April 17, 1995.
The petitioner was again declared unsuitable for promotion.
He filed an affidavit in the form of replication so as to impugn the order dated April 17, 1995.
The respondents filed a reply to this affidavit.
Since the respondents had passed a fresh order during the pendency of the petition, the parties have confined their challenge to the order dated April, 1995 on the grounds pleaded in the additional affidavit and the reply thereto.
In this situation, the necessity of referring to the pleadings in the original writ petition is obviated.
A few facts as relevant for the decision of this controversy may be briefly noticed.
3.
The petitioner joined the service of the Board on October 13, 1960.
He was promoted as an Uppter Division Clerk, an Assistant, an Assistant Superintendent and a Deputy Superintendent.
On February 11, 1986, he was promoted as a Superintendent.
In June 1992, two posts in the rank of Under Secretary became available.
M/s UP Miglani and SK Sharma, respondents 3 and 4 were posted against these posts.
Thereafter, even respondents 5 and 6 were promoted.
Finally, vide order dated September 28, 1993, respondents 3 to 5 were appointed as Under Secretaries on regular basis with effect from the dates of the occurrence of the vacancies in the years 1992-93.
Another post had become available in the year 1993.
Respondent No. 7 was promoted against it vide order dated September 6, 1993.
The petitioner was over- looked initially on the ground that two departmental enquiries were pending against him.
Later on, it was observed that his record of service was poor and thus, he was not suitable for promotion.
Consequently, the petitioner filed the present petition in which a prayer for the quashing of the order promoting his juniors as also of the orders by which his representations against the adverse remarks had been rejected.
He further prayed that the respondents be directed to promote him with effect from the date his juniors were promoted.
It was during the hearing of this petition that the respondents passed the order dated April 17, 1995.
A copy of this order was furnished to the petitioner.
He filed an additional affidavit to challenge this order.
The respondents filed a reply thereto.
It is the validity of this order which falls for examination in this petition.
Broadly, the petitioner alleges that the request for re-consideration of the matter had been made by the counsel for the Board and the review was not carried out under the directions of this Court.
He alleges that the action of the respondents in ignoring him for promotion is arbitrary, illegal and malafide.
The respondents have controverted this.
What is the correct position ?

Besides the above, it has been pointed out that the petitioner was awarded the punishment of stoppage of one increment without cumulative effect and the period of suspension from August 27, 1990 to May 8, 1992 was treated as extra-ordinarily leave without pay vide orders dated June 8, 1993 in view of the charge sheet served on him vide letter dated November 30, 1990.
The charge against the petitioner was that he had failed to take the material on books and had misbehaved inasmuch as he had used unparliamentary language with the Chief Engineer/MM while working as Superintendent of the Stationery Cell.
The punishment was imposed vide order dated November 15, 1994.
The first occasion when persons junior to the petitioner were promoted, arose in June 1992.
The petitioner had been ignored for promotion on the ground that in view of the instructions issued by the Government and adopted by the Board, he "was not eligible for promotion on the post of Under Secretary in June 92 when his juniors were given promotion as he was facing charge sheets and Enquiry Officer had been appointed".
For the same reasons, the petitioner had been ignored for promotion in October 1992.
The next occasion was in February1993.
On this occasion, he has been ignored on account of not only the pendency of the charge sheet but also on the ground that he had "earned 100% good ACRs upto 31385 and 455% good ACRs from 1485 to 31.11.1992 and as such, he has not been found eligible for promotion".
From September 1993 to March 1995, the petitioner has been ignored on the ground that he had failed to earn 70% good reports for the period from April 1, 1985 onwards.
Are the respondents right ?
6.
A brief reference to the instructions may also be made.
The procedure to be followed in the case of selection of officers for promotion to higher posts was initially laid down in the instructions dated April 13, 1972.
It was inter alia provided "only such officials/officers who have obtained at least 50% 'Good' or better than average reports on the posts from which they are being considered for further promotion, and the over-all assessment can be classified as better than 'Average' should be placed in the slab of suitable officials/officers."
It was also provided that if in a particular case, the total number of reports earned on the posts from which the officers are considered for further promotion, are far less than 10 years, then the reports earned on the lower posts should also be considered so that reports for a period of 10 years are kept in view for determining the suitability.
However, in such a case, the greater weightage had to be given to the reports earned on the higher posts.
This policy was modified by the State Government vide letter dated May 9, 1985.
It was decided that "in future only such official/officer(s) who have obtained at least 70% or more reports of "good" or better categories during the last 10 years should be considered eligible for promotion to a higher post".
Copies of these instructions have been produced as Annexures E-16 and E-19 with the additional affidavit filed by the petitioner.
With the written statement filed by the respondents, a copy of the letter dated July 1, 1991 issued by the Chief Secretary to Government Haryana, has also been produced as Annexure R-3.
In view of the court decisions, the State Government had re-considered the matter and decided that "the guidelines dated 951985 should be implemented with immediate effect, according to which while taking into consideration the ACRs for 10 years in the case of promotion, the ACRs upto the year 1984-85 should be 50% good and thereafter these should be 70% good or even more".
All these instructions have been adopted by the Board.
7.
The other set of instructions to which a reference has been made, may also be noticed.
These are contained in the letter dated November 18, 1971 which relate to the "procedure to be followed in cases where the turn of an officer/official whose conduct is the subject of an enquiry comes for promotion to a higher post".
It has been inter alia provided that where on a preliminary investigation, a prima facie case has been established against a Government employee and a regular department enquiry under Rule 7 of the Punjab Civil Services (Punishment and Appeal) Rules, 1952 (which relates to the imposition of a major penalty), then, the employee " should not be promoted until the enquiry has been completed and he has exonerated".
It has been further provided that an employee against whom action is proposed to be taken under Rule 8 (which relates to the imposition of a minor penalty), "the matter should be considered in the light of the nature of allegations and the quantum of penalty that is proposed to be awarded.
The criterion should be whether or not if the allegations were to be proved and the prescribed penalty were to be imposed, then the promotion of the employee would still be justified on the basis of his entire record".
In other words, promotion should not be withheld only because disciplinary action under Rule 8 of the Punjab Civil Services (Punishment and Appeal) Rules, 1952 or any comparable rule is contemplated or is pending against a Government employee and instead in such cases the decision should be taken keeping in view the nature of allegations, the quantum of the penalty proposed and the overall record."
A copy of these instructions has been produced as E-22 with the additional affidavit.
These were adopted by the Board on March 8, 1974.
It is in the light of the above-said instructions that the validity of the impugned order passed by the respondents has to be examined.
8.
The question that arises for consideration is as to what is the true import of the instructions and is the action of the respondents in conformity therewith ?
9.
On a perusal of the instructions issued vide letter dated April 13, 1972 and thereafter, the following position emerges :-  (i) Upto May 8, 1985, officers who had obtained atleast 50% or better than average reports during the last 10 years were considered as suitable for promotion.
(ii) On May 9, 1985, it was decided that in future, only such officers who have obtained at least 70% or more reports of good or better categories during the last 10 years should be considered eligible for promotion to a higher post.
(iii) In view of the court decision, the Government had opined that "the guidelines dated 9.5.85 should be implemented with immediate effect according to which while taking into consideration the ACRs for 10 years in the case of promotion, the ACRs upto the year 1984-85 should be 50% good and thereafter these should be 70% good or even more".
In my view, the import of the above instructions was that if the claim for promotion to a higher post had to be considered prior to May 9, 1985, an officer with 50% good or better than average reports in the preceding 10 years could be considered suitable for promotion.
It the promotion has to be made after May 9, 1985, it is necessary that the officer should have 70% good or better reports during the preceding 10 years so as to be considered as suitable for promotion.
The instructions don't imply that record has to be divided into two water-tight compartments.
These do not permit that the reports upto the years 1984-85 and thereafter have to be considered separately so that an officer should have 50% good reports upto the year 84-85 and 70% good reports thereafter.
In other words, in June 1992, which is the relevant point of time in the present case, an officer with 70% good reports during the preceding 10 years can be considered suitable for promotion.
The action of the Board in dividing the reports into two separate periods i.e the first period ending with March 31, 1985 and the second period from April 1, 1985 onwards so as to deny the petitioner the benefit of the good reports earned by him in the preceding years, is not in consonance with the plain language of the instructions.
10.
Mr. Kamal Sharma, counsel for the respondent-Board submitted that such an interpretation is not in conformity with the instructions dated July 1, 1991 whereby it was decided that the guidelines dated 9.5.85 had to be implemented with immediate effect.
The counsel is not right in his submission.
The implication of the letter dated July 1, 1991 which was adopted by the Board in November 1992 was only that the instructions were prospective and not retrospective.
In other words, in case of promotion after May 9, 1985, a person with less than 70% good reports could not be considered as suitable for promotion.
11.
As for the instructions issued by the Government regarding consideration of claims during the pendency of the charge sheet vide letter dated November 18, 1971, it deserves mention that the implication of the instructions is only that a person against whom proceedings for imposition of a major penalty are pending, should not be promoted until the enquiry has been completed and he has been exonerated.
In case, the proceedings are for imposition of a minor penalty, the promotion can't be withheld merely on account of the pendency of the proceedings.
Decision has to be taken keeping in view the nature of the allegations and the quantum of the penalty proposed to be imposed as well as the record of service.
These instructions do not mean that a person becomes ineligible for promotion merely because proceedings for imposition of a major penalty had been initiated.
Even after the passing of the final order, the officer has a right to be considered for promotion in the light of the final order passed by the authority.
In other words, when only a minor penalty has been imposed, it can't be said the officer is ineligible for promotion.
His claim has to be considered on the basis of his record of service.
Stoppage of an increment without future effect cannot imply that even the punishment of withholding a promotion has been awarded.
12.
It is in the light of the above that the validity of the impugned action has to be examined.
The first occasion on which the petitioner's claim had to be considered for promotion was in June 1992.
The respondents have considered the reports for the period from April 1, 1980 to March 31, 1992.
On their own showing, the petitioner had earned 10 reports out of which 7 were good or above.
In fact, one of the reports was 'Outstanding' and two were 'Very Good'.
His integrity was never suspected.
Three reports earned by him were 'Average'.
He had, thus, earned at least 70% 'Good' reports.
However, the promotion was been denied to him on a two-fold basis viz. the first period of service has been taken as April 1, 1980 to March 31, 1985.
During this period, all the reports of the petitioner were Good or above.
The second period taken into consideration is April 1, 1985 to March 31, 1990.
Therein, out of five reports, two have been described as 'Good'/'Very Good' while three were 'Average'.
The percentage has been worked out as 40%.
Since the instructions dated July 1, 1991 had been adopted by the Board in November 1992 and it would not have been possible for the respondents to justify this action, the promotion has been denied to him on account of the pendency of the charge sheets.
As already noticed, the proceedings had been concluded in November 1994 when the punishment of stoppage of one increment without cumulative effect had been imposed.
On April 1, 1995 when the impugned order was passed, the petitioner's claim for promotion could not be rejected merely on account of the pendency of the charge sheets.
His claim had to be considered on the basis of the final decision.
That has been done.
Accordingly, the impugned order in so far as it was held that the petitioner "was not eligible for promotion to the post of Under Secretary in June 1992 when his juniors were given promotion as he was facing the charge sheets and Enquiry Officer had been appointed", cannot be sustained.
The petitioner had admittedly got 70% good reports.
On the basis of his record, he was not ineligible for promotion.
No penalty of withholding of promotion had been awarded to him.
Consequently, the petitioner was wrongly considered as ineligible.
Similarly, even in October 1992, the petitioner had been denied promotion on the same basis.
Even this action suffers from the same infirmity.
13.
The next occasion is February 1993.
The petitioner's claim has been rejected with the following observations :-  "During 2/93, his name was again considered when a vacancy arose against which Shri SR Sharma was posted in his own pay scale against the post of Under Secy./Admn.
Officer as Shri Rana was still facing charge- sheets/show cause notices.
He has earned 100% Good ACRs upto 3131985 and 455% Good ACRs from 1485 to 30.11.92 and as such he has not been found eligible for promotion."
14.
The claim has been rejected on a two-fold basis.
Firstly, that he was facing charge sheets and secondly that the percentage of good reports earned by him from April1, 1985 to November 30, 1992 was 455% viz. less than 70%.
This cannot be sustained.
Both the grounds are untenable in law.
The reports for the relevant period had to be considered cumulatively.
The petitioner had earned more than 70% good reports.
Similarly, the mere pendency of the charge sheets was not a relevant ground in the year 1995 when the impugned order was passed.
Even for the subsequent periods, the procedure adopted by the authorities suffers from the same infirmity.
15.
In view of the above, it is clear that the impugned order passed by the Board on April 17, 1995 is not in conformity with the instructions.
16.
In the writ petition as originally filed by the petitioner, a challenge had been made even against the orders by which his representations against the adverse remarks had been rejected.
It had also been pointed out that the order of stoppage of increment had been challenged by him by filing CWP No. 2439 of 1995.
Since the petitioner's claim is being accepted on the ground that the impugned order is not in conformity with the instructions issued by the Government, I am not going into the validity of the orders by which the petitioner's representations against the adverse remarks were rejected.
It would, thus, be open to the petitioner to agitate this matter separately in accordance with law.
17.
In view of the above, the writ petition is allowed and it is held that the petitioner was wrongly ignored for promotion.
The respondents are, accordingly, directed to consider his claim for promotion with effect from the date/s persons junior to the petitioner were promoted.
The petitioner shall be entitled to the consequential reliefs that may become due to him in the nature of re-fixation of pay and arrears of salary.
The petitioner has argued the case in person.
Still, he will be entitled to his costs which are quantified as Rs. 3000/-.
Petition allowed.
