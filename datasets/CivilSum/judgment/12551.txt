RS Mongia, J - In this writ petition the petitioner has challenged two orders namely :-  (i) order dated 971993 (annexure P-14) passed by the Registrar Co-operative Societies Haryana, (in short RCS') by which it had been ordered that the petitioner cannot be granted relaxation in the qualifications for the post of Cane Manager; and  (ii) Order dated 5101993 (annexure P-15) of the Board of Directors Sonepat, Co-operative Sugar Mills Ltd Sonepat, a Co-operative Society registered under the Co-operative Societies Act (hereinafter called 'Sugar Mills') by which the petitioner had been reverted from the post of Cane Manager to the post of Cane Marketing Officer.
An interim prayer was also made in the writ petition for staying the operation of the order of the reversion.
2.
The Motion Bench while admitting the writ petition on 12111993 ordered that the stay matter be put up before a Single Judge on 16111993.
On November 16, 1993 when the stay matter came up for hearing, written statement was filed on behalf of the Sugar Mills in the Court and the case was adjourned to 26111993 for arguments on stay matter.
Petitioner's reversion was ordered to remain stayed till further orders.
After some adjournments, the stay matter came up for hearing on 22121993.
3.
All the parties in the case suggested that instead of deciding the stay matter the main case in self be disposed of.
Consequently I heard the parties in the main case.
4.
Facts necessary for the disposal of the writ petition may be noticed.
Petitioner is B.Sc.
with Botany Zoology and Chemistry.
He also obtained degree of LL.B.
He joined the Sugar Mills as a Labour Officer on 9111976 which post later on was designated as Labour Welfare Officer.
His services were terminated on January 3, 1979.
The petitioner raised an industrial dispute and the Labour Court gave an award in his favour on 491984, ordering his reinstatement.
The Management of Sugar Mills challenged the award of the Labour Court by way of a writ petition in this Court.
However, the same was ultimately dismissed.
After the Labour Court award, the petitioner who was working as Labour Welfare Officer, was ordered to look after the work of Cane Marketing Officer and his work as Labour Welfare Officer was to be looked after by some body else.
5.
On 4111991, the Management of the Sugar Mills promoted the petitioner to the post of Cane Manager and was initially put on probation for a period of one year.
Since, according to the Management, the petitioner did not have the requisite qualifications for the post of Cane Manager, the case was moved to the RCS for relaxation of the qualifications.
On 7.11.1992 the probation period of the petitioner was extended by six months.
It is mentioned in the order (Copy Annexure P-10) that the probation period was being extended as the decision regarding the relaxation of qualifications by the RCS was still awaited.
Till 551993, RCS had not conveyed his decision regarding relaxation of the qualifications and the Management of the Sugar Mills, vide order dated 551993 (Annexure P-11) further extended the period of probation by another six months.
6.
On 2371993 a letter was addressed by the Commissioner and Secretary to Government of Haryana, Co-operative Department to the RCS (Copy Annexure P-12) which was in reference to a letter dated 15.1.1993 of the RCS mentioning therein that the Government had considered the matter and found that the promotion of the petitioner as Cane Manager was not in accordance with the Rules and therefore, he may be reverted to the lower post.
The petitioner challenged the above order of the Government by filing C.W.P.
No. 8922 of 1993 which was admitted and operation of the impugned order was stayed.
During the pendency of the writ petition, the Government withdrew the impugned order dated 2371993, thereby rendering the writ petition infructuous.
7.
Before the writ petition was disposed of on November 9, 1993, order dated 971993 by the RCS (Annexure P-14) to which reference has already been made above, declining to grant relaxation regarding qualification in favour of the petitioner, had already been passed.
The petitioner had submitted before the Bench to allow him to amend the writ petition to enable him to challenge the order dated 971993 of the RCS.
However, the Bench while refusing to allow amendment observed that the petitioner would be at liberty to challenge the said order in accordance with law and as far as the apprehension of the petitioner that he would be reverted immediately after the disposal of the writ petition it was ordered that the petitioner would not be reverted for one week to enable him to seek appropriate relief.
Though resolution/order regarding reversion of the petitioner had been passed on 5101993 (Annexure P/15) during the pendency of the earlier writ petition yet the same was not communicated and given effect to till the present writ petition was filed on 10111993 challenging the two orders Annexures P-14 and P-15 referred to above.
8.
Under the Punjab Co-operative Societies Act, 1961 Rules were framed known as Punjab Co-operative Societies Rules, 1963, Rule 28 of 1963 Rules provides for as to how qualifications and conditions of service of employees of the Co-operative Societies are to be laid down.
The said rule reads as under :-  "28.
Qualifications and conditions of service of the employees.
(1) The qualifications and conditions of service subject to which any person may be employed by a co-operative society or a class of co- operative societies shall be such as may be determined by the Registrar from time to time.
(2) Where the Registrar is of the opinion that it is necessary or expedient so to do, he may by order for reasons to be recorded in writing relax the provisions of this Rule with respect to any co-operative society or class of co-operative Societies to such an extent as he may consider proper."
9.
The 1961 Act, so far as the State of Haryana is concerned, was repealed by Haryana Co-operative Societies Act, 1984 and 1963 Rules framed under the 1961 Act were repealed by the Haryana Co-operative Societies Rules, 1989, framed under the 1984 Act.
Vide letter dated 2961987 the RCS in exercise of the powers under Rule 28 of the 1963 Rules (supra) approved the qualifications of the staff working in the Co-operative Sugar Mills.
The qualifications for the post of Cane Manager which were approved were B.Sc.
(Agriculture) with 10 years' experience in cane development and cane marketing in supervisory post.
On 481988, the qualifications which were again prescribed by the RCS for the post of Cane Manager, Junior Scale (with which we are concerned) were B.Sc.
(Agriculture) with 10 years experience as Deputy Cane Manager/Cane Development Officer and Cane Marketing Officer in Sugar Mills.
10.
Rule 29 of 1989 Rules is in the following terms :-  "Employees of Co-operative Societies subject to the provisions of Section 37 of the Act, every society shall make rules to regulate the recruitment and conditions of service of its employees with the approval of the Registrar."
11.
On 2741992, the Sugar Mills passed a resolution to adopt the service rules for the employees of the Sugar Mills duly approved by the RCS.
It may be observed here that new rules had been approved by the RCS under Rule 29 of 1989 Rules ibid.
According to these Rules, the Cane Manager was to have the following qualifications :-  "B.Sc.
Agriculture with 5 years experience as Cane Manager B.Sc.
Agriculture with 7 years experience as Cane Development Officer/Cane Marketing Officer/Deputy Cane Manager in a Sugar Mill."
12.
Having noticed the detailed facts of the case as above, the stage is set to notice the points raised by the learned counsel for the petitioner to impugn the two orders dated 971993 and 5101993, Annexure P-14 and P-15 respectively.
1.
No relaxation of qualifications by the RCS was required because when the petitioner was promoted as Cane Manager on 4111991 no statutory qualifications for the post had been prescribed.
2.
The Rules which were adopted by the Sugar Mills on 2741992 cannot be made applicable to any appointment/promotion prior to the adoption of the Rules.
In other words, the qualifications laid down by the Rules could only be made applicable to the employees appointed after 2741992.
3.
The RCS did not lay down any qualifications for the post vide communication dated 2961987 (Annexure R/3/2 to the written statement) nor by the later communication dated 481988 (Annexure P-8) and in case any such qualifications are held to be laid down, the same cannot be enforced as there was no power with the RCS to lay down any qualifications.
4.
The resolution/order dated 5101993 reverting the petitioner is based on the non-relaxation of the qualifications by the RCS and the work during probation having not been found satisfactory.
In fact the matter regarding alleged unsatisfactory work was never considered in the meeting of the Board of Directors of the Sugar Mills and therefore the order of reversion is vitiated.
13.
Points No. 1 to 3  As far as points 1, 2 and 3 are concerned, suffice it to say that under Rule 28 of 1963 Rules, the RCS was competent to lay down the qualifications and conditions of service of employees to be employed by the Co-operative Societies.
It was under these powers that RCS had laid down qualifications in the years 1987/1988 to which reference has already been made above, I do not agree with the submission of the learned counsel for the petitioner that the 1963 Rules which were made under the powers derived from the 1961 Act stood repealed ipso facto on the repeal of the 1961 Act by 1984 Act.
According to Section 132 of the 1989 Act, only 1961 Act was repealed and not the 1963 rules.
The 1963 Rules were repealed by the 1989 Rules.
Rule 113 of the 1989 Rules is very clear in this regard which may be reproduced here :-  "113.
Repeal - The Punjab Co-operative Society Rules, 1963 are hereby repealed : Provided that any action taken, order issued by laws made under the provisions of the rules hereby repealed shall in so far as it is not inconsistent with the provisions of these rules, be deemed to have been taken, issued or made under the provisions of these rules."
It would be seen from Rule 113 (supra) that 1989 Rules repealed 1963 Rules and any action taken, order issued, bye laws etc. made under the old Rules i.e. 1963 Rules were specifically saved.
So when the petitioner was promoted the qualifications which were statutorily laid down in the year 1987/1988 under Rule 28 of the 1963 Rules were applicable in the case of the petitioner.
I agree with the learned counsel for the petitioner that the qualifications which were laid down on 2741992 under Rule 29 of the 1989 Rules, would not be applicable to the case of the petitioner as the petitioner's promotion was prior to the adoption of the Rules on 2741992.
The qualifications which were laid down w.e.f.
2741992 were less in rigor than the qualifications laid down in 1987/1988.
According to the qualifications laid down in 1992, the experience required for the post was seven years; whereas according to the earlier qualifications experience required for the post was ten years.
It is not disputed that the petitioner did not have the requisite experience and the academic qualifications for the post when he was promoted and consequently he could only be allowed to continue on the post if the RCS had relaxed the qualifications under the Rules.
14.
Point No. 4  So far as point No. 4 is concerned, suffice it to observe that the petitioner not only lacked the academic qualifications for the post i.e. B.Sc.
(Agriculture) he also lacked experience.
From the perusal of the original record produced by the respondents, I find that while Sugar Mills asked for the relaxation of the qualifications to the RCS, reference had been made to the Rules adopted on 2741992, which as observed in the earlier part of the judgment, were less in rigor as only seven years experience for the post was required; whereas according to the qualifications laid down in 1987/1988, ten years experience was required.
The RCS did not think the case to be appropriate one to relax both academic qualifications and experience, though as per the case put to him, the petitioner was only short by about six months in experience.
In fact according to the qualifications laid down in 1987/1988, the petitioner was short by three and a half years in experience at the time of his promotion.
It cannot be said that there is any arbitrariness on the part of the RCS as it is open to the competent authority to see whether in a given case academic qualifications and experience which are required for a particular type of post should or should not be relaxed.
It cannot be doubted that while the matter was before the RCS, he knew the fact that when the case for relaxation was being rejected, the petitioner had already gained some experience on the higher post.
As observed above here was a case before the RCS where the incumbent lacked even the academic qualifications.
So far as the instances given by the petitioner that in some other cases the RCS had relaxed the qualifications suffice it to say that two cases cannot be parallel as far as relaxation is concerned.
The instances are of different posts and as far as one instance of the same post as held by the petitioner is concerned, it may be stated that in that case the incumbent only lacked some experience and not academic qualifications.
It cannot, therefore be said that the decision of the RCS declining relaxation in qualifications was in any way arbitrary or discriminatory.
Point No. 5  15.
So far as 5th point is concerned, it may be observed that in the agenda note that was circulated the performance of work of the petitioner being not good during the period of probation was also mentioned.
The petitioner has placed on record some affidavits of the elected Directors to show that while passing the resolution dated 5101993 (Annexure P-15), the performance of work was not discussed.
Suffice it to say that the facts regarding performance of work of the petitioner were in the agenda note regarding the probation of the petitioner.
The respondent-Sugar Mills has denied the assertion that there was no discussion regarding the performance of work of the petitioner.
Be that as it may, the petitioner could not have continued on the promoted post as the RCS had declined to grant relaxation in the qualifications.
Petitioners being ineligible could not have continued on the post.
The counsel for the respondent also produced before me the record regarding the petitioner's performance on the promoted post to highlight that the adverse comments regarding his work were being brought to the notice of the petitioner from time to time.
For the foregoing reasons, I find no merit in this petition which is hereby dismissed with no order as to costs.
