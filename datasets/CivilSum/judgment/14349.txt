VK Bali, J (Oral) - Amrik Singh and Hem Raj, Assistant Linemen employed with Punjab State Electricity Board, through present petition filed by them, under Article 226 of the Constitution of India, seek issuance of writ in the nature of mandamus directing the respondents to pay them pay scale of Rs. 90- 140 instead of Rs. 87/- per month.
2.
In support of the claim, referred to above, it has, inter-alia, been pleaded that petitioner No. 1 initially joined the respondent Board as Workcharge T.Mate on 21.12.1976 and he was then promoted provisionally to the post of Assistant Lineman on 15.9.1979.
He was regularised in service w.e.f.
1.12.1979 as ALM.
However, while regularising his services, it was mentioned that this pay would be fixed at Rs. 87/- per month instead of regular pay scale of Rs. 90-140, which was admissible to the post on which he was regularised.
The position with regard to regularisation of services of petitioner No. 2 is also same.
3.
Learned counsel for the petitioners vehemently contends that once the pay scale admissible to the post of Assistant Lineman, on which post the petitioners are working, is Rs. 90-140, there is no question for the respondents to keep them in the fixed pay of Rs. 87/- per month.
4.
Pursuant to notice issued by this court, respondents have entered defence and filing written statement, contested the claim of petitioners.
5.
Mr. Kumar, learned counsel for the respondents contends that pay scale of the petitioners was fixed vide order, Annexure R4/1 in 1979 and the condition as mentioned in the order, referred to above, cannot be challenged at this stage inasmuch as even a civil suit filed for the relief aforesaid, would be barred by time.
For his aforesaid contention, learned counsel relies upon two judgments of the Supreme Court in State of Madhya Pradesh and Anr. v. Bhai Lal Bhai and others, A.I.R. 1964 SC 1006 and M/s Tilokchand Motichand and others v. HB Munshi and another, A.I.R. 1970 SC 898.
He then contends that it is permissible for an employer to fix the pay scale of a temporary/ad hoc employee lower than the one admissible to regular employees.
For this contention, he relies upon a Full Bench judgment of this Court in Ranbir Singh v. State of Haryana and others, 1998(2) RSJ 535 : 1998(2) SCT 189 (P&H)(FB).
6.
In view of the objections that have been raised by learned counsel for the respondents, learned counsel for the petitioners states that he will limit the relief to be granted to the petitioners to three years from the date this writ was filed.
In view of the statement made by learned counsel for the petitioners, technical objections raised by Mr. Kumar, learned counsel for the respondents pale into complete insignificance.
As mentioned above, it could not be disputed that the pay scale admissible to the post, on which the petitioners are working is 90-140.
That being so, we partly allow this petition and direct the respondents to fix the petitioners in the pay scale of Rs. 90-140 w.e.f.
1.12.1979, the date on which their services were regularised.
The arrears shall, however, be limited to three years from the date this petition was filed.
7.
Parties are, however, left to bear their own costs.
Petition partly allowed.
