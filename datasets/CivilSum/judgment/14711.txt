RS Mongia, J (Oral) - This judgment of mine will dispose of CWP Nos. 5890 of 1991, 606 and 1387 of 1990 and 5889 of 1991, as common questions of law and facts are involve.
2.
The petitioners in these writ petitions had got admission in the Diploma in Homoeopathic Medicine and Surgery course (in short DHMS), in the year 1987 or thereafter.
The course is of 3-1/2 years' duration.
First year and 2nd year are of one year duration each and 3rd year is of 1-1/2 years duration.
The Examining Body of the students of the DHMS is the Council of Homoepathic System of Medicine, Punjab (hereinafter called the Council).
It is agreed on all hands that the examinations etc. held by the Council are governed by the Regulations framed by the Central Council of Homeopathy.
These Regulations are known as Homeopathy (Diploma Course) Regulations, 1983 (hereinafter called the Regulations).
Regulations concerning the Ist DHMS examination, 2nd year DHMS examination and 3rd year DHMS examination are Regulations 8, 9, 10 and 11.

3.
Broadly the case of the petitioners in these writ petitions is that since under the Regulations, a candidate is given in all four chances to clear all the subjects of a particular examination, the candidate is ipso facto entitled to the admission to the next higher class irrespective of the fact whether he has cleared all the papers of the lower class.
On the other hand, the case of the Council is that there is no provision in the Regulations which permits admission to the higher case unless a candidate has cleared all the subjects of the lower class.
According to the Council, the admission, which is granted by the Principal of the College to a candidate to continue the studies in the next higher class irrespective of the fact whether the said candidate has cleared all the papers of the lower class, is against the Regulations.
The Council had not permitted the petitioners to appear in the examination of the higher class because of the reasons that they had not cleared the papers of the lower class and they were wrongly allowed by the Principal to continue studies and complete the course of the higher class.
4.
The learned counsel for the petitioners argued that in the Regulations there is no bar that a candidate cannot be allowed to continue studies in the higher class till he has passed all the papers of the lower class.
The argument proceeded that if four chances are given to a candidate to clear all the papers of a particular class, then this concession would become wholly nugatory and illusory if that candidate is not allowed to continue his studies in the next higher class and also appear in the papers of the next higher class along with the papers of the lower class in which he had failed, inasmuch as he would be waiting one year or two years depending upon as to in which chance he clears all the papers of the lower class.
In support of his submissions, learned counsel for the petitioners relied upon a Division Bench judgment of this Court in Jaininder Mohan and others v. The Council of Homoeopathic System of Medicine, Punjab and another, 1991(3) SCT 95 (P&H) (C.W.P.
No. 481 of 1990) decided on 27th March, 1991.
The learned counsel particularly relied upon the following observations of the Division Bench :-  Mr. VK Jindal counsel for the Punjab Council on his part, sought to contend that in the case of all the petitioners, whether admitted before or after August 1986, the condition in Regulations 9 and 10 of the central Regulations, regarding the attendance of courses in the subjects for the examination for a period of not less than one year or 1-1/2 years, respectively, must be deemed to commence from the date on which all the subjects of the previous examination were passed, meaning thereby that a candidate, who in, the annual examination, is required to re-appear in one or more subjects, even if he clears these papers in the supplementary examination, he would not be eligible to appear for the next higher class annual examination, as his period of study from the date of the supplementary examination to the annual examination would be less than one year 1-1/2 years as the case may, the stress in this behalf being upon the absence of any specific regulation in the central Regulations to the effect that upon passing the re-appear papers in the supplementary examination, the result would relate back to the date of the annual examination.
This is indeed a contention that cannot be sustained.
There is no doubt no specific provision in the central Regulations, which in so many words says so, but at the same time there is no such bar provided there either.
A reasonable and harmonious construction of clauses (iv) to (vii) of Regulations-11 of the central Regulations clearly implies the reading into them of such a provision.
Anamolous results would in fact arise if they were not to be so construed, as it would mean that if a candidate has to re-appear in one or more subjects, he would, on this account alone, lose a year as even if he were to pass in these subjects in the supplementary examination, the period left for study for the next higher class examination would be less than a year and he would thus inevitably have to study for the next year's course from the next academic session.
Not only this, it would also mean that he would have to sit idle from the date of passing of the supplementary examination till the commencement of the next session.
Such an intention cannot be imputed to the framers of the Regulations.
There is Judicial precedent too in the passing of the supplementary examination relating back to the annual examination.
In a somewhat similar situation, where the same central Regulations came up for consideration, DV Sehgal, J in C.W.P.
No. 7811 of 1987 (Sanjeev Kumar Nayyar v. Council of Homoeopathic System of Medicines) held, that on the passing of the re-appear subjects in the supplementary examination, the result would relate back to the date of the annual examination.
The same view, though with regard to other Regulations, was expressed by this Court in Harinder Kaur Chandok v. The Punjab School Education Board, A.I.R. 1988 Pb.
& Hy.
244, and by the Division Bench in the Punjab School Education Board v. Harinder Kaur, 1990(2) RSJ 217.
5.
From the above quotation from the judgment of the Division Bench, two things emerge :-  (i) That by harmonious construction of clauses (iv) to (vii) of Regulation 11, quoted above, a candidate who has failed in an examination, may be allowed to continue studies in the higher class till the candidate appears in the Supplementary examination to clear the papers of the lower class; and (ii) That a candidate may pass a particular examination in the first, second, third or fourth chance but for the purposes of seeing as to when the candidate would be deemed to have passed that examination, it would be taken as if the candidate had passed the examination when the first annual examination of that class had been taken by the candidate.
On the basis of the observations of the Division Bench quoted above the learned counsel for the petitioner submitted that the chances which are taken by the candidate to clear the papers after first chance, are all supplementary examinations or supplementary chances, and, therefore, if more than one chances are given, then the candidate is to be allowed to join the higher class and continue studies irrespective of the fact whether the has cleared the papers of the lower class or not.
As far as the observations of the Division Bench are concerned, no doubt on first glance these seem to support the contention of the learned counsel for the petitioners.
However, on careful reading of the observations it becomes apparent that these were confined only to the first supplementary examination after the annual examination.
For the sake of illustration, the observations of the Division Bench are that supposing a candidate had failed in three papers of 1st year in the annual examination, then he is to be allowed to join the second year and if he clears the papers of the first year in the supplementary examination, he would be allowed to continue his studies in the second year.
This was because if he was not allowed to join the higher class and the candidate passes the papers of the lower class in the supplementary examination, yet he would waste one year since he was not allowed to join the second year after his failure in some papers or even in all papers of the first year.
However, the said observations, cannot be extended further that even if the candidate does not clear the papers of the lower class in the first supplementary examination, he is still to be allowed to continue his studies in the next higher class.
Of course, he would get in all four chances to clear the papers of a particular class and by virtue of this concession, the candidate would have to join the same class again unless he fails to clear all or some of the papers of a particular class in four chances.
Accordingly, petitioners could not have been allowed to continue in the higher class after they had failed to clear all the papers of the lower class in the first supplementary examination.
6.
Having held that the petitioners were not entitled to continue studies in the higher class after having failed to clear all the papers of the lower class in the first supplementary examination, what should be their fate in these writ petitions.
The fact remains that the Principal of the College had allowed them to continue their studies in the higher class in spite of the fact that they had not cleared all the papers of the lower class in the first supplementary examination and by virtue of interim orders of this Court, they have been allowed to appear in the examination of the higher class along with the papers of the lower class, in which they had failed, and some of them are continuing studies in the higher class.
I am of the view that in equity petitioners should not suffer as all of them are either attending classes of 3rd year or have appeared in the 3rd year examination but their results have not been declared.
7.
In this view of the matter, while dismissing the writ petitions, the respondent-Council is directed not to raise any objection regarding the petitioners and declare their results of the examinations which they have taken.
This will, however, be not cited as a precedent because the directions are being given on peculiar facts and circumstances of these cases.
There will be no order as to costs.
8.
Before parting with the judgment, I may observes in C.W.P.
No. 606 of 1990 that the case of the petitioners regarding re-checking of some answer-books is pending with the Council.
The learned counsel for the respondent-Council states that the matter could not be finalised because of the pendency of the writ petition.
The Council will take expeditious steps now to finalise the matter regarding re-checking of the papers concerning the petitioners in this writ petition.
Petitions dismissed.
