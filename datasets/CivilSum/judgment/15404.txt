Swatanter Kumar, J - Petitioner approached this Court under Articles 226 and 227 praying that show cause notice dated 651999 Annexure P-8 to the writ petition be quashed and the respondents be directed to release all the retiral benefits payable to the petitioner alongwith interest at the rate of 15% per annum from the date they were due till payment.
2.
The facts are hardly in controversy in the present case.
The petitioner was admittedly an employee of the respondents and had retired from service on 28th February, 1988.
During the pendency of the petition, not only dues of the petitioner including pension, gratuity, computation and leave encashment etc. were paid but also vide order dated 3132002 the show cause notice dated 651999 was dropped and no order of recovery was passed against the petitioner for which show cause notice have been issued.
3.
In view of the above, very limited controversy falls for consideration of the Court in this case is whether the petitioner is entitled to any interest on the delayed payments and if so, at what rate and from which date.
4.
According to the petitioner, he had retired on 28th February, 1998 and no payments were made to him till September 1999.
Show cause notice was issued on 6th May, 1999 to which the petitioner filed reply within 15 days.
No further enquiry or investigation were held by the respondents but ultimately the proceedings were unconditionally dropped vide order dated 3132002.
Thus, according to the petitioner, there is delay in disbursement of retiral benefits.
The delay is unreasonable and for no valid reason whatsoever.

5.
On the other hand, learned counsel appearing for the respondents contended that there was a vigilance report on the basis of which show cause notice was issued to the petitioner which was ultimately dropped on 31st March 2002 while all the dues were cleared to the petitioner by May 2002 and as such there was no delay and in any case there is no negligence on the part of the respondents in disbursement of the retiral benefits to the petitioner.
6.
Having heard the learned counsel for the parties at some length, we are of the considered view that some delay is certainly attributable to the respondents and there is inaction of their part.
The matter could be dealt with more expeditiously.
The petitioner had retired on February 28, 1998 while the payment, which were even undisputed, were disbursed to the petitioner in September 1999.
The proceedings were initiated by service with a show cause notice for the recovery against the petitioner in May 1999 but the final order was passed only in the year 2202.
There is nothing before us to indicate as to what the respondents were doing for a long period of three years particularly, when the proceedings culminated into dropping of the show cause notice.
No fault was found with the petitioner and as such it would be difficult to deny the petitioner the relief of interest.
However, on the question of percentage we would certainly take into consideration the fact that the mater was pending before the department and the department passed appropriate order though with some delay.
As such patent negligence is not attributable to the action of the respondents.
The petitioner thus would be entitled to receive interest at the rate of 9% per annum for the period commencing May 1998 i.e. nearly three months after the date of retirement to 9th May 1999 when the show cause notice was served upon the petitioner.
For the intervening period, as some proceedings were pending against the petitioner, the petitioner is granted interest at the same rate of interest.
No costs.
Petition allowed.
