RL Anand, J - This is a defendant's appeal and it has been directed against the judgment and decree dated 25.9.1999, passed by the Court of Additional District Judge, Faridabad who affirmed the judgment and decree of the trial Court dated 15121997 vide which the suit of the plaintiff Shri Surender Kumar Arora, for declaration was decreed and a declaration was granted in his favour that he is entitled to the benefit of ad hoc service starting from 25.8.1981 to 7121987.
2.
The suit was decreed by the first appellate Court for the reasons given in para Nos. 13 and 14 of its judgment which are reproduced as under :-  13.
"With the aforesaid proposition of law laid down in Chambel Singh's case (supra), there is no dispute whatsoever but the fact remains that the appellant had been appointed against an existing vacancy and there was no break in his service and finally he was given regular appointment on 7121987 vide letter of appointment Ex.P-2.
That being the position, the period 25.8.81 to 7121987 has to be counted for fixation of seniority.
Exactly, under similar circumstances, a co-employee of the respondent namely, Shyam Sunder Singla instituted a suit against the appellant which was decreed by the trial Court on 331997 and the said judgment dated 331997 was assailed in appeal by the appellant and the said appeal was then dismissed by Shri.
KC Gupta, District Judge, Faridabad in Civil Appeal No. 44 of 1997 per judgment dated 10111997, a copy of which is placed on the trial Court file.
Therein also the terms of appointment letter and subsequent regularisation of the appointee were identical.
The said decision of the learned District Judge was further upheld in Regular Second Appeal No. 683 of 1998 decided on 2631998 by the High Court of Punjab and Haryana.
Counsel for the respondent showed me a copy of the said judgment dated 2631998 rendered by the High Court in RSA No. 683 of 1998.
That being the position, there appears to be no reason for giving a different treatment to the respondent in the present case.
14.
Respondent's counsel has also placed reliance upon a Supreme Court judgment reported as Direct Recruit Class-II Engineering Officers' Association and other v. State of Maharashtra and others, AIR 1990 SC 1607 wherein it was held that even if the initial appointment is not made according to rules, but if the appointee continues in service uninterrupted until regularisation of service, then the period of his officiating service has to be counted towards seniority.
Respondent in this case had addressed a letter dated 15.2.1986 to the appellant vide Ex.P-7 requesting the latter to regularise his service with effect from 25.8.1981 when he was appointed on ad hoc basis.
The appellant sent reply thereto on 1431986 vide Ex. P-8 stating therein that as and when his name was received from employment exchange, then the matter of his regular appointment was to be considered.
That being the position, period of the respondent's seniority has to be counted from the date of his initial appointment on ad hoc basis and gradation list in which the period of his ad hoc service from 25.8.1981 to 7121987 has been ignored, has rightly been quashed by the trial Court which adjudicating issues No. 1 to 3.
I, therefore, confirm the said findings of the trial Court."
Learned counsel for the appellant submitted that the plaintiff has not led any evidence to show that his appointment was within the Rules and as such he was not entitled to the benefit of ad hoc service in view of the judgment reported as Chambel Singh v. State of Haryana, 1995(1) PLR 153 : 1994(4) S.C.T.
800.
He submitted that the appointment of the plaintiff-respondent was purely ad hoc arrangement and in these circumstances, the suit of the plaintiff could not be decreed by the Courts below.
3.
I do not subscribe to the argument raised by the learned counsel for the appellant.
The Courts below after appraising the evidence came to the conclusion that the appointment of the plaintiff was against the existing vacancy and there was no break in his service.
Finally he was given regular appointment on 7121987.
In theses circumstances, it cannot be said that the appointment of the plaintiff from 25.8.1981 to 7121987 was only ad hoc.
Rather, it was according to the Rules.
The plaintiff was earlier put on ad hoc basis but against the existing vacancy and in these circumstances, he was entitled to the benefit of service for which he remained as ad hoc.
No merit.
Dismissed.
Appeal dismissed.
