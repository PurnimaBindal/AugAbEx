RS Mongia, J - All the four petitioners are the members of the Punjab Superior Judicial Service.
The petitioners prior to 23121977, 1431977, 191977 and 191977, respectively, were working as Senior Sub-Judges in the State of Punjab against permanent substantive posts of the Selection Grade Sub Judges, which was Rs. 1300-1500.
Their substantive pay in the said scales was Rs. 1450/- per month before they were promoted to the Punjab Superior Judicial Service on the dates mentioned above.
The next date of increment for each of the petitioner was 111978, 1111977, 1.12.1977 and 1111977 respectively.
2.
The petitioners, as observed above, were promoted to officiate as Additional District and Sessions Judges against temporary posts on the respective dates mentioned in the paragraph above.
On promotion, their basic pay became Rs. 1600/- on the dates of their annual increments mentioned above, in the pay-scale of Rs. 1200-2000 admissible to the District & Sessions Judges.
Thus, on 111978 when the Punjab Civil Services (Revised Scales of Pay), Rules, 1979 (hereinafter called the Revised Scales of Pay), came into force, the existing emoluments of the petitioners in their officiating capacity as Additional District and Sessions Judges in the pay scales of Rs. 1200-2000 was fixed at Rs. 1660/-(basic pay, plus Rs. 363/- DA i.e. in all Rs. 2023/- per month.
3.
From the facts mentioned above, it is evident that the petitioners on 111976, were in service in dual capacity.
Firstly, they are holding posts of Selection Grade Sub-Judges in substantive capacity holding lien against those posts and secondly, they were working in officiating capacity, as Additional District and Sessions Judges.
4.
The Revised Pay Rules upgraded the pay scales of all Punjab Government employees, including the petitioners.
The Selection Grade of Rs. 1300-1500 of the Subordinate Judges was first replaced by a scale of Rs. 1775-2000 and subsequently in 1983 by a scale of Rs. 2000-2400 with effect from 111978.
The time scale of the District and Sessions Judge (including Additional District & Sessions Judge) was revised from Rs. 1200-2000 to Rs. 1580-2400.
5.
Respondent No. 2 fixed the pay of the petitioners under the Revised Pay Rules on the basis of their existing emoluments of Rs. 2023/- in the officiating capacity.
Their pay was fixed (in the revised pay scale of Rs. 1580-2400) at Rs. 2000 + Rs. 98 personal pay.
This pay was fixed according to the respondents under Rule 6 read with second proviso of the Revised Pay Rules.
It is this fixation of pay that has been challenged by the petitioners by way of the present writ petition.
6.
The primary grievance of the petitioners is that the pay of several officers junior to them, who were drawing pay at lesser stages than theirs' before the enforcement of the Revised Pay Rules had been fixed at higher stages as compared to them and that they had not been given the benefit of sub-rule (2) of Rule 6 of the Revised Pay Rules, as also Rule 414 (1) and (2) read with Rule 4.4(a)(i) of the Punjab Civil Services Rules, Volume I, Part 1.
It was also contended that the pay fixed by the respondent was even otherwise not according to the Rules, firstly, because the Revised Pay Rules forbid fixing the pay at a stage lower than the existing emoluments and secondly, because the second proviso to sub-rule (1) of Rule 6 which scales down the initial pay fixed under sub-rule (1) read with first proviso is not applicable because the stage of pay it contemplates, does not factually exist in case of the petitioners.
7.
Rule 3(c) of the Revised Pay Rules defines existing scale; whereas Rule 3(d) defines existing emoluments.
Rule 6 talks of fixation of pay in the revised scale; whereas Rule 7 contemplates the date of increment in the revised scale.
For ready reference, the above noted Rules are reproduced :-  "3.
Definitions :- In these rules, unless there is anything repugnant in the subject or context.
(a) ---------------------------- (b) --------------------------- (c) "existing scale" means the scale of pay of a Government employee in respect of the post held by him in substantive or officiating capacity on the appointed day; (d) "existing emoluments" means aggregate of (i) basic pay of a Government employee in the existing scale on the appointed day; (ii) Dearness Allowance, Dearness pay, Additional Relief, Additional Dearness Allowance sanctioned upto 12 monthly average Consumer Price Index-320 (1960 = 100) i.e. upto and including the instalment of Additional Dearness Allowance sanctioned with effect from the first day of January, 1978; (iii) ad hoc pay, if any, where such ad hoc pay is abolished with effect from the appointed day; and (iv) special pay or part of special pay, if any which was granted in lieu of a higher time scale and has been abolished as a separate component in the revised scale with effect from the appointed day.
Note :- Normal increment due and granted to any employee with effect from the first day of January, 1978 shall also be treated as part of existing emoluments for the purpose of these rules.
6.
Fixation of pay in the revised scales :- (1) The initial pay of a Government employee in the revised scale shall, unless in any case it is otherwise directed, be fixed separately in respect of his substantive pay in the permanent post on which he holds a lien or would have held a lien had it not been suspended and in respect of his pay in the officiating post or posts, as the case may be held by him in the following manner :-  (i) Where a single existing scale has been replaced by a single revised scale, the pay in the revised scale in which a Government employee is placed shall be fixed at the stage next above the existing emoluments on the appointed day.
In case the benefit so accruing is less than the amount equal to the rate of increment at the relevant stage in the revised scale, the employee may be granted another increment in the revised scale of pay : Provided that, in all cases where the benefit still falls short of twenty rupees per month the employee be given further increment(s) in the revised scale so as to give him a minimum benefit of twenty rupees per month :  Provided further that where the revised pay fixed at such stage exceeds the existing emoluments by more than seventy-five rupees the pay shall be fixed at the scale which the revised pay, so fixed, does not exceed the existing emoluments by more than seventy-five rupees and the difference between the existing emoluments plus seventy-five rupees and the revised pay shall be allowed as personal pay to be absorbed in the pay at the time of next increment ;  (ii) Where more than one existing scale has been replaced by a single revised scale, the initial pay in the revised scale shall be fixed in the manner indicated in clause (i), as if each of the existing scales had been singly replaced by the revised scales.
(iii) Where an existing scale has been replaced by two revised scales, initial pay of the person fitted in the lower or higher revised scale shall be fixed in the manner indicated in clause (i), as if the existing scale had been replaced by a single lower or higher revised scale, as the case may be : Provided that if the existing emoluments plus the benefit permissible in clause (i) or (ii) or (iii) work out to be less than the minimum of the revised scale, the pay shall be fixed at the minimum of the revised scale  Provided further that if the existing emoluments plus the benefit permissible in clause (i) or (ii) or (iii) exceeds the maximum of the revised scale, the pay shall be restricted to the maximum thereof, and the difference shall be treated as personal pay.
(iv) Where a Government employee is holding a permanent post and is officiating in a higher post and the scales of pay applicable to those two posts are merged into one scale, the pay shall be fixed under clause (ii) with reference to the officiating post only provided he has continuously officiated in that post for not less than one year and the pay so fixed, shall be treated as substantive pay.
Where such a Government employee has not completed one year's continuous service in the higher officiating post on the appointed day, his pay in the revised scale shall be fixed separately with reference to his substantive pay and officiating pay in the existing scale and his pay in the revised scale as fixed with reference to the officiating pay shall be treated as substantive pay in that scale after rendering service for the period by which it fell short of one year on the appointed day, provided it is certified by the appointing authority that he would have continued to officiate in the higher officiating post during this period, had the revised scales not been introduced.
If, however, the appointing authority certifies that he would have reverted to the lower post during this period, his pay in the revised scale would from the date on which he would have reverted be regulated on the basis of the pay fixed on the appointed day with reference to his substantive pay in the lower post.
(v) Where two pay scales in the same line of promotion are replaced by a single revised scale, the revised pay of the Government employee working in the higher scale will not be fixed at a stage in the revised scale lower than that admissible to a Government employee working in the lower existing scale drawing basic pay at the same or lower rate as the employee working in the higher scale is drawing.
(2) If pay as fixed in higher officiating post under sub-rule (1) is equal to or lower than the pay as fixed in substantive post or a lower officiating post, officiating pay shall be refixed at the stage next above the substantive pay or the lower officiating pay, as the case may be.
7.
(1) Date of increment in the revised scale :- The next increment of a Government employee whose pay has been fixed in accordance with rule 6 shall be granted on the date on which he would have drawn his increment had he continued in the existing scale :  Provided that -  (i) Where the revised pay is fixed at the minimum of the time scale and on such fixation the revised pay exceeds the existing emoluments by more than rupees seventy-five, the next increment shall be granted on the date it falls due in the revised scale; (ii) The next increment shall be granted on the next succeeding day following the appointed day, to a Government employee whose pay fixed on the appointed day in the revised scale is at the same stage as the one fixed for another Government employee drawing pay at a lower stage than his in the same existing scale."
Rule 6(1) of the Revised Pay Rules, inter alia, provides that the initial pay of a Government employee be fixed separately in respect of his substantive pay in the permanent post on which he holds a lien as well as in respect of his pay in the officiating post.
Sub-rule (2) of Rule 6, which provides that if the pay fixed in the higher officiating post of an employee is equal to or lower than his pay as fixed in the substantive post, his officiating pay shall be re-fixed at the stage next above the substantive pay.
It is evident from the reading of Rules 6(1) and 6(2) that where an employee held a lien against a permanent post but was officiating in a higher post on the appointed day, it was necessary to work out his pay relevant to the substantive post as well as to the officiating post.
8.
It is manifest that the pay of an employee so fixed as indicated above, cannot be lower than the pay of the employee junior to him who was drawing pay at a lower stage than the stage of the senior employee.
It has, in fact, been admitted by the respondents that several officers junior to the petitioners as Sub-Judges drawing pay at lower stages than the stage of the petitioners, in the existing scale of pay of Rs. 1300-1500 before 111978, when the Revised Pay Rules were enforced, has been fixed at a higher stage than the petitioners' and those juniors were drawing higher salary than them.
The petitioners cannot be made to suffer because on the relevant date i.e. 111978 they were officiating as Additional District & Sessions Judges.
In other words, can it be permitted that if the petitioners were not officiating in the promoted ranks then they would have got more pay than the one which was fixed in their officiating promoted ranks.
It seems that the mistake has occurred because the respondents have not complied with the mandatory provisions of Rules 6(1) and 6(2) of the Revised Pay Rules, inasmuch as their pay in the substantive post as well as in the officiating post was not worked out separately.
The above fallacy can be illustrated by the factual position based on the record.
9.
The existing emoluments of the petitioners qua their substantive posts as on 111978 was Rs. 1500/- plus Rs. 100 (DA) plus Rs. 363/- (Addl.
DA) total Rs. 1963/-in the unrevised pay scale of Rs. 1300-1500.
They were entitled in the first instance to Rs. 2000/- in their substantive ranks being the minimum of the revised pay scale of Rs. 2000-2400.
One of their juniors Shri HL Garg, as mentioned in the gradation list (Annexure P.2) who was appointed in the P.S.C.
(Judicial Branch) on 11.2.1963, was promoted to the Selection Grade on 1121975 and his existing emoluments as on 111978 were Rs. 1863/-, i.e. less than the petitioners.
Similarly, the existing emoluments of all the officers junior to Shri HL Garg, such as Sarvshri Gurdial Singh, ML Signal, Bhagwan Singh, Naranjan Singh, JC Aggarwal, DN Sharma, Shamsher Singh RL Anand, RS Sharma, Bakhshish Kaur, Manmohan Singh Ahluwalia, HC Modi, AS Sodhi, SS Tiwana, IC Aggarwal, GS Khurana, PK Garg and HS Mander, who were at the same stage of Selection Grade of Rs. 1300-1500 on 111978 were Rs. 1863/- or less.
The pay of Shri HL Garg as well as of all the officers aforementioned who were junior even to Shri Garg was also fixed at Rs. 2000/- in the revised pay scale.
At this stage, proviso (ii) to Rule 7 above would come into play.
The pay of Shri HL Garg, who was drawing pay at lower stage before the enforcement of the Revised Pay Rules, as compared to the petitioners, gets the same pay on 111978.
This would entitle the petitioners to next increment on 211978 in the substantive capacity i.e. Rs. 2100/- in the pay scale of Rs. 2000-2400.
10.
Thus, it would be seen that had the petitioners not been promoted to officiate as Additional District & Sessions Judges, their pay would have been fixed at Rs. 2100/- as on 211978.
In the officiating capacity their pay was admittedly fixed at Rs. 2000/- (+ Rs. 9800 personal pay) which is lower than their pay as they would have got in the substantive post.
This entitles the petitioners to have their pay re-fixed as Additional District and Sessions Judges in the time-scale of Rs. 1580-2400 under Rule 414(1) and (2) read with Rule 4.4(a)(i) and Rule 248 of the Punjab Civil Service Rules, Vol.
I, Part 1.
These Rules are reproduced below :  "4.14(1) - Subject to the provisions of Rule 413 and 416 a Government employee who is appointed to officiate in a post shall draw the presumptive pay of that post.
4.14(2) - On an enhancement in the substantive pay, as a result of increment or otherwise, the pay of such Government employee shall be re-fixed under sub-rule (1) from the date of such enhancement as if he was appointed to officiate in that post on that date where such re-fixation is to his advantage.
248-  Presumptive pay of a post :- When used with reference to any particular Government employee, means the pay to which he would be entitled if he held the post substantatively and were performing its duties, but it does not include special pay unless the Government employee performs or discharges the work or responsibility, on consideration of which the special pay was sanctioned.
4.4.
(a)(i).-  (i) When appointment to the new post involves the assumption of duties or responsibility of greater importance (as interpreted for the purpose of rule 413) than those attaching to such permanent post, he will draw as initial pay the stage of the time-scale next above his substantive pay in respect of the old post."
11.
The pay of the petitioners as on 211978 in the rank of District & Sessions Judges, thus should have been fixed at Rs. 2200/- with next increment to follow on 111979 for petitioner No. 1, 1111978, for petitioner Nos. 2 and 4 and 1121978 for petitioner No. 3.
12.
In Annexures R-1 and R-2 to the written statement, the respondents have practically conceded the case of the petitioners.
It has been admitted that the petitioners' pay in the substantive capacity after stepping up as was done in the case of their juniors under order dated 30th November, 1983 of Hon'ble Mr. Justice RN Mittal (as his Lordship then was) with effect from 181978 would be fixed at Rs. 2100/-.
However, the respondents are not right while they mention in Column 6 of the Annexure R1 that officiating pay if re-fixed under Rule 414(2) of the Civil Services Rules, would also be at the same stage i.e. Rs. 2100/-.
Moreover, as observed above, the date of refixation in the case of the petitioners has to be 211978 and not 181978, which was the date when the Officers junior to the petitioners and senior to Shri HS Mander were equated with Shri HS Mander, who had started drawing pay equal to his seniors on 111978.
Consequently, the pay in Column No. 7 of Annexure R-1 should have been mentioned as Rs. 2300/- and not Rs. 2200/- on the next date of increments which have already been given above and not the dates mentioned in Column No. 7 of Annexure R-1.
13.
The pay fixation of the petitioners at Rs. 2200/- (+ Rs. 98/- as personal pay) on the existing emoluments of Rs. 1963/- appears to be in violation of Rule 6(1), which essentially forbids pay fixation at a stage lower than the existing emoluments.
Main Rule 6 and the first proviso set down the pay fixation formula complete in all respects prescribing the minimum an maximum limits of the benefit.
The second proviso of Rule 6 prescribes an additional ceiling to the benefit.
These two provisos have to be harmoniously construed.
Construing these provisos harmoniously along with other Rules quoted above, to the best advantage of the petitioners, on the basis of existing emoluments of Rs. 1963/- per month in the substantive rank, the petitioners' pay should have been fixed at Rs. 2000/- on l.1.1978 in the substantive rank and Rs. 2100/- on 211978 under Rule 7(i) and (ii) of the Revised Pay Rules, as persons juniors to them and drawing lesser pay, had also been fixed at Rs. 2000/- per month on 111978.
Since on 211978, the petitioners were officiating as Additional District & Sessions Judges, they would be entitled to be fixed at Rs. 2200/- in the pay scale of Rs. 1500-2400 under Rule 414(1) and (2) read with Rule 4.4(a)(i) of the Civil Service Rules.
To sum up, in case the petitioners' pay had been correctly fixed under sub-rule (1) of Rule 6 ignoring the second proviso, it would be Rs. 2200/- as on 111978.
However, if the pay is fixed as indicated above, first in the substantive rank and then in the officiating rank, it would be Rs. 2200/- on 211978 with the next increments on the dates they fell due respectively to the petitioners.
14.
For the foregoing reasons, this writ petition is allowed and the pay as fixed by the respondents is quashed.
The respondents are directed to refix the pay of the petitioners under the Revised Pay Rules and other Rules mentioned above first in the substantive rank and then in the officiating rank.
In other words, Rs. 2000/- in the substantive rank on 111978, Rs. 2100/- on 211978 in he substantive rank under Rule 7(1) and (2) in the pay scale of Rs. 2000-2400 and Rs. 2200/- on 211978 in the officiating rank of Additional District & Sessions Judges in the pay scale of Rs. 1580-2400.
Further they be given next increments on respective dates of increments of the petitioners, i.e. in case of petitioner No. 1 on 111979 and so on, for petitioner Nos. 2 and 4 on 1111978 and so on and so far as petitioner No. 3 is concerned the date would be 1121978 and so on.
The petitioners would be entitled to the arrears calculated in the above manner.
The petitioners would further be entitled to interest at the rate of 12% per annum on the arrears so calculated from the date of accrual to the date of payment.
These directions be carried out within three months from the receipt of this judgment.
There will be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
