SS Sodhi, J - The challenge here is to the directions given by the learned single Judge to the State of Haryana regarding Shri Lalit Kumar Gupta of the Haryana Civil Services, namely, to :  (1) sanction the crossing of the efficiency bar by him with effect from February 16, 1968;  (2) consider him for grant of selection grade from the date his junior Shri Jiwan Singh Jain was granted it in 1973 and that too by ignoring the adverse remarks for the years 1967-68 and 1969-70; and  (3) issue him an integrity certificate, again by ignoring the adverse remarks against him.
To give the relevant factual background, Shri Lalit Kumar Gupta was appointed to the PCS on February 16, 1960 and he was confirmed in this service on February 16, 1963.
On the re-organisation of the State of Punjab, he was allocated to Haryana in November, 1966.
2.
It was on the completion of seven years service on February 16, 1968, that Shri Lalit Kumar Gupta became due to cross his efficiency bar.
He had, however, been given adverse confidential reports for the years 1967-68 and then again for 1969-70.
A notice, Annexure P/2, was consequently, served upon this respondent on March 20, 1970 to show cause why he should not be stopped at the efficiency bar for three years with cumulative effect.
An explanation was submitted but it was not accepted.
Resultantly, by the order of June 14, 1971, Annexure P/3, the petitioner was stopped at the efficiency bar for three years with cumulative effect i.e. up to February 16, 1971.
3.
Later, for the next three years too, Shri Lalit Kumar Gupta was again stopped on the efficiency bar i.e. for the years 1972, 1973 and, 1974.
Those orders being founded upon the adverse reports earned by him during these years.
4.
It was with effect from February 16, 1975, that respondent Shri Lalit Kumar Gupta was for the first time allowed to cross the efficiency bar by the order of February 11, 1976, Annexure P/5.
He was also later allowed to cross the next efficiency bar with effect from February 1, 1978 and was eventually promoted to the selection grade with effect from January 1, 1981.
5.
The learned single Judge after adverting to Rule 4.8 of the Punjab Civil Services Rules and note 3 thereunder as also the instructions Annexure P/1 to the effect that the record and conduct of the government employee on the date of his crossing the efficiency bar be taken into account, held that Shri Lalit Kumar Gupta had wrongly been stopped at his first efficiency bar which he was due to cross on February 16, 1968.
The main ground for holding so being that the adverse report for the year 1967-68 had been recorded after February 16, 1968 and, therefore, on that date i.e. date on which he became due to cross efficiency bar, there was no adverse report against him.
6.
The other reason that appears to have weighed with the learned single Judge in directing the expunction of the adverse remarks for the year 1967-68 and 1969-70 was that there was a vigilance enquiry against Shri Lalit Kumar Gupta for 1966 and 1974.
This enquiry pertained to the period when he was working as a Judicial Magistrate at Jagadhari.
The complaint against him had been enquired into by the High Court and later dropped but despite this the vigilance enquiry continued culminating in a charge-sheet being served upon him in 1975.
This charge-sheet was eventually dropped in 1979.
It was the view of the learned single Judge.
It is quite possible that the reporting officer having knowledge of the fact that the vigilance enquiry is pending against the petitioner, it was mentioned in the A.C.R.
that his reputation for integrity is bad."
7.
Dealing with this matter, reference was also made by the learned single Judge to the consolidated instructions, regarding recording of confidential reports, issued by the government of Haryana in 1972, whereby the Punjab Government's circular of October 4, 1956 had been adopted, in terms of which an adverse report regarding integrity was required to be fortified with reasons for it.
8.
Both on account of the government instructions of 1972 as also the opinion expressed by him with regard to the likely effect of the vigilance enquiry against Shri Lalit Kumar Gupta upon the reporting officer, it was held by the learned single Judge that it was incumbent upon the State Government to expunge the adverse remarks for the years 1967-68 and 1969-70 and to evaluate his record thereafter for the grant to him of the selection grade and also for the issuance of integrity certificate for being brought on the select list and for promotion to the Indian Administrative Service.
9.
The line of reasoning adopted by the learned single Judge, we are constrained to observe is not one, which we can, with respect, endorse.
10.
At the very outset, there is the aspect of delay and laches on the part of the respondent Shri Lalit Kumar Gupta which clearly disentitles him to seek expunction of adverse remarks for the two years in question, namely 1967-68 and 1969-70, as also the subsequent orders stopping him at the efficiency bar until he was eventually allowed to do so with effect from 1975.
It is pertinent to note, in this context, that the present writ petition was not filed till November, 1981 and no explanation to account for this delay, is forthcoming.
11.
Even otherwise, the reasons for disregarding reports as put forth by the learned single Judge cannot stand security.
No doubt the report for the year 1967-68 was recorded after February 16, 1968, when Shri Lalit Kumar Gupta was due to cross the efficiency bar but the important point, to note is that this report had been recorded before the matter of crossing of efficiency bar was considered.
At any rate, both the adverse remarks for the year 1967-68 and 1969-70 were there when stoppage at the efficiency bar for the years 1972, 1973 and 1974 were ordered.
12.
As regards the reference to the likely effect upon the reporting authority of the vigilance enquiry against Shri Lalit Kumar Gupta, counsel for the respondent could point to no material on record to establish any connection between the remarks given to this respondent for these years and the enquiry pending against him.
In other words, these remarks were not, in any way, on account of this vigilance enquiry, there being no nexus between the adverse remarks and the vigilance enquiry or even the charge-sheet.
13.
Turning next to the instructions of government referred to by the learned single judge, it must be appreciated these are but guidelines only and do not provide any justifiable cause for enforcement of them in proceedings under Article 226 of the Constitution.
In any event, in the context of the circumstances here, no occasion is provided to impute any infirmity to the adverse remarks for 1967-68 and 1969-70 on account of these instructions.
14.
Before proceeding further, it also deserves mention that another ground for challenging the adverse remarks against Shri Lalit Kumar Gupta were the allegations of mala fide made by him against Smt. Om Prabha Jain who had contested the election to the Haryana Vidhan Sabha from the Kaithal constituency in February, 1967.
No such allegations find mention in any representation of explanation submitted by Shri Lalit Kumar Gupta against his adverse remarks.
It was only when he submitted his representation with regard to his selection grade and that too, for the first time in 1978 that they were aired.
There was, at any rate, no occasion for Smt. Om Prabha Jain to reply to them as she had been killed in a car accident.
15.
Such, thus, being the situation, the learned single Judge clearly fell in error in holding that the adverse remarks against Shri Lalit Kumar Gupta for the year 1967-68 and 1968-69 as also those during the years 1970 to 1974 be ignored in the matter of grant of selection grade and the issuance of an integrity certificate to him for being considered for promotion to the Indian Administrative Service.
16.
We consequently, hereby set aside the direction that the adverse remarks against Shri Lalit Kumar Gupta be expunged and he be considered for his selection grade and for issuance of an integrity certificate in disregard of these adverse reports.
In the result, the judgment of the learned single Judge is hereby over-ruled and the writ petition filed by Shri Lalit Kumar Gupta consequently dismissed.
The appeal is accordingly accepted.
In the circumstances, however, there will be no order as to costs.
Appeal accepted.
