Jawahar Lal Gupta, J. (Oral) - The petitioner, a constable in the Punjab Police prays for the issuance of a writ of mandamus directing the respondents to depute him for the lower School Course.
A few facts which are relevant for deciding this claim, may be noticed.
2.
The petitioner served the Indian Army from 1969 to May 1985.
After his discharge from the Army, the petitioner was enrolled as a Constable on May 3, 1986.
He worked as such till April 26, 1990 when he resigned from the service.
On November 14, 1990, he was re-enrolled.
On August 13, 1992, the break in service from April 26, 1990 to November 13, 1990 was condoned.
3.
On December 5, 1992, a test was held for selecting constables for admission to the lower School Course.
The petitioner was allowed to take this test.
He competed and was placed at No. 5 in order of merit.
However, when the Senior Superintendent of Police notified the list of selected candidates as approved by the Deputy Inspector General of Police on December 16, 1992, the petitioner's name was not included.
Aggrieved by this, the petitioner submitted a representation on December 22, 1992.
It was forwarded by the Senior Superintendent of Police to the Deputy Inspector General.
However, it did not bear any result.
Consequently, the petitioner approached this Court through the instant writ petition on January 7, 1993.
The motion Bench directed the issue of notice to show cause to the respondents.
They appeared.
In the written statement, the respondents inter alia averred that vide order dated January 7, 1993, the order dated August 13, 1992 by which the break in the petitioner's service had been condoned, has been cancelled.
The Petitioner then amended the writ petition so as to impugn even the order dated January 7, 1993.
It has been inter alia averred that this order is bad as it was passed without the issue of any notice to the petitioner and that he has a right to be deputed for the course in accordance with the order of merit determined by the Departmental Promotion Committee.
4.
The respondents have filed a written statement.
It has been inter alia averred that "the period of absence from April 26, 1990 to November 13, 1990 was inadvertently condoned by respondent No. 3 vide order dated 13892."
It has been further averred that the petitioner was allowed to appear in the written test held on 5121992 for lower School Promotion Course "due to inadvertence."
The petitioner was selected by the Departmental Promotion Committee.
However, his name was not approved by respondent No. 2 "because he was not eligible for the same according to the provisions contained in rule 13.7(2) of the Punjab Police Rules 1934 ---" as he had not completed three years' service.
A reference has been made to the provisions of the Punjab Civil Service Rules as well as Police Rules to show that the action of the respondents is legal and valid.
However, the petitioner's averment that the order dated January 7, 1993 was passed without the issue of any notice to him has not been specifically controverted.
Counsel for the parties have been heard.
The two questions that arise for consideration are :-  (i) Was the order dated January 7, 1993, a copy of which has been produced as Annexure P-5, violative of the principles of natural justice ?
(ii) Was the petitioner eligible for being deputed for the lower School Course ?
5.
As for the first question, it is the admitted position that vide order dated August 13, 1992, a copy of which has been produced as Annexure P-1 with the writ petition, the Senior Superintendent of Police, Kapurthala had ordered that the service of the petitioner from May 3, 1986 to April 25, 1990 "is counted towards civil pension and period of interruption i.e. 26490 to 13111990 is condoned in accordance with the provisions of PPR 92 (6) read with PPR 12.24".
This order was admittedly cancelled vide order dated January 7, 1993 without the grant of any opportunity whatsoever.
Could this be done ?
6.
A perusal of Rule 92(6) of the Punjab Police Rules Volume I shows that "when a lower subordinate is re-enrolled" the Superintendent of Police can "allow his previous police service to count for pension subject to the conditions in Rule 1224".
It was in exercise of this power that the Senior Superintendent of Police had passed the order dated August 13, 1992.
It was ordered that the service from May 3, 1986 to April 26, 1990 shall count for pension and that the interruption of service from April 26, 1990 to November 14, 1990 was condoned.
Apparently, the action of the respondents was in conformity with the provisions of Rule 92(6).
In any case, this order conferred certain benefits on the petitioner.
Later on, the petitioner could not be deprived of these benefits without the grant of a due and reasonable opportunity.
If such an opportunity had been granted, the petitioner could have shown that the provisions of the Punjab Civil Service Rules to which a reference has been made in the written statement like Rule 423, were not attracted in view of the specific provisions in the Punjab Police Rules.
He could have further shown that even under the provisions of the Punjab Civil Service Rules and more particularly Rule 423, the benefit granted to the petitioner was admissible and that the order passed by the Superintendent of Police on August 13, 1992 was not violative of the provisions of the rules.
The denial of an opportunity prevented the petitioner from puttingforth his view-point and prejudiced his interest.
His rights were affected without giving him a hearing.
This was violative of the principles of natural justice.
It was unfair.
It has resulted in material mis-carriage of justice.
The impugned order is consequently vitiated.
It follows that the order dated January 7, 1993 a copy of which is at Annexure P-5 was passed in violation of the principles of natural justice and is vitiated.
7.
As for the second question, it deserves notice that under Rule 13.7(2)(b), a constable who has passed the Matriculation Examination and has "put in more than three years of service" is eligible to compete and be selected for admission to the lower School Course.
Firstly, the petitioner having served as a constable from May 3, 1986 to April 26, 1990 had admittedly completed service of almost four years as a constable.
Secondly, the competent authority having passed the order dated August 13, 1992 condoning the break from April 26, 1990 to November 14, 1990, the petitioner was, by all standards qualified and eligible to not only compete for selection but for even being deputed for the course.
The respondents had not only permitted the petitioner to take the test but had even compiled his result.
The averment that the petitioner had stood 5th in order of merit has not been denied.
It is thus apparent that he had proved his merit.
Still further, the order dated August 13, 1992 had not been cancelled on December 16, 1992 when his name was excluded from the list of persons selected for being deputed for the course.
It is apparent that in spite of the order dated August 13, 1992 by which the break in service had been condoned, the respondents had arbitrarily ignored the petitioner from being deputed for the course.
It is only when the petitioner had staked his claim for being deputed for the course that the order dated January 7, 1993 was passed and the order regarding condonation of break was cancelled.
This was clearly a device adopted by the respondents to deny the petitioner what was rightfully his due.
8.
After consideration of the matter, it appears that the petitioner was fully eligible for being deputed for the course.
The action of the respondents in ignoring him was wholly illegal.
9.
In view of the above, it is held that the order dated January 7, 1993 (Annexure P-5) was illegal.
It is consequently quashed.
Furthermore, it is held that the petitioner was eligible to be deputed for the lower School Course on January 1, 1993.
The action of the respondents in ignoring him was wholly arbitrary and unfair.
Since the petitioner had approached this Court without any loss of time and he is not to blame for the delay in his being deputed for the course, the respondents are directed to send him for the lower School Course immediately.
It is further declared that consequential benefits which may ensue from his qualifying the course shall be granted.
The petitioner will also be entitled to his costs which are assessed at Rs. 1000/-.
Petition allowed.
