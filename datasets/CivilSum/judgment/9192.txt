VK Bali, J - In this petition filed under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India, the petitioners seek writ on the nature of Mandamus directing the respondents to grant them the pay scale of Rs. 510-940 and subsequently corresponding revised scale of Rs. 1350-2400 with effect from January 1, 1986 with all the arrears and interest calculated at the rate of 18 per cent on the basis of principle known as `equal pay for equal work'.
2.
The facts on which the aforesaid relief has been sought reveal that the petitioners were appointed as Dragline Operators on work-charge basis on various dates as mentioned in Annexure P-1, which are from 111986 to 1101987.
At the time of their appointment, they were not given the pay-scale meant for the post of Dragline Operator and instead they were only given the pay-scale of Operator.
The second Punjab Pay Commission prescribed the pay scale of Rs. 510-940 for the Dragline Operators of the Irrigation Department whereas Operators of the same department were recommended for pay scale of Rs. 400-600.
It is stated that the Secretary, Department of Finance, issued a letter dated 741989 to the Secretary, Irrigation and Power as also the Chief Engineer (Construction), S.Y.L.
Canal Project, Punjab, asking them to revise the pay scale of the work-charge employees, who were working in the Irrigation Department.
Vide aforesaid letter, which has been placed on record as Annexure P-2, Dragline Operators were recommended the pay-scale of Rs. 1350-2400.
They were earlier in the pay-scale of Rs. 510-940.
The Operators in the aforesaid letter have been recommended the pay-scale of Rs. 950-1800 instead of Rs. 400-600, which they were drawing earlier.
It requires to be mentioned here that as per Annexure P-2, the Dragline Operator has been shown at two places - one at Sr. No. 53 and the other at Sr. No. 69.
The Operator has been shown at Sr. No. 121.
Whereas the Dragline Operators shown at Sr. No. 53 had 14 posts and a pay-scale of Rs. 1350-2400 was recommended to be paid, the Dragline Operator mentioned at Sr. No. 69 had one post and was recommended pay-scale of Rs. 1200-2100.
The existing pay-scale of the Dragline Operators mentioned at Sr. No. 53 was Rs. 510-940, whereas the pay-scale of the Dragline Operator shown at Sr. No. 69 was Rs. 450-800 and as referred to above a pay-scale of Rs. 1200-2100 was recommended for the said post.
The operator mentioned at Sr. No. 121 was recommended the pay-scale of Rs. 950-1800 whereas the existing pay-scale of the said post was Rs. 400-600.
After issuance of orders dated 741989 (Annexure P-2) the petitioners were given the pay-scale of Rs. 1200- 2100 for sometime and on representation made by them that a pay-scale of Rs. 1350-2400 be given to them, as they were working as Dragline Operators, instead of giving a rise, their pay-scale was reduced to Rs. 950-1800, which was meant for the post of Operator.
This led to the filing of representation by the petitioners and when no tangible result came forth, a writ was filed in this Court, bearing No. 13461 of 1989.
The petitioners were, however, directed to file proceeding under the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.
The aforesaid order was passed by this Court on 27101989, which runs thus :-  "The petitioners are claiming salary as per the revised pay-scale of Dragline Operators and are also claiming arrears of pay.
According to the learned counsel, the petitioners were not given any opportunity while withdrawing the benefit allowed to them.
After hearing the learned counsel for the petitioners, we find that it is a fit case where proceedings should be taken under the Industrial Disputes Act (hereinafter called the Act) where all the facts raised in the Writ Petition could be settled.
Consequently, the petitioners are directed to approach the authorities under the Act for getting the necessary relief, if any.
The Writ Petition is disposed of accordingly."
3.
The petitioners thereafter, obviously, approached the Labour Court, Patiala, under Section 33-C(2) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.
The matter was contested by the respondents before the Labour Court and vide order dated 2011992 the claim of the petitioners was declined.
The petitioners claim for higher pay-scale, as has been mentioned above, and for successfully doing so, they wish that orders of the Labour Court (Annexure P-9) be set aside.
4.
The Labour Court decided the controversy against the petitioners basically on two grounds.
First ground was that claim of the petitioners could not be decided under Section 33-C(2) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, as proceedings under the relevant Section were in the nature of execution proceedings, where the Labour Court was to calculate the money due to a workman from the employer, but when the computation of money is dependent upon some admitted existing right then only the procedure under Section 33-C(2) of the Act can be taken and where there is dispute of any right as such, the only course open to the employee shall be to seek reference under Section 10 of the Act.
The other ground taken so as to negative the claim of the petitioners was that they were initially given the pay-scale of Rs. 400-600, which has now been revised to Rs. 950-1800 and they were never given the pay-scale of Rs. 510-940, which has now been revised to Rs. 1350-2400, which was asked for by them.
5.
Learned counsel appearing for the petitioners has assailed the aforesaid findings by contending that if the claim is based upon some award, settlement or statute, the Labour Court has jurisdiction to entertain the same under Section 33-C(2) of the Act and as right of the petitioners flows from Annexure P-2, providing revision of pay-scale, the same should have been granted to them.
It is further contended that the petitioners are entitled to the pay-scale given to Dragline Operator, which duty was being performed by the petitioners, on the principle of `equal pay for equal work'.
6.
This petition has been opposed and written statement has been filed by the respondents through MM Singh Gill, Executive Engineer, Kharar Construction Division, SYL Canal Project, SAS Nagar, Mohali.
It is mainly pleaded that the petitioners were not appointed as Dragline Operators and in fact they were appointed as Operators in the pay-scale of Rs. 400-600.
Copies of the applications submitted by some of the petitioners (Annexure R-2 and R- 3) have been placed on the records to show that they applied only for the post of an Operator.
It is further pleaded that scale of Dragline Operator has been mentioned at two serial numbers, i.e., Sr. No. 53 and the other at item No. 69 of the Schedule (Annexure P-2) and a perusal thereof would reveal that at Sr. No. 53 only 14 posts of Dragline Operators were granted the pay-scale of Rs. 1350-2400, which was revised from Rs. 510-940.
At Sr. No. 69, one Dragline Operator was granted pay-scale of Rs. 1200-2100, which was revised from the pay-scale of Rs. 450-800.
At Sr. No, 121, there were 100 posts of Operators, who were getting pay-scale of Rs. 950-1800, which was revised from Rs. 400-600 and inasmuch as the petitioners were working as Operators, their pay-scale was revised to Rs. 950-1800 to which only they were entitled.
It is further pleaded that work and duties of the Operators and the Dragline Operators are different so the principle of `equal pay for equal work' cannot be applied in this case.
Insofar as the case of the petitioners that they were initially put in the pay-scale of Rs. 1200-2100 is concerned, it is maintained by the respondent that it was on account of a mistake and immediately when the error was detected, the same was rectified.
The petitioners also filed replication.
7.
After hearing the learned counsel for the parties, I am of the considered view that this petition has no merit and, thus, deserves to be dismissed.
The petitioners were appointed as Operators and not as Dragline Operators.
The very applications given by some of the petitioners (Annexures R-2 and R-3 would demonstrate that they applied specifically for the post of an Operator.
All through they had been getting the pay-scale of an Operator and it is only for a very small period that they were given a higher pay scale, which is admissible to Dragline Operator, but that, as stated in the written statement, was on account of mistake, which was rectified immediately when the same came to the notice of the respondents.
Revision of pay-scales, as evidenced through Annexure P-2, does not advance the case of the petitioners.
As mentioned above, the Dragline Operators have been mentioned both at Sr. Nos. 53 and 69.
The one shown at Sr. No. 53 has 14 posts, incumbents of which were in the pay-scale of Rs. 510-940, which was revised to Rs. 1350-2400.
The Dragline Operator mentioned at Sr. No. 69 has only one post, pay-scale of which was revised from Rs. 450-800 to Rs. 1200-2100.
Insofar as the petitioners are concerned, they were appointed as Operators and are at Sr. No. 121 of Annexure P-2.
They were 100 operators, who were earlier in the pay-scale of Rs. 400-600, which was revised to Rs. 950-1800.
This order of revision of pay-scales in no way supports the claim of the petitioners to get pay scale of Rs. 1350-2400, which is equivalent to the Dragline Operator, shown at Sr. No. 53 in Annexure P-2.
At no given time the post of Operator was equated with that of Dragline Operator and insofar as case of the petitioners that even though they were appointed as Operators, but they were doing the duties of Dragline Operators is concerned the same has been strongly denied by the respondents.
No evidence was brought before the Labour Court to prove that the petitioners were doing the same duties as were assigned to the Dragline Operators.
The finding of the Labour Court that claim of the petitioners was not competent under Section 33-C(2) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, is also correct.
The claim of the petitioners was not based upon existing or admitted rights nor it was in consequence of some statute, settlement, award or order having been passed on that account.
No case at all has been made out that might need interference by this Court in its writ jurisdiction under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India and the petition is, thus, dismissed.
There shall, however, be no order as to costs.
