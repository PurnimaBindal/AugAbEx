RL Anand, J - The State of Haryana (defendant in the trial Court) has filed the present appeal and it has been directed against the judgment and decree dated 10.2.1993 passed by Addl.
District Judge, Narnaul, who reversed the judgment and decree dated 12121990 passed by Sub-Judge Ist Class, Narnaul, and decreed the suit of plaintiff-respondent Ramesh Chand, and granted him a declaration that he was entitled to get the salary of Pump Operator w.e.f.
August, 1978 and he was entitled to be regularised as Pump Operator when he completed four years of service and he had been working continuously as Pump Operator w.e.f.
August, 1978.
2.
The pleadings of the parties can be summarised in the following manner :-  Plaintiff Ramesh filed the suit for declaration that he was initially appointed as a work-charge T/Mate on 131976 but he was promoted to the post of Pump Operator on 151977 for three months and he was again promoted w.e.f.
1.12.1977 and continued to work as Pump Operator upto April, 1978.
The reversion of the plaintiff was for a brief period but he had been working as Pump Operator during the said period.
He was again shown to have been reverted to the post of T/Mate for the months of May, June and July, 1978 and was paid salary for the post of T/Mate, although he had been working as Pump Operator.
He was again shown to have been promoted from August, 1978 to July, 1980 but was again shown as T/Mate in the record.
During this period also the plaintiff had been working as Pump Operator.
Again in February, 1982 he had been shown to have been promoted as Pump Operator for four months and was again paid the salary of T/Mate.
Therefore, despite the fact that he had discharging the duties of Pump Operator, his services were regularised on the post of T/Mate w.e.f.
1.1.1984.
But in view of the Govt. instructions, his services should have been regularised as Pump Operator.
Some persons junior to him were promoted ignoring him and a notice under Section 80 CPC was given to the defendants calling upon them to admit the claim of the plaintiff but to no effect.
Hence the present suit.
The notice of the suit was given to the defendants, who filed the written statement and denied the allegations.
According to the defendants, the plaintiff was appointed as Chowkidar and not as T/Mate and that no junior person had been promoted.
The services of the other persons in other Sub- Divisions were also taken into consideration and the plaintiff was paid the salary of the post on which he had remained posted and his claim was never ignored.
The plaintiff was working as T/Mate on 31121981, the relevant date for regularisation of services and on that date he had not completed four years of service as Pump Operator as required under the Government instructions.
The plaintiff remained posted as Chowkidar, T/Mate and Operator from 131976 to 31.12.1983, described as under :-  Sr. No. Period Designation 1.

3.
From the above pleadings of the parties, the trial Court framed the following issues :-  1.
Whether the action of the defendants in ignoring the plaintiff for the post of Pump Operator and regularisation of the services of the persons junior to him on the said post and showing the plaintiff's reversion at the intervals as mentioned in the plaint is wrong, illegal and mala fide, against service rules, against principle of natural justice and equity and is null and void, as alleged ?
OPP 2.
Whether the plaintiff is entitled to the regularisation of his services as Pump Operator and if so from what date ?
OPP 3.
Relief.
4.
The parties led oral and documentary evidence in support of their respective case and vide judgment and decree dated 12121990, the learned Sub Judge Ist Class, Narnaul dismissed the suit of the plaintiff for the reasons given in paras No. 7 and 8 of the judgment, which read as under :-  "7.
The undisputed case of the parties is that according to the Govt. instructions all work-charge employees were to be regularised in service who had completed more than four years in service and had also been recruited through the employment exchange.
Such employee who were not recruited through employment exchange were to be removed from service forthwith.
Ex.D1, the copy of Govt. instructions dated 2341982 may be referred to in this behalf besides Ex.D3, copy of instructions dated 14101982 8.
The plaintiff had very fairly admitted in his cross-examination that his total length of service on the post of a pump-operator was less than four years.
The plaintiff has also stated that although he was shown to have been reverted in the papers yet he had been all-along discharging the duties of a pump-operator.
This self-serving statement of the plaintiff does not stand to logic.
Had the plaintiff been working as Pump-Operator and been paid the salary of a T/Mate, he would have at one time or the other, risen in protest against the injustice alleged to have been meted to him.
The inaction of the plaintiff coupled with his conduct points to the only conclusion that the testimony of the plaintiff is incorrect so far as he claims to have been working as a pump-operator throughout after April, 1976.
The plaintiff has also not brought any definite evidence on record to the effect that the total length of the services of the other employees promoted as Pump-Operators was less than his own total service.
In these circumstances, I am of the confirmed view that the services of the plaintiff were rightly regularised as a T/Mate and the same could not be regularised on the post of a Pump-Operator.
I also hold that the plaintiff was never reverted to the post of T/Mate only on papers and was actually made to work as a T/Mate during the period in question.
In view of my above discussion, it is evident that the orders of the concerned authorities are legal, binding and in accordance with the Govt. instructions.
No fault can be found with the orders under challenge."
5.
Aggrieved by the judgment and decree of the trial Court, Ramesh Chand filed the appeal before the first Appellate Court, which vide judgment and decree dated 10.2.1993 reversed the judgment and decree of the trial Court and decreed the suit of the plaintiff in the above terms and this time the State of Haryana (defendants in the trial Court) has filed the present appeal.

12.
When the plaintiff had been working as Pump Operator on regular basis, his service on the post of Pump Operator comes to more than four years.
For the said submission, reference was made to TN Sankarasundaram v. Director of Stationery and Printing, Madras cited in 1983(2) SLR 183 holding that Administrative instructions - Government bound to follow its own instructions - not open to it to say that it had issued the administrative instructions and that the same could be violated even though these may adversely effect the Government servant.
Like it, it was also held in DP Pathak v. State of Punjab and three others cited in 1980 All India Services Law Journal 305 holding that if there are no rules prescribing any qualification for appointment to a post, the Government in its executive jurisdiction can prescribe the qualifications.
Such executive instructions may have the same effect as the rules have and on appointment in their violation can give a person a right to challenge the same.

5.
The learned counsel appearing on behalf of the State submitted that as per the policy of the State Government the plaintiff has not worked continuously against the post of Pump Operator for four years as on 31121981 and, therefore, he is not entitled to be considered for regularisation.
The counsel submitted that the judgment of the trial Court was correct when the trial Court had taken into consideration the admission of the plaintiff in the cross-examination when he admitted that his total length of service on the post of Pump Operator was less than four years.
He further submitted that the plaintiff stated that although he was shown to have been reverted in the papers yet he had been discharging the duties of Pump Operator and to that extent there is no corroboration to the statement of the plaintiff.
Had the plaintiff was reverted to the post of T/Mate in an illegal manner or that he had been discharging the duties of the post of Pump Operator but paid for the post of T/Mate, the plaintiff would have in normal circumstances raised hue and cry.
He would have issued a notice in writing to the defendants for the alleged injustice being done to him.
Since there is no definite evidence that the total length of the service of the plaintiff as Pump Operator was four years or more as on 31121981, therefore, he could not lay claim for the regularisation of his service as a Pump Operator.
The learned counsel also submitted that during the relevant four years the plaintiff had either been discharging the duties of T/Mate or that of Pump Operator but his length of service was not continuous for four years against the post of Pump Operator and, therefore, the suit has been rightly dismissed by the trial Court.
6.
On the contrary, Mr. IS Balhara, the learned counsel appearing on behalf of the respondent has supported the judgment of the first Appellate Court by stating that the plaintiff throughout had been discharging the duties of Operator, especially w.e.f.
111978 to 31121981.
The department in order to cause harm and against the interest of the plaintiff had been making fictitious entries in the official record so as to accommodate other persons for their promotions.
The plaintiff throughout had been discharging the duties of Operator, so much so his junior persons, who were favourable to the administration, had been promoted.
But so far as the plaintiff is concerned, he has been shown working either as T/Mate or as Operator.
Mr. Balhara submitted that adverse inference should be drawn against the defendants when they did not produce the relevant record in the trial Court.
Had the complete record of the department been produced by the defendants, the plaintiff would have shown that throughout he had been discharging the duties of Operator from 111978 to 31121981.
The counsel submitted that even as per graph, which has been relied upon by the defendants, it stands established that the plaintiff was discharging the duties of Operator as on 111978.
No circumstance has been shown how the plaintiff became T/Mate from Operator and under what circumstances the post of Operator was abolished so as to accommodate the plaintiff as T/Mate.
7.
After considering the rival contentions of the parties, I am of the opinion that there is a merit in the contentions raised by the learned counsel for the respondent.
Though the appellants through the statement of Shri SK Goel DW-1 had tried to show that the plaintiff's services were not continuous against the post of Operator for a period of four years as on 31121981, yet this Court is not convinced with this explanation and with the statement of Shri SK Goel for the reason that the plaintiff examined Nathu Ram Clerk, who was asked to bring the summoned record but he could not produce the same, as a result of which the evidence of the defendants was closed by the Govt. Pleader.
From the various bills and receipts it could be shown as to whether the plaintiff was discharging the duties of T/Mate or Operator.
Any way, truth has to come out and it has come out in this case.
Before I touch that aspect, let me first examine the policy of the Government in this regard, which is reflected in the letter Ex.D1, dated 2341982, which reads as follows :-  "The matter regarding the regularisation of the work charged employees has been considered and it has been decided that all the work charged employees working in the Haryana State, who have completed 4 years of continuous service on 31121981 should be regularised in consultation with the finance Department."
This letter was issued by the Chief Engineer, D-II, Irrigation Department, Haryana to all the Superintending Engineers, Irrigation Department, Haryana.
Under these circumstances the sole point which survives for determination is whether the plaintiff had completed four years continuous service as on 31121981 as Operator or as a T/Mate or as a Chowkidar.
8.
Now let us see the graph of service which has been relied upon by the defendants in the written statement.
The reading of the same would show that from 131976 to 3041976 the plaintiff has been shown working as Chowkidar.
From 151976 to 3181976 he worked as T/Mate.
Again from 191976 to 31.12.1976 he worked as Chowkidar.
From 1.1.1977 to 30.4.1977 he worked as T/Mate.
From 151977 to 3151977 the plaintiff worked as Operator.
From 191977 to 7121977 he was again discharging the duties of T/Mate.
From 8.1.1977 to 3041978 he was discharging the duties of Operator.
Meaning thereby as on 111978 the plaintiff was discharging the duties of an Operator.
In these circumstances, it was necessary for the defendants to establish under what circumstances the plaintiff was reverted to the post of T/Mate w.e.f.
151978.
There is a grain of truth in the allegations of the plaintiff when he stated that though he has been shown as T/Mate and has been paid the salary of T/Mate but throughout he was discharging the duties of Operator.
This statement of the plaintiff has not been rebutted successfully by the defendants.
If the plaintiff was working as Operator as on 111978 and he worked continuously upto 31121981 as per the policy of the Government as contained in the letter Ex.D1, there is no difficulty on my part to hold that the plaintiff performed the duties of Operator as a work charged employee for a continuous period of four years.
In order to show the break in the designation of the services of the plaintiff if the department had shown some times as Operator and some times as T/Mate, without indicating under what circumstances the post of Operator was abolished and why the plaintiff was reverted to the post of T/Mate, the benefit of such jugglery should go to the plaintiff and the civil Court will always be competent to lift the veil and see the real purpose of the department when it had been unilaterally showing the plaintiff as T/Mate and some times as Operator.
To continue with the discussion, the graph would further show that from 151978 to 3171978 the plaintiff has been shown as T/Mate, but again he was shown working as Operator w.e.f.
181978 to 581980.
Again from 681980 to 3091982 the plaintiff has been shown working as T/Mate.
But from 1101982 to 30.4.1983 the plaintiff worked in the capacity of an Operator.
The graph would further show that most of the time the plaintiff has been shown working as Operator.
In this view of the matter, the first Appellate Court was right in concluding that the plaintiff had worked for four years as Operator upto 31121981 and this Court is of the view that the plaintiff was discharging and performing the duties of an Operator during the relevant period of four years and thus he was entitled to be considered for regularisation to the post of Operator from the date when the claim of the plaintiff became ripe for regularisation as per the policy of the State Government.
It is a settled principle of law that the Government is bound by its policy decisions and it is not open for the Government to say that the instructions which were issued were only administrative one and the violation of the same cannot be enforced.
In this view of the matter, I do not see any merit in this appeal and dismiss the same with no order as to costs.
Appeal dismissed.
