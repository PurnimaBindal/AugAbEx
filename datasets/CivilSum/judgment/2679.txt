Hemant Gupta, J - This order shall dispose of CWP Nos. 8176-CAT of 2003 and CWP No. 8177-CAT of 2003, as both the petitions raise common questions of law and facts.
However, for the facility of reference, facts are taken from CWP No. 8176-CAT of 2003.
2.
The petitioners are challenging the order dated 10122002, passed by the Central Administrative Tribunal, Chandigarh Bench, Chandigarh (hereinafter referred to as `the tribunal'), whereby the original application filed by the petitioners for grant of 1989 as the year of allotment to the petitioners as Members of the Indian Administrative Service, was declined.
3.
The petitioners are directly recruited Officers to Punjab Civil Services (Executive Branch).
As the members of the State Civil Services, the petitioners became eligible for consideration for promotion to the Indian Administrative Service (I.A.S.)
in accordance with the Indian Administrative Service (Appointment by Promotion), Regulations, 1955.
The Selection Committee considered the case of the State Civil Services Officers in its meeting held on 2931993.
The petitioners fell in the zone of consideration, during the year 1993-94 i.e. 141993 to 3131994.
The petitioners were selected as their names were included in the select list for promotion to the Indian Administrative Service from amongst the members of the State Civil Services.
The select list was approved by the Union Public Service Commission as well.
It is the case of the petitioners that there were substantive vacancies in the Indian Administrative Service for filling up before 31121993, to accommodate all the petitioners and that the petitioners acquired a right to promotion according to their seniority in the year 1993, therefore, the year of allotment is to be granted by giving deemed date of appointment in the wear 1989 instead of 1990.
4.
It is pointed out that after the meeting of the Select Committee on 2931993, some members of the State Civil Services filed an original applications before the Tribunal, the main being OA No. 336 of 1993.
The Tribunal passed an interim order on 23.4.1993 that the select list shall not be the basis for issuing promotion orders to the State Civil Services Officers provided separate list has not been prepared for the vacancies for the years 1992-93 and 1993-94.
5.
Shri Birender Pal Singh Kalhon, also a member of State Civil Services filed CWP No. 4090 of 1993 before this Court, wherein an interim order was passed on 26.5.1993 that the select list prepared in the meeting committee 1931993, will be sent to the Union of India for follow up action, but its implementation was stayed till the next date of hearing.
The said interim order passed by this Court on 26.3.1993 came to be vacated on 24.12.1993 and the respondents were directed to pass appropriate orders implementing the recommendations of Union Public Service Commission and to issue appointment orders to the selectees according to the Rules.
It may be noticed that the said writ petition was dismissed later on after the judgment of the Hon'ble Supreme Court in L Chandra Kumar v. Union of India, 1997(2) SCT 423 : AIR 1997 SC 1125, on 1591997.
6.
On the other hand, the interim order passed by the Tribunal on 2341993 came to be vacated on 1211994.
It was thereafter that the petitioners, whose names were recommended in terms of the recommendations of meeting of the select committee held on 2931993, came to be appointed on 1211994.
7.
Thereafter, vide order Annexure P.1 dated 7101994, the petitioners were assigned 1990 as the year of allotment.
The representation submitted by the petitioners to grant 1989 as the year of allotment was declined.
The challenge to assignment of the seniority, has remained unsuccessful before the Tribunal, primarily for the reason that it is not a case of gross or inordinate delay.
The judgment of the Hon'ble Supreme Court in Antulay AR v. RS Nayak, AIR 1988 SC 1531 was considered and found that the maxim that the mistake of the Court shall prejudice none, is not a source of general power to reopen and to rehear adjudication which has otherwise assumed finality.
The area of operation is generally procedural.
It was also found that when the Select Committee was considering the appointments in its meeting held on 2931993, it was considering the appointments for the period upto 3131994 starting from 141993.
Therefore, the appointments made even upto 3131994 would be within reasonable time.
Thus, it was found that keeping in view the definition of year in the 1955 Regulations, the applicants have been given appointment within the year, therefore, the 1990 as the year of allotment cannot be said to be illegal.
8.
Learned counsel for the petitioners has vehemently argued that the petitioners were not appointed on account of the interim orders passed by this Court and the learned Tribunal.
Though the stay was vacated by this Court on 23121993, but the Tribunal vacated it on 1211994 and on the same day, the petitioners were appointed.
Therefore, it is apparent that the petitioners were declined appointment in the year 1993 only on account of interim order passed by the Court.
Therefore, it is alleged that the maxim `actus curiae nemimum gravabit' would be applicable and thus, the petitioners cannot be denied the benefit of appointment in the year 1993.
It is further argued that the maxim nunc pro tune i.e. if on account of delay in Court, a party suffers, the Court has the power to remedy it, would be applicable.
9.
We have heard learned counsel for the parties.
There is no dis to about the two maxims relied upon by the petitioners that the acts of the Court will cause prejudice to none, and/or on account of delay, the Court has the power to remedy the consequences of delay on account of its order.
But we are of the opinion that the said question is not really relevant in the present case.
The year has been defined in the 1955 Regulations Regulation 2(1) of 1955 Regulations, as reproduced by the Tribunal in its order, read as under :  "Year means the period commencing on the 1st of April and ending on 31st day of March of the subsequent year."
10.
The said definition has been substituted vide Notification dated 31121997 when Clause 2(1) was substituted defining "year" to mean the period commencing on the first day of January and ending on the 31st day of December of the same year.
But in the present case, the definition reproduced above is relevant.
11.
The year of allotment is required to be determined in terms of said Rule reads as under :  "Rule 3(3) (ii)  The year of allotment of a promotee officer shall be determined in the following manner :  (a) For the service rendered by him in the State Civil Service upto twelve years, in the rank not below that of a Deputy Collector or equivalent, he shall be given a weightage of four years towards fixation of the year allotment;  (b) he shall also be given a weightage of one year for year completed three years of service beyond the period of twelve years, referred to in sub-clause (a), subject to a maximum weightage of five years.
In this calculation, fractions are to be ignored.
(c) The weightage mentioned in sub-clause (b) shall be calculated with effect from the year in which the officer is appointed to the service.
Provided that he shall not be assigned a year of allotment earlier than the year of allotment assigned to an officer senior to him in that select list or appointed to the service on the basis of an earlier select list."
12.
As per the above Rule, the year referred to in Rule 3(3)(ii) of the Rules, in the absence of any other definition or meaning can safely be the one as defined in Regulation 2(1) of 1995 Regulations.
Therefore, the petitioners appointed within the year as defined in Rule 3(3)(ii) of the Rules, have been rightly allotted 1990 as year of allotment.
13.
Regulation 5 of the 1955 Regulations contemplated all the relevant time in the year 1993 that the Select Committee is to consider the case of a member of the State Civil Services, if he holds a substantive post in the State Civil Service and has not completed less than 8 years of continuous service on the first day of April of the year for which the Committee meets.
Regulation 5(2) of 1955 Regulations was amended on 3031989.
As per the amended Regulations, the Select Committee was to consider the case of a member of the State Civil Service on the first date of April of the year in which it meets.
However, the reference to first day of the April, has since been substituted by first day of January in the year 2000.
Regulation 5 as it existed after the amendment on 3031989 reads as under :  "5.
Preparation of a list of suitable officers.
- (1) Each Committee shall ordinarily meet at intervals not exceeding one year and prepare a list of such members of the State Civil Service as are held by them to be suitable for promotion the Service.
The number of members of the State Civil Service to be included in the list shall be calculated as the number of substantive vacancies anticipated in the course of the period of 12 months, commencing from the date of preparation of the list, in the post available for them under rule 9 of the Recruitment Rules plus twenty per cent of such number or two, whichever is greater.
(2) The Committee shall consider for inclusion in the said list, the cases of members of the State Civil Services in the order of a seniority in that service of a number which is equal to three times the number referred in sub- regulation (1)] :  Provided that such restriction shall not apply in respect of a State where the total number of eligible officers is less than three times the maximum permissible size of the Select List and in such a case the Committee shall consider all the eligible officers :  Provided further that in computing numbers for inclusion in the field of consideration the number of officers referred to in sub-regulation (3) shall be excluded : Provided also that the Committee shall not consider the case of a member of the State Civil Service unless, on the first day of April of the year in which it meets he is substantive in the State Civil Service and has completed not less than eight years of continuous service (whether officiating or substantive) in the post of Deputy Collector or in any other post or posts declared equivalent thereto by the State Government."
14.
A reading of the above reproduction would show that the Select Committee is to consider the member of State Civil Service on the first day of April of the year in which the Select Committee is meeting and after such member has completed not less than 8 years of continuous service in the post of Deputy Collector or like.
In terms of Sub Clause (1), the Select Committee is to meet at intervals not exceeding one year to prepare a list of members of the State Civil Service, suitable for promotion.
The number of substantive vacancies anticipated in the course of period of 12 months commencing from the date of preparation of the list was to be prepared.
Since the Select Committee held its meeting on 2931993, the committee was to consider the members of the State Civil Service with reference to first day of April, 1993, for appointment.
However, the number of vacancies against which recommendations could be made were for a period of next 12 months ie till March, 1994.
Still further, the members of the State Civil Service were considered for appointment to the Indian Administrative Service in respect of the vacancies which were arising from 30.10.1992 till 2821994, though the vacancies against which the petitioners could be appointed became available on 31121993.
Therefore, the fact that there was an interim order granted by the High Court or by the Tribunal, does not have any effect of deferring the appointments of the petitioners.
The appointments could be made upto March, 1994.
Mere availability of vacancies in the cadre of Indian Administrative Service is not to determine the fact of grant of year of allotment.
The year of allotment has to be assigned from the date the petitioners are actually appointed.
However, in case of delay in appointment on account of some interim order, the Court has the jurisdiction to undo the wrong, but in the present case, the petitioners are claiming the year of allotment from the date, the vacancies have arisen though the Select Committee was to consider the members of the State Civil Service for vacancies arising upto March, 1994.
Therefore, mere fact that there was an interim order passed by Tribunal or by the this Court, is wholly inconsequential.
The petitioners have been appointed on 1211994 i.e. soon after the vacancies for all the petitioners became available on 31121993.
15.
Thus, we do not find that there is an error or jurisdiction in the orders passed by the Tribunal, which may warrant interference in the writ jurisdiction of this Court.
Hence, both the writ petitions are dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
