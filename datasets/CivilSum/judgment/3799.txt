Adarsh Kumar Goel, J - This order will dispose of Civil Writ Petition Nos. 792 and 5980 of 1996.
2.
In Civil Writ Petition No. 792 of 1996 filed on 1311996, prayer is for grant of Freedom Fighters' pension from the date of the Scheme i.e. 1581972.
It has been alleged that the petitioner joined Indian National Army (for short `I.N.A.')
in 1943 and remained under detention for nine months; the petitioner was awarded `Tamra Patra' on 1581972 and though he was entitled to monthly pension under "Swatantarta Sainik Samman Pension Scheme, 1980", he was not awarded the same.
He made representations and vide letter dated 2781993, he was awarded pension of Rs. 1,000/- per month w.e.f.
22.7.1993 but arrears were not paid.
He made a representation (Annexure P-6) dated 2361994 for grant of pension to which no reply has been received.
This court in judgment dated 15.1.1972 has held that pension was payable w.e.f.
1581972 with 12% interest.
3.
Notice of motion was issued.
Reply has been filed inter alia stating that several pensioners filed a writ petition in Delhi High Court for the same relief being Civil Writ Petition No. 7819 of 1992 wherein following order was passed :-  "Since petitioner are already getting pension, it is not necessary to pass any order giving them further interim relief."
4.
In Union of India v. Ganesh Chander Dolai, S.L.P.
2325 of 1995, the Hon'ble Supreme Court vide judgment 2441995 upheld payment of pension from a prospective date in doubtful cases.
It is further submitted that mere holding of Tamra Patra was not sole proof for eligibility under the scheme.
It is submitted that judgment of this Court relied on by the learned counsel for the petitioner was on different set of facts.
Civil Writ Petition No. 5980 of 1996 was filed on 20.4.1996 alleging that the petitioner underwent imprisonment from 191942 to 121944 at District Jail, Multan and was entitled to pension under the scheme.
The petitioner made a representation which has been rejected vide order dated 2711995 without any speaking order.
5.
Notice of motion was issued and reply has been filed that rejection of representation of the petitioner was based on a verification report dated 2091994 of Deputy Commissioner and there was nothing to show that certificate issued to the co-prisoner relied on by the petitioner could be acted upon in absence of a person issuing certificate being proved to be a co-prisoner.
6.
Learned counsel for the petitioners pressed the claim for direction to award pension and the learned counsel for the Union of India submitted that the case be decided on merits.
7.
I have considered the rival submissions and perused the record of the cases.
8.
In Mukund Lal Bhandari and others v. Union of India and others, A.I.R. 1993 SC 2127, Para 5, the Hon'ble Supreme Court considered the question as to date from which benefit of the Scheme was required to be given.
It was observed that having regard to the object of the scheme which was not to reward or compensate the sacrifices made in the freedom struggle but to honour and to mitigate the sufferings of those who had given their all for the country in the hour of need and therefore, benefit of scheme could not be extended retrospectively.
It was also noticed that pension was not the only benefit of the scheme but there was provision for preference in employment, allotment of accommodation, admission to schools and colleges to their kith and kin etc.
It was held that the scheme should be applicable from the date of application.
It was also observed that it was not possible for the Court to scrutinise the documents and pronounce upon their genuineness which was the function of the Government.
In Union of India v. RT Adavariou, 2000(4) SCT 329 (Guj.)
: A.I.R. 1997 SC 2069, the Hon'ble Supreme Court held that High Court was not justified in directing grant of pension under the scheme by appreciating the evidence produced in the writ petition.
In Gurdial Singh v. Union of India and others, (2001)8 S.C.C.
8, it was held that having regard to the fact that writ petition was filed in the year 1996 (23 years after the scheme commenced), benefit of pension should be given from the date of filing of the writ petition.
9.
In view of the above law on the point, particularly the decisions of the Apex Court in Mukund Lal and Gurdial Singh (supra), it cannot be held that the benefit of the scheme has to be given from the date of commencement thereof.
View taken in the judgment of this Court (Annexure P-8) stands superseded by the view taken by the Apex Court.
Moreover, in the judgment of this Court, facts were different as it was undisputed that the petitioner therein had been in imprisonment for more than seven months.
10.
Pension having already been granted in the case of Balbir Singh (C.W.P.
No. 792 of 1996); no further direction is called for and the writ petition is dismissed.
11.
Pension having been declined in Piara Singh's case (C.W.P.
No. 5980 of 1996) and matter being of appreciation of evidence, no direction for grant of pension can be issued.
Petition is dismissed.
If the petitioner has any further proof which may be relevant, he will be free to approach the concerned authorities.
Petition dismissed.
