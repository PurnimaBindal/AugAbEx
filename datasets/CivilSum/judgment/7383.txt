Adarsh Kumar Goel, J - The respondent filed a suit for declaration that he was entitled to pension equal to the employees, who retired on or after 3131985 and was also entitled to restoration of commuted portion of pension after completing 181 months or 70 years of age, whichever is earlier.
2.
The case of the plaintiff is that he retired on 3151982 from the Agriculture Department of the Punjab Government.
He was granted pension and also commuted value of the pension, which according to the entry in the pension book was to be recovered in 181 months or on attaining the age of 70 years, whichever is later.
According to the plaintiff, the commuted pension ought to have been restored in 181 months or on attaining the age of 70 years, whichever is earlier.
3.
The suit was contested.
Reference was made to Rule 115 of CSR Volume II and table given in Annexure to the said Rule.
4.
The trial Court decreed the suit.
It was held that in view of judgment of the Apex Court in DS Nakara and others v. Union of India, 1983(1) All India Services Law Journal 130, an employee, who retired before 3131985, was to be treated at par with the employees retiring after 3131985.
It was further held that since full commutation of pension was to be recovered in 181 months, the State had no right not to restore the full pension after commutation had been fully recovered.
5.
Hence this appeal.
6.
Counsel for the appellants refers to decisions of the Apex Court in State of Punjab and others v. Boota Singh and another, 2000(3) Supreme Court Cases 733 and State of Punjab and another v. JL Gupta and others, 2000(2) SCT 8 (SC) : 2000(3) Supreme Court Cases 736, wherein it was held that an employee retiring prior to 3131985 could not claim the benefit, which was extended to employees retiring subsequently, as the new benefit sought to be given to the employees retiring subsequently was not retrospective.
Following the said judgments, on 732001 this Court stayed execution of the decree.
7.
Counsel for the respondent is unable to distinguish the judgments of the Apex Court in Boota Singh's case (supra) and JL Gupta's case (supra).
Accordingly, following the judgments of the Apex Court, the impugned decree with regard to the finding that the respondent will be entitled to have parity with the employees retiring after 3131985 is set aside.
8.
Counsel for the State also questioned the decree to the extent that the commuted pension is to be restored after 181 months on the ground that in instructions Ex. DW2/A dated 22101993, the word `later' is mentioned, but he is unable to show any rule which would justify this interpretation nor any rationale for the same.
9.
Counsel for the plaintiff also refers to judgment of the Apex Court in Welfare Association of Absorbed Central Government Employees in Public Enterprises and others v. Union of India and another, 1996(2) SCT 788 (SC) : AIR 1996 Supreme Court 1201, wherein it was held that after 15 years, commuted value of the pension has to be restored.
10.
The view taken by the Courts below that since the commuted pension has already been recovered in 181 months, the same was to be restored cannot be held to be in any way erroneous.
Accordingly, this part of the decree is up- held.
The appeal is partly allowed accordingly.
Appeal partly allowed.
