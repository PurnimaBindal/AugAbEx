MM Kumar, J - This petition filed under Article 226 of the Constitution prays for issuance of a writ of mandamus directing the respondents to regularise the services of the petitioner as per policy instructions dated 2751993 which are issued by the State Government (Annexure P-2).
A further prayer has also been made for quashing order dated 30.9.1994 (Annexure P-7) declining the representation made by the petitioner in pursuance to the directions issued by this Court in Civil Writ Petition No. 3614 of 1994, decided on 891994.
2.
Brief facts of the case are that the petitioner has been employed by the Superintending Engineer, P.W.D.
(B&R) Department, Ambala Circle since April, 1988.
He has been continuously working on daily wages with the respondent- department at the Sub-Division, Ladwa in the Maintenance Cell for maintaining the roads and buildings.
The details of service rendered by him has been furnished in the form of a chart which reads as under :-  Period Days April, 1988 to March, 1989 271 April, 1989 to March, 1990 279 April, 1990 to March, 1991 308 April, 1991 to March, 1992 268 April, 1992 to March, 1993 331 3.
The aforementioned chart shows that the petitioner has worked for 240 days in every completed calendar year, continuously for five years.
These details have not been disputed.
He filed Civil Writ Petition No. 3614 of 1994 which came up for hearing before a Division Bench of this Court.
The Division Bench disposed of the writ petition on 891994 by making the following observations :-  "Learned Asstt.
AG Haryana is directed to impress upon the Govt. that looking to the length of service of the petitioner and his period of working, the Govt. may consider to relaxation in the requirement of physical working on 3131993 as a condition for regularisation of service."
4.
Thereafter, the petitioner sent a legal notice through his counsel (Annexure P-5) which has been rejected by the impugned order dated 30.9.1994.
It has also been pointed out by learned counsel for the petitioner that services of the petitioner have been regularised w.e.f.
3092003.
A copy of the order has been placed on record which is Mark `A'.
5.
The averments made in paragraphs 10, 11 and 16 of the writ petition reveal that the petitioner was not offered work on 3131993 on which date the petitioner was required to have completed five years of service and was to be in service in accordance with the instructions dated 2751993 (Annexure P-2).
It has further been pointed out that the respondents have intentionally deprived the petitioner from working on that date so as to snatch the benefit of instructions dated 2791993 providing for regularisation.
In this regard reliance has been placed by the petitioner on instructions dated 17121992 (Annexure P-1).
According to the directions issued by the respondent-State services of all adhoc employees were to be terminated if their services were not required.
6.
In the written statement, the stand taken by the respondent-State is that as per instructions dated 2751993, the petitioner is required to complete five years of continuous service on 3131993 and he should have been in service on that date.
It has further been assented that the break should not be more than 30 days.
It has also been alleged that the petitioner left the job at his own and was working at the Co-operative Bank, Ladwa for about six months.
7.
Mr. Pritam Saini, learned counsel for the petitioner has argued that once the petitioner was not offered employment in the month of March, 1993, he has to get employment in order to make both ends meet.
According to the learned counsel, the fault committed by the respondents of not offering employment to the petitioner cannot result into any prejudice to the petitioner.
The learned counsel has relied upon instructions dated 17.12.1993 and argued that the Superintending Engineer-respondent No. 2 did not have any option except to terminate the services of a daily wager.
The learned counsel has placed reliance on a Full Bench judgment of this Court in the case of Tek Chand v. State of Haryana, 2002(1) SCT 308 (P&H), to argue that if for no fault of the petitioner he has not been employed on the date specified in the instructions, then the benefit of regularisation cannot be denied to him on that score.
The learned counsel has further argued that irrespective of the instructions, the petitioner is entitled to be regularised as per the law laid down by this Court in the cases of Ram Dhari, Keeper Mini Zoo (sic) Lake, Rohtak v. The Chief Wild Life Warder, Haryana and anr., 1996(1) SCT 615 (P&H) : 1996(1) RSJ 245; Giri Raj and others v. State of Haryana, 1997(2) RSJ 506 and Dharam Singh v. State of Haryana, 2003(1) SCT 946 (P&H) : 2003(1) RSJ 763.
The learned counsel has maintained that on the basis of aforementioned law, the cut off date of 3131993 is not sacrosanct so as to make it binding on the petitioner to be present on the job on that date specially when no fault of the petitioner can be found.
8.
Mr. SS Brar, learned State counsel has referred to the averments made in paragraph 7 of the written statement of reiterate his stand that the petitioner was absent of his own volition and not present on 3131993.
Therefore, he was not considered for regularisation.
He also pointed out that there are breaks in service of the petitioner which are not condonable.
9.
After hearing learned counsel for the parties, perusing the policy instructions dated 2751993 (Annexure P-2) and the observations made by the Full Bench of this Court in Tek Chand's case (supra), I am of the considered opinion that this petition deserves to be allowed.
If on 3131993 the petitioner was not employed by the respondents, then no fault of the petitioner could be found.
It is not acceptable that he would leave the job where he was working for the last five years and was likely to get the benefit of regularisation.
The ratio of the judgment in Tek Chand's case (supra) fully applies and the petitioner is covered by the policy instructions dated 2751993 (Annexure P-2).
Moreover, terminating service of an employee when he was approaching to complete five years of service in pursuance to instructions dated 17121992 (Annexure P-1) would be extremely unfair and would result into violation of Articles 14 and 16(1) of the Constitution.
Therefore, the cause of the petitioner is meritorious.
10.
For the reasons recorded above the instant petition is allowed and the order Annexure P-7 is quashed.
The respondents are directed to consider the petitioner for regularisation of his services on the post of Beldar w.e.f.
3131993 and grant him all the consequential benefits.
The needful shall be done within four months from the date of a copy of this order is received by the respondents.
However, it is made clear that the petitioner would not be entitled to any benefit for the period after 3092003 in view of is regularisation w.e.f.
3092003 vide order Mark `A'.
Petition allowed.
