GL Raina, J - The petitioner while posted as Block Medical Officer, Soibugh, got vide the Director Health Services' order communicated under No. Est-3/1-133/2955-64 dated 3.11.1998 transferred and attached with Chief Medical Officer, Baramulla.
Respondent, Doctor Bashir Ahmed Ahangar came vide the said order posted as Block Medical Officer, Soibugh.
2.
This transfer order having the effect of attaching the petitioner to the office of CMO, Baramulla is labelled as unreasoned and without jurisdiction.
Furthermore, the competence/jurisdiction of the Director Health Services to order the petitioner's transfer is challenged on the ground that the petitioner being a gazetted officer had no jurisdiction or authority to order his transfer and the order is thus illegal on account of want of jurisdiction.
3.
The impugned order is assailed on yet another ground namely that is unconstitutional and violative of the petitioner's legal rights as the order has the effect of attaching the petitioner, without intimating the post against which he is to be adjusted.
The attachment is said to be the outcome of the malice as in the background in which the petitioner was divested of drawing and disbursing powers and had been dubbed as a misappropriator.
The Director's order is thus said to call for judicial intervention.
4.
Without much an ado can safely say that the order cannot be given the label of being motivated by malice or extraneous considerations.
Annexure A issued prior in time to the impugned order, indicates that the drawing and disbursing powers of the petitioner were withdrawn by the Director on account of the petitioner having resorted to alleged intentional or unintentional financial irregularities.
It is in this alleged background that the Director attached the petitioner.
It is not open herein to judge the sufficiency or otherwise of the reasons which prompted the Director to shift the petitioner from Soibugh and attach him with the CMO, Baramulla.
The thrust of the petitioner's case is that the Director though being a Major Head of Department lacked jurisdiction/competence to order the petitioner's transfer who is a gazetted officer of the rank and Assistant Surgeon holding the charge of BMO.
It is in this context that attention is invited to entry No. 98 Schedule 1-C of the J. and K. Service Regulations wherein the Government has delegated the powers of transfer and posting to the Major HODs.
This power is exercisable under the schedule by HOD in respect of all officers in the scale of pay the maximum of which did not exceed Rs. 1000/-.
This delegation took effect in terms of Notification No. 62 dated 25th of June, 1966.
It is contended that notwithstanding that the Director Health Services is a major HOC, he could not have ordered the transfer and attachment of the petitioner because the petitioner is on the date of the passing of the impugned order working in the pay scale of Rs. 8000-13500.
The counsel for the petitioner lays stress on the point that the reprint of the CSR issued in 1991 has not been changed qua the extent of delegation of powers of Major HODs in Entry 98, Schedule 1-C, so the Director Health Services could not have ordered the petitioner's transfer as his power to order transfer was limited in respect of officers in the pay scale the maximum of which does not exceed Rs. 1000/-.
5.
The argument sought to be projected in support of the contention can be beat by any stick.
6.
There is perhaps no controversy on the point that before the year 1973 the pay scale of Assistant Surgeon in the Medical Department was Rs. 300-750.
This pay scale got revised in terms of SRO 149 of 1973 to the pay scale of Rs. 540-950.
The entry in Schedule 1-C No. 98 pertains to pay scale of 1966 so the relevance of this entry to limit the extent of the HODs power of transfer is to be read conjointly with the revised pay rules as is directed by Rule 18 of the Revised Pay Rules issued as SRO 149/1973.
7.
Vide SRO 91/1982 the Assistant Surgeon's grade got revised to the scale of Rs. 1000-2000.
Yet again vide SRO 370/1987 the Assistant Surgeon's grade got revised to Rs. 1900-4000.
Vide SRO 75/1992 the grade got revised to Rs. 2400-4000 and vide SRO 18/1998 the scale of pay of Assistant Surgeon/BMO got revised to Rs. 8000-13500.
8.
Vide Rule 23 of the J. and K. Civil Services (Revised Pay) Rules of 1982 (SRO 81) the power of transfer, sanction of leave etc. as stood delegated to the authorities prior to the issuance of this SRO stood correspondingly delegated with the same authorities in corresponding revised scales of pay.
Yet again Rule 21 of SRO 370/1987 provided that the delegation of powers in respect of transfer, sanction of leave etc. shall be exercised in the corresponding revised pay scale by the authorities to whom those powers stand delegated as on the date of issuance of the SRO.
Rule 16 of the J. and K. Civil Service (Revised Pay) Rules, 1992 issued under SRO No. 75 provided that delegation of powers in respect of transfer, sanction of leave, etc. shall be exercised in the corresponding revised scale by the authorities to whom those powers stood already delegated.
9.
SRO 18, nomenclatured as the J. and K. Civil Services (Revised Pay) Rules, 1998, provides under Rule 21 that the delegation of power in respect of transfers, sanction of leave etc. shall be exercised in the corresponding revised scale by the authorities to whom these powers stand delegated.
10.
These enabling Rules in the abovesaid Pay Revisions Rules have to be read conjointly with entry No. 98 Schedule 1-C of the CSR.
Read in their proper perspective the power to transfer all officers who were in the scale of pay the maximum of which did not exceed Rs. 1000/- in the year 1966 were delegated to Major Head of the Departments.
The pay scale maximum of which did not exceed Rs. 1000/- in the year 1966 covered the Assistant Surgeon, Medical Department, as well.
By revision of pay scales from time to time the rules provided for regulation of delegation of powers simultaneous to the revision of pay scales.
There is no second opinion on the point that entry 98 Schedule 1-C of the CSR has to be read along with the revised pay rules issued from time to time, the latest being in the shape of SRO 18 of 1998.
If the Major HOD had the power to transfer the Assistant Surgeon, whose maximum pay in the pay scale was not more than one thousand in the year 1966, the subsequent rules providing for revised pay scales had the provision to regulate the effect of the revision in pay scales on the delegated powers in respect of transfers, sanction of leave etc.
It is in this situation not open to doubt the Director's power/jurisdiction to order the transfer of Assistant Civil Surgeon whose pay scale is not Rs. 8000-13500.
11.
I am of the considered opinion that the challenge thrown to the Director's competence to issue the transfer order of the petitioner is misconceived.
The Director Health Services had, and has at present as well, the power to transfer the Assistant Surgeon/BMOs as such Head of the Department and the power to transfer the Assistant Surgeon has been delegated to him in terms of Entry 98 of Schedule 1-C of the CSR read with the Pay Revision Rules issued from time to time; the latest being SRO 18 in the line.
12.
The petition is misconceived and is at this stage dismissed.
The interim direction passed on 25.11.1998 shall stand vacated.
Petition dismissed.
