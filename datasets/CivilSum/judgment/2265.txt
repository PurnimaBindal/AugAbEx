Ashutosh Mohunta, J - In order to challenge the judgments and decrees passed by both the Courts below, the plaintiff has filed this appeal.
2.
In brief, the facts giving rise to the present appeal are that the plaintiff filed the present suit to seek a declaration that his actual date of birth was 351933 and not 4.9.1932 and that the orders dated 251989 and 20101989 whereby his prayer for correction of the date of birth was rejected by defendant Nos. 1 and 2 in the official record were illegal and arbitrary.
The plaintiff placed reliance on the School Leaving Certificate (Ex. P-9) supplied by the Pakistan Authorities.
The Sub-Judge Ist Class, Chandigarh, after hearing the counsel for the parties and on examining the evidence adduced on record dismissed the suit vide judgment and decree dated 2791990 on the ground of delay and laches as well as on the ground that it was very much doubtful that the certificate (Ex. P-9) relied upon by the plaintiff pertains to him.
The appeal filed by him was also dismissed by the Additional District Judge, Chandigarh, vide judgment and decree dated 1611993 on the ground of delay and laches.
3.
I have heard the learned counsel for the parties and gone through the evidence as well as the case-law cited by them.
4.
The primary contention raised by the learned counsel for the appellant is that the period of limitation should be counted from the date of rejection of the prayer of the appellant by the defendant-authorities and not from the date of knowledge and, accordingly, the Courts below have erred in not granting the declaration sought for by the plaintiff.
In support of his contention Mr. SP Gupta, learned Senior Advocate, appearing on behalf of the appellant, has placed reliance on Raghubir Jha v. State of Bihar and others, AIR 1986 Supreme Court 508; SS Rathore v. State of Madhya Pradesh, AIR 1990 Supreme Court 10.
The counsel further contended that the prayer of the appellant with regard to change in date of birth cannot be rejected merely on the ground of delay.
In support of this contention, the counsel has relied upon Shaukat Ali v. Indian Airlines, 1990(1) SLR 31, which is a Single Bench judgment of the Delhi High Court.
5.
The contentions raised by the learned counsel for the appellant have vehemently been controverted by Mr. Ashish Kapoor, counsel appearing on behalf of respondent No. 1.
6.
The short question that arises for determination before this Court is to the following effect :-  Whether or not the date of birth of the plaintiff can be changed at the end of his service career ?
7.
It is the case of the plaintiff-appellant that he was studying in 9th Class in the Government High School, Kamalia when he migrated from West Pakistan to India at the time of partition of the country.
In that school his date of birth was recorded as 351933, which is also proved from the School Leaving Certificate (Ex. P-9).
However, at the time of his joining the school in India after partition, his father wrongly got recorded his date of birth as 4.9.1932.
The plaintiff passed his Matriculation Examination in the year 1949 from the Panjab University.
In the year 1963 he allegedly came to know about his actual date of birth being 351933 from his uncle and, accordingly, he wrote a letter to Headmaster of Government High School, Kamalia (in Pakistan) on 891963.
Thereafter, he issued various reminders dated 8.7.1968, 1671974, 991981, 941984, 431985 and 8.4.1986 to him for the purpose.
Ultimately, in reply to his last letter, the plaintiff received the School Leaving Certificate (Ex. P-9) recording his date of birth as 351933.
However, a look at the dates on which the plaintiff wrote letters to the Headmaster of the School would show that he wrote first letter on 891963.
Thereafter he gave reminder on 8.7.1968, i.e. after a period of almost five years.
The second reminder was given after a gap of six years, i.e. on 1671974.
The third reminder was given by the plaintiff on 991981, i.e. after seven years of the issuance of the second reminder.
The third reminder was given by him after almost three years thereafter.
It shows the casual approach of the plaintiff in pursuing his case.
In this way, the concerned authorities in Pakistan almost took about 25 years in supplying the certificate (Ex. P-9) to the plaintiff-appellant.
At the fag end of his service career with the Indian Oil Corporation, the plaintiff filed the present suit in the year 1990 for getting his date of birth corrected.
Though in the authorities cited by the learned counsel for the appellant it has been held that the period of limitation ought to be counted from the date of rejection of the prayer and not from the date of knowledge of the plaintiff, yet in the present case, the laxity shown by the appellant while pursuing his case with the Pakistan Authorities for getting the School Certificate cannot easily be digested.
In Union of India and others v. Mrs. Saroj Bala, 1996(3) SCT 669 (SC) : 1997(2) Recent Services Judgments 451, it has been held by their Lordships of the Supreme Court that the application for correction of date of birth after 18 years is unthinkable.
In contrast to it, in the present case the appellant filed the present suit after a period of almost 27 years of his alleged date of knowledge in the year 1963.
He might have joined his service career somewhere in the early fifties as he had passed the Matriculation Certificate in the year 1949.
In this way he took a period of almost 38 or 40 years after joining service to file the present suit for correction of the date of birth.
In Hindustan Lever Ltd v. SM Jadhav, 2001(2) SCT 369 (SC) : 2001(2) Recent Services Judgments 427, it has been held by their Lordships of the Supreme Court that "at the fag end of career a party cannot be allowed to raise a dispute regarding his date of birth."
To the same effect is the judgment of the Supreme Court in Union of India v. Ram Suia Sharma, 1996(7) Supreme Court Cases 421.
In this view of the matter, I am of the considered opinion that after this long gap the appellant cannot be allowed to change his date of birth in order to delay his superannuation from the Indian Oil Corporation.
8.
Apart from the above, it is very much doubtful if the certificate (Ex. P-9) relates to the appellant.
As per his statement, the appellant joined initiated (initially ?)
his studies in the same school and studied there upto 9th Class.
However, in the certificate (Ex. P-9) the date of admission is given as 141947 and the student to whom it pertains was admitted in the 9th Class.
It is not the case of the plaintiff-appellant that he was admitted in the 9th Class in the Government High School, Kamalia.
In view of this serious contradiction, it can be held that the certificate (Ex. P-9) does not relate to the appellant and no reliance can be placed thereon in order to make correction in the date of birth of the plaintiff.
Moreover, both the Courts below have given a concurrent finding of fact, which cannot be interfered in the second appeal.
No substantial question of law arises for determination in the present appeal.
Consequently, there is no merit in this appeal.
It is, accordingly, dismissed.
However, I make no order as to costs.
Appeal dismissed.
