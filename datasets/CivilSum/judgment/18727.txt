RL Anand, J - Shri Subhash Chander Seth, has filed the present Civil Writ Petition under Articles 226/277 of the Constitution of India for the issuance of a writ of cetiorari quashing the impugned show cause notice dated 381995 (Annexure P-6) and he has further prayed that a writ of mandamus be issued against the respondents to release the pension and other retiral benefits with interest at the rate of 18%.
Further it was prayed by the petitioner that the directions be issued to the respondents to grant the benefit of additional five year qualifying service which is permissible to him as per instructions dated 181980 and 231994, Annexures P-5 and P-10 respectively.
2.
The case set up by the petitioner is that he joined as S.D.O.
in P.W.D.
(B&R Branch) on 2031970.
He was promoted as Executive Engineer on 731980.
The petitioner was sent on foreign deputation under UNDP scheme to Somalia and remained there from April, 1987 to June, 1990.
Initially he was on deputation for six months but the deputation period was extended to one year and then further extended to three years.
There was a civil war in Somalia otherwise the period of deputation would have been extended further because of the good work of the petitioner.
In the year 1995 the petitioner had put in about 25 years of service and on account of unavoidable family circumstances, he made a request for a voluntary retirement vide letter dated 12121995 and he further stated in the notice that he was ready to deposit three months salary in lieu of the period of notice.
On 27121995, the petitioner again requested for voluntary retirement and enclosed a Bank draft of Rs. 24,500/- on account of the salary of 2 months 14 days in lieu of three months notice period excluding the period for which notice was given from 12121995 to 27121995.
After submitting a request for the voluntary retirement on 12121995, the respondent No. 2 delivered a letter No. 11698/E.1 dated 14121995 regarding three months notice to retire the petitioner compulsorily.
The notice served by the respondents for compulsorily retirement was illegal, therefore, the petitioner served a legal notice on 2031996 upon the respondents.
3.
In short, the stand of the petitioner is that he wanted to seek voluntary retirement for which he served a notice upon the respondents, therefore, upon the expiry of three months he would be deemed to have been retired from service and by virtue of the voluntary retirement he is entitled to the benefit of service for five years towards the pension.
Also it was prayed by the petitioner in the writ petition that he is entitled to the pension on the revised rate which is not being released to him for the reasons best known to the respondents authorities.
4.
Notice of the writ petition was given to the respondents who filed the reply and denied the allegations.
The stand of the respondents is that the petitioner was not interested in serving the department and that he had been applying for leave in peace meals since 1992 to 1995 and did not care for getting the leave sanctioned from the competent authority.
Therefore, it was decided by the competent authority to retire the petitioner compulsorily from Government service after attaining the age of 50 years by giving three months notice.
The petitioner had attained the age of 50 years on 2441995.
The three months for voluntary retirement of the petitioner was given by the department in the public interest on 1781995.
The said notice was sent to the petitioner through embassy of India in Washington for service.
When the petitioner came to know about the action being taken by the competent authority regarding voluntary retirement he returned to India and submitted a three months notice seeking voluntary retirement vide his letter dated 12121995.
Voluntary retirement has been sought by the petitioner with a mala fide intention to save himself from the action of voluntary retirement as mentioned above.
The petitioner himself deposited the Bank draft of Rs. 24,500/- with a view to avoid the compliance of the notice dated 1781995 issued by the competent authority regarding his voluntary retirement in the public interest.
The record of the petitioner was bad.
He had been earning adverse remarks.
The petitioner earned average reports during the years 1985-86, 1986-87 and 1990-91.
He was also conveyed the adverse remarks for the period 8/90 to 1/91.
The representation made by the petitioner was duly considered and rejected.
The action of the Government for retiring the petitioner compulsorily is legal and correct.
With this broad defence, the respondents prayed for the dismissal of the writ petition.
5.
The petitioner filed a replication in which he reiterated the allegations made in the writ petition by denying those of the written statement.
The matter at one stage was also referred to the Lok Adalat but it could not be settled by the Lok Adalat as is evident from the order dated 22.12.1999.
6.
I have heard Shri Gurdev Singh, learned Counsel, appearing on behalf of the petitioner and Shri Rajbir Sehrawat, learned Counsel appearing on behalf of the respondents and with their assistance have gone through the record of the case.
6.
The first point which survives for determination is whether the petitioner shall be deemed to have been retired voluntarily from service on the expiry of three months of the service of the notice and the next point for determination would be whether the petitioner has been rightfully retired compulsorily from service.
7.
Before I deal with this issue, it will be appropriate for me to refer to the various documents which have been relied upon by the respective parties in support of their case.
8.
Annexure P-1 is the notice dated 12121995 given by the petitioner seeking voluntary retirement with effect from 18121995.
He also made a mention that he is ready to deposit the amount of three months salary in lieu of three months notice.
Annexure P-2 is the letter dated 22.12.1995 written by the petitioner in which it has been prayed by the petitioner that if any amount becomes recoverable that may be deducted from his gratuity or pensionary benefits.
Annexure P-3 is the copy of the letter dated 14121995 issued by the Engineer-in-Chief to the petitioner.
Annexure P-4 is the reminder issued by the petitioner in which he has made a prayer that his case for voluntary retirement may be expedited.
Annexure P-6 is the statement of allegations issued by the department on 381995 and Annexure P-7 is the reply of the petitioner to the charge-sheet.
Annexure P-8 is the order dated 351996 issued by the Government of Haryana vide which the petitioner was retired compulsorily from service in public interest.
9.
In rebuttal, the respondents have placed on record Annexure R-1 orders dated 8.10.1992 vide which the sanction was granted by the Engineer-in-Chief for 195 days leave to the petitioner from 1081992 to 20.2.1993.
Annexure R-2 is the other letter issued by the Engineer-in-Chief to the petitioner vide which the petitioner was intimated that his prayer for extension of leave has been declined and he was advised to report for duty failing which he will be treated as absent from duty Annexure R-3 is the order dated 1781995 vide which the Government of Haryana opined that it is in the public interest to retire the petitioner from service on his attaining the age of 50 years by giving three months notice and it was further declared that on the expiry of three months the petitioner shall be deemed to have been retired compulsorily under Rule 532-A(c) of the Punjab Civil Services Rules Volume I Part-I as applicable to the State of Haryana.
Annexure R-4 is the copy of the order dated 25.3.1992 to show that representation of the petitioner was declined.
Annexure R-5 is the copy of the order dated 25.3.1999 vide which the Government of Haryana had decided to drop the disciplinary proceedings initiated against the petitioner vide memorandum dated 381995.
10.
The learned Counsel for the petitioner submits that his client has completed the qualifying service of 20 years, therefore, he had the right to seek voluntary retirement.
In pursuance of that right given to the Government servant, the petitioner has given the notice dated 12121995 and on the expiry of the three months of the said notice, he would be deemed to have been retired voluntarily.
The action taken by the Government for compulsorily retiring the petitioner is a mala fide affair.
The Government had no jurisdiction to pass the order of compulsorily retirement after the expiry of three months.
At the most it was a case of absence from duty and, therefore, the action on the part of the respondent authorities to retire the petitioner compulsorily is stigmatic, vindictive and should be set aside.
It was also argued by the learned Counsel for the petitioner that his client is not being given the benefit of pension in the revised pay scales and to that extent some directions should also be given to the respondents.
11.
The learned Counsel for the petitioner has also relied upon 2001(2) SCT 339 (SC) : 2001(2) RSJ 87, State of Gujarat v. Umedbhai M Patel ; 1999(1) RSJ 792, Dr. AN Chopra v. State of Haryana and others, 1993(3) SCT 621 (SC) : AIR 1990 SC 1368, Ram Ekbal Sharma v. State of Bihar, and another.
These cited judgments are not helpful to the petitioner.
In these very judgments, it has been held that when the services of a public servant are no longer useful to the general administration, the official can be compulsorily retired for the sake of public interest.
The public interest has not been defined in Civil Services Rules.
It will be a question of fact depending on facts to facts of each case.
The petitioner was not useful to the department.
He had been remaining absent from duty.
The department thought that it is a liability on the exchequer.
It has not been shown that the order has been passed in mala fide circumstances.
Therefore, I do not see any illegality in the order of compulsorily retirement of the petitioner.
12.
On the contrary, learned Counsel for the respondents submitted that the letter was written by the petitioner on 12121995 and before that the respondent authorities had already started the action against the petitioner for compulsorily retirement, on 1781995, as is evident from Annexure R-3.
The service record of the petitioner was taken note of.
The petitioner evaded the service of the notice and tried to become clever by issuing notice dated 12121995.
Prior to that, the departmental inquiry was pending against the petitioner.
The Government has absolute powers in public interest to retire a public servant compulsorily.
It is not a stigmatic order.
No penal consequences are attracted when a public servant is retired compulsorily and, therefore, the writ petition should be dismissed.
13.
After considering the rival contentions of the parties, I am of the opinion that the notice Annexure P-1 dated 12121995, issued by the petitioner, is in fact has been issued by him in order to forestall the action of the Government.
The Government had already taken a decision on 1781995 that it is in the public interest to retire the petitioner from service on his attaining the age of 50 years.
The petitioner evaded the service of this notice and instead issued notice dated 12121995.
When the action of the Government was prior in time, the petitioner cannot be deemed to have been retired voluntarily on 18121995 or on the expiry of three months from 12121995.
The final order was passed by the Government on 351996.
There would have been some merit in the submission of the counsel for the petitioner had the Government issued the notice subsequent to 12121995 but here the things are to the contrary.
The notice was issued on 1781995, about 4 months prior to 12121995.
It has not been shown on the record that the order of compulsorily retirement is a mala fide or arbitrary in nature.
Therefore, I am not inclined to accept the first contention raised by the learned Counsel for the petitioner.
14.
With regard to the second contention raised by the learned Counsel for the petitioner that his client is not being paid the pension after taking into consideration the revised pay scales, I give directions to the respondents that in case the petitioner is entitled to the revised pension on the basis of the revised pay scales, this aspect of the case shall be examined and finally disposed of by the respondents authorities within six months from the receipt of the copy of this judgment.
15.
So far as the main prayer of the petitioner that the order of compulsorily retirement should be set aside, is concerned, it has no merit.
The writ is dismissed with the above observations.
Petition dismissed.
