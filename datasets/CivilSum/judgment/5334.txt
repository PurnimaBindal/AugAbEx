Swatanter Kumar, J - Learned counsel for the petitioner, while relying the Division Bench judgments of this Court in the cases of Om Parkash, Postman v. The Director Postal Services (Posts and telegraphs Department), Punjab Circle, Ambala, and others, 1971(1) S.L.R.
648 and Rajinder Singh v. Board of School Education Haryana and another, 1996(4) R.S.J.
417, to contend that the impugned order of dismissal from service passed by the respondents being retrospective in its nature is vitiated in law and, as such, is liable to be set aside.
2.
Before we comment upon the merits of the case, reference to basic facts would be necessary.
3.
Petitioner was employed as a constable under the Punjab Police with Constable No. 545/KPT on 23rd November, 1989.
According to the petitioner, he was falsely implicated in F.I.R. No. 93 dated 15101994 lodged under sections 302, 307, 324, 148, 149 IPC and Sections 25, 54, 59 of the Arms Act, at Police Station Jhabhal District Amritsar.
4.
The petitioner was convicted and was awarded sentence.
Vide judgment and order dated 24101996.
The appeal against the said judgment preferred by the petitioner is pending in this court and is admitted for regular hearing.
On 681998, an order was passed by the Senior Superintendent of Police, Kapurthala that in view of the Punjab Police Rules (hereinafter referred to as rule 162), the petitioner is ordered to be dismissed from service retrospectively.
Copy of the said order has been annexed to the petition as Annexure P/1.
Though the order has been passed on 681998, but the services of the petitioner has been dismissed with effect from 24101996.
Compelled by these circumstances, the petitioner has filed the present writ petition.
5.
Upon notice, the respondents filed a reply in which it is stated that the petitioner was convicted under section 302 IPC and sentenced to life imprisonment.
It was admitted that his appeal against conviction is pending in the High Court.
According to the respondents, the police is a disciplinary force and the petitioner absented frequently from duty indulging in undesirable activities.
Earlier also, on 4111992, the petitioner was discharged from service for remaining absent from duty.
However, at that point of time sympathetic view was taken and reinstated by the DIG, Jalandhar, who is appellate authority.
He still did not mend his ways.
Thus, the competent authority exercising its power under section 162 has dismissed the petitioner from service.
It was not disputed that the order was passed, which is retrospective in its operation.
6.
At this stage, it would be appropriate to refer to the impugned order :-  "ORDER Constable Satinder Pal Singh No. 525/KPT has been placed under suspension with effect from 71094 for absenting himself form duty without leave or permission and again w.e.f.

Now the Addl.

7 years RI and fine Rs. 1,000/- U/s 307 IPC and in default further RI 1- 1/2 months.
1-1/2 years RI U/s 324/149 IPC.
3 years RI U/s 326/149 IPC and fine Rs. 200/- and 6 months RI U/s 323/149 IPC all these sentences imposed to the convict under various sections of IPC shall run concurrently.

7.
It is clear from the impugned order that the same has been made effective from the previous date i.e. 24101996.
Thus, the impugned order suffers from a legal infirmity apparent on the face of it.
An order of dismissal becomes effective only from the date of its communication to the delinquent official and not even from the date when it is passed by the disciplinary authority, much less from retrospective effect.
The Hon'ble Supreme Court in the case of State of Punjab v. Amar Singh Harika, A.I.R. 1966 Supreme Court 1313 held as under :-  "The mere passing of an order of dismissal is not effective unless it is published and communicated to the officer concerned.
An order of dismissal passed by an appropriate authority and kept on its file without communicating it to the officer concerned or otherwise publishing it does not take effect as from the date on which the order is actually written out by the said authority; such an order can only be effective after it is communicated to the officer concerned or is otherwise published."
8.
The above principle was reiterated with approval again by the Hon'ble Apex Court in the case of State of Punjab v. Khemi Ram, A.I.R. 1970 Supreme Court 214 and the court further held that the order is effective from the date of communication but the date of receipt of the order by the delinquent official is not material  9.
In view of the above settled proposition of law, a Division Bench of this Hon'ble Court in Rajinder Singh's case (supra) held that order of dismissal cannot be passed with retrospective effect.
We are in respectful agreement with the view expressed by the Division Bench of this court in Rajinder Singh's case (supra) and have no hesitation in holding that the order of dismissal cannot be made operative retrospectively.
The relationship of master and servant can be brought to an end only from the date of the order or from a subsequent date thereto.
Besides that, the petitioner would be treated as member of the force on the rolls of the police till the date of dismissal.
He would also gain certain benefits in terms of service and consequential relief.
To take away such right with retrospective effect is not permissible in law.
10.
We have no sympathy with the petitioner, keeping in view his conduct and the judgment of conviction passed by the competent court against him for the offence under section 302 IPC.
His conduct in service is certainly not worthy of any appreciation.
The appellate authority had taken a liberal view earlier and had reinstated the petitioner, of course, to create worse situation against him.
The authority rightly exercised the power under Rule 162 of the Rules, which empowers the competent authority to dismiss an enrolled police official who is convicted and sentenced imprisonment on a criminal charge.
It is an admitted case before us that the judgment of conviction and order of sentence has not been stayed in appeal.
In other words, the conviction and sentence against the petitioner stands and thus, within the purview and scope of rule 162, there is no error on the part of the respondents in passing the impugned order dated 681998.
The only error which can be traced in the impugned order is that it is retrospective in nature, whereas the respondents could terminate the service of the petitioner with effect from 681998.
It is the fault of respondents, who did not pass the order of dismissal on 24101996 or any other subsequent date before 681998, which they were duly empowered and authorised in terms of the above rule.
11.
For the reasons afore-stated, we allow this writ petition only limited to the extent that the order of dismissal from service shall take effect from 681998 the date on which the impugned order was passed.
the petitioner would be entitled to consequential relief till that date in accordance with the rules.
12.
Writ Petition is allowed limited to the extent afore-noticed.
However, without any order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
