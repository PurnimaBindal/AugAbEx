V K Bali, J - This order will dispose of Civil Writ Petition No. 2696 of 1990 and Civil Writ Petition No. 4463 of 1990 as identical question of fact and law are involved in both the petitions.
Facts have, however, been extracted from Civil Writ Petition No. 2696 of 1990.
2.
Petitioners Avtar Singh and others who were all Managers in New Bank of India at various places seek directions to be issued to the respondents to call them for interview for filling the posts of Middle Management Grade Scale III as they are senior to the persons who have been so called for interview.
The facts on which the relief aforesaid has been asked for reveal that all the petitioners are working in New Bank of India at various places located in the States of Punjab, Haryana and Chandigarh.
Respondent No. 1 is a nationalised Bank wholly owned by the Government of India and in its present form it came into existence on the promulgation of the Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertaking) Act, 1980.
By virtue of provisions contained in Section 4, the management and control of then existing Bank ie New Bank of India Ltd was transferred and was vested in the newly created New Bank of India.

3.
The petitioners were, however, in the last category namely officers (Scale Rs. 400-1100) which was for Managers and it was revised to Rs. 700-1800 for Accountants.
As per table reproduced above, the category of officers was in the pay scale of Rs. 400-1000 for Managers which was revised to Rs. 700-1800/- for Accountants.
The category of officers consisted of Managers and Accountants.
The Managers were equated to the higher posts and in fact they were promoted from the posts of Accountants.
A person who was considered fit for promotion was placed above the Accountant and while doing so he was given certain perks.
The Bank also provided them a vehicle and extra allowance for petrol as also allowance for the post.
The confidential reports of the Accountants were also recorded by the Managers.
4.
On the aforesaid array of facts, it is pleaded that the Managers were placed higher than the Accountants.
With a view to substantiate the aforesaid assertion, it is also pleaded that the orders of appointment always referred to promotion of person from the post of Accountant.
One copy of such order has been placed on the records as Annexure P1.
Petitioner Mr. K N Agarwal is stated to have been confirmed as Manager after about eight months of passing of orders of his promotion which is Annexure P1.
Earlier to the nationalisation of the Banks, it had endeavoured to revise the pay scales of the officers.
So far as the Accountants are concerned, they were lowest in the hierarchy of their pay scales and their pay scales were revised.
This was on account of an agreement said to have been arrived at between the management and the officers.
The aforesaid revision of pay scales of Accountants came into existence with effect from 171979.
The case of the officers for higher pay scales had not been finally decided when the Bank came to the nationalised.
It was, however on 111983 that the Bank decided to give effect to the recommendations of the Pillai Committee with regard to the revision in pay scales of the Managers which was to be effective from 1541980.
As referred to above, the Accountants' scales were revised with effect from 171989.
Immediately, on nationalisation, however, the petitioners, who were working as Managers and their juniors, who were working as Managers and were put in the same grade, which was categorised as Junior Management Grade Scale.
However, on 121983, some of the Accountants and some of the Managers were brought in the Middle Management Grade Scale II.
This was done because some of the Managers happened to be working in the branches at a time when at a particular date the deposit in the Bank happened to exceed Rs. 50 lacs or above.
Similarly, some of the officers, working in the administrative office as Managers even though junior to the petitioners, were also placed in the Middle Management Grade Scale II.
Some of the Accountants, who were working in the office, were also put in the higher scale, i.e. Middle Management Grade Scale II.
The petitioners, who were working as confirmed Managers on 1541980 and continued to work so and even though they were always senior to the Accountants, were kept in the lower grade, i.e.
Junior Management Grade Scale I The Board of the Bank framed regulations known as 'the New Bank of India (Officers) Service Regulations, 1982' (hereinafter to be referred as the Regulations).
The same came into effect on 111983.
In view of Regulation 17 promotions to all grades of officers in the Bank were to be made in accordance with the policy made by the Board from time to time having regard to the guidelines of the Government of India, if any.
Under the powers vested in the Bank of virtue of Regulation 17, the Bank framed statutory policy known as 'New Bank of India (Officers) Promotion Policy, 1983.
Regulation 17 reads as follows :-  "17(1) Promotion to all grades of officers in the Bank shall be made in accordance with the policy laid down by the Board from time to time having regard to the guidelines of the Government, if any.
(2) For the avoidance of doubts, it is clarified that this regulation shall also apply to promotions of any category of employees to the Junior Management Grade."
5.
Regulation 18(1) provides that each year Bank would frame a list of officers in its service showing their names in the order of seniority on all India basis and copy of such list shall be kept in every branch or office of the Bank.
Regulation 18(5) further provides that seniority amongst the officers inter se as existing immediately prior to the appointed date shall not be affected.
Regulation 8(4) which is with regard to fitment in the new scale reads as follows :-  "8(4) Where in the course of aforesaid scheme of fitment, officers have to be fitted in two different scales depending on whether they are located in the Head Office or in the field or metropolitan areas or other areas; the mere fact that on the appointed date they happen to be posted at a particular place or office shall not by itself entitle them to a fitment in a particular grade and the Bank may take suitable changes in the placement so as to fit them in an appropriate grade, having due regard to their inter se seniority."
6.
The case of the petitioners is that no rational policy, as was required, was ever made and there was no proper method adopted for fitment and the respondents started fitment of the persons in the new pay scales only by pick and choose.
Since there was discrimination, it led to resentment resulting into a writ petition filed by one of the aggrieved persons in the Andhra Pradesh High Court.
The writ petition, which came up before the said Court, was allowed and it was held that the mere fact that an officer happened to work on a particular date at a particular branch should not determine his status while processing the fitment.
It was accordingly held that irrespective of the category of the Branch the effect had to be given to Regulation 8(4) and the criterion to be adopted was to see inter se seniority as also length of service.
The aforesaid petition was filed by one Sainathan, who was given the benefit and his name now figures in the seniority list of the officers Scale Grade-II at Sr. No. 157 as on 31.12.1986.
The petitioners on the aforesaid facts plead that inasmuch as they were working as Managers before 1541980 before regulation of the promotion policy was put into effect and they were selected to this higher post from amongst Accountants after going through the process of interview, even though their old pay scales were equivalent to the pay scale of the Accountants prior to 171979 but inasmuch they were getting additional perks and allowances and were discharging duties of higher responsibility, including controlling of Accountants, their further promotion in the Middle Management Grade Scale-II ought to have been automatic.
They were, however, grouped with Accountants and placed below them resulting into their juniors scoring over them in all regards.
The petitioners further plead that even as Accountant, they are senior to many of their colleagues, who have been fitted in Scale-II as on 121983 without following any procedure whatsoever.
With a view to strengthen the last plea, it is made out by them that petitioner No. 1 was appointed direct as Manager in pay scale of Rs. 400-1110 on 22101973, but his name does not figure in the list of officers of Middle Management Grade Scale-II as on 31121984.
Petitioner No. 2 joined as Accountant on 28.5.1977 and he was thereafter promoted as Manager on 921978.
Petitioner No. 3 has also same service particulars.
However, one Chanan Gupta who had joined the Bank as Accountant on 12.7.1976 was shown in the seniority list of Scale-I as on 111984 at Sr. No. 342 and petitioner No. 1 in the said seniority list has been shown at Sr. No. 155.
The case of petitioner No. 1 and Chanan Gupta aforesaid for promotion to Scale-II was considered and later was rejected in the interview, which was held in December, 1984.
Petitioner No. 1 was, however, promoted in Middle Management Grade Scale-II w.e.f.
1531985.
Without considering the matter, the respondent-Bank, however, promoted Chanan Gupta on 7.4.1989 retrospectively w.e.f.
1.7.1983 in Middle Management Grade Scale-II.
Similarly, officers in seniority list of Scale-I at Sr. No. 322, D P Gandotra, Sr. No. 868 C C Thomas, and Sr. No. 946 R Chandershekhran, who were Accountants on 161976, 21.1.1979 and 21.9.1979, respectively, and remained as such, had been promoted retrospectively to the Middle Management Grade Scale-II by the order passed in February, 1990.
The petitioners made a number of representations, reference of which has been given in paragraph 31 of the petition, the last being in February, 1990, but when no tangible result came forth, they filed the present petition seeking directions to be issued to the respondents to call them to fill up the Middle Management Grade-Scale-II posts on account of their seniority to the persons, who have been called for interview as meanwhile vide a circular dated 19.6.1990 the Bank wanted to fill up by promotion 100 posts of Scale-III, wherein it was laid down that length of service in Scale-II would be five years and cut-off date for experience would be 31121991.
7.
Cause of petitioner has been opposed and in the reply that has been filed by way of an affidavit of Shri Y P Sehgal, AGM of the Bank, at Regional Office, Chandigarh, by way of preliminary objections, it is pleaded that the question agitated now stands concluded against the petitioners by virtue of a Division Bench judgment of this Court in Letters Patent Appeal No. 310 of 1988, New Bank of India v. K B Sharma, decided on 2331990.
It is further made out that the petition suffers from laches and is liable to be dismissed on that account alone.
The Officers Service Regulation, 1982, were brought into force w.e.f.
111983 and the categorisation and fitment of the officers were made in accordance with the said regulations as on 31121982.
Before doing so, the petitioners were called upon to give their option as per the prescribed proforma, which the petitioners submitted to the Bank.
The petitioners were accordingly placed in Junior Management Grade Scale-I.
Thereafter seniority list as on 111984 for the Junior Management Grade Scale-I was drawn and issued.
On that basis the promotion process from Junior Management Grade Scale-I to Middle Management Grade Scale-II was carried out and the promotions were made in March, 1985.
Petitioner No. 1, who came under the zone of consideration, was actually promoted in Middle Management Grade Scale-II.
The petitioners challenged the said categorisation after a lapse of about eight years and for this precise reason, it is made out that the petition is belated and deserves to be dismissed.
It is further pleaded that petitioners being covered under the OSR were placed in JGMS-I.
They were called upon to give their options as per the prescribed proforma.
They submitted their options and agreed to be governed by OSR and their fitment as per the said OSR.
In the letter asking for the option, it was mentioned that in terms of Regulation 7 of OSR, they were to be placed in JGMS-I in the pay scale of Rs. 700-1800/- and in case they desired to opt for the fitment under OSR, they might exercise their options within 30 days of the issuance of the said letter.
Petitioners gave their unconditional options as per letter.
After the fitment of the petitioner in JGMS-I along with others who were placed in the scale, seniority list was drawn.
Petitioners never challenged the said seniority list.
They accepted their position not only in JGMS-I but in the seniority list as well.
They never made any representation within 30 days as per Regulation 13 if they had any grievance.
Non-filing of any such appeal, it is pleaded, clearly shows that petitioners accepted their fitment and seniority for which they had even given options and that being so, now they have no right to file any petition at this stage.
Petitioner No. 1, who came under the zone of consideration and was eligible to be considered for promotion to the next higher scale viz Middle Management Grade Scale-II (MMGS-II), was called for interview which was held in the end of 1984 for promotion to MMGS-II.
He participated in the said promotion process and was promoted on the basis of said interview in March, 1985.
Thus, not only that he accepted his fitment in JGMS-I as on January 1, 1983 but on the basis of that fitment be participated in the promotion process for next higher post and accepted the promotion to MMGS-II in March, 1985 without any protest.
Insofar as petitioner Nos. 2 to 4 are concerned, it is pleaded that they were not eligible for consideration to the next higher grade of MMGS-II and thus were not called for the said interviews held in the end of 1984.
The plea regarding non-impleading of officers from Sr. No. 215 onwards, who are stated to be about 250, has also been taken.
Besides raising the preliminary objections aforesaid, some facts have been given in paragraph 4 which, it is stated, have been withheld by the petitioners.
On merits also the case of petitioners has been opposed.
It has been pleaded that as per Regulation 7 of the OSR, petitioners were rightly fitted into JMG/Scale-I.
The plea of petitioners that they were equated to the higher posts and should have been given fitment in MMG/Scale II is stated to be totally misconceived and untenable.
The case of petitioners has been individually dealt with to show that they are not entitled to the relief asked for.
8.
Petitioners have filed replication to the written statement filed by respondents and besides controverting various points as noticed above, it has further been pleaded that petitioners have been subjected to glaring discrimination and given arbitrary treatment by denying them rightful place in the seniority list of MMGS-II.
With a view to highlight the plea aforesaid, it has been pleaded that one Shri S P Bindra was promoted as Manager after the appointed date i.e. January 1, 1983 and was posted at Area Office, Bhopal which is an administrative office.
He joined at Bhopal on March 8, 1983 and was fitted in MM-II w.e.f.
March 8, 1983.
On the other hand petitioner No. 2 was already the Manager since February, 1978 on the appointed date i.e. January 1, 1983 and had joined as such in the Administrative Office, Agra on February 9, 1983 ie about one month before Shri Bindra joined.
Petitioner No. 2 had already handed over the charge of the last branch as on December 31, 1982 ie before the appointed date.
However, he has been denied fitment in MM-II without any legal justification and reasonable excuse.
Similar is the case of Shri Chanan Gupta whose letter for transfer as officiating Manager was issued on December 31, 1982 ie on the eye of appointed date Shri Chanan Gupta was placed in scale II w.e.f.
July, 1983.
Shri Lalit Kumar Dang, it is pleaded, was a Clerk in the Bank on May 28, 1977 when petitioner No. 2 joined as an officer.
Mr. Dang was promoted as an officer on July 9, 1977 whereas petitioner No. 2 was promoted as Manager in February, 1978.
Shri Dang applied for promotion as Manager in 1982 but he was not actually promoted and posted to officiate as Manager at Area Office, Meerut on April 24, 1982.
He was placed in MM-II w.e.f.
February 1, 1983, though he was junior to petitioner No. 2.
It is further pleaded that one R. Rajshekhran, was promoted as an Officer from the clerical cadre in October, 1977 whereas petitioner No. 2 joined as Officer in May, 1977.
Similarly, he was promoted as Manager in June, 1979 and petitioner No. 2 stood already promoted as Manager in February, 1978.
The name of Shri Rajashekharan was not included in the list of MM-II till December, 1989 but on his representation so made, he was given fitment in January, 1990 retrospectively w.e.f.
May 13, 1982.
Example of Ashok Kumar Gupta, who was an officer when petitioner No. 2 was Manager in February, 1978, has also been cited to show discrimination.
Mr. Gupta was promoted as Manager in October, 1982 and posted as such in Staff Training College, Chandigarh whereas petitioner No. 2 was also selected for posting in the staff Training College, Chandigarh in May, 1980 as Manager.
Mr. Gupta was fitted in MM-II on 121983 w.e.f.
October, 1982 whereas petitioner No. 2 was denied the same arbitrarily.
The case of S Sainathan is also stated to be relevant.
Sainathan, who joined as Clerk on July 12, 1973, was promoted as Officer on July 14, 1977.
He applied for promotion as Manager in 1981 and was selected and posted as Officiating Manager on February 3, 1981 at a small branch at Chikadapalli.
He remained there posted upto January 1, 1983 ie the appointed date.
His name was not included in the list of Managers fitted in MM-II on February 1, 1983.
His name was included as JM-I at serial No. 595 whereas name of petitioner No. 2 appeared at serial No. 553 in the first ever published seniority list in 1984.
Mr. Sainathan filed a writ petition in the Andhra Pradesh High Court (C.W.P.
No. 14725 of 1984) claiming that he should be fitted in MM-II w.e.f.
February 3, 1981, the date on which he was promoted as Officiating Manager.
Ultimately, the said petition was allowed.
Matter went upto Supreme Court but the judgment of learned Single Judge was upheld.
Petitioner No. 2 it is stated, was senior to Sainathan as an Officer as also as Manager.
Even with regard to deposit of Rs. fifty lacs it has been pleaded that the concept of achieving deposit of Rs. 50 lacs was not followed by the bank in many cases which too have been detailed in the rejoinder.
9.
Vide order dated May 8, 1992 in Civil Misc.
Application No. 3583 of 1992, petitioner was allowed to file replication subject to all just exceptions.
Copy of the replication was ordered to be given to counsel for the respondents.
This case, after some arguments were addressed, had to be adjourned from time to time and during the course of arguments learned counsel for the petitioners had taken this Court through the pleadings in the replication every time with a view to highlight the discrimination meted out to the petitioners.
At no given stage, whatsoever, but for on the date when the judgment was reserved, learned counsel appearing for the respondent-bank prayed to file reply to this rejoinder.
The prayer being totally belated and at the conclusion of arguments which, as referred to above, spanned or number of dates wherein learned counsel for the petitioners had brought to the notice of the Court the facts mentioned in the replication, was declined.
10.
Mr. Nijjar, learned Senior Advocate, appearing on behalf of petitioners, perhaps realising his difficulty in making out a case on the basis of regulations as also the scheme that came into being after the Bank was nationalised in view of the judgment of Division Bench of this Court in L.P.A. No. 310 of 1988 (New Bank of India v. K B Sharma and Ors.) decided on March 23, 1990, has chosen to confine his arguments only on violation of Articles 14 of the Constitution of India by showing differential treatment meted to the petitioners vis-a-vis their counter-parts who were similarly situate.
It is argrued that Shri S P Bindra was promoted as Manager after the appointed date i.e. January 1, 1983 and was posted at Area Office, Bhopal which is an Administrative Office.
He joined at Bhopal on March 8, 1983 and was fitted in MM-II w.e.f.
March 8, 1983.
Petitioner No. 2 was already the Manager since February, 1978 even on the appointed date and had joined as such in the Administrative Office, Agra on February 9, 1983 ie about one month before Mr.Bindra joined.
Petitioner aforesaid was denied fitment in MM-II without any legal justification and without there being any difference in his case and that of Mr. Bindra.
Similarly, one Chanan Gupta, who was posted as Officiating Manager on December 31, 1982, was placed in Scale MM-II w.e.f.
July, 1983.
Shri Lalit Kumar Dang, who joined the Bank as Clerk on May 28, 1977 when petitioner No. 1 had joined as an Officer, was promoted as an Officer in July 1977.
Petitioner No. 2 was promoted as Manager in February, 1978 and yet Mr. Dang, who applied for promotions as Manager in 1982 but was not actually promoted and posted to officiate as Manager, was placed in MM-II w.e.f.
February, 1983.
It is argued that he was junior to petitioners.
Mr. Rajashekharan was promoted as Officer from the clerical cadre in October, 1977.
Petitioner No. 2 had joined as an Officer in May, 1977.
Mr. Rajashekharan was promoted as Manager in June, 1979 whereas petitioner No. 2 was already promoted on the said post in February, 1978.
The name of the Shri Rajashekharan was not included in the list of MW-II till December, 1989 but on his representation he was given fitment in MW-II January, 1990 retrospectively w.e.f.
May 13, 1982.
Some other cases of similar nature have also been given as have been detailed while dealing with the contents of replication and need no repetition.
There being absolutely no reply to the facts narrated above, this Court is left with no choice but for to record a finding that the respondents had discriminated against the petitioners.
As mentioned above, all the petitioners are senior to those who have been accorded better treatment, even though while giving the facts in the replication comparison has been made only between petitioner No. 2 and the persons mentioned therein.
In the circumstances, even though the policy and guidelines of fitment after rationalisation of the bank might have been upheld by the Division Bench of this Court, the respondents cannot apply the said policy or the guidelines, as the case may be, at random and by method of pick and choose.
The persons, who are equally situate, have to be treated alike and if persons junior to petitioners have been placed in MW-II earlier to the fitment of petitioners, action of the respondents has to be dubbed as arbitrary, thus, violating Article 14 of the Constitution of India.
11.
The plea of respondents that the petition is highly belated and should be dismissed on that count, has no substance for the reason that the persons junior to petitioners were considered and actually promoted to MW-II as late as in 1989-90.
The details of such persons have been given while narrating the facts extracted from the petition as well as replication.
If the case of others, who were junior to petitioners for fitment in MW-II was being considered either by the Bank itself or through the representations filed by the persons concerned upto 1990, then it cannot be said that the petition is belated as the writ petition was filed in 1990 itself.
12.
For the reason stated above, these petitioners are allowed.
A direction is issued to the respondents to consider the case of petitioners for their fitment in MW-II w.e.f.
the date the persons immediate junior to them were placed in the said grade.
In case the petitioners are favourably considered, then, obviously, their case for promotion in Middle Management Grade-III shall also be likewise considered.
Let this exercise be done within a period of two months from today.
Parties are, however, left to bear their own costs.
Petition allowed.
