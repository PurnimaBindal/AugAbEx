GS Singhvi, J - In this petition, the petitioner has prayed for quashing order dated 1842002 (Annexure P-9) passed by the Central Administrative Tribunal, Chandigarh Bench (for short, 'the Tribunal') in OA No. 1101-PB of 2001.
2.
In the Civil Miscellaneous application filed by him during the pendency of the writ petition, the petitioner has prayed for disposal of the writ petition in view of the fact that CWP No. 16865-CAT of 2001 filed by Union of India against orders dated 16.5.2001 and 2.8.2001 passed by the Tribunal in OA No. 116-PB of 2001 and RA No. 79 of 2001 respectively was dismissed by this court on 23.10.2001.
3.
We have heard learned counsel for the parties and perused the record.
4.
The petitioner is a member of Scheduled Caste.
While working as Senior Telecom Operating Officer (TOA) he appeared in the qualifying examination held in September 1997 for the post of Junior Accounts Officer (JAO) and he was declared fail on the ground that he had not secured the minimum marks.
He applied for review of result as per the relaxed parameters, but the concerned authority did not accept his request.
He then filed an application under Section 19 of the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985 for directing the non- applicants to review his result.
The Tribunal dismissed the application by observing that a similar issue had been decided against the applicant in OA No. 148-HP of 2001-Ranjit Singh Berry v. Union of India and others, decided on 13112001.
5.
The petitioner has challenged the order of the Tribunal by contending that the reasons assigned by it for dismissing his application are legally unsustainable.
He has placed reliance on order dated 6102000 passed by the Tribunal in OA No. 513-PB of 1999-Manjit Kaur-II v. Union of India and others dated 14122000 and 23.10.2001 passed by this Court in C.W.P.
No. 17196-CAT of 2000-Union of India and Others v. Central Administrative Tribunal and others and CWP No. 16865-CAT of 2001-Union of India and others v. Central Administrative Tribunal, Chandigarh Bench and another respectively.
6.
Shri DR Sharma, learned counsel for the respondents fairly stated that the writ petition may be allowed because the orders passed by the Tribunal in Manjit Kaur's case (supra) and Paramjit Kaur's case (supra), who too had appeared in the examination and were not given the benefit of relaxed parameters were dismissed by this Court.
7.
In view of the above, the writ petition is allowed.
The impugned order is quashed and the Original Application filed by the petitioner is allowed with the direction that he be given relief in terms of the orders passed by the Tribunal in the cases of Paramjit Kaur and Manjit Kaur.
Petition allowed.
