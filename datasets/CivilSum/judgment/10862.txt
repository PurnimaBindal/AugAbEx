Adarsh Kumar Goel, J - The question involved in this batch of writ petitions bearing CWP Nos. 13491, 13640 and 14881 of 1999 and 8102 of 2001 is whether compassionate appointment can be granted after expiry of a long period of time on the ground that the claimant (i.e. heir of the person who died in harness) was minor at the time of death of the employee concerned and became major after long period.
2.
Since this is a common question to be decided in these writ petitions.
We propose to dispose of these writ petitions by our common judgment.
3.
Facts of CWP No. 11658 of 2001 may first be seen.
The employee in question died on 14.6.1989.
His widow first applied on 3061989 praying that deceased's son be given appointment after he becomes major.
She made another application dated 16101989.
This writ petition has been filed by son of the deceased who claims that his date of birth was 3041982 and he was entitled to compassionate appointment after attaining the age of 17 years for which purpose he represented to the authorities but the authorities refused to appoint him as per decision dated 2682000 (Annexure P-6).
4.
Learned counsel for the petitioner Mr. Suresh Kumar Monga, submitted that in view of para 4 of policy of the Haryana Government dated 3181995 to the effect that if a vacancy was not available, the head of the department concerned will take up the matter with other departments and submit a quarterly report to the Chief Secretary, maintain a seniority list of pending cases and case will be kept alive till the time appointment is offered against a vacancy, the petitioner was entitled to appointment even though he became eligible for appointment on attaining the age of 17 years in the year 1999 i.e. 10 years after the death of the concerned employee.
In support of his submission, he relied upon Rampat v. Haryana State Electricity Board, Ambala and another, 1994(1) SCT 386 (P&H)(DB) : 1994(1) RSJ 96, Jagdeep Kumar v. State of Haryana and others, 1996(1) SCT 731 (P&H)(DB) : 1996(1) RSJ 475 and Daya Kaur v. Haryana State Electricity Board and another, 1996(2) SCT 447 (P&H) : 1996(2) RSJ 179.
Learned counsel fairly submitted that a contrary view was taken by a Division Bench of this Court in Mahipal v. State of Haryana and other, 1999(2) SCT 829 (P&H)(DB) : 1999(2) RSJ 53 but the same was distinguishable for the reason that para 4 in the policy dated 3181995 was not considered.
We find that this Court relied upon various Supreme Court decisions and was very much concerned with the policy dated 851995 read with decision dated 3181995 with which we are concerned in this case.
In para 27 of the said judgment, it was observed that "the said policy could apply only to a person eligible for appointment and cannot be treated as reservation for a minor.
Not only this, this Court also referred to two earlier decisions on interpretation of the very similar policy, namely, Surinder Singh v. State of Haryana, 1996(1) SCT 767 (P&H)(DB) : 1996(2) SLR 230 and Bijinder Singh v. State of Haryana, 1997(1) SCT 756 (P&H)(DB) : 1997(3) RSJ 669.
Further the learned Counsel for the parties have also cited Sanjay Kumar v. State of Bihar and other, 2000(4) SCT 323 (SC) : JT 2000(10) SC 156, Director of Education (Secondary) and another v. Pushpendra Kumar and others, 1998(2) SCT 790 (SC) : JT 1998(4) SC 155, Umesh Kumar Nagpal v. State of Haryana and others, 1994(3) SCT 174 (SC) : 1994(2) SLR 677, State of Haryana and others v. Rani Devi and another, 1996(4) SCT 63 (SC) : 1997(2) RSJ 337, Ram Kumar v. State of Haryana and others, 1997(1) SCT 271 (P&H) : 1997(2) RSJ 517, Indian Bank v. K Usha and another, 1999(1) SCT 869 (SC) : 1998(1) RSJ 509, Pritpal Singh v. State of Haryana and another, 1998(2) RSJ 227, Bijinder Singh v. State of Haryana and another, 1997(3) RSJ 669, Gurdarshan Singh v. State of Haryana and others, 1998(1) RSJ 557, Arvind Kumar Sethi v. State of Haryana, 1997(2) SCT 315 (P&H)(DB) : 1997(2) RSJ 717, State of Rajasthan v. Shri Umrao Singh, 1995(1) SCT 46 (SC) : 1994(6) JT 312, State of Haryana v. Naresh Kumar Bali, 1994(4) JT 184, Mohit Chopra v. Punjab National Bank and others, 1997(4) SCT 507 (P&H)(DB) : 1998(1) RSJ 456, Daljit Kaur v. Life Insurance Corporation of India, 1994(4) SCT 161 (P&H)(DB) : 1995(1) RSJ 568, Balkar Singh v. State of Haryana, 1995(1) SCT 199 (P&H)(DB) : 1995(2) RSJ 249, Surinder Singh v. State of Haryana and others, 1996(2) SLR 230 and Haryana State Electricity Board v. Naresh Tanwar and another, 1996(2) SCT 778 (SC) : 1996(2) JT 542.
5.
In view of the fact that compassionate appointment is an exception to the general requirement of Articles 14 and 16 (making appointment after opportunity to eligible persons) and the object of appointment is to provide immediate relief, we are of the view, following the above case law and in particular DB judgement of this Court in Mahipal's case (supra) that a minor who is not eligible for appointment, cannot claim the right to seek appointment on becoming eligible subsequently.
The dependent or heir of the deceased can be appointed if eligible and if appointment is sought within a reasonable period (the period of 3 months laid down under the policy of the Haryana Government appears to be reasonable).
The person either acquiring eligibility afterwards or not applying, though eligible, within a reasonable time, cannot be given compassionate appointment.
6.
In this view of the matter, we find no merit in this writ petition and the same is dismissed.
7.
In CWP 14881 of 1999, Mr. Rameshwar Malik, learned Counsel for the petitioner argued that he could not be denied appointment on the ground that one of his parents was still alive which bar has been introduced under the policy dated 851995 and was not applicable on the date of death of petitioner's father on 2181995.
We are unable to accept the submission in view of the decision of this Court in Mahipal v. State of Haryana and others, 1999(2) RSJ 53 wherein a similar contention was rejected on the ground that there was no vested right to a compassionate appointment and the policy on the date when appointment is to be made, was to be seen.
The further obstacle in the way of the petitioner is that the petitioner was minor at the relevant time and though the authorities expressed the view that he will be considered after he becomes major, the petitioner can claim no such right.
There can be no question of promissory estoppel or legitimate expectation when denial of right is on the principle of public policy (principles referrable to Article 16).
This writ petition is also liable to be dismissed.
8.
In CWP No. 13491 of 1999, Mr. YP Malik, learned Counsel for the petitioner has raised similar contentions but in the said case also on the date of death of the employee, the petitioner was only 10 years of age.
Similar contentions are raised in CWP No. 13640 of 1999 by Mr. Jagbir Malik, learned Counsel for the petitioner.
In the said case, the employee in question died on 2221994 and the petitioner at that time was minor and after he became eligible, his case was recommended on 6111998.
He also submitted that in view of the assurance given to the petitioner by the authorities, the petitioner was entitled to be given compassionate appointment.
We do not find any distinction from the other cases referred to above.
The said writ petitions are also, therefore, liable to be dismissed.
9.
CWP No. 8102 of 2001 also raises the same issue.
For the reasons already recorded, the said writ petition is also liable to be dismissed.
The above mentioned writ petitions are accordingly dismissed.
10.
Before we part with the judgment, we may notice one more submission made by counsel for the petitioners that though petitioners were, denied appointments, the authorities were making appointments in similar circumstances long after death of concerned employee to heirs of such employee who subsequently become major.
We direct the authorities to ensure that no such appointments are made as may result in discriminatory implementation of the policy as interpreted by this Court and appropriate clarificatory order should be issued by the concerned Secretary to the Government within one month from today.
Petition dismissed.
