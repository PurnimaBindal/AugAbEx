Jawahar Lal, Gupta, J - The petitioner is working as an Assistant Engineer (Electrical) with the Jagraon Co-operative Sugar Mills Limited, Jagraon.
He was appointed as such vide order dated April 15, 1991.
A copy of this order is at Annexure P.1.
This post was in the scale of Rs. 800-40- 1200/50-1400/60-1700.
The petitioner was granted a basic pay of Rs. 1300/- in this scale.
2.
The pay scale of Rs. 800-1700 was revised to Rs. 2000-3500 w.e.f.
April 1, 1991.
His pay in the revised scale was fixed at Rs. 2,700/-.
Actually, the pay should have been fixed at Rs. 2,580/-.
Consequently, the respondent passed an order dated February 5, 1996 regarding the refixation of the petitioner's salary.
A copy of this order is at Annexure P.9 with the writ petition.
The petitioner submitted a representation.
He complained that the order had been passed without giving him any opportunity.
Accordingly, the petitioner was granted an opportunity to defend his case.
Ultimately, vide order dated July, 18, 1996, the petitioner's contention was rejected.
Hence this petition.
The petitioner prays that the impugned action in refixing his pay and ordering recovery of the excess amount paid to him be quashed.
3.
The respondents contest the petitioner's claim.
It has been inter alia averred that the respondent is only a Society registered under the Co-operative Societies Act.
It is not even an instrumentality of the State.
It is not amenable to the writ jurisdiction of this Court.
Reference in support of this contention has been made to not only the decisions of this Court but also to the judgment of the Apex Court in Civil Appeal No. 622 of 1975 (K.L.
Chadda v. State of Punjab etc.) decided on February 7, 1990.
On merits, it has been pointed out that according to the instructions issued by the State Government vide letter dated September 9, 1988, a benefit of 10% increase in pay was admissible to the employees who had been appointed to the service prior to the revision of pay scales.
However, since the petitioner had joined the service after the revision of pay scales w.e.f April 1, 1991, this benefit was not admissible to him.
It had been wrongly granted.
On noticing the mistake, the error was rectified.
The excess amount paid to the petitioner has been ordered to be recovered in instalments.
The allegation that the order is violative of the principles of natural justice has been denied.
It has been pointed out that a notice dated June 3, 1996 had been given to the petitioner.
He was given personal hearing and thereafter the impugned order dated July 18, 1996, a copy of which is at Annexure P.11 with the writ petition, was passed.
The respondent maintains that the action is legal and valid.
4.
Learned counsel for the parties have been heard.
In K.L.
Chadda v. State of Punjab, Civil Appeal No. 622 of 1975, it has been categorically ruled that ``that Sugar Mills is a Co-operative Society registered under the Punjab State Co-operative Societies Act.....The High Court was, therefore right in the light of the decision of this Court in Ajay Hasia (1981(2) SCR 1979) in holding that a writ petition was not maintainable''.
In view of this decision, the writ petition is not maintainable.
Even otherwise, on merits, the petitioner has no legitimate cause for complaint.
It is the admitted position that he was appointed to the service of the Respondent-Society on April 15, 1991 in the pay scale of Rs. 800-1700.
He was given the basic pay of Rs. 1,300/-.
Subsequently, the pay scale was revised and raised to Rs. 2000-3500 w.e.f April 1, 1991.
Learned counsel was repeatedly asked by us as to how he was entitled to the grant of a basic pay of Rs. 2,700/- in the revised pay scale.
He was unable to give any explanation.
Still further, he did not dispute that persons drawing the basic pay of Rs. 1,300/- on April 1, 1991 were to get a pay of Rs. 2,580/- in the revised scale.
Still further, even in the representation submitted by the petitioner, he had not even remotely suggested that his pay had been rightly fixed at Rs. 2,700/- on April 22, 1991 when he had actually joined the service.
In this situation, it is held that the petitioner had been wrongly given a pay of Rs. 2,700/-.
The respondents were not bound to perpetuate the wrong.
They were entitled to rectify the mistake.
They have done so.
It cannot be said that the respondents acted illegally.
5.
Learned counsel for the petitioner placed reliance on the decision of Their Lordships of the Supreme court in Union of India v. M Bhaskar and others, 1996(4) SCT 56 to contend that even if the pay had been wrongly fixed, the petitioner cannot be called upon to refund the excess amount.
6.
The submission is misconceived.
In this case, the higher salary had been paid in pursuance of the judgment of the Central Administrative Tribunal.
There is no such order in favour of the petitioner.
It was also observed by their Lordships that in case of ``Pre-1987 Apprentices in whose favour the judgment had been delivered by any CAT and which had become final earlier because no appeal was carried to this Court or if carried the same was dismissed''.
Furthermore, it was also said that the benefit `` would be available to no other''.
Thus, the higher pay had been paid on account of a binding order.
The case is clearly distinguishable.
Learned counsel had also referred to the decision in Sahib Ram v. State of Haryana, 1955(2) RSJ 139.
In this case the Court was dealing with the validity of the order of relaxation of the qualifications pased by the Principal.
No such question is involved in the present case.
No other point was urged.
In view of the above, there is no merit in this writ petition.
It is, consequently, dismissed.
However, the parties are left to bear their own costs.
