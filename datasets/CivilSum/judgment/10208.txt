MM Kumar, J - The petitioners have approached this Court by invoking the extra ordinary writ jurisdiction under Article 226, with a prayer that the last line in the circular dated October 19, 1993, issued by respondent-Board stipulating non-payment of arrears prior to October 1, 1993, be declared as illegal with a further prayer to issue direction to the respondent-Board to fix the pay of the petitioners in the revised master pay scale, after allowing compensatory increments to them with effect from January 1, 1986.
The petitioners have also prayed for payment of all arrears, allowances, gratuity, leave encashment and all other consequential benefits of revision of pension.
2.
The undisputed facts are that all the petitioners have retired from service between 1987 to 1993.
The respondent-Board revised the pay scale of its employees on June 26, 1989 with effect from January 1, 1986 by issuing a notification Annexure P-1.
On account of the fact that the date of increments of various employees had fallen on different dates, another circular was issued on May 31, 1989 by creating a master scale of Rs. 750-20-950-25-1075- 1 1 25-1200-30-1470-1530-30-1560-40-1640-40-1720-40-1840-40-1920-40-2000-50-2050-2150-50-2250-2350-60-2460-2580.
In other words, additional incremental was offered in the master scale upto the scale of the Rs. 2580.
As the petitioners were in the pay scale of Rs. 3600-4000/-, no benefit of circular Annexure P-3 could be granted to them.
However, on persuasion of the petitioners and other employees, the respondent-Board issued another circular on October 19, 1993 revising the master scale and a new master scale was devised.
In para 2 of the circular Annexure P-4, the scale of Rs. 3600-4000 was also included for the purposes of grant of compensatory increment.
The respondent-Board in accordance with the revised master scale has given the notional benefit of the revised master scale to the petitioners by fixing their pay by adding the benefit of compensatory increment but has refused to pay them the arrears prior to the decision of the respondent-Board taken on October 1, 1993.
Against the afore- mentioned decision taken in para 3 of the circular dated October 19, 1993, the petitioners filed a representation dated April 25, 1994 Annexure P-5.
However, the same did not evoke any favourable response from the respondent-Board, resulting in filing of the present writ petition.
3.
Mr. Gurminder Singh, learned counsel for the petitioners has argued that once the petitioners have been given the benefit of compensatory increment, the other benefit towards arrears of pay etc. has to be granted to all the beneficiaries under circular Annexure P-3 dated May 31, 1989 because there is no rationale to exclude the petitioners from that class of beneficiaries.
According to the learned counsel, the petitioners in the pay scale of Rs. 3600-4000 would constitute a class with those who have been given the compensatory increment under Annexure P-3, dated May 31, 1989.
The learned counsel has further argued that the cut off date can no doubt be fixed by the board but after fixing a cut off date by Annexure P-3, namely dated January 1, 1986, then there is no rational basis to create a further classification amongst the employees of the board.
Therefore, the petitioners are entitled to the benefits of arrears especially when the notional benefits have been granted to them.
4.
Mr. Pankaj Gupta, learned counsel for the board has argued that the right of the board to create a classification by fixing a cut off date, cannot be questioned, especially when the fixation of the date is based on reasons of financial exigencies and host of other factors.
According to the learned counsel, if such view is taken, a large number of employees would become entitled to the financial benefits, which would result into huge financial burden of crores of rupees on the board.
He has referred to the averments made in para 7 of the written statement to argue that keeping in view the financial position of the respondent-Board, the benefit of arrears to the retirees after October 1, 1993 have not been extended.
According to the learned counsel all retirees after October 1, 1993 constitute one class and it cannot be suggested that it is a classification within the class of those employees.
In order to substantiate his argument, the learned counsel has placed reliance on a judgment of the Supreme Court in the case of Union of India v. PN Menon, 1994(2) RSJ 638 and a Division Bench judgment of this court in the case of Bansi Lal and others v. The State of Punjab and another, 1996(3) SCT 331 (P&H) : 1996(3) RSJ 366.
He has also raised an objection that there is two and half years delay in filling the present writ petition.
5.
After hearing learned counsel for the parties, I am of the considered view that this petition deserves to be allowed.
The respondent-Board has conceded in its circular letter dated October 19, 1993, Annexure P-4 that the petitioners were entitled to the grant of compensatory increments in accordance with the new master scale introduced on October 19, 1993.
It has also been accepted that the benefit of compensatory increment would be given on the same terms and conditions as stipulated in circular letter dated May 31, 1989.
Annexure P-3 which covers large number of employees of the respondent-Board except the petitioner and some others who had been working in the pay scale of Rs. 3600-4000/-.
It is thus clear that in this case, no equation of posts or work between two categories employees is required to be determined.
The benefits of arrears arising from the grant of compensatory increment as given by circular letter dated May 31, 1989 wef.
January 1, 1986 has been extended to those employees of the respondent-Board, who are covered by the master scale devised in the letter dated May 31, 1989.
However, the benefit of arrears has been denied to the petitioners merely on the ground that they have been granted the benefit on October 19, 1993 albeit w.e.f 111986.
There is no rational basis to justify denial of arrears of compensatory increments granted to the petitioners vide Annexure P-4 and paying the arrears to some others who were covered by the master scale devised on May 31, 1989.
6.
The object of revision of pay scales from a particular date is always based on the uptrend shown by the price index.
If the price index has shown uptrend, it affects all the employees.
It cannot be said that a particular class of employees is affected by the price index and the other class is not affected.
Therefore, it cannot be accepted that for employees who are covered by circular letter dated May 31, 1989, Annexure P-3 would constitute a class different than the one covered by the terms of circular letter dated October 19, 1993, Annexure P-4.
As a matter of fact, the employees covered by the circular letter dated October 19, 1993 have been given the benefit of compensatory increments after a period of 4 years and they have suffered on that account.
Despite the benefit of compensatory increments having been granted, the arrears have been refused to them.
In Purshottam Lal and others v. Union of India and another, AIR 1973 SC 1088, a Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court has held that a particular category of employees cannot be excluded from the grant of benefit of the pay scales while extending the same benefit to other categories.
Rejecting such an argument, their Lordships of the Supreme Court observed as under :-  "Mr. Dhebar contends that the recommendations of the Pay Commission and while doing so to determine which categories of employees should be taken to have been included in the terms of reference.
We are unable to appreciate this point.
Either the Government had made reference in respect of all Government employees or it has not.
But if it has made a reference in respect of all Government employees and it accepts the recommendations, it is bound to implement the recommendations in respect of all Government employees.
If it does not implement the report regarding some employees only it commits a breach of Arts.
14 and 16 of the Constitution.
This is what the Government has done as far as these petitioners are concerned.
X X X X X X X X  X X X X X X X  In the result, the petition is allowed and it is directed that the revised pay scales of the petitioners will have effect from July 1, 1959, in accordance with the recommendations of the Pay Commission.
We further direct that the petitioners should be paid the amount payable to them as a consequence of the revision of the pay scales with effect from July, 1959.
The petitioners will have the costs of this petition."
7.
In paragraph 7 of its written statement, the respondent-Board has made an attempt to plead financial burden of crores of rupees but it has failed to give any detail as to why the financial burden is going to come on the respondent-Board and how the respondent-Board is not in a position to sustain the same.
On repeated queries made by the Court, learned counsel for the respondent-Board remained unable to explain as to number of employees who are likely to be benefited.
There is nothing on record to substantiate these bald averments made in paragraph 7 of the written statement.
Therefore, I have no hesitation in rejecting the submission of financial burden made by learned counsel for the respondent-Board.
8.
The other argument that there is a delay of 2-1/2 years in filing the writ petition and on that account the petition deserves to be dismissed would not require any serious consideration because the petitioners have gone to Employees Lok Adalat on May 5, 1994 and then a meeting of the respondent-Board was held on March 14, 1995.
The respondent-Board was not able to take any conclusive decision in its meeting and decided to refer the case for legal advice which was given by the Legal Section of the respondent-Board in May, 1995.
The present writ petition was filed on 1631996.
Therefore, even on facts there is hardly any delay in filing the present petition.
Moreover, in matters concerning revision of pay scales, delay ordinarily does not constitute a bar for filing a writ petition because it is a recurring cause of action.
Therefore, the objection with regard to delay and laches is without substance and the same is rejected.
9.
The judgement of the Supreme Court in PN Menon's case (supra) as well as a Division Bench judgment of this Court in Bansi Lal's case (supra), do not advance the case of the respondent-Board because in both the cases, the question of cut off date for granting liberalised retrial benefits was under consideration.
However, no question of that nature has been raised in the present case because the benefits have been given but the arrears have been denied.
Therefore, the aforementioned judgments have no bearing on the facts of the present case.
10.
For the reasons recorded above, the writ petition is allowed.
The respondent-Board is directed to pay the arrears to the petitioners in accordance with Annexure P-4 by fixing their pay w.e.f.
January 1, 1986 with all consequential benefits.
Let the needful be done within a period of four months from the date of receipt of a copy of this order, failing which the petitioners shall be entitled to interest @ 12% per annum from the date, the amount is payable till the date of its actual payment.
Petition allowed.
