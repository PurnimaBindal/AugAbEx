THB Chalapathi, J - The petitioner filed this writ petition invoking the extra-ordinary jurisdiction of this Court to quash the order dismissing him from service by issuing a writ of certiorari under Article 226 of the Constitution of India.
2.
The petitioner was enrolled as a Constable in Haryana Police on 19121973 in District Ambala.
The petitioner was granted leave for four days on 30111989 and he was to report back on 5121989, but he reported for duty on 15121989.
Thus he remained absence for a period of 10 days.
Again he was granted leave on 22121989 and was to report back on 31121990, but he joined duty on 1.1.1990.
Thus he was absent for a day.
He also absented himself for some time on 1911990 and on 28.2.1990.
The petitioner was granted 10 days earned leave on 31.3.1990 and he reported back on 11.4.1990, but he absented himself on 12.4.1990.
In April 1992 he absented himself for 13 days and in May 1992 he absented for 21 days.
3.
The petitioner was served with a chargesheet and enquiry was also held.
In the enquiry, it was found that he was a habitual absentee.
On the basis of the report of the Inquiry Officer, a Show Cause Notice was issued to him on 17.5.1993 to show cause why he should not be dismissed from service to which the petitioner gave a reply stating that his absence from duty prior to July 1992 was condoned and he was sanctioned leave of the kind due and therefore, the absence for the period from 771992 cannot form the basis of an enquiry and he cannot be said to be a habitual absentee.
For his absence for the period from 771992 to 2671992 the petitioner submitted that he was operated for Ulcer and remained in Hospital during that period and he also filed the Certificate during the course of enquiry and the said fact was also admitted by the Inquiry Officer.
Therefore, it cannot be said that he wilfully absented himself from duty during the period from 771992 to 2671992.
Therefore, it is contended that the order passed by the Superintendent of Police dated 1961993 (vide Annexure P-3) dismissing the petitioner from service is liable to be set aside.
The appeal filed by the petitioner against the order of the Superintendent of Police dated 1961993 was also dismissed by the Deputy Inspector General of Police on 1491994 vide Annexure P-5 and the revision petition filed by the petitioner was also dismissed by the Director General of Police, Haryana, on 671995 vide Annexure P-8.
The petitioner filed this writ petition for quashing these orders passed by the Superintendent of Police, Deputy Inspector General of Police and the Director General of Police.
4.
The learned Counsel for the petitioner contended that there cannot be any enquiry in regard to the absence of the petitioner for the period prior to 671992 since the absence stands condoned by his superior officers and the absence has been treated as the leave of the kind due.
So the absence from service prior to 671992 cannot form the basis of an enquiry and for imposing a punishment on the ground that the petitioner was a habitual absentee.
He further submitted that for the period from 771992 to 2671992 he was admitted in the Hospital for undergoing operation for Ulcer and that the Inquiry Officer found, as a matter of fact, that the petitioner underwent operation for Ulcer during that period.
Therefore, the absence of the petitioner cannot be termed as wilful.
Therefore, the order of dismissal passed by the Superintendent of Police on 1961993 (Annexure P-3) and the Consequential orders passed by the Deputy Inspector General of Police dated 1491994 (Annexure P-5) and the Director General of Police dated 671995 (Annexure P-8) are liable to be set aside.
5.
It is, on the other hand, contended by the learned counsel for the respondents that the past record of the petitioner clearly shows that he was a habitual absentee from service.
Therefore, the Punishing Authority can take into account his conduct and impose the punishment on the delinquent official and the Court in exercise of its extra-ordinary jurisdiction will not interfere with the order of the punishment imposed on the delinquent and the writ petition is liable to be dismissed.
6.
There is no dispute of the fact that the petitioner remained absent for few days prior to 671992.
But that absence was condoned and the same was treated as leave of the kind due to the delinquent.
Therefore, the absence from duty prior to 671992 cannot be the subject matter of an inquiry since the absence has already been condoned and treated as leave.
In this view, I am fortified with the decision of this Court in State of Punjab v. Chanan Singh, 1988(2) SLJ 216 and also the decision of Gujarat High Court in Bhursinh Hamsinh Rajput v. State of Gujarat reported in 1982(1) SLR 697 wherein it has been held that once the absence was condoned and the same was treated as leave, the absence which has been condoned cannot form the basis of an inquiry.
Therefore, it cannot be said that the petitioner is a habitual absentee since his absence for the earlier period prior to 671992 had been condoned.
7.
The only thing that remained which can form the subject matter of the inquiry is absence of the petitioner from 771992 to 2671992.
There is no denial of the fact that he remained admitted as an indoor patient in Civil Hospital, Jagadhri from 771992 to 2671992.
When the petitioner absented himself because of his being admitted in Hospital at Jagadhri for undergoing an operation for Ulcer, can it be said that the petitioner absented himself without any reason and whether it amounts to gravest act of misconduct on the part of the petitioner which invites the extreme penalty of dismissal from service.
Rule 162 of the Punjab Police reads as follows :-  "Dismissal shall be awarded only for the gravest acts of misconduct or as cumulative effect continued misconduct proves incorrigibility and complete unfitness for police service.
In making such an award, regard shall be had to the length of service of the offender and his claim to pension."
Thus rule 162 of the Punjab Police Rules envisages a gravest act of misconduct on the part of the delinquent so as to enable the authorities to impose extreme penalty of dismissal from service.
8.
In the present case, the absence from duty cannot be described as wilful or wanton.
He absented from duty because of his admission in the Hospital at Jagadhri for undergoing operation for Ulcer.
This fact has also been admitted by the Inquiry Officer.
9.
This Court in State of Punjab v. Parkash Chand Constable reported in 1992(1) SLR 174 : 1992(1) SCT 123 (P&H) held that "every act of absence does not amount of gravest misconduct."
To arrive at this conclusion the learned Judges relied upon the decision in State of Punjab v. Darshan Singh, 1989(1) SLR 191.
When the absence is on reasonable ground, it cannot be said that it amounts to gravest act of misconduct.
Therefore, the absence from duty from 771992 to 2671992 cannot be said to be a gravest act of misconduct.
Therefore, I am of the opinion that the punishment of dismissal from service is not warranted on the facts of the present case.
10.
Accordingly, I allow the writ petition and quash the orders passed by the Superintendent of Police dated 1961993 (vide Annexure P-3), Deputy Superintendent of Police dated 1491994 (vide Annexure P-5) and the order of Director General of Police dated 671995 (vide Annexure P-8).
The petitioner shall be reinstated in service forthwith and shall be entitled to all consequential benefits.
No order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
