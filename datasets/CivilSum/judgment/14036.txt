Jawahar Lal Gupta, J. (Oral) - The petitioner has approached this Court through the present writ petition primarily with the prayer that a writ of mandamus be issued directing the respondent-authorities to consider him for promotion as Inspector in the Punjab Roadways w.e.f.
September 1, 1952.
A few facts as relevant for the decision of this case may be briefly noticed.
2.
The petitioner had joined services as a conductor on December 1, 1948.
He was promoted as Inspector w.e.f.
October 13, 1959.
It appears that certain persons junior to the petitioner were promoted as Chief Inspectors.
Aggrieved by the action, the petitioner filed CWP No. 4854 of 1971.
This writ petition was allowed vide order dated February 1, 1980.
The order reads as under:  "Petitioner Madan Lal was employed as a Conductor in the Punjab Roadways in 1948 and was promoted as Inspector with effect from 13.10.1979.
His main contention is that he was entitled to be promoted as Inspector in 1957 and then in 1958, when those Conductors who were much junior to him were promoted.
It is the admitted case of the parties that in 1957 respondents No. 6 and 9 had been promoted as Inspectors and again in 1958, respondents No. 4, 5, 8 and 10 had been promoted as Inspectors.
After perusing the record, the learned State counsel has frankly conceded that the case of the petitioner for the purpose of promotion was not considered at the time when the above mentioned respondents had been promoted as Inspectors.
The learned State counsel further states at the Bar that the case of the petitioner will be considered for the purpose of promotion as Inspector from the dates when the said respondents had been promoted as Inspectors.
Consequently, the writ petition is allowed and it is directed that the petitioner will be considered for the purpose of promotion as Inspector from the dates when respondents No. 4, 5, 6, 8 and 10 had been promoted.
In the circumstances of the case, there will be no order as to costs.
February 1, 1980 Sd/- Harbans Lal Judge"  In pursuance to the above directions, the Director State Transport, Punjab issued an order by which the petitioner was promoted as Inspector w.e.f.
June 7, 1955 (AN) instead of October 13, 1959.
He was further promoted to the post of Chief Inspector w.e.f.
February 18, 1968 instead of March 10, 1972 when he had been actually promoted.
Still further, he was granted promotion to the post of Station Supervisor Grade-1 w.e.f.
February 24, 1975.
A copy of the order dated July 21, 1980 by which these promotions had been granted, has been produced as Annexure P.4 with the writ petition.
3.
The petitioner was not satisfied with this order.
He submitted a representation to the Director.
He prayed that the date of promotion as Inspector should be changed from June 7, 1955 to September 1, 1952.
When he did not get any favourable order, the petitioner served a notice on the respondents.
Finally, the matter was considered by the Joint Secretary to the Government, Transport Department.
Vide order dated December 21, 1982, it was held that the petitioner was entitled to be promoted as Inspector.
It was further held that the dates of petitioner's promotion as Inspector and Chief Inspector viz. June 7, 1955 and February 18, 1968 were correct.
However, his date of promotion as Station Supervisor Grade-1 was changed to November 6, 1972 instead of February 24, 1975.
Still not satisfied the petitioner has filed the present writ petition.
He prays that the order dated December 21, 1982 be quashed and that the respondent authorities be directed to promote him as Inspector w.e.f.
September 1, 1952.
4.
The respondents contest the petitioner's claim.
In the written statement filed on behalf of respondent Nos. 1 and 2, it has been pointed out that the petitioner has been promoted with effect from the dates persons junior to him were promoted.
Thus, he has no cause for grievance.
5.
Counsel for the parties have been heard.
Ms. Jagdeep Bains, learned counsel has contended that the petitioner had a right to be promoted as Inspector with effect from the date Natha Ram junior to him had been promoted.
'She further points out that according to the final seniority list of Station Supervisors'.
Shiv Bhola Kohli had been promoted as Inspector on August 21, 1950.
He was junior to him as he had been recruited as a conductor on December 1, 1948.
Thus, she claims that the petitioner is entitled to the relief as prayed for in this petition.
The claim has been controverted by Mr. Ashok Aggarwal, Additional Advocate General, Punjab.
6.
Admittedly, the petitioner had filed CWP No. 4854 of 1971.
This writ petition, as already noticed, was disposed of with the direction that "the petitioner will be considered for the purpose of promotion as Inspector from the dates when respondents No. 4, 5, 6, 8 and 10 had been promoted."
In the judgment, it was also noticed that respondent Nos. 6 and 9 had been promoted in 1951 while respondent Nos. 4, 5, 8 and 10 had been promoted as Inspectors in 1958.
It is also admitted that in pursuance to the directions of the Court, the petitioner was promoted as inspector w.e.f.
June 7, 1955.
Even consequent changes in the dates of promotion as Chief Inspector and Station Supervisor Grade-1 were made.
Resultantly, it is clear that the directions as given by the court were complied with.
7.
Ms. Bains contends that the compliance is not complete.
She points out that persons junior to the petitioner had been promoted with effect from earlier dates.
She refers to the cases of respondent Nos. 3 to 9.
In fact, it is pointed out that they had been promoted as Inspectors during the period from August 21, 1950 to May 29, 1952.
Thus, the petitioner has a right to be considered for promotion with effect from the date respondent No. 3 had been promoted viz. August 21, 1950.
8.
This contention cannot be accepted for the reason that the petitioner had not made such a claim in CWP No. 4854 of 1971 filed by him.
The part of the claim which was not made by the petitioner in the earlier petition cannot be allowed to be raised in the present petition.
It also deserves notice that even before the respondent-authorities when the petitioner made a claim for retrospective promotion, it was not specifically contended that his juniors had been promoted during the years 1950 to 1952.
The present writ petition was filed in the year 1985.
The claim is being made after the lapse to almost 35 years.
Such a claim cannot be entertained at this stage.
Thus, the claim that the petitioner has a right to be considered for promoted In the year 1950 cannot be sustained.
9.
Ms. Bains contends that Natha Ram, a person junior to the petitioner had been promoted as inspector with effect from September 1, 1952.
Thus, the petitioner should be granted promotion from that date.
10.
Even this contention cannot be accepted.
Firstly, it deserves notice that this issue has been considered by the respondent-authority in paragraph 15 of the impugned order.
It has been noticed that the promotions granted to Natha Ram were temporary in nature.
He was actually granted promotion w.e.f.
June 711955.
The petitioner has also been promoted with effect from that date.
Thus, the petitioner cannot complain that there has been any discrimination against him.
Secondly, grant of promotion to the petitioner with effect from the year 1952 would admittedly affect the interests of Natha Ram whose seniority has admittedly been determined on the basis of his having been promoted w.e.f.
June 7, 1956.
He has not been made a party in the present petition.
Thus, no order to his prejudice can be passed in the present proceedings.
11.
Ms. Bains contends that the respondents had given wrong dates regarding the promotion of various persons in CWP No. 4854 of 1971.
Thus, the relief as prayed for in the present writ petition deserves to be granted.
12.
This contention cannot be accepted at the present stage.
The judgment between the parties was delivered on February 1, 1980.
It has attained finality.
The petitioner cannot now agitate the matter at this belated stage.
13.
Before parting with the case, it may also be observed that the petitioner as well as the private respondents have already retired from service.
At this balated state, no ground for interference is made out.
14.
Ms. Bains contends that on refixation of dates of promotion, the arrears have not been correctly calculated.
No such prayer has been made out in the petition.
Therefore, the contention cannot be accepted.
No other point has been raised.
In view of the above, there is no merit in this petition.
It is, consequently, dismissed.
In the circumstances, the parties are left to bear their own costs.
Petition dismissed
