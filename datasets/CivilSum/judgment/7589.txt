JS Narang, J - This judgment would dispose of CWP No. 6135 of 2002, CWP No. 6136 of 2002 and CWP No. 6137 of 2002 as common question of law has been raised.
The facts have been taken from CWP No. 6135 of 2002.
2.
The petitioner had joined as Beldar in January 1990 with respondent No. 4 on daily wages.
However, it has been claimed that he was paid wages at the rate of Rs. 1500/- PM and lastly had been paid at the rate of Rs. 1530/- PM It has been alleged that his services had been terminated with effect from 1.2.1996 without complying with the provisions of Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 (hereinafter referred to as the Act).
The demand notice dated 1931996 was served upon the respondent and in pursuant thereto, the reference was made to the Labour Court vide order dated 1921997.
It has been claimed that the services of the petitioner had been terminated without compliance of the provisions of the Act.
Further, the principle last come first go has also been violated as contained under Section 25-G of the Act.
The post against which he had worked is still existing.
Resultantly, the order of termination is illegal, void and not sustainable under law.
3.
On the other hand, the stand of the management is that the petitioner had been engaged as daily wager from February, 1990 to January, 1996 subject to availability of work, he had been paid wages at DC rates.
He had been disengaged vide letter dated 911996 as remaining work was got executed through regular labour.
Since the workman did not fulfil the conditions for regularisation, as such, had been disengaged.
4.
However, the perusal of the impugned award shows that the Labour Court has observed that the plea of the management is that the workman had not completed 240 days as mandatorily required under Section 25-B of the Act.
Whereas, perusal of the reply to the claim statement submitted by the respondent does not show such plea having been taken by the management.
Infact, it has been admitted that the workman had been engaged as daily wager with effect from February 1993 to January, 1996.
The Labour Court has further noticed that the witness of the management Shri Inderjit Bishnoi, who has appeared as MW-1, has produced Photo Copies of the muster roll which have been exhibited as Ex.M-1 to M-13.
From perusal thereof, the Labour Court has inferred that the workman had worked for 225 days during 12 months preceding to the date of alleged termination.
Thus, for invoking Section 215-F of the Act, the workman has not satisfied the conditions as envisaged under Section 25-B of the Act.
Thus, in view of the judgment of this Court rendered in re : Malkiat Singh v. Labour Commissioner, UT Chandigarh, 1996(3) RSJ 4 : 1996(3) SCT 164 (P&H), it has been observed that the workman is not entitled to calculate Sundays and national holidays for completing the period of 240 days and further reliance has been placed upon a judgment of the apex Court in re : Union of India v. Rajesh Kumar, 1993(3) S.C.T.
169 : AIR 1993 SC 1317.
It has been further followed in re : Malkiat Singh v. Labour Commissioner, UT Chandigarh, 1996(3) RSJ 4.
Resultantly, the reference has been answered against the workman and the claim has been dismissed vide awarded dated 2222001.
5.
The petitioner workman being dissatisfied with the aforesaid award has challenged the same by way of the present petition.
Learned counsel for the petitioner has argued that the Labour Court has erred in law and facts of the case in holding that the petitioner has not fulfilled mandatory requirement as envisaged under Section 25-B of the Act of having worked 240 days in 12 calendar months preceding to the date of termination.
It has been further argued that infact no such plea has been taken by the management.
Thus, it is not understanable from where the Labour Court has inferred that the workman has not worked for 240 days.
The perusal of muster roll shows that the workman has worked for more than 240 days and this fact was known to the management and therefore, no such plea has been taken by it.
6.
This court had directed the production of record vide order dated 2242002.
Upon perusal of the record, notice of motion had been issued vide order dated 14112002.
The petitioner had been required to file an additional affidavit in support of his claim of having fulfilled the condition as envisaged under Section 25-B of the Act, in pursuant thereto, affidavit dated 23102003 has been filed.
The perusal of the same shows that the petitioner workman had worked for more than 240 days whereas from production of muster roll with effect from March, 1995 to November, 1995, which have been exhibited as Ex.M-3 to M-10 and Ex.M-11, the period worked out to be 225 days.
In this manner, the computation seems to have been reflected for a period of 9 months and not 12 months.
If the muster roll for the months of February 1995 and December, 1995 are taken into consideration, the working days as reflected by the workman as 23 and 29 are taken into consideration, a period of 52 days shall be required to be added.
Keeping this fact in view, the Deputy Advocate General, Haryana had been required to obtain necessary instructions accordingly, vide order dated 1612004 but on the adjourned date, i.e., 322004, another request was made for some more time but on the adjourned date, i.e., 922004, learned DAG, Haryana showed her inability to obtain the instructions accordingly.
7.
However, from the perusal of the reply submitted by the Government before the Labour Court, we find that a categoric admission has been made and further an averment has been made in reply to para No. 1 that the workman had worked with effect from February, 1993 to January, 1996 and that nothing has been claimed that he absented himself in any month in between.
Reply to the para deserves to be noticed, which reads as under :-  "1.
That para-1 of the claim statement is admitted as a matter of record.
It is, however, submitted that the workman was engaged as daily wager with effect from 2/93 to and he remained till 1/96 on the work with the answering respondents.
He was paid his wages at the rates duly approved by the Deputy Commissioner and has not a fixed rates as claimed."
8.
After hearing learned counsels for the parties and perusal of the record especially the affidavit sworn by the workman which has not been controverted despite ample opportunity granted to the State, we are left with no other alternative but to accept that the workman had worked for more than 240 days in 12 months preceding to the order of termination.
We further find that no plea has been taken by the Government that the workman has not worked for 240 days as is necessarily required in pursuant to Section 25-B of the Act for invocation of the rigor of Section 25-F of the Act.
Resultantly, we allow this writ petition and quash the impugned award dated 2222001 and remit the proceedings before the Labour Court, Ambala.
We may observe that the Labour Court should be cautions enough in taking such views especially when no pleas have been taken and which ultimately incorrectly culminated into non-suiting the workman in respect of his claim.
It is unfortunate that such kind of argument has been raised on behalf of the management which is contrary to the record and which obviously has not been supported by any one despite the opportunity granted by this Court.
The parties are directed to appear before the Labour Court, Ambala on 242004.
9.
It is further observed that the petitioners have been unnecessarily harrassed, therefore, the costs are awarded to the petitioners which are quantified at Rs. 5000/- each which shall be payable by the respondents before the Labour Court on the date fixed.
It is further observed that the Labour Court shall make an endeavour to dispose of reference expeditiously and preferably within three months from the date of appearance of the parties.
10.
It is further clarified that if the exigencies so require, proceedings may be held by giving absolutely short adjournments.
In the eventuality, if the costs are not paid on the date fixed, the officer responsible for such non- compliance shall pay additional costs of Rs. 1000/- each, which shall be reimbursable by the Government under any count whatsoever.
Petition allowed.
