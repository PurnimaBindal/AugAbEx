Mehtab S Gill, J - In the petition under Article 226 of the Constitution of India, the petitioner has prayed for the issuance of a writ in the nature of certiorari for quashing the order dated October 27, 1997 (Annexure P-7) passed by the General Manager, Haryana Roadways, Rohtak, whereby the claim of the petitioner for regularising his services was rejected.
2.
Petitioner has averred that he joined as a Helper on November 17, 1992 in the Haryana Roadways, Rohtak and is continuously working with the General manager, Haryana Roadways, Rohtak (respondent No. 3).
His work and conduct remained good throughout the tenure of his service.
The petitioner successfully completed three years of his service on November 16, 1995 without any break/interruption in service.
Inspite of the instructions issued by the Government of Haryana, the service of the petitioner were not regularised.
An appeal was filed by the petitioner before the Transport Commissioner, Haryana, Chandigarh (respondent No. 2) for regularising his services but no decision was conveyed to him, a copy of which is annexed with the writ petition as Annexure P-1.
It has been also averred that several representations were made before respondent Nos. 2 and 3 stating therein that persons junior to the petitioner have been regularised and this act of the respondents in not regularising the services of the petitioner is unfair and discriminatory.
Petitioner came to know that Shri Pardeep Kumar (respondent No. 4), who was junior to the petitioner, was brought on regular establishment.
It has been further averred that the petitioner has completed five years of services as a helper on daily wage basis with the General Manager, Haryana Roadways, Rohtak (respondent No. 3).
3.
Petitioner filed Civil Writ Petition No. 10244 of 1977 in this Hon'ble Court, which came up for hearing before a Division bench of this Court and the same was disposed of vide order dated July 21, 1997 (copy Annexure P-6) directing the respondents to decide the representation of the petitioner dated April 15, 1997 (copy Annexure P-3) by passing a speaking order.
The General manager of Haryana Roadways, Rohtak decided the representation of the petitioner vide order dated October 27, 1997 (Annexure P-7) stating therein that the petitioner did not fall within the purview of the instructions issued by the Government and he was not entitled for regularisation.
4.
Petitioner filed another Civil Writ Petition No. 104 of 1998 in this Hon'ble Court, which was disposed of by a Division Bench of this Court on March 22, 1999.
The writ petition was dismissed as withdrawn with liberty to the petitioner to file a fresh one.
Notice of motion was issued.
Written statement was filed on behalf of respondent Nos. 1 to 3.
5.
We have heard learned counsel for the petitioner and the respondents.
6.
Learned counsel for the petitioner has drawn my attention to Annexure P-5, which is an extract from the instructions dated March, 7 1996 issued by the Government of Haryana for the regularisation of services of Work charged/casual/daily rated employees.
As Annexure P-5 was incomplete, learned counsel for the petitioner was directed to place on the record complete instructions of the Haryana Government dated March 7, 1996 and March 18, 1996 issued in the matter.
Accordingly, copies of the instructions of the State Government were placed on the record, which are attached as Annexures P-11 and P-12.
These instructions pertain to regularisation of services of the Work charged/casual/daily rated employees.
Para 2 of the circular letter dated March 18, 1996 issued by the Chief Secretary to Government.
Haryana reads as under :-  "This matter has further been considered and after careful consideration it has now been decided to regularise the services of all those workcharged/casual/daily rated employees who have completed 3 years service on 31st January, 1996 and fulfil other conditions laid down in Haryana Govt. letter of even number dated 7th March, 1996."
7.
From the above quoted para, it is evident that the services of all those work charged/casual/daily rated employees will be regularised, who had completed three years service on January 31, 1996 and also fulfilled certain other conditions as enumerated in the instructions dated March 7, 1996 (Annexure P-11).
8.
Learned counsel for the respondents has stated that as per the abovesaid instructions, the services of those employees could be regularised, who had completed three years of service on January 31, 1996 and were also in service on January 31, 1996 and had worked for a minimum period of 240 days in each year.
He has further stated that the petitioner remained absent from duty with effect from November 24, 1995 to December 31, 1995, i.e., for more than one month and was thus not eligible for regularisation of his services under the Government Instructions dated March 7, 1996 read with instructions dated March 18,1996 (copies Annexures P-11 and P-12).
9.
Haryana Government instructions dated March 181996 (Annexure P-12), relevant para of which is reproduced above stipulate that an employee who has rendered three years service on January 31, 1996 becomes eligible for the regularisation of his services.
In the present case the petitioner had completed three years of his service with the respondents on November 16, 1995 without any break.
Thus, he fulfils the requisite conditions for becoming eligible for the regularisation of his services in terms of the Government instructions dated March 7, 1996 and March 18, 1996.
The absence of the petitioner from November 24, 1995 to December 31, 1995, i.e., for more than 30 days at a time was after he had already completed three years service.
The same does not stand in his way so as to make him ineligible for regularising his services.
10.
Learned counsel for the petitioner while relying on a judgment of the Division Bench of this Court rendered in the case of Anand Kumar v. Haryana Urban Development Authority etc., 1995(1) RSJ 230 : 1995(1) SCT 677 (P&H)(DB) and Full Bench judgment of this Court rendered in the case of Tek Chand and others v. State of Haryana and others, 2002(1) SCT 308 (P&H)(FB) : (Civil Writ Petition No. 2067 of 1997) decided on August 8, 2001 and has contended that a break in service or interruption in service of daily wager which has been brought about on account of circumstances beyond the control of daily wager/casual employees cannot be a ground for denying him the benefit of regularisation in service.
11.
In Anand Kumar's case (supra), a Division Bench of this Court has observed as under :-  "....If a break in service or interruption in service takes place due to the action of the employer, it cannot be treated as break in service for the purposes of circulars issued by the Government of Haryana.
Likewise, if an interruption in service is due to sickness or authorised leave, or accident, or a legal strike or a look-out, such interruption cannot be treated as break in service, so as to disentitle an employee the benefit of regularisation in service."
12.
The Full Bench of this Court in Tek Chand's case (supra), has however, held that the State cannot deny the benefit of regularisation to an employees who has been forced to remain away from the job for more than a month on account of the action or inaction of the authority itself.
It has been further held that if an employee has remained absent from duty for any reason which is not attributable to employer, he shall not be eligible for regularisation.
13.
The petitioner in this case had, however, already competed three years service on November 16, 1995 and his absence from duty for more than 30 days ie from November 24, 1995 to December 31, 1995 cannot be looked into and regularisation cannot be denied to him for his this absence.
14.
Accordingly, the writ petition is allowed and the order dated October 27, 1997 (annexure P-7) passed by respondent No. 3 is quashed.
The respondents are directed to regularise the services of the petitioner w.e.f.
February 1, 1996.
The petitioner shall be entitled to all the consequential benefits.
However, parties are left to bear their own costs.
Petition allowed.
