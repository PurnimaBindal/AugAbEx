Swatanter Kumar, J - The petitioner in this petition under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India prays for issuance of an appropriate writ, order or direction directing the respondents give financial/pecuniary benefits to the petitioner from the date his juniors with same qualifications were promoted.
2.
The petitioner was initially appointed on adhoc basis as SS Master on 10121981.
Services of the petitioner as well as one Shri Karan Singh were regularised on 1111986.
Karan Singh was promoted as lecturer in Geography on 591994, ignoring the claim of the petitioner, which compelled the petitioner to file CWP No. 2308 of 1996.
However, the petitioner was also promoted as lecturer in Geography on 1041997 with effect from 591994.
In terms of the order, the petitioner was not entitled for arrears of pay prior to his actual date of joining.
Aggrieved from this direction of the respondents, the petitioner served a legal notice upon them on 8.12.1998 and having failed to get any relief from that quarter, the petitioner has filed the petition.
3.
Upon notice, the respondents filed detailed written statement.
The facts are hardly in dispute.
It is submitted that petitioner was promoted to the post of lecturer in Geography on 1041997 with effect from 591994, but no arrears were allowed to the petitioner.
The petitioner relied upon the judgment of this court in the case of Kali Ram Sharma and others v. State of Haryana, 1997(1) SCT 54 (P&H) : CWP No. 3405 of 1985, decided on 581996, in support of his contention.
Earlier the petitioner was considered junior to Karan Singh, as such Karan Singh was promoted prior to the petitioner on 111992.
As the petitioner had never worked on the promotional post of lecturer, he was not entitled to any relief.
4.
The narrow controversy that arises for determination before this Court is whether the petitioner could be denied pecuniary benefits by the impugned order when he was actually promoted to the post of lecturer with effect from 591994.
This controversy need not detain us any further as this very question stands fully settled by the same view taken by different Benches of this Court.
However, it will be appropriate only to refer to the case of Avtar Singh v. State of Haryana and another, 1998(1) RSJ 317, where the Division Bench held as under :-  "In other words, the respondents admit their fault for not promoting the petitioner with effect from the due date.
We are of the view that in such circumstances if for no fault of the incumbent, he is not promoted and is deprived to work on the promoted post, he cannot be denied the pay of the promoted post, when he is promoted retrospectively with effect from the due date.
A Division Bench of this Court in Civil Writ Petition No. 11749 of 1996, titled Krishan v. Haryana State Federation of Consumers' Co-operate Wholesale Stores and another, 1997(1) SCT 686 (P&H) decided on 10121996 and another decided on 10121996 and another Division Bench in Sudershan Kumar v. State of Haryana and another, 1997(2) SCT 278 (P&H) : 1997(2) RSJ 416, in similar circumstances held that the incumbent is entitled to the arrears of pay.
Following the aforesaid judgments, we allow this writ petition and quash the order annexure P/3 and direct the respondents to release the arrears of the petitioner with the effect from the date of his retrospective promotion.
The arrears be released to the petitioner within a period of three months.
In case the arrears are not released as aforesaid, the petitioner would be entitled to interest thereof at the rate of 12% per annum."
5.
With respect we adopt the reasons given and follow the view taken by the different Benches as afore-noticed.
Resultantly, we have no hesitation in allowing this petition.
6.
In the earlier writ petition No. 2308 of 1996 the petitioner had succeeded and the Division Bench had quashed the promotion of Karan Singh in preference to the petitioner.
However, the respondents were directed to consider the petitioner for promotion post vide judgment dated 2011997.
The said judgment has attained finality between the parties and, therefore, the irresistible conclusion is that the respondents should have promoted the petitioner in any case, as back as from 591994, the date on which others were promoted.
Thus, no fault is attributable to the petitioner for the period of 1994 to 1997 for which he had raised the demand after succeeding in the writ petition in the year 1997.
7.
For the reasons aforestated, we allow this petition and direct the respondents to consider the case of the petitioner for payment of arrears and grant all pecuniary benefits to the petitioner of the promotion post for the period indicated.
However, the parties are left to bear their own costs.
Petition allowed.
