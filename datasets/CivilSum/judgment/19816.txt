RS Mongia, J (Oral) - The petitioner was initially appointed as Road Inspector on 4th April, 1975, by the Administrator, Municipality, Ambala City, in the pay scale of Rs. 90-140, and he was given six advance increments.
It has been averred in the writ petition and not controverted in the written statement that at that time the qualification for the post of Road Inspector was Matriculate with five years' experience as Work Inspector/Work Mistri.
It is also not disputed that the petitioner fulfilled the said qualifications at the time of his appointment.
The State of Haryana framed rules known as Haryana Municipal Service (Integration, Recruitment and Conditions of Service) Rules, 1982 (hereinafter referred to as the Rules).
Appendix 'A' to the said rules prescribed the scales of pay of the posts in various services (this has been attached as Annexure P.1 to the writ petition).
These revised pay scales as mentioned in Appendix 'A' were to be made effective from 1st February, 1979.
The pay scale, which was fixed by these rules for Road Inspectors who were previously in the grade of less than Rs. 150-350 (Personal) was Rs. 350-500 (Personal) and Rs. 480-760 (Personal) for those Road Inspectors who were previously in the grade of Rs. 150-350.
The petitioner had been fixed in the pay scale of Rs. 350-500 for the reason that at the time when the Rules came into force, he was in a grade less than Rs. 150-350.
The Work Inspector, which is a lower post than the Road Inspector, was given pay scale of Rs. 400-600.
This was given to those Work Inspectors, who were in a grade less than Rs. 150-350.
Relevant extract of Appendix 'A' is reproduced below :-  "(b) Road Inspectors.
Rs. 350-5-400/10-500 (Personal) For persons in grade less than Rs. 150-350; Rs. 480-15-600-EB-20-700-30-760 (Personal) for persons at present in the grade of Rs. 150-350.
(c) Work Inspectors Rs. 400-10-490/540-15-500-EB-20-600 (Personal) for those drawing less than Rs. 150-350).
2.
The grievance of the petitioner is that he should have been granted the pay scale of Rs. 480-760 and not Rs. 350-500.
In this respect, the petitioner had made a representation in October, 1984 to the Municipal Committee (copy of which is attached as Annexure P2).
The Administrator of the Committee had recommended the case of the petitioner to the Deputy Commissioner, Ambala for onward transmission to the Government.
The proposal sent by the Administrator, which has been quoted by the petitioner in the petition, is reproduced below :-  "Shri K.K.
Unnunny has made a request that he is a Matriculate and completed one year training in Civil Engineering group and has 15 years service in this office.
His work and conduct has all along appreciated.
He was getting the grade of Rs. 90-140 prior to 1982, when the Municipal Services Rules were formulated.
Accordingly, he has been given the scale of Rs. 350-500, under item No. 9-B of the said rules.
The lesser grade was because of his predecessor being illiterate and not well conversant with the technical nature of the job.
The applicant was also given the same grade, although it was Rs. 150-350 at that level.
Keeping in view his qualification, technical qualification, work experience, knowledge of his job, the request may kindly be acceded to and he may be allowed the grade of Rs. 480-760 in relaxation of Rule 26 by the Govt. Sd/-  Overseer.
11.2.1985.
"The request is genuine and may kindly be recommended to Govt. Sd/- Secy.
11.2.1985.
Sd/- E.O.
1521985.
Approved as proposed.
Sd/- Admn.
1521985."
3.
Since, no action was taken on the said recommendation, the petitioner filed the present writ petition, claiming that he should be granted the pay scale of Rs. 480-760.
4.
Mr. HS Gill, Senior Advocate, learned counsel for the petitioner, submitted that no doubt the petitioner had accepted the lower pay scale, i.e. 90-140 at the time of his appointment, but that should not constitute an estoppel against him to claim the higher pay scale, when the scale of Road Inspector was revised.
According to the learned counsel, all non-Diploma Road Inspectors were performing the same duties and functions whether they were in pay scale of less than Rs. 150-350 or were in the pay scale of Rs. 150-350.
Once the pay scales of Road Inspectors were revised, all the Road Inspectors, according to him, should get the same pay scale on the theory of 'equal pay for equal work'.
The learned counsel for the respondents, however, submitted that the petitioner had accepted the lower pay scale of Rs. 90-140, when he joined the service as Road Inspector and the pay scale of those Road Inspectors who were getting pay scale less than Rs. 150-350 was revised to Rs. 350-500, and, therefore, the petitioner was only entitled to the pay scale of Rs. 350-500 and not Rs. 480-760 which was given to those Road Inspectors who were in the pay scale of Rs. 150-350.
It has further been argued that as is clear from the recommendation of the Administrator, which has been reproduced above, that the petitioner was given lesser grade because his predecessor being illiterate and not conversant with the technical nature of the job was given lower pay scale, therefore, the petitioner could not claim the revision of pay which was given to those Road inspectors who were in the pay scale of Rs. 150-350.
5.
After hearing the learned counsel for the parties, I find merit in the submissions of the learned counsel for the petitioner.
It is not disputed that the nature of duties and functions which were being performed by the petitioner who was in a pay scale lesser than Rs. 150-350, was the same as that of the Road Inspectors who were in the pay scale of Rs. 150-350.
That being so, irrespective of the fact that the petitioner was initially in a lower pay scale, would not debar him to claim equal pay for equal work, i.e. the same pay scale as was being revised for those Road Inspectors who were in the pay scale of Rs.150-350.
Accordingly, the petitioner is entitled to the pay scale of Rs. 480-760 and would also be entitled to further revision of pay scale, if any, when pay scale of Rs. 480-760 may have been revised.
6.
The question that arises now is regarding the payment of arrears.
The petitioner had filed his first representation of 17th October, 1984 and the present writ petition was filed on 30th July, 1987.
Since prior to 17th October, 1984, the petitioner had not raised any claim in writing with the respondents, I hold that the petitioner would be entitled to the arrears from the date of his representation, i.e. 17th October, 1984.
7.
Consequently, the writ petition is allowed and the petitioner is held entitled to the revised pay scale of Rs. 480-760, to be calculated from 1st February, 1979, (i.e. from the date w.e.f.
which the pay scales were revised), but the arrears would be restricted from 17th October, 1984.
The arrears to be calculated and paid to the petitioner within two months of the receipt of this order.
There will be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
