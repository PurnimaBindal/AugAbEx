ML Singhal, J - Through this writ petition filed under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India, petitioner has prayed for the issuance of a writ of certiorari for quashing order/Memo No. E/10/95-6 Health 1/1680 dated 3151994 (Annexure P-4) passed by Superintendent to Secretary to Government of Punjab, Health and Family Welfare Department, terminating his services and further for issuance of writ, direction or order especially in the nature of mandamus directing respondent-State of Punjab to place on record the order on the basis of which his services have been terminated and to treat him in service and to regularise his services keeping in view his length of service and to grant him all consequential benefits alongwith interest.
2.
Facts :  Petitioner applied for the post of Medical Officer in response to the advertisement that appeared in the press on 14.10.1988 for recruitment to certain posts of Medical Officers (short term basis).
Vide appointment letter dated 1481989 (Annexure P-1), he was appointed as Doctor/Medical Officer on short term basis.
As per letter of appointment Annexure P-1, offer of appointmentt was only for a short term basis for a period of six months or till the availability of the regular candidate from the Punjab Public Service Commission, Patiala or whichever is earlier.
He continued to serve the government continuously in pursuance of appointment up to 371995 i.e. when his services were terminated.
He served the government when terrorism was at its peak.
During that period, he received threatening letters.
For security reason, he was given pistol by the police.
His relations with his wife were not cordial.
He filed application under Section 9 of the Hindu Marriage Act on 2511995.
Due to the filing of that application his wife Daljit Kaur became inimical towards him.
His wife's parents and other relatives are influential persons.
They used their influence and manipulated false FIR on 1421995 at Police Station, Kotwali, Hoshiarpur against him that he was in possession of un-licensed pistol.
He was immediately arrested.
He produced certificate from DSP (Intelligence) Hoshiarpur mentioning therein that he was in possession of a pistol issued to him for safety purpose by the police.
In view of that certificate, he was granted bail on 931995.
After his release on bail, he submitted joining report in office on 1431995.
He was, however, not allowed to join duty due to the registration of the case.
Civil Surgeon, Jalandhar-respondent No. 3 wrote letter to respondent No. 2 Director Health and Family Welfare, Punjab, Chandigarh on 1431995 inquiring him whether he should permit him to join duty still when case was lying registered against him.
It was also mentioned that he was working as doctor (short term basis).
Annexure P-2 is the copy of that communication.
On 951995 respondent No. 2 recommended to the government after due enquiry that he had been got arrested by his in-laws due to the cases pending in the Court, therefore his services be extended up to 141995 to 3091995.
Annexure P-3 is the copy of that communication.
Instead of giving extension, Superintendent to respondent No. 1 i.e. Secretary to Government of Punjab, Health and Family Welfare Department informed respondent No. 2 that his services could not be extended beyond 3131995 and further his request for joining duty had been rejected after due consideration.
Annexure P-4 is the copy of that order.
After the receipt of annexure P-4 on 371995, petitioner on 771995 submitted application to respondent No. 2 with a copy to respondent No. 1 for giving him the detailed order of consideration and the reason for no extending his service.
It is alleged that as per Rule 8 read with Rule 11 of the Punjab Civil Medical Services (Class-II) Rules, 1982 (hereinafter referred to as the Rules), the period of probation is two years.
In the appointment letter Annexure P-1, it was mentioned that appointment was for six months.
He, however, remained in continuous service after the expiry of six months.
His services were discontinued in contravention of the terms and conditions of the appointment letter.
It is alleged that one Dr. Manoj Kumar who was also appointed on short term basis like him was allowed to join duty inspite of enquiry pending against him vide order dated 2.3.1995 passed by respondent No. 1.
Order Annexure P-4 wherethrough his services were terminated and not extended beyond 3131995, is illegal, unjust and improper.
It is arbitrary.
As per appointment letter annexure P-1, his services were liable to be terminated at the end of 6 months or on the appointment of a regular candidate, whichever was earlier.
In this case, he had already served more than 5 years and till date, no regular candidate had been sponsored by the Punjab Public Service Commission.
There was thus no ground for the termination of his services.
He had completed the period of probation, which was only two years to the entire satisfaction of the respondents.
His services were got terminated by his in-law, as is discernible from Annexure P-3 whereby Direct Health and Family Welfare Department, Punjab asked for permission of the Secretary to Government of Punjab (Department of Health and Family Welfare) to permit him to join duty beyond 3131995.
Inspite of the recommendation of respondent No. 2 vide Annexure P-3 to permit him to join duty, he was not permitted to join duty.
One Dr. Manoj Kumar's services were extended by respondent No. 1 despite the pendency of enquiry against him.
He ought to have been regularised.
In view of the law laid down in State of Haryana v. Piara Singh, 1992(4) SLR 770 : 1992(3) SCT 201 (SC), State was required to frame policy for regularisation of the services of its employees.
Had he been given proper opportunity before his services were terminated, he would have convinced the respondents as to the efficacy of his claim that he was entitled to be continued in service beyond 3131995.
3.
Respondent Nos. 1 to 3 contested that petition.
It was urged that he was appointed purely on short term basis.
As per condition laid down in para 2 of his appointment letter, his services could be terminated at any time without assigning any reasons or without any notice thereof.
He was appointed on short term basis for a period of six months.
His services were extended from time to time in public interest.
His services were not extended upto 3131995 due to registration of a criminal case against him.
It was not correct that he served continuous upto 371995.
He was arrested by the police of Police Station Kotwali Hoshiarpur on 1421995 under Sections 363, 365, 452, 17, 506/34 of the Indian Penal Code and released on bail on 931995.
His services were continued to be extended upto 3131995 but his services were not extended beyond 3131995 due to his involvement in the aforesaid criminal case registered against him.
He was not allowed to join duty by the Punjab Govt. vide memo No. 1/10/95/6/HBI/16/180 dated 3131995 and his request was also rejected by the Government for continuance in service.
It was urged that Rule 8 read with Rule 11 of the Rules does not come in so far as he is concerned as his was a short term appointment.
The services of Dr. Manoj Kumar had already been terminated by the Government vide their memo No. 17/73/95/6HBI/17101 dated 13.6.1995-Annexure R-1 due to registration of a criminal case against him by the police of PHC Hajipur.
4.
I have heard the learned counsel for the petitioner, learned Additional Advocate General (Punjab) and have gone through the record.
5.
The basis for refusal to extend the services of the petitioner beyond 3131995 is the registration of a criminal case against him under Sections 363, 365, 452, 427/506/34 of the Indian Penal Code at Police Station Kotwali, Hoshiarpur on 1421995 in which he remained in custody from 1421995 to 931995.
It may be mentioned here that he was released on bail on 931995.
It is not disputed that he served as Medical Officer with effect from 1481989 to 3131995 i.e. for more than 5 years.
It is not disputed that his was a short term appointment enuring for six months in the first instance but was to be extended till the availability of a regular incumbent from the Punjab Public Service Commission.
It is not disputed that the Government framed policy as envisaged in Annexure P-6, for regularising the services of those doctors who had completed more than four years as on 3091993.
Petitioner had thus completed more than 4 years up to 3091993.
6.
Learned counsel for the petitioner submitted that he should have been regularized in terms of policy as envisaged in Annexure P-6.
It was submitted by him that case FIR No. 19 was registered against him under Sections 363/365/452/427/506/34 of the Indian Penal Code at Police Station Kotwali, Hoshiarpur on 1421995.
It was submitted that he had completed more than 6 years of service on 3131995 i.e. when he was refused extension in service and his services were terminated.
It was further submitted that he was acquitted in criminal case by the Court.
If there was any sting attached to his continuance in service due to the registration of that criminal case, that sting ceased to have any effect because of his acquittal.
He was convicted under Section 25 of the Arms Act by the Chief Judicial Magistrate, Hoshiarpur and was sentenced to under go RI for a period of 5 months and to pay fine of Rs. 500/- vide order dated 23101997.
This case had also arisen during the investigation of a case under Sections 363/365/452/427/506/34 of the Indian Penal Code ibid.
In case under Section 25 of the Arms Act also he was acquitted by the Additional Sessions Judge, Hoshiarpur in appeal.
7.
It was submitted by the learned counsel for the petitioner that acquittal placed him in the same position as if he had never been put up on trial.
It was submitted that acquittal blotted out the existence of guilt altogether.
It is as if no case had ever been registered against him.
In support of his submission, he drew my attention to Ramsinghi Viraji Rathod, Parmanand Society v. State of Gujarat, 1971 SLR 743 where it was held that where a government servant was placed under suspension on criminal prosecution, his acquittal from the Court will entitle him to full pay for the suspension period.
There is no conception like "honourable acquittal" or "full exoneration" in criminal trial.
8.
In Union of India v. Jayaram, AIR 1960 Madras 325, a Division Bench of Madras High Court held that there is no concept like "honourable acquittal" in Criminal Procedure Code.
The onus of establishing the guilt of the accused is on the prosecution and if it fails to establish the guilt beyond reasonable doubt, the accused is entitled to be acquitted.
Where the servant was suspended because there was a criminal prosecution against him and he was acquitted therein, and reinstated he is entitled under the general law, to the full pay during the period of his suspension.
9.
In Mohan Lal v. Union of India and others, 1982(1) SLR 573 learned Single Judge of Delhi held that if government servant is acquitted on benefit of doubt, it is complete acquittal on merits.
10.
In Ram Deo Tewari v. Union of India and others, 2001(5) SLR 641 it was held that since the petitioner was acquitted by the criminal court, he became entitled to be put back into service.
Merely because he was acquitted, giving benefit of doubt, is no ground to deny him any relief.
11.
It was submitted that the petitioner's acquittal in the criminal case removed the bar, if any on his continuation in service.
It was submitted that the condition in the appointment letter Annexure P-1 that his appointment shall be on short term basis for a period of six months or till the availability of a regular incumbent from the Punjab Public Service Commission whichever is earlier, with a further condition, that his services shall come to an end automatically after the expiry of that period of six months is bad.
It is arbitrary, oppressive, unconscionable and unconstitutional.
In support of this submission, he drew my attention to Gordhan Singh Gulia v. State of Haryana and others, 1996(1) RSJ 243 : 1996(3) SCT 412 (P&H) where it was held that condition contained in Annexure P-1, limiting appointment of the petitioner up to 3061995 with a condition that his service shall come to an end on that day and he would stand relieved on 3061995 is arbitrary, oppressive, unconscionable and unconstitutional.
12.
In Ms Rajni Bala v. Sate of Haryana, CWP No. 10098 of 1995 the Hon'ble Division Bench of this Court held as under :  "In view of the principles laid down by the Supreme Court, we are of the opinion that where an ad hoc or temporary appointment is made after consideration of the candidature of all eligible persons in accordance with the equality clause, the action of the employer in limiting the appointment up to a particular date with a stipulation of automatic termination of service, even though the post is not abolished and a regularly selected person is not available, will have to be treated as wholly arbitrary, irrational, unjust, oppressive, and unconscionable and the same is liable to be struck down being contrary to Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
It may be a different situation where an appointment is given for a specified work and the post is created only for that work and service of the employee is terminated due to cessation of work or where a condition is incorporated in the contract of employment that the service of the employee will stand terminated on the availability of the selected candidate, however, there cannot be any justification to limit the appointment to a particular date merely because the employer chooses to describe the employment as ad hoc.
In our opinion, the employer cannot use his prerogative to lay down the terms and conditions of employment by incorporating a condition that the service of the employee will stand terminated on a particular date even though the post continues to remain available and the employer requires manpower for doing the work in relation to that post.
Such condition in the order/letter of appointment in case of the teachers cannot but be termed as unreasonable and contrary to public interest.
The student community as a whole is the worst sufferer on account of non-availability of teachers and the executive authorities cannot be permitted to act arbitrarily by incorporating wholly unreasonable conditions of employment in the order of appointment of the teachers."
13.
It was submitted that the State cannot press into service this condition in the order of appointment that his would be a short term appointment enuring for a period of six months or till the availability of a regular incumbent from the Punjab Public Service Commission for terminating the services of the petitioner after 3131995 when he had put in more than 5 years and was entitled to be considered for regularisation in terms of a policy as envisaged in Annexure P-6.
His services could not be terminated due to the registration of the aforesaid criminal case.
His services could not be terminated after 3131995 arbitrarily when he was awaiting regularisation.
14.
In view of what has been said above, it is held that the petitioner was entitled to be reinstated into service on his acquittal in the aforesaid criminal cases.
He is also declared entitled to be considered for regularisation in terms of the policy as envisaged in Annexure P-6.
He will, however, not be entitled to any wages for the period for which he remained out of job on account of the non-grant of extension to him in service in the wake of registration of the aforesaid criminal cases under Sections 363/365/453/427/ 506/34 and 25 of the Arm Act on 1421995 against him.
In case, he is regularised, he will be entitled to all the increments for the period during which he remained out of job on account of order Annexure P-4, will, however count towards the computation of pension and other retiral benefits.
He will, however, not be entitled to any arrears on any score whatsoever.
Annexure P-1 stands quashed.
In the above said terms, the writ petition stands disposed of.
Petition allowed.
