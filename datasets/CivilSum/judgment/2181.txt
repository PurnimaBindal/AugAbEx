MMS Bedi, J - Through the instant writ petition the petitioner seeks a direction to the respondents to allow her to join the duties as per the directions of Hon'ble Supreme Court in Civil Appeal Nos. 5584-5585 of 1999 (Paramjit Kaur and others v. State of Punjab and others), decided on 7112000 (Annexure P-3) and for quashing of order dated 29112005 (Annexure P-8) passed by Director Health Services and Family Welfare, Punjab, respondent No. 2 refusing to permit her to join her duties on the post of Multi Purpose Health Workers (Female).
She was selected again 150 posts advertised for Multi Purpose Health Workers (Female) in the year 1992.
She had completed the formalities regarding production of medical fitness certificate as well as statements of declaration, copies of which have been attached as Annexures P-1 and P-2.
2.
The facts relevant for adjudication of this writ petition in the matter of appointment of 150 posts of Multi Purpose Health Workers (Female) advertised in the year 1992 are that the petitioner was one of the selected candidates on the basis of her academic qualification and merit.
The petitioner's name existed in the list of 150 candidates, who were given appointments on selection by the Subordinate Services Selection Board, Punjab.
The respondents had also selected additional 632 candidates resulting in lot of litigation.
But ultimately the matter was finally settled by order dated 7112000 passed by Hon'ble Supreme Court in SLP No. 5584-5585 of 1999 (Annexure P-3).
The petitioner was ordered to be appointed as Multi Purpose Health Worker (Female).
As per directions of the Apex Court the petitioner was issued appointment letter dated 1012001 (Annexure P-4).
She was required to submit her joining report within 21 days at her place of posting.
She claims that she had submitted her joining report at Primary Health Centre, Harpal Pur within the prescribed period.
However, she could not join the duty as her husband was ill and because of that reason, she could not submit her medical fitness certificate.
She was not allowed to join in the absence of medical certificate.
She was to be medically examined by the Civil Surgeon concerned but she had already submitted her medical fitness certificate at the time of her first entry in the service when she continued in service for 8 months.
Therefore, she submitted a representation dated 1242002 (Annexure P-5) to the Health Minister, Punjab requesting that she should be allowed to join her duties as her husband was seriously ill and was being looked after by her.
She submitted that she was not able to join her duty as her husband ultimately died on 2362001.
After getting her husband's death certificate she had gone to join her duty but was not allowed to join.
It was further stated in the representation that after the death of her husband she was turned out of the house by her father-in-law and that her father had already died.
Request in the said representation has been made for permission to allow her to join the service.
Thereafter, the Director Health Services & Family Welfare, Punjab wrote a letter dated 762002 (Annexure P-6) to the Principal Secretary, to Government of Punjab, Health and Family Welfare Department and forwarded the copies of the representations of the petitioner with a request for permitting her to join the duties as due to the death of her husband she could not join.
As no action was taken, she submitted another representation dated 28.10.2002 (Annexure P-7) to respondent No. 2 regarding giving her permission to join the duties.
In response to her representation, a letter dated 29112005 issued vide endorsement dated 23122005 (Annexure P-8) was received by the petitioner wherein it was mentioned that she was appointed on the post of Multi Purpose Health Worker (F) vide appointment letter dated 2012001 and she was required to join her duties within 21 days.
She did not make any request to any authority for extending her joining period.
Apart from this a ban had been imposed by the Government on the recruitments and all the vacant posts are likely to be abolished.
In view said facts, the petitioner would not be allowed to join her duties on the post of Multi Purpose Health Worker (F).
3.
The petitioner has sought a writ in the nature of certiorari for quashing order dated 29112005 (Annexure P-8) on the ground that she had in fact submitted her joining report within the prescribed period of 21 days but could not join her duties as her husband was seriously ill before his death on 23.6.2003; she was within the zone of 150 candidates, therefore, she should have been allowed to continue in service as appointed initially in accordance with her original selection and that the ban on recruitment subsequent to the appointment order cannot be made applicable to her case.
4.
Pursuant to notice of motion, the writ petition was contested by respondents 1 to 5 by filing a joint written statement taking up the preliminary objection that the petitioner, after having been selected along with other candidates was issued an appointment letter on 1012001 and she was given 21 days to join duty.
She neither joined the duty within the period of 21 days nor she gave any information for extension in her joining time.
A strong reliance has been placed on Punjab Civil Services (General and Common Conditions of Service) Rules, 1994 (for short `the Rules') to contend that extension in joining time upto a maximum period of four months from the date of issue of appointment letter is permissible but the petitioner did not represent for extension of the joining time within a period of four months from the date of appointment, as such, she cannot be permitted to join.
In paragraph 2 of the reply on merits, the respondents have taken the following plea :-  ".... All the selected candidates were directed to join duty within 21 days after getting medical fitness certificate from the concerned Civil Surgeon.
As per report received from the Senior Medical Officer, PHC, Harpalpur vide letter No. 1439 dated 12112002, the petitioner came in their office on 622001 without any Original Certificate and Medical Fitness Certificate.
She was directed to come with relevant documents.
After 622001, she did not come to join her duty within the stipulated period and did not produce relevant documents.
A representation dated 11.7.2001 was received on 2132002 from the petitioner regarding permission to join duty which was forwarded to the Govt. of Punjab, Department of Health and Family Welfare vide letter No. E6(1)Pb- 02/3880 dated 762002.
The Punjab Govt. Department of Health and Family Welfare rejected the representation of the petitioner regarding permission to join duty vide its memo No. 1/12/2002-4 HB5/251999 dated 29112005."
5.
Learned counsel for the petitioner Shri SN Saini has contended that the petitioner had, pursuant to the appointment letter approached the Senior Medical Officer within time but on account of unfavourable family circumstances and illness of her husband she was unable to join her duties.
It is a case of great hardship for the petitioner that she was selected pursuant to an advertisement in the year 1992 as ANM The selection of all the ANMs.
was quashed for none of the fault of the petitioner.
After the final adjudication of the litigation, the Hon'ble Supreme Court had upheld the selection of the petitioner vide judgment dated 7112000 (Annexure P-3).
He has also relied upon the affidavit wherein she had deposed that she had served the department on the post of Multi Purpose Health Worker (F) at Primary Health Centre, Amargarh up to February 1995.
She was issued a fresh appointment order dated 1012001 (Annexure P-4).
She submitted her joining report on 622001 to S.M.O.
Incharge Primary Health Centre, Harpalpur i.e. within 21 days from the receipt of order dated 1012001.
On the other hand learned counsel for the respondents Shri BS Chahal stuck to the plea taken in the written statement contending that the petitioner was required to submit her joining report within 21 days after receiving the appointment letter but she did not join within 21 days nor sought extension of her joining time.
A strong reliance has been placed on the Rules that joining time cannot be extending beyond the maximum period of four months from the date of issue of appointing letter.
6.
We have heard learned counsel for the petitioner as well as counsel for the respondents and also summoned the original record pertaining to the appointment of the petitioner.
A perusal of the original record indicates that the petitioner, on the basis of her merit, was given an appointment letter vide memo No. E6(1)Pb-94/3955 dated 2961994.
In the said letter she had been directed to report for duty within 21 days and appear before the Civil Surgeon, Ludhiana for medical examination and get the medical fitness certificate.
She had joined her duties on 15.7.1994 before noon and was posted at P.H.C.
Salar (Amargarh).
It is apparent from the record that after having served for 8-9 months, the services of the petitioner were dispensed with pursuant to the order of the High Court.
So far as the appointment letter dated 1012001 is concerned, this has been issued by the respondents pursuant to the directions issued by the Hon'ble Supreme Court.
The original record indicates that appointment letter dated 1012001 was the second appointment letter issued to the petitioner against the same post for which she had earlier submitted her joining report.
A copy of the representation dated 1242002 (Annexure P-5) submitted by the petitioner to the Director Health Services and Family Welfare, Punjab shows that she had submitted that she had been appointed vide appointment letter dated 2961994 and had joined on 15.7.1994 her duties at Amargarh, District Sangrur.
Her services were dispensed with after 8-9 months pursuant to the orders of the High Court.
Thereafter, pursuant to the orders of the Hon'ble Supreme Court she was reinstated on 1012001.
Her husband remained seriously ill during the interregnum period, as such she was not able to join her duties.
Her husband died on 2362001.
Her representation was recommended through Transport Minister.
After the death of her husband on 2362001, the petitioner had submitted an application dated 11.7.2001 before respondent No. 2 that she should be permitted to join her duties.
From the sequence of the above events, as reflected from the original record, it is thus clear that the original order of appointment of the petitioner as Multi Purpose Health Worker (F) was issued on 2961994 and she had joined on 15.7.1994 and had served the department for a few months.
She was selected against the 150 posts advertised in the year 1992.
In addition to the selection of the said 150 Multi Purpose Health Workers (F), 632 candidates were also selected in the year 1994.
The selection of 632 candidates was challenged by filing CWP No. 9575 of 1994 and vide order dated 211995 (Annexure R-1) the selection of 632 ANMs.
was quashed and a direction was given to the respondents to fill up 150 posts of Multi Purpose Health Workers (F) in view the academic qualifications of the candidates and no marks would be provided for the interview.
The High Court vide judgment dated 2031995 in CWP No. 1762 of 1995 (Annexure R-2) ordered that appointment, if any, would be subjected to the result of the writ petition.
Keeping in view order dated 2031995 the process of appointment of 150 candidates on the basis of academic qualification was stopped by the respondents.
Thereafter, vide judgment dated 19121995 (Annexure R-3) passed in CWP No. 1762 of 1995, order dated 21.3.1995 passed in CWP No. 9575 of 1994 was modified to the extent that the petitioners in the said writ petition would be permitted to continue in service till regular selection was made.
A direction was issued to the respondents to make a fresh selection and issue appointment to the selected candidates within six months.
The candidates, whose services were terminated were ordered to be taken back in service within a period of one month.
CWP No. 1762 of 1995 was finally disposed of with the above said observations.
Thereafter, fresh selection of 556 candidates was made by the Subordinate Services Selection Board, Punjab and appointment orders were issued on 2731997 to the selected candidates and the services of all non-selected candidates were also terminated vide letter dated 2731997.
The method of selection was again challenged vide CWP No. 4892 of 1997 and the High Court dismissed the same vide judgment dated 22101997 (Annexure R-4).
The judgment dated 211995 passed in CWP No. 9575 of 1995 became unenforceable in view of the subsequent judgment dated 22101997 passed in CWP No. 4892 of 1997.
Subsequently, the matter came up for final adjudication before the Hon'ble Supreme Court in Civil Appeal Nos. 5584-5585 of 1999 along with Civil Appeal Nos. 5586-5587.
The Hon'ble Supreme Court finally settled the controversy vide judgment dated 7112000 (Annexure P-3) and held that the candidates, who come within 150 posts on the basis of their academic qualification pursuant to order dated 211995 passed by this Court (Annexure R-1) should be appointed.
7.
The details of the above said litigation clearly depicts that the selection of the petitioner, which was made in the year 1994 against 150 advertised vacancies and held proper in CWP No. 9575 of 1994 (Annexure R-1) dated 211995 was finally affirmed by the Hon'ble Supreme Court on 7112000.
It has been admitted in the written statement that in view of order dated 211995 passed in CWP No. 9575 of 1994 by this Court, the process of appointment of 150 candidates on the basis of academic qualification was stopped vide orders passed in CWP No. 1762 of 1995.
The petitioner does not seem to be at fault.
Though she had joined the service in accordance with appointment letter on 15.7.1994, she had to suffer as the matter regarding appointment of Multi Purpose Health Workers (F) had been the subject-matter of litigation and non-implementation of the directions of the Court on account of uncertainty.
The matter was finally settled by the Hon'ble the Supreme Court upholding the appointment of the petitioner, who was one of the 150 candidates originally selected, appointed and joined in the year 1994.
8.
The petitioner, thus, cannot be penalized on account of the actions of the respondents stopping the process of appointment of 150 candidates on the basis of their academic qualifications pursuant to the directions of this Court.
The need of issuance of fresh appointment letter arose after the appointment of the petitioner along with other candidates was finally upheld by Hon'ble the Supreme Court vide judgment dated 20112000 (Annexure P-3).
The fresh appointment letter was only a continuation of the earlier appointment letter issued to the petitioner on 2961994.
Joining of the petitioner pursuant to the second appointment letter was a mere formality and did not tantamount to the first joining after the appointment letter issued pursuant to the directions of the Hon'ble Supreme Court.
It appears that the petitioner has been denied a right to join her duties merely on the basis of technicality without appreciating her legal right to join the duty after her selection made in the year 1994 having been finally affirmed by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in 2000 resulting in issuance of fresh appointment letter dated 1012001 (Annexure P-4).
9.
We have also considered the contention of the learned counsel for the respondents that the petitioner is not entitled to join her duties on account of the statutory provisions of the Rules prescribing a maximum period of four months from the date of issue of appointment letter.
We have carefully perused the provisions of the Rules.
The said rules were framed vide Notification dated 451994 and were made applicable to all Posts of Class-I, Class-II and Class-III vide amendment made in the said rules on 18122001.
These rules were made applicable to only Group A, Group B and Group C services constituted in connection with the affairs of the State of Punjab as per pay scales given in the appendix.
Besides this there does not appear to be any specific statutory condition imposed in the said rules indicating that the joining period cannot be extended beyond the period of four months from the date of appointment.
Our attention was drawn to Rule 8 of the Rules pertaining to seniority to contend that by implication the said rule restricts the extension of joining period beyond four months after the date of appointment.
Rule 8 of the Rules is extracted below :-  "Seniority.
- The seniority inter se of persons appointed to posts in each cadre of a Service shall be determined by the length of continuous service on such post in that cadre of the service: Provided that in the case of persons recruited by direct appointment who join within the period specified in the order of appointment or within such period as may be extended from time to time by the appointing authority subject to a maximum of four months from the date of order of appointment, the order of merit determined by the Commission or the Board, as the case may be, shall not be disturbed : Provided further that in a case of person is permitted to join the post after the expiry of the said period of four months in consultation with the Commission or the Board, as the case may be, his seniority shall be determined from the date he joins the post : Provided further that in a case any person of the next selection has joined a post in the cadre of the concerned Service before the person referred to in the proceeding proviso joins, the person so referred shall be placed below all the persons of the next selection, who join within the time specified in the first proviso : Provided further that in the case of two or more persons appointed on the same date, their seniority shall be determined as follows :- (a) a person appointed by direct appointment shall be senior to a person appointed otherwise; (b) a person appointed by promotion shall be senior to a person appointed by transfer; (c) in the case of person appointed by promotion or transfer, the seniority shall be determined according to the seniority of such persons in the appointments from which they were promoted or transferred; and (d) in the case of persons appointed by transfer from different cadres, their seniority shall be determined according to pay, preference being given to person who was drawing a higher rate of pay in his previous appointment; and if the rates of pay drawn are also the same, then by their length of service in these appointments and if the length of service is also the same, an older person shall be senior to a younger person.
Note.
- Seniority of persons appointed on purely provisional basis or on ad hoc basis shall be determined as and when they are regularly appointed keeping in view the dates of such regular appointment."
10.
After a careful perusal of the above said rule, we are of the opinion that the same pertains to the determination of inter se seniority of persons appointed to the post in a particular cadre of service and is relevant when the length of continuous service of an employee is required to be determined for the purpose of determining his inter se seniority vis-a-vis another employee of the same cadre.
We are of the opinion that the above said rule does not, in any manner, create an absolute bar prohibiting an employee to join service after a period of four months of the order of appointment.
Rule 8 of seniority, cannot, by implication be applied to determine the period of joining after the order of appointment.
11.
Mr. Chahal has also not been able to establish that the post of Multi Purpose Health Worker (F) falls within Group A, Group B or Group C service.
We do not find force in the contention of the learned counsel for the respondents that the Rules prohibit the extension of joining period beyond the period of four months.
Even otherwise, we have held in earlier part of the judgment that the petitioner in the present case had originally joined on 15.7.1994 pursuant to her original appointment order dated 2961994, as such the action of the respondents in not permitting her to join her service after the direction of the Hon'ble Supreme Court is illegal.
It is also admitted in the written- statement that the Senior Medical Officer, Primary Health Centre, Harpalpur had reported that the petitioner had come to his office on 622001 without any original certificate or medical fitness certificate and that she was directed to come with the relevant documents but she did not come to join her duties within the stipulated period and did not produce the relevant documents after 622001.
The said admission indicates that the petitioner had approached the concerned Senior Medical Officer within 21 days when fresh appointment letter was issued to her.
As she had earlier submitted her original certificates and medical fitness certificate, which were already with the respondents, she cannot be denied the right to join her duties keeping in view her family circumstances, which have not been controverted in the written statement i.e. her husband was seriously ill and had died on 2362001, her father has also died and her father-in-law has turned her out of the house.
We are of the opinion that it is not a case where the petitioner has abandoned the job and refused to join after having been issued the appointment letter.
We also do not find any force in the contention of the learned counsel for the respondents that on account of ban by the Government on recruitment, the petitioner cannot be appointed.
The ban, if any, was not in operation when the original appointment letter was issued to the petitioner or when the orders were passed by the Hon'ble Supreme Court.
Denying appointment to the petitioner would tantamount to the violation of the orders of the Apex Court.
12.
Learned counsel for the petitioner has submitted that the petitioner is even ready to waive off her right to the salary and other allowances as she has not actually worked on the post after issuance of the appointment letter.
Accordingly, the writ petition is allowed and order dated 29112005 (Annexure P-8) is quashed and a direction is hereby issued to the respondents to permit the petitioner to join her duties within a period of one month after the receipt of the certified copy of the judgment.
She will be entitled to all consequential benefits except the payment of wages/salary for the period for which she did not discharge her duties.
The original file be returned to the respondents against proper receipt.
Petition allowed.
