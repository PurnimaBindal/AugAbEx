SS Nijjar, J - This judgment will dispose of CWP Nos. 14911, 14912 and 14913 of 2003 as common facts are involved and the question raised in all the petitions is also identical.
For the sake of convenience, where ever necessary reference will be made to the facts as pleaded in C.W.P.
No. 14911 of 2003.
2.
The petitioner claims that he was appointed as a Baildar on daily wages on 121989.
He is working as such till date with the respondent-Haryana Urban Development Authority (hereinafter referred to as `HUDA)'.
According to the petitioner the work allotted to him was of regular nature.
The State of Haryana issued instructions dated 731996 (Annexure P-1) which provide that casual and daily rated employees, who have completed 5 years of service on 3111996 and were in service on that date were entitled for regularisation provided that they had worked for a minimum period of 240 days in each year and the break in service in any year is not more than one month at a time.
The aforesaid instructions were subsequently modified by instructions dated 1831996 when the period of 5 years service was reduced to 3 years service.
The other conditions laid down in Annexure P-1 remained the same.
These instructions are appended as Annexure P-2 to the writ petition.
On the basis of the aforesaid instructions, the petitioner claimed regularisation by making necessary representations to the respondents.
The representations made by the petitioner were not decided by the respondents.
The petitioner, therefore, served legal notices dated 2322002 and 1992002 (Annexures P-3 and P-4) respectively.
When the aforesaid legal notices were not decided, the petitioner filed C.W.P.
No. 19656 of 2002 which was decided by this Court on 12122002.
A direction was issued to respondent No. 2 to take a final decision on the legal notice dated 1992002 by passing a well reasoned speaking order within a period of three months from the date of receipt of a certified copy of the order.
The respondents have passed order Annexure P-5.
It has been observed in the order that the petitioner has undoubtedly completed more than 3 years service as on 3111996 and was in service on 3111996.
He had also completed 240 days in each year.
However, he remained absent from duty w.e.f.
1041993 to 1751993 i.e. 38 days and from 11.2.1994 to 133.1994 i.e. for 31 days.
Although the petitioner has submitted medical certificate for condonation of the period of absence, the request was not accepted by the respondents.
The respondents have, therefore, rejected the claim of the petitioner for regularisation on the ground that there is a break in the service for more than one month in a year.
The written statement has been filed by the respondents stating the same reasons for declining the relief to the petitioner.
3.
Mr. Namit Kumar has submitted that even if the position as depicted by the respondents is accepted, the petitioner would still be entitled to regularisation.
He submits that petitioner would be entitled to regularisation provided the petitioner had completed 3 years of service even on a day prior to 3111996.
According to the learned counsel, the cut off date of 31l1996 is wholly arbitrary and cannot be sustained as it would be violative of Article 14 of the Constitution of India.
In support of his submissions, he relied upon a Division Bench judgment of this Court rendered in Kavita Gandhi v. State of Haryana and others, 1997(4) Recent Service Judgement 760.
In the aforesaid case, the petitioner approached the respondents for regularisation on the basis of the following service record :-  31190 to 30191 No break and completed more than 240 days service.
31191 to 30192 -do- 31.1.92 to 30193 -do- 31193 to 30194 -do- 31.1.95 to 30196 -do- 4.
The respondents had declined the relief on the ground that there was 41 days break in service of the petitioner from 691994 to 16101994.
After considering the matter, the Division Bench has observed that in order to become eligible for consideration for regularisation of service under a particular notification of the State Government where the prescribed years of completed service with reference to the cut off date mentioned in the notification applies to such employees also who have completed those number of years of service prior to the cut off date and not necessarily, immediately preceding the said date.
In coming to the aforesaid conclusion, the Division Bench also relied upon a FB judgment of this Court in Jagdish Lal and others v. State of Punjab and others, 1988(2) PLR 43.
In the aforesaid judgment, while considering a similar situation, the Full Bench had observed as under :-  "The very object of deciding to regularise the services of ad hoc employees, as seen from some of the earlier orders was : "Whereas by continuation of the ad hoc appointments made as above as an administrative necessity, the ad hoc employees have acquired necessary experience and their ouster after a considerable period of service would entail hardship to ad hoc employees as a whole and accentuate the problem of unemployment, the President of India is pleased to decide...." If this was the very object of regularising the service, we are unable to find any reason as to why we should take in that the continuous service of one year immediately preceding April 1, 1985, was alone to be taken into account."
5.
It is not disputed in the present case that the petitioner had completed 3 years service even prior to 3111996.
He has been in continuous employment of the respondents since 121989.
The relief of the regularisation has been denied to the petitioner only on account of 2 breaks in service which have been mentioned above.
6.
In view of the law laid down by the Division Bench in the case of Kavita Gandhi (supra) and in the judgment of the Full Bench rendered in Jagdish Lal's case (supra) the relief of regularisation cannot be denied to the petitioner.
7.
In view of the above, this and connected petitions are allowed.
Order Annexure P-5, in all the three petitions, is quashed.
The respondents are directed to pass necessary orders for regularising the petitioners' service with effect from the dates they are eligible.
The consequential benefits be paid to the petitioners within two months after the passing of order of regularisation.
Petitions allowed.
