HS Bedi, J - By this judgment, I propose to dispose of Civil Writ Petitions No. 2780 of 1980 and 868 of 1981.
The facts have been taken from the earlier writ petition.
2.
The petitioners were appointed as Clerks in the Punjab Civil Secretariat, Chandigarh, on ad hoc basis during the years 1975-76 and their services were duly regularised vide order dated May 3, 1977 with effect from 1st of April, 1977.
Respondent Nos. 2 to 28 were recruited directly, as Clerks through the Subordinate Service Selection Board and they joined service as such on April 14, 1977.
The claim of the petitioners is that the petitioners having been regularly appointed with effect from 1st of April, 1977, were entitled to claim seniority over the respondents in view of the Rule 9 of the Punjab Civil Secretariat (State Service Class III) Rules 1976 (hereinafter called the `Rules').
3.
In fact, the petitioners placing reliance on various decisions of the Supreme Court have contended that once their services had been regularised, they were entitled to count their ad hoc service as well in the determination of their seniority.
It appears that as the respondents were not giving the due seniority to the petitioners and continued to show the private-respondents to be senior in the gradation list annexures P-1, a representation dated May 7, 1980 was submitted to the official respondents but the same was declined vide order dated August 5, 1980 annexure P-4 to the petition.
The petitioners have impugned the seniority list annexure P-1 as also the order of rejection of the representation annexure P-4 before this Court.
4.
In reply to the petition, the respondent-State has taken the stand that the period of ad hoc service could not be counted towards seniority in view of Rule 9 of the Rules.
It has also been contended placing reliance on annexure R-1 dated March 15, 1962, that seniority of the candidates recommended by the Subordinate Service Selection Board, was required to be determined with reference to the date of issue of the Board's letter recommending the candidates for appointment and not from the date when they were actually appointed.
A supplementary affidavit was also filed on behalf of respondent No. 1. i.e. the State of Punjab in which the dates on which respondent Nos. 2 to 28 were recommended by the Subordinate Service Selection Board were set out.
The details are given below :-  Respondent Nos. 2 and 3, 8.2.1977 Respondent Nos. 4 to 8, 17.2.1977 Respondent No. 9, 21.1.1977 Respondent No. 10, 22.2.1977 Respondent No. 11, 16.12.1976 Respondent No. 12 to 28, 6/7.4.1977 It will be seen that the recommendations with respect to the appointment of respondent Nos. 12 to 28 was made on 6/7.4.1977 which is after the date on which the services of the petitioners were regularised.
The respondent-State apparently finding it difficult to meet the case of the petitioners with regard to respondent Nos. 12 to 28 even while taking shelter under Annexure R-1, accordingly, brought down respondent Nos. 12 to 28 in the seniority and put them junior to the petitioners.
The said respondents aggrieved by the aforesaid action have filed the connected Civil Writ Petition No. 868 of 1981 in this Court.
5.
Mr. Patwalia, learned counsel for the petitioners, in Civil Writ Petition No. 2780 of 1980, has placed reliance on rule 9 in order to contend that the seniority inter se of the members of the service in each cadre was to be determined by the length of continuous service in that cadre of the service and as such the petitioners, having had their services regularised with effect from 1st of April, 1977, would be deemed to be senior to the respondents, who were admittedly appointed after that date.
Elaborating the argument further he has urged that once the services of an ad-hoc employee have been regularised the period of ad-hoc appointment must also count towards determining seniority.
He has relied on judgments of the Supreme Court in support of his case Direct Recruit Class II Engineering Officers Association and others v. State of Maharashtra and others, JT 1990(2) SC 264 and GP Doval and others v. Chief Secretary Government of UP and others, 1984(2) S.L.R.
555.
The learned counsel for the respondents have relied upon annexure P-1 to the petition and also to the Single Bench judgment of this Court rendered in Civil Writ Petition No. 3120 of 1987 decided on 1871991, Jagdish Kumar etc. v. State of Punjab etc. in order to controvert the stand of the petitioners.
After hearing learned counsel for the parties I find that there is substance in the arguments raised by learned counsel for the petitioners.
It will be clear from a reading of Rule 9 already referred to above that continuous service on a post will be a determining factor with regard to the question of seniority.
Admittedly the petitioners had their services regularised with effect from 1st April, 1977, whereas the respondents were appointed subsequently.
Annexure R-1 which stipulates that date of recommendation made by the Subordinate Service Selection Board would be the relevant date in determining the question of appointment, cannot have any force in view of the statutory Rule 9 ibid.
This has been held in a Single Bench decision of this Court rendered in Sinder Pal v. State of Punjab, 1992(1) SCT 56 (P&H) : 1991 R.S.J.
372, where a similar rule and the instructions Annexure P-1 to the present petition were under discussion.
This is what the Court had to say :  "A bare perusal of Rule 11 of the Rules shows that the seniority of the members of the service inter se is determined by the date of their continuous appointment in the service but in the case of members recruited by direct appointment the seniority of the members shall be determined in accordance with the order of merit in which they are placed by the Board at the time of selection and persons appointed as a result of an earlier selection must rank senior to those selected as a result of a subsequent selection.
This rule makes no reference to the date of recommendation by the Board and the instructions dated 1531962 referred to in para No. 8 of the written statement cannot over-ride the statutory rules governing the fixation of seniority."
This being the situation the judgment in Jagdish Kumar case (supra) would not have any relevance as in that matter, there were no rules which were under consideration of the Court and that matter was decided entirely on the basis of executive instructions.
6.
Moreover, I find that even as per annexure R-1, which is on the file of Civil Writ Petition No. 868 of 1981, the Government had itself decided in Para No. 5 as under :-  "After approval by the Appointing Authority the regularisation of their appointments shall date back to 1st April, 1977 from which date their seniority shall be determined vis-a-vis candidates appointed on regular basis after selection through the prescribed agencies."
In view of the aforesaid paragraph, even if there was some arguments in favour of the petitioners by virtue of annexure R-1 that too, has been met.
7.
In am also of the view that keeping in view the judgment of the Supreme Court in Direct Recruit Class II Engineering Officers Association and others v. State of Maharashtra and others (supra) that once the services of the petitioners had been regularised they would relate back to the date of their initial appointment and as such the ad hoc service of the petitioners would also have to be kept in view in determining the seniority and other benefits.
For this reason as well, the petition must succeed.
In the context, relevant paragraph 47(B) of the aforesaid judgment is reproduced below :  "If the initial appointment is not made by following the procedure laid down by the rules but the appointee continues in the post uninterruptedly till the regularisation of his service in accordance with the rules, the period of officiating service will be counted."
8.
For the reasons recorded above, Civil Writ Petition No. 2780 is allowed whereas Civil Writ Petition No. 868 of 1981 is dismissed.
There will, however, be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
