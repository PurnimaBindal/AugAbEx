TS Doabia, J - Heard counsel.
As per the petitioner, he has completed more than 240 days of service and therefore, his service could not be brought to an end unless and until he was paid retrenchment compensation under section 25(F) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.
2.
The respondents have taken a stand that in the year 1988-89 from April to March, the petitioner had actually rendered 209 days of service.
According to the respondent, the petitioner has not completed 240 days of service.
3.
It be seen that in the matter of calculating 240 days, paid holidays and Sundays have to be taken into consideration.
In one year, there would be 52 Sundays and if these are added then there total number of days on which petitioner would have worked comes to 261 days.
For this no proof is required.
The above view was expressed by the Supreme Court of India.
In the case of Workmen of American Express International Banking Corporation v. Management of American Express International Banking Corporation, AIR 1986 Supreme Court 458.
It was observed :-  "That the qualification for relief under Section 25-F is that he should be a workman in an industry and has been in continuous service for not less than one year under an employer.
What is continuous service has been defined and explained in Section 25-B of the Act.
In view of sub-section(2) of Section 25-B the workman shall be deemed to be in continuous services if he has "actually worked under the employer" for particular period.
The expression "actually worked under employer" cannot mean those days only when the workman worked with hammer, sickle or pen.
But must necessarily comprehend all those days during which he was in the employment of the employer and for which he had been paid wages either under express or implied contract of service or by compulsion of statute, standing orders etc.
Thus, Sundays and other paid holidays should be taken into account for the purpose of reckoning the total number of days on which the workman could be said to have actually worked."
In this view of the matter, it is a case where the petitioner would be deemed to have been completed more than 240 days of service.
The retrenchment compensation having not been paid, he would be entitled to reinstatement.
Such was the view expressed by the Supreme Court in the case of State Bank of India v. N Sundar Moni, AIR 1976 SC 1111, Hindustan Steel Ltd v. Presiding Officer, Labour Court, Orissa, AIR 1976(4) SC 222, Santosh Gupta v. State Bank of Patiala, AIR 1980(3) SC 340, Mohanlal v. Bharat Electronics Ltd, AIR 1981(1) SC 225 andKarnatak SRTC v. M Baraiah, AIR 1981(1) SC 2445 and Punjab Land Development and Reclamation Corporation Ltd, Chandigarh v. Presiding Officer, Labour Court, Chandigarh and others, AIR 1990(3) SC 682.
There is no dispute that enforcement can be made under Article 226 of the Constitution of India.
See : Makhtyar Singh v. Food Corporation of India, and others, MPLJ 902.
This petition is accordingly allowed.
The action of the respondents in bringing an end to the service tenure of the petitioner is set aside.
The petitioner, however, would not be entitled to any backwages, as these cannot be determined under Article 226 of the Constitution of India.
The petitioner to report before the respondent No. 1 on 1st of June, 1995.
