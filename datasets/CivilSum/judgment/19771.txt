AL Bahri and VK Bali, JJ.
- In this writ petition filed under Articles 226 and 227 of the Constitution Smt. Sandhya Negi, an Assistant in the office of Punjab State Leather Development Corporation Ltd, Chandigarh, challenges order Annexures P.11 and P12 whereby her seniority has been disturbed.
2.
The petitioner initially worked as sales girl with effect from January 1, 1982.
On August 18, 1982 she was appointed as a Typist on a consolidated salary of Rs. 500/- by order Annexure P.1.
She was made regular on December 1, 1982 and fixed in the pay-scale of Rs. 400-600.
Respondent No. 2 Sh.
Sat Dev Sharma was appointed as Clerk on 24111982.
Before that he was working as Peon.
The petitioner was placed in the senior scale of pay of Clerks on August 18, 1986 and respondent No. 2 was placed in that scale on November 24, 1986.
In this manner the petitioner was considered as senior.
A tentative seniority list was circulated on October 17, 1988.
Since no objections were filed, the same was finalised on February 13, 1989.
In these lists the petitioner ranked at Sr. No. 1 and respondent No. 2 at Sr. No. 2.
On August 18, 1988 the petitioner was promoted as Assistant and respondent No. 2 was promoted as Assistant on November 24, 1988.
3.
On August 4, 1989-Annexure P.9, show-cause notice was served on the petitioner as to why she should not be shown junior as she was made regular on December 1, 1982 whereas respondent No. 2 was appointed on November 24, 1982.
Reply to the show-cause notice was submitted by the petitioner which resulted in passing of the impugned order Annexure P11 dated March 1, 1990.
The seniority was assigned to the petitioner with effect from December 1, 1982 and thus she was made junior to respondent Sat Dev Sharma.
Likewise promotion orders of these two persons were changed vide order Annexure P12 on April 2, 1990.
4.
The sole question for consideration in this case is as to whether the period of service of the petitioner from August 18, 1982 to December 1, 1982 is also to be taken into consideration while determining her seniority.
No Rules on the subject regarding determination of seniority of employees of the respondent-Corporation have been produced.
In the absence of any such Rules the general principle that length of service would determine seniority, would be applicable.
Admittedly, the petitioner was appointed as Typist-cum-Clerk on August 18, 1982.
The mere fact that she was appointed on a consolidated salary of Rs. 500/- will not change the character of the post held by her or that she could not be held to hold the post.
It was on that very post that subsequently order of regularisation was passed on December 1, 1982 and she was placed in the pay scale of Rs. 400-600.
By paying her a consolidated salary of Rs. 500/- it could not be said that the post of Typist-cum-Clerk which she was holding was in any manner lower in rank to the post of clerk on which she was made regular subsequently.
Furthermore, as per the narration of the facts, the respondent-Corporation all along treated the petitioner as senior and gave her promotions first at the stage of placing her in the senior scale of pay of Clerks and thereafter to the post of Assistant.
Apart from that a seniority list was circulated on October 17, 1988 showing the petitioner at Sr. No. 1 and respondent No. 2 Sat Dev Sharma at Sr. No. 2.
This tentative seniority was not challenged as no objections were filed by respondent No. 2 and it was finalised on February 13, 1989.
What transpired subsequently that made the respondent-Corporation to issue show-cause notice is not clear.
Maybe, somebody interested in respondent No. 2 raked up the issue and succeeded in getting the show-cause notice issued.
However, on merits we find that seniority of the petitioner and respondent No. 2 was rightly fixed showing petitioner at Sr. No. 1 and there was no justification to interfere therewith and in consequence to change the order of promotion.
For the reasons recorded above, this writ petition is allowed.
Orders Annexures P.11 and P12 are quashed.
The petitioner will get costs from respondent No. 1 the Corporation.
Costs are quantitied at Rs. 1000/-.
Order accordingly.
