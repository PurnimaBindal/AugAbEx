RS Mongia, J - This judgment of ours shall dispose of Letters Patent Appeal No. 97 of 1982 as well as Letters Patent Appeal Nos. 1251 of 1983 and 322 of 1984.
All these three appeals have been filed by the State of Punjab.
L.P.A. No. 97 of 1982 is against C.W.P.
No. 3340 of 1972.
CWP Nos. 5791 of 1981 and 2600 of 1983 (against which L.P.A. Nos. 1251 of 1983 and 322 of 1984 respectively have been filed), were allowed as being covered by the judgment in C.W.P.
No. 3340 of 1972 against which L.P.A. No. 97 of 1982 has been filed.
2.
The writ-petitioners (now respondents) were employed as Tubewell Operators in the Punjab Irrigation Department.
They were drawing salary in the grade of Rs. 45-1-65.
It was alleged by the writ petitioners that the Tubewell Operators serving in the Public Health Department of the Punjab State were given a pay rise inasmuch as they were allowed to draw the salary in the grade of Rs. 110-180 with effect from 11th August, 1971.
It was further mentioned that the Tubewell Operators of the Public Health Department had raised an industrial dispute and their pay was raised with effect from 1st February, 1968.
The writ-petitioners made a grievance in the writ petition that they were doing the same type of work as the Tubewell Operators in the Public Health Department and they should also be given the same pay scale i.e. Rs. 110-180 from the date the same was given to the Tubewell Operators in the Public Health Department.
The learned Single Judge allowed the writ petition on 5th February, 1981 and directed that the petitioners be given the same pay scale as was being given to the Tubewell Operators of the Public Health Department from the date it was given to them.
3.
A Review Application No. 60 of 1981 was filed by the State Government against the judgment of the learned Single Judge dated 5th February, 1981, inter alia, it was said that in the Irrigation Department, the Tubewell Operators are to be Marticulates, whereas in the Public Health Department where they were known as Pump Drivers, the minimum qualifications were to pass the One Year Electrician Certificate from the Industrial Training Institute and in fact there were four types of Pump Drivers in the Public Health Department :-  (i) Pump Drivers Grade II - Rs. 100-160.
Qualification : Certificate in Electrical Course, from ITI  (ii) Pump Drivers Grade I - Promotion from Grade II in the scale of Rs. 110-180.
(iii) Pump Drivers Class 'A' - Rs. 100-160, and  (iv) Pump Drivers Class 'B' - Rs. 80 - 120.
4.
With effect from 1st January, 1978, the Pump Drivers Grade I, Grade II and Pump Drivers Class 'A' were amalgamated and put in a scale of Rs. 400-600 and Pump Drivers Class 'B' were put in the pay scale of Rs. 325-495.
The case set up in the Review Petition was that at the most the Pump Operators of the Irrigation Department could be equated with Pump Drivers Class 'B' of the Public Health Department.
To support all these assertions, official documents were put on record along with the Review Petition.
However, the Review Petition was dismissed by the same learned Single Judge on 12th November, 1981, with the observations that the State should have made queries from the concerned department before the written statement was filed in the original writ petition.
Since the department concerned had started collecting the information after about 8 months of the date of the judgment dated 5th September, 1981, there was no justification to allow the review as the evidence on the basis of which the review was sought, could be collected by the department concerned earlier and there was no explanation coming forth on the pleas and the documents which were relied upon in the Review Petition could not be made part of the written statement.
5.
It will be interesting to note that similar writ petitions were also filed by same other similarly situated persons like the writ petitioners.
These were CWP Nos. 2769 to 2771 of 1981.
These petitions were put up for hearing before the Division Bench and the learned counsel appearing for the State conceded that these were squarely covered by the judgment in C.W.P.
No. 3340 of 1972, decided on 5th February, 1981.
The Division Bench allowed all these writ petitions on 7th August, 1981.
The Special Leave Petitions by the State Government against the above said judgments (S.L.P.
Nos. 9195 to 9797 of 1981) were dismissed by the Supreme Court on 19th February, 1982.
6.
After the Review petition of the State was dismissed on 12th November, 1981, the State filed the present appeal (L.P.A. No. 97 of 1982).
The Admitting Bench passed the following order :-  "Admitted.
Stay operation of the judgment under appeal ad-interim."
It will be interesting to note that the heading of the Letters Patent Appeal reads as under :-  "Letters Patent Appeal under Clause X of the Letters Patent, against the judgment of Hon'ble Mr. Justice MR Sharma dated November 12, 1981 in the above review petition in C.W.P.
No. 3340 of 1972."
The prayer clause of the Letters Patent Appeal reads as under :-  "It is, therefore, prayed that the Letters Patent Appeal of the appellant be accepted and the operation of the orders of the judgment dated 521981 and 12111981 may kindly be stayed."
7.
Some of the writ petitioners filed Contempt Petition in this Court (C.O.C.P.
No. 33 of 1983) and the learned Judge hearing the contempt petition, held that the Letters Patent Appeal was against the judgment dated 12th November, 1981 in the Review Petition and was not an appeal against the main judgment dated 5th February, 1981 and since the Admitting Bench had stayed the operation of the judgment under appeal, (i.e. judgment dated 12th November, 1981 in Review Petition), therefore the judgment dated 5th February, 1981 was in operation and had not been stayed.
As a consequence of that all the petitioners and other similarly situated Tubewell Operators got the benefit of the judgment and were paid the arrears also.
8.
In view of the documents and the material brought in the Review Petition, we were inclined to take a view that the petitioners were not entitled to the same pay scale i.e. Rs. 110-180 as was being enjoyed by the Pump Operators Grade I in the Public Health Department, but in view of the two facts i.e. in C.W.P.
No. 2769 to 2771 of 1981, teh Division Bench had granted the relief to those writ-petitioners, though on the concession of the State Counsel, which judgment had been upheld by the Supreme Court and if we tken a different view now the result would be that the writ petitioners in C.W.P.
No. 2769 to 2771 of 1981 would get higher pay scale, whereas the writ petitioners in the writ petitions out of which the present LPAs.
have arisen, would get a lower pay scale.
Otherwise also there will be two contradictory judgments of this Court.
Moreover, we find that the Letters Patent Appeals filed by the State are against the order in the Review Petition and not against the main judgment dated 5th February, 1981.
This was also so held by the learned Single Judge who was hearing the contempt petition.
That being the position now we refrain from upsetting the judgment of the learned Single Judge dated 5th February, 1981.
9.
Before parting with the judgment, we would like to observe that the State counsel should have been careful before conceding before the Division Bench in CWP Nos. 2769 to 2771 of 1981 that the matter was covered by a Single Bench judgment, because his concession has affected the decision on merit in the present letters patent appeals.
Further the State should have been careful in filing the letters patent appeals not only against the judgment in the Review Petition, but should have also filed an appeal against the main judgment of the learned Single Judge, dated 5th February, 1981.
10.
Resultantly, the Letters Patent Appeals fail and are dismissed, leaving the parties to bear their own costs.
Appeal dismissed.
