VM Jain, J - This regular second appeal has been filed by the plaintiff, against the judgment and decree passed by the learned Additional District Judge, whereby the appeal filed by the defendants was accepted, judgment and decree passed by the trial Court were set aside and the suit of the plaintiff was dismissed.
2.
Facts of the case in brief are that Joginder Singh, plaintiff, filed a suit for declaration and mandatory injunction against defendants, alleging therein that he was wrongly retired on 31121978 even though the should have been retired on 31121980 at the age of 60 years, being an employee of the PEPSU State.
It was alleged that the plaintiff was entitled to two years service benefit with consequential benefits along with interest etc.
It was alleged that the plaintiff was born on 11.1.1921 and was retired on 31121978 as Canal Patwari at the age of 58 years.
It was alleged that plaintiff had joined as Canal Patwari during PEPSU regime and that under PEPSU Government, age of superannuation was 60 years, so far as inferior service was concerned.
It was alleged that on 11111956, plaintiff was allocated to Punjab State and retired at the age of 58 years.
It was alleged that service conditions of the plaintiff could not be altered to his disadvantage by the Punjab State, and as such action of the defendants in retiring the plaintiff at the age of 58 years instead of 60 years was illegal and void.
Said suit was contested by the defendants by filing written statement.
It was alleged that service of plaintiff could not be said to be inferior service, as cadre of Patwari relates to Class III service and that age of superannuation of plaintiff was 58 years, like other Class III services.
It was alleged that the plaintiff was allocated to Punjab Government as Class III employee.
It was alleged that the plaintiff was rightly retired at the age of 58 years.
After hearing both sides, learned trial court decreed the suit of the plaintiff and defendants were directed to refix the pay of the plaintiff, keeping in view his remaining service of two years and to allow pecuniary benefits towards his pension.
Aggrieved against the judgment and decree passed by the trial court, defendants filed appeal.
Learned Additional District Judge, after hearing both sides, accepted the appeal, set aside the judgment and decree of the trial court and dismissed the suit of the plaintiff.
Aggrieved against the same, plaintiff filed present regular second appeal in this court.
3.
Notice of motion was issued.
I have heard the learned counsel for the parties and have gone through the record carefully.
4.
It has been submitted before me by the learned counsel for the plaintiff- appellant that even if the suit of the plaintiff was barred by time, in respect of arrears of salary for two years from 58 years to 60 years, yet suit of the plaintiff could not be said to be time barred, in respect of arrears of pension, as it has a continuing effect and the plaintiff was entitled to re- fixation of the pension, keeping in view the retirement age of 60 years instead of 58 years.
Reliance has been placed on the law laid down by this court, in the cases reported as State of Punjab v. Surjan Singh, 1996(4) SCT 771 (P&H) : 1997(1) Recent Services Judgments 776, Mangat Rai v. State of Punjab, 2000(3) SCT 230 (P&H) : 2000(2) Recent Services Judgments 293, and Bidhi Chand v. State of Punjab and others, 1999(1) SCT 481 (P&H) : 1998(4) Recent Services Judgments 448.
5.
On the other hand, learned counsel appearing for the defendants- respondents submitted before me that plaintiff is not entitled even to the refixation of his pension keeping in view the retirement age as 60 years from 58 years, in view of the fact that suit filed by the plaintiff regarding age of retirement was hopelessly barred by time and even otherwise plaintiff would not be entitled to refixation of his pension, once his claim regarding age of retirement has been declined being barred by time.
Reliance been placed on the law laid down by this court, in the cases bearing 2001(3) SCT 64 (P&H) (DB) : CWP No. 14367 of 2000, Mohan Lal and others v. State of Punjab and another, decided on 2082002, and CWP No. 9474 of 1999, Pyara Lal and another v. State of Punjab and others, decided on 2882002.
Reliance has also been placed on Jagdish Chander Walia v. State of Punjab, 1996(4) Recent Services Judgments 336, and the law laid down by the Hon'ble Supreme Court, in the case reported as State of Punjab and others v. Gurdev Singh, Ashok Kumar, 1991(3) SCT 93 (SC) : AIR 1991 SC 2219.
6.
After hearing the learned counsel for the parties and after perusing the record, I find no merit in this appeal and the same is liable to be dismissed.
The plaintiff had retired from service as Canal Patwari on 31121978 on attaining the age of 58 years.
Case of the plaintiff is that he should have been retired at the age of 60 years on 31121980.
Plaintiff had filed the present suit on 27.4.1999 i.e. after more than 20 years of his retirement.
Learned appellate court had rightly found that the suit filed by the plaintiff was hopelessly time barred not only in respect of age of his retirement but also in respect of refixation of his pension keeping the retirement age as 60 years instead of 58 years.
If the suit filed by the plaintiff, with regard to his retirement at the age of 58 years, is liable to be dismissed being barred by time, in my opinion, the plaintiff would not be entitled to claim the refixation of his pension on the ground that he was to retire at the age of 60 years and not at the age of 58 years.
In fact, the plaintiff had retired at the age of 58 years and the claim of the plaintiff that he should have been retired at the age of 60 years was liable to be dismissed at time barred.
Under such circumstances, there would be no occasion to allow the refixation of his pension keeping in view the retirement age as 60 years instead of 58 years.
Various authorities relied upon by the learned counsel for the plaintiff appellant, in my opinion, would have no application to the facts of the present case, especially when in the present case, there would be no occasion to refix the pension once the claim of the plaintiff regarding date of retirement has been found to be time barred.
Under such circumstances, retirement of the plaintiff at the age of 58 years stands affirmed.
That being so, there would be no occasion for the refixation of the pension of the plaintiff-appellant.
7.
In CWP No. 14367 of 2000 (supra), petitioners were Canal Patwaris, who had retired on attaining the age of 58 years during the period from 1990 to 1995 and they had filed the writ petition in October 2000 to the effect that in fact they were entitled to continue in service upto the age of 60 years, as they were recruited in erstwhile State of PEPSU, where they were entitled to continue in service upto the age of the 60 years and that the action of the State Government in retiring them at the age of 58 years was illegal.
The petitioners in the said writ petition had claimed full salary for the period of two years.
A Division Bench of this court had dismissed the writ petition holding that petitioners had retired almost more than 5 to 10 years prior to the filing of the writ petition and that there was inordinate long delay in approaching the court.
Even on merits, it was found that claim of the petitioners that they belong to Class II service and were entitled to continue till the age of 60 years was not tenable, even under the rules governing them in the erstwhile State of PEPSU.
Furthermore, contention of the petitioners that even if the claim for pay etc. is declined, benefit should be given for the purpose of calculating retiral benefits, was declined by the Division Bench it was held that the once petitioners had lost right to challenge the date of retirement, they could not claim revision of their pay or refixation of the retiral benefits.
Reliance was also placed on the decisions of their Lordships of the Supreme Court in the case of State of Haryana & Others v. Amar Nath Bansal, 1997(2) SCT 685 (SC) : JT 1997(1) SC 374.
The law laid down in CWP No. 14367 of 20000 (supra) was followed by the Division Bench of this Court in CWP No. 9474 of 1999 (supra), which also pertains to Canal Patwaris in the erstwhile State of PEPSU, who had retired from service in the year 1991 on attaining the age of 58 years.
The writ petition filed by them in the year 1999, almost after 8 years was dismissed by a Division Bench of this Court, placing reliance on the case of Mohan Lal (supra), as also on another judgment of this Court, in 1994(1) SCT 382 (P&H) (DB) : CWP No. 8186 of 1994 Krishan Lal Aggarwal v. State of Punjab and others, decided on 891994.
8.
In view of the law laid down by the Division Bench of this Court, in the cases referred to above, in my opinion, the plaintiff appellant would not be entitled to claim even refixation in the pensionary benefits or the payment of arrears in this regard.
In view of the above, finding no merit in this appeal the same is hereby dismissed.
Appealm dismissed.
