MM Kumar, J - Challenge in this petition filed under Article 226 of the Constitution is to the order dated 9101984 passed by the Chief Executive Officer of the Khadi and Village Industry Commission (for brevity, `the Commission') rejecting the claim of the petitioner for the pay scale of Rs. 250-400 w.e.f.
1641957 as recommended by the Patel Committee report despite the fact that it was duly adopted by the Commission.
The Chief Executive Officer also rejected the claim of the petitioner that the post of Assistant Organizer in Village Oil Industry is to be treated as equivalent to the post of Assistant Organizer in Khadi section and that the petitioner was entitled to all the benefits regarding seniority and promotion.
The aforementioned order was passed in pursuance to the directions issued by this Court on 1051977 in Civil Writ Petition No. 2981 of 1975 and further directions issued on 2681982 in Letters Patent Appeal No. 243 of 1977.
2.
Brief facts of the case necessary for the disposal of the instant petition are that the petitioner a Science Graduate was a permanent employee of the Health Department of the erstwhile State of Punjab.
He applied for the post of State Development Officer as it was known at that time in the erstwhile Khadi and Village Industry Board (for brevity, `the Board').
His application was forwarded by the Director, Health Services, Punjab vide his Endst.
No. 6263/EL/2 dated 30101955 to the Zonal Director of the Board.
The Director, Health Services, Punjab, imposed a condition that in case of his selection to the post of State Development Officer (Village Oil Industry) of the Board, he would have to resign from the Health Department (Annexure P-1).
But later on, the Director, Health Services did not adhere to the condition of resignation and agreed for retention of his lien in the Health Department vide his memo No. 65/E-L-2 dated 611956 (Annexure P-2).
The petitioner was selected as a direct recruit for the post of Development Officer at a consolidated salary of Rs. 200/- pm in the Board and an appointment letter dated 1211956 was issued (Annexure P-3).
The petitioner joined as such in the Board on 1631956.
Board taken over by `the Commission'  3.
The Parliament enacted the Khadi and Village Industry Act, 1956 which was enforced on 2491956.
Under Section 4 of the Act, the Commission was constituted on 141956.
On 241957, the Commission passed a resolution to take over the staff of the Board on the same pay which they were drawing in the Board.
The petitioner along with others was taken over by the Commission.
Equivalence of post  4.
The case of the petitioner as pleaded in the petition is that he was transferred to Khadi Industry and promoted as Assistant Organiser, Peripetatic Parties (Khadi Part) (for brevity, `PP Scheme') on a consolidated salary of Rs. 250/- vide order dated 3031957.

The entries in the service book have also been relied upon to substantiate the claim of equivalence of two posts by the petitioner besides placing reliance on documents Annexures P-5 and P-6.
The question of revision of pay scale of the employees of the Board and later the Commission was under active consideration of the authorities at the time when the petitioner joined the service of the Board in the year 1956.
A committee known as Jhaver Bhai Patel Committee recommended the revision of pay scales w.e.f.
141957 in respect of regular posts under the Commission.
The recommendations made by the Patel Committee were accepted by the Commission vide its resolution No. 7 dated 13/1481957.

15.
I have thoughtfully considered the rival contentions raised by the learned counsel for the parties and am of the view that the following issues would emerge:  i) Whether the appointment of the petitioner to the Village Oil Industry Section of the Board was from the open market or he was on deputation being an employee of the Punjab Government ?
ii) Whether the petitioner was transferred from Village Oil Section to PP Scheme or his appointment to the PP Scheme was fresh one ?
iii) Whether the petitioner is entitled to the benefits of the recommendations made by the Patel Commission w.e.f.
141957.
If yes, then whether he is entitled to be placed in the pay scale of Rs. 250-400 w.e.f.
1641957 ?
and iv) Whether the post of Assistant Organiser under the PP Scheme should be deemed to be equivalent to Assistant Organiser in the Board.
Regarding (i)  16.
The question as to whether the petitioner was on deputation from the Punjab Government to the Board/Commission is to be determined by making reference to various documents on record.
On 31101955 (Annexure P1), the application of the petitioner for the post of Organiser in Oils Department was forwarded to the Zonal Director (Northern Zone) All India Khadi and Village Industries Board, Ambala Cantt by the Director, Health Services Punjab.
There was a stipulation made in the communication (Annexure P1) that in the event of his selection for the post applied for, he would have to resign from the post of Storekeeper held by him in the Health Department.
However, on 811956 (Annexure P2) a modification was issued that his lien would be retained for the post of Storekeeper in case he is selected for any of the posts applied for by him.
On 1211956 (Annexure P.3) the petitioner was selected as State Development Officer in the Village Oil Industry of the All India Khadi and Village Industries Board, Bombay.
Accordingly, the petitioner joined on 1631956 in pursuance of the order dated 1211956.
The letter dated 1211956 inter-alia provides that the petitioner was being appointed as State Development Officer in Village Oil Industry against one of the 20 posts sanctioned by the Government of India, Ministry of Commerce and Industry vide letter dated 2741955 read with their letter No. 42/Cot.Ind(12), 55 dated 8/1691955 for implementation of the Board's Scheme for development of the Village Oil Industry.
It is also pertinent to mention that he was put on probation for a period of three months and during his period of probation his services were liable to be terminated without any notice on the ground of unsatisfactory work or conduct.
After the period of three months of satisfactory completion of the probation period his services were to be continued on the temporary post.
He was, thereafter, transferred to Khadi Industry Board and promoted as Assistant Organiser in the PP Scheme on a consolidation salary of Rs. 250/- pm on 3031957 vide Annexure P4.
17.
A close analysis of the letter of appointment alongwith documents Annexures P1 and P2 would show that the petitioner was appointed to the Village Oil Industry Section of the Board from the open market and that he was not on deputation from the Punjab Government.
It is well known that a person who is on deputation is never put on probation and moreover he becomes entitled to some deputation allowance which ordinarily is reflected in the order of appointment.
It is further evident that question of his termination by the Board is recorded in the appointment letter (Annexure P.3) would not arise if he was on deputation because in such a case the deputation employee is repatriated to his parent department.
The retention of the lien in the Department of Health is no basis to conclude that the petitioner was on deputation because the lien is retained just to ensure that if the probationer has not completed his period of probation satisfactorily and successfully then he may have secured position by coming back to the post on which he had lien.
This petition becomes evident from the communications Annexures P1 and P2.
It appears to be well settled that there is no automatic suspension of lien on the appointment of a civil servant to a permanent substantive post outside the cadre.
Moreover, the same view has been taken of the documents by Hon'ble Mr. Justice RN Mittal in his judgment dated 1051977 while deciding C.W.P.
No. 2981 of 1975.
In this regard reference may be made to Rule 314 of the Punjab Civil Services Rules.
Vol I, Part I, which became the subject-matter of interpretation in the case of TR Sharma v. Prithvi Singh, 1976(1) SLR 55 and also in the case of Ram Lal v. State of Punjab 1989(4) SCC 99.
In both the judgments, the view taken is that Rule 314 of the Rules cannot be interpreted to the detriment of an employee and the retention of lien of the petitioner, therefore, in the parent department of Health cannot lead to the conclusion that he was on deputation.
As observed earlier, the object of retention of lien in the parent department was only one namely that in case he fails in completing his probation period on the newly appointed post then he would re- partriate to the department of Health instead of being on the road.
Therefore, I am of the firm opinion that the further appropriate to mention that he tendered his resignation from the Department of Health which was accepted from the date of his joining the Board i.e. 1631956.
On that score also once the resignation of the petitioner has been accepted to enable him to join another post with proper permission then it has to be treated as a direct recruitment and cannot be considered as appointment on probation.
Issue No. (ii)  18.
The next question which requires determination is whether the petitioner was transferred from Village Oil Section to PP Scheme or his appointment to the PP Scheme was a fresh appointment could be answered with reference to order dated 3031957 (Annexure P4).
According to this order, the petitioner was transferred to the post of Assistant Organiser on a consolidated salary of Rs. 250/- pm w.e.f.
the date he was to assume charge.
The afore-mentioned order has been issued by the Chief Executive Officer of All India Khadi and Village Industries Board, Bombay who used to be Head of the Department and also dealt with the services in the Commission.
The word `transfer' has been used deliberately and not inadvertently.
The conclusion that he was transferred finds support from the endorsement made by the said Director while forwarding his appeal to the Chief Executive Officer with the following remarks as mentioned in para 15 of the petition :  "That it is a fact that Shri AR Lamba was transferred to Khadi Section in a higher scale in the interest of the Commission's work as services of an efficient person were required in Khadi Section in Zone without any request from him and also that he was given assurance that the post of Assistant Organiser PP (Khadi Part) to which he was being promoted was equivalent to the post of Assistant Organiser (VOI) next higher post to the post held by him in Village Oil Industry DO and would be revised at par with that..........." 19.
On 27111957, the Chief Executive Officer addressed a letter to the Director Health Services, Punjab from which an inference could be drawn that the petitioner was transferred from the Oil Section to Khadi Part, PP Scheme.
In the letter it has been mentioned that he was earlier appointed as State Development Officer in the Village Oil Industry and was subsequently promoted as Assistant Organiser on a pay of Rs. 250/- pm consolidated from which an inference can be drawn that petitioner was transferred from Oil Section to Khadi Section, PP Scheme.
Therefore, by no stretch of imagination it could be concluded that the appointment of the petitioner in PP Scheme was a fresh appointment.
For all intents and purposes it was an appointment by transfer.
Issue No. (iii)  20.
The question whether the petitioner was entitled to the benefit of the recommendations of the Patel Commission again has to be answered by referring to various documents and the report of the Patel Commission itself.
The report itself proclaims that Patel Commission was entrusted with the work of examining the whole question of revision of pay and allowances and was to formulate recommendations in this regard.
It is the admitted position that Khadi and Village Industries Commission was established on 141957 and that it was generally understood that the revised scales of pay and allowances would be applicable w.e.f.
141957 irrespective of the date on which they would be finally sanctioned.
When the report was submitted to the Chairman, Khadi and Village Industries Commission, Bombay, a letter was addressed to him on 2261957 stating that every effort has been made to examine the position as exhaustively as possible after discussing the same with a number of concerned persons.
It has further been pointed out that the Patel Commission had tried to implement the Government of India Pay Scales by recommending their adoptions as far as possible in the Khadi and Village Industries Commission by fitting the scales to the large number of posts with different designations.
The employees in the new pay scales were to draw full allowances admissible to them as per rules of the Government of India.
It was further observed that the Patel Commission had tried to cover the whole field staff but could not deal with question of staff under the Trading Schemes.
Similarly, he also could not deal with individual schemes of Khadi and Industry, although the general recommendation made by him could be adopted for the staff under those schemes with some modifications.
After the receipt of the report, the Commission passed a resolution in its meeting held on 13/1481957 and resolved that the decision of the Commission would apply to the staff under the Khadi Vibhag also.
In spite of the aforesaid recommendations, the petitioner was not given the benefit of new scale of pay w.e.f.
141957.
He made several representations and ultimately he was given some benefit of the revised scales w.e.f.
171963 the date on which his resignation in the Health Department was accepted w.e.f.
1631956.
21.
In this regard my attention has been invited to the judgment dated 1051977 rendered in CWP No. 2981 of 1975 (supra) and it has been argued that once the recommendations have been made by the Pay Commission then its recommendations have to be adopted for all employees.
One set of employees cannot be excluded by giving benefit to others.
In this regard reference may be made to a judgment of the Constitution Bench in the case of Purshottam Lal and ors. v. Union of India and ors., 1973(1) SLR 633.
Speaking for the Constitution Bench, CJ Sikri, observed as follows :  "We are unable to accept this contention.
The terms of reference are wide, and if any category of Government servants was excluded material should have been placed before this Court.
The Pay Commission has clearly stated that for the purposes of their enquiry they had taken all persons in the civil services of the Central Government or holding civil posts under that Government and paid out of the Consolidated Fund of India, to be Central Government employees.
It is not denied by Mr. Dhebar that the petitioners are paid out of the Consolidated Fund of India.
Mr. Dhebar contends that it was for the Government to accept the recommendations of the Pay Commission and while doing so to determine which categories of employees should be taken to have been included in the terms of reference, we are unable to appreciate this point.
Further the Government has made reference in respect of all Government employees or it has not.
But if it has made a reference in respect of all Government employees and it accepts the recommendations it is bound to implement the recommendations in respect of all Government employees.
If it does not implement the report regarding some employees only it commits a breach of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
This is what the Government has done as far as these petitioners are concerned."
22.
Once it is held that recommendations of the Patel Commission are applicable to the case of the petitioner then the question arises whether the petitioner was entitled to be placed in the grade of Rs. 250-400.
It has already been found that the petitioner was promoted by transfer from the post of State Development Officer Oil Section to the post of Assistant Organiser in the PP Scheme.
It is further pertinent to mention that in the Oil Section he was drawing a salary of Rs. 200/- pm whereas in the Khadi Part PP Scheme he was transferred and promoted at a consolidated salary of Rs. 250/- pm A perusal of the judgment delivered by the Ld.
Single Judge in CWP No. 2981 of 1975 (supra) shows a reference to the service book and entry therein that the petitioner was transferred on promotion and not against an equivalent post.
It is pertinent to mention that reference has also been made to the comments made by the Director on the appeal filed by the petitioner on 20.11.1968 as noticed in the paras above which further shows that he was transferred to Khadi Part PP Scheme in the higher scale.
Despite the fact that the matter was referred by the Commission to the Services Board at various stages and the Board after examining the relevant record had come to the same conclusion.
In the afore- mentioned circumstances there is no doubt that the petitioner was entitled to the scale of pay on his transfer to Khadi Part PP Scheme which he would have drawn had to got promotion in the Oil Section.
His next promotion in the Oil Section would have been as Assistant Organiser on which post he was already appointed in the Khadi Part PP Scheme.
It is relevant to point out that the petitioner was promoted at the time of transfer from the post of State Development Officer to the post of Assistant Organiser in the Khadi Part PP Scheme.
He was definitely given higher consolidated pay.
Therefore, the appropriate course would have been to allow the petitioner to the grade of Assistant Organiser in the Village Oil Industry.
The post of Assistant Organiser in the Village Oil Industry had been equated with that of Regional Organiser by the Patel Committee and the pay scale of Regional Organiser is Rs. 250-400 which was subsequently raised to Rs. 350-475.
In this regard reference may be made to the decision of the Services Board which reads as under :  "The Board came to the conclusion that Sri Lamba's case was not considered in all its aspects and felt that the post of A.O.P.P.
(Khadi Part) was higher than the State Development Officer (Village Oil Industry) and at least equal to the post of Assistant Organiser (Village Oil Industry) was not realised by the Administration.
The Board, therefore, decided to recommend to the Commission that Shri Lamba's pay scale should be revised w.e.f.
1641957 to that of the Regional Organiser in the pay scale of Rs. 250-400 which was subsequently revised to Rs.
350-475."
On the basis of the aforementioned facts and circumstances no doubt is left that the petitioner was entitled to pay scale of Assistant Organiser in the pay scale of Rs. 250-400 w.e.f.
1641957.
Issue No. (iv)  23.
The question whether on the basis of nomenclature the post of Assistant Organiser in the Village Oil Industry as well as in the Khadi Part PP Scheme should be deemed to be equivalent has to be decided by reference to various factors like their pay scale and the set of hierarchy, and set up in the different parts of the Board/Commission.
It has come on record that the post of Assistant Organiser in the Village Oil Industry was in the pay scale of Rs. 200-400, in the Fibre Department it was Rs. 150/- consolidated and in the other two departments Rs. 100-300/-.
Therefore, the designations also become different after the report of the Patel Commission.
Assistant Organiser in the Village Khadi and Oil Industry was designated as Regional Organiser in the pay scale of Rs. 250-400.
In the Fibre Department as Supervisor-3 in the grade of Rs. 100-300, that in Atta Chakki, as Supervisor 2 in the grade of Rs. 160-330 and that in the intensive area scheme, as Area Assistant Organiser in the scale of Rs. 100-300.
Therefore, it is clear that the nomenclature of the two posts of Assistant Organiser is deceptive and cannot be equated.

However, it has to be accepted that the Commission is estopped from denying that the post of Assistant Organiser in the Khadi Part PP Scheme before the Patel Commission's recommendation was equivalent to the post of Assistant Organiser in Village Oil Industry which has later been redesignated as regional Organiser.
It has already come on record that assurance was given by the Director that the post of Assistant Organiser in the Village Oil Industries would be treated at par with the Assistant Organiser in the Khadi Part PP Scheme.
In this regard, reference may also be made to the endorsement of the State Director while forwarding the appeal of the petitioner dated 28111968 wherein he has stated that the petitioner was given an assurance that the post of Assistant Organiser in Khadi Part PP Scheme would be treated as equivalent to the post of Assistant Organiser in Village Oil Industry.
It is further apparent that only because of the afore-mentioned assurance, the petitioner had joined Khadi Part PP Scheme.
If no assurance was given then the petitioner might not have joined the Khadi Part PP Scheme.
Therefore, the principle of equitable estoppel would be attracted in the instant case as has been laid down by the Supreme Court in numerous judgments including MP Sugar Mills v. State of UP, AIR 1979 SC 621 and Gujarat Financial Corp v. M/s Lotus Hotels Pvt. Ltd., AIR 1983 SC 848 and Union of India v. Godfrey Phillips India Ltd, 1985(4) SCC 369.
In view of the above, the writ petition is allowed and the orders dated 9101984 and 872003 are hereby quashed.
The petitioner is held entitled to the pay scale of Rs. 250-400 w.e.f.
1641957 with all consequential benefits towards arrears, fixation, revision of pay, pension, promotion etc.
He is also entitled to be considered for promotion to the post of Assistant Director, Deputy Director and Director w.e.f.
the date Shri MS Gokhale was promoted because the petitioner came in the scale of Rs. 250-400 w.e.f.
1641957 whereas Sh. Gokhale was given this pay scale w.e.f.
2361957.
The respondents shall carry these directions expeditiously preferably within a period of three months of the receipt of copy of this judgment.
The petitioner is also entitled to costs which are quantified at Rs. 10,000/-.
Petition allowed.
