NK Sodhi, J - Petitioners who claim to be working as senior clerks with the Punjab State Agricultural Marketing Board (hereinafter referred to as 'the Board') have filed this petition under Article 226 of the Constitution challenging the action of the State Government in not granting to them the senior scale to which they say they had become entitled to in terms of office order No. 17(1991) dated January 1, 1991 passed by the Board.
2.
By a notification dated May 22, 1973 issued by the State of Punjab under sub-section (2) (xxviii) of Section 43 of the Punjab Agricultural Produce Market Act, 1961 it was ordered that the employees of the Board and those of the market committee shall be governed by the Punjab Civil Services Rules or such other regulations or instructions as may have been or may hereafter issue by the Government from time to time.
A circular letter dated April 18, 1980 (Copy Annexure P-2 with the petition) was issued by the Government of Punjab, Department of Personnel and Administrative Reforms, to all Heads of Department and others concerned regarding bifurcation of cadre strength of clerks and issued guidelines for appointments to the posts of senior clerks.
The existing cadre of clerks was bifurcated in the ratio of 50 :50 carrying the following two scales :  (i) Clerks - Rs. 400-600  (ii) Senior Clerks - Rs. 510-800  Thereafter, another circular letter was issued by the State Government on April 17, 1984 laying down guidelines for appointment to the posts  Senior Clerks, Para 2 of this letter which is relevant for our purposes reads as under :  "2.
The matter has been considered by the Government further and it has been decided that the following conditions would be a prerequisite for placement as a Senior Clerk/Steno-typist Grade-I :  (i) The official should have completed three years' service in the lower scale of Clerk/stenotypist;  (ii) he/she should have completed the probationary period satisfactorily; and  (iii) he/she should have a satisfactory record of service.
The other conditions for placement in the senior scale would remain unchanged."
In the meantime, the State of Punjab on the basis of the recommendations of the Pay Commission decided to revise pay scales of its employees and made the revision effective with effect from 111986.
This decision of the State Government was adopted by the Board as per its office order No. 875 of 1989 dated June 27, 1989 (Annexure P-4 with the petition).
It would be pertinent to observe here that the unrevised grade of clerks of Rs. 400-600/- was revised to Rs. 950-1800/- and the unrevised senior scale of Rs. 510-800/- was revised to Rs. 1200-2100/- in the ratio of 50 : 50 as before.
3.
All the service rules, regulations and instructions issued by the State Government from time to time were applicable to the Board as per notification dated March 22, 1973, the Board in terms of the Government circular letter dated April 18, 1980.
(Annexure P-2 with the petition) designated as many as 71 clerks including the petitioners as senior clerks who fulfilled the conditions, laid down by the State Government in its guidelines dated April 17, 1984, the relevant part of which has been reproduced above.
This was done by the Board as per its office order No. 17(1991) dated 111991 (Annexures P-7 with the petition).
The clerks were designated as senior clerks w.e.f.
the dates mentioned against their names.
A bare look at this order shows that all the petitioners had been designated as senior clerks with effect from April 1, 1990 or prior thereto.
In other words, the entitlement of the petitioner to be designated as senior clerk had been determined by the Board w.e.f.
the dates prior to 1.4.1990.
Again, by office order No. 1637 dated 281189 one Sita Ram was designated as senior clerk with effect from 10.6.1988 and further by another order dated 1091987, the Board designated as many as 41 clerks as senior clerks, as according to it, they fulfilled the conditions laid down in the Government circular dated April 17, 1984.
4.
Till all this time, there were no statutory rules governing the service conditions of Class-III employees of the Board.
The State Government in exercise of its powers conferred on it by Section 43 of the Act, framed the Punjab State Agricultural Marketing Board (Class-III) Service Rules, 1988.
These Rules apply to all posts specified in Appendix 'A' in which posts of clerks and senior clerks have been mentioned at Sr. Nos. 42 and 47 respectively.
These are non-technical posts and it is provided that 50 per cent of the posts carry pay scale of Rs. 400-600 (unrevised).
It follows that the remaining 50 per cent posts would carry the scale of Rs. 510-800 as mentioned against the posts of senior clerks at Sr. No. 42 in Appendix 'A'.
5.
As already observed earlier, the State Government had revised pay scales of its employees w.e.f.
111986 and with a view to remove some anomalies which had arisen as a result of the implementation of revised pay scales, it issued a circular letter dated February 20, 1991 (Annexure P-8 with the petition).
It is interesting to note that while removing anomalies in the pay scales of its employees the State Government through this circular letter divided the pay scales of clerks in the following three categories with scales mentioned against each :  (A) Rs. 950-1800/- with a start at Rs. 1000/-  (B) Rs. 1200-2100/-  (C) Rs. 1509-2640/-  It was further stated in this circular letter that scale (B) would be granted to only 40 per cent posts in the grade of clerks who completed 5 years service and their designation will be senior clerks.
Scale (C) was granted to about 40 per cent in the grade of clerks who completed 10 years of service and they were designated as Junior Assistants.
It would be seen that this letter apart from dividing the pay scales in three categories had also reduced the quota of clerks in the grade of Rs. 1200-2100/- from 50 per cent to 40 per cent and placed 40 per cent clerks in a higher scale of Rs. 1500-2640/- and designated them as Junior Assistants.
When the Board after designating the petitioners and several others as senior clerks wanted to implement the said scales, the auditors raised an objection that in view of the circular letter dated February 20, 1991 issued by the State Government the petitioners and some others like them were to entitled to senior scale as they did not fulfil the revised conditions mentioned in the said letter.
It was at this stage that the petitioners filed the present writ petition impugning amongst others the circular letter dated February 20, 1991 (Annexure P-8 with the petition).
6.
It was forcefully contended on behalf of the petitioners that since they fulfilled the conditions prescribed by the State Government in its circular letter of April 17, 1984 (Annexure P-3 with the petition) for placement and designation as senior clerks and the Board having designated them as such, the auditors had no justification in raising any objections and that the Board and the State Government were bound to grant to them the senior scale of Rs. 1200-2100/-.
7.
Mr. Arun Kumar Walia, learned Assistant Advocate General, Punjab appearing on behalf of the respondents, on the other hand, contended that since the conditions for placement and designation as senior clerks had subsequently been revised by the State Government as per its circular letter of February 20, 1991 and the petitioners did not fulfil those conditions, they were not entitled to the grant of senior scale.
8.
Having heard counsel for the parties at length, I am of the opinion that the petition deserves to succeed.
It is not disputed that the State Government in its circular letter dated April 17, 1984 had laid down three conditions which have been reproduced above for placement as senior clerks and that the petitioners fulfilled those conditions.
Not only this, the Board as per its order dated 111991 (Annexure P-7) had designated the petitioners as senior clerks with effect from the dates mentioned against their names in the said order.
It is common case of the parties that the dates with effect from which the petitioners were designated as senior clerks were prior to February 20, 1991 on which date the impinged circular letter altering those conditions and dividing pay scale of clerks into three categories as referred to earlier.
In fact, Harbhajan Kaur, one of the petitioners was designated as a senior clerk w.e.f.
1.4.1990 and all the other petitioners had been designated w.e.f.
an earlier date.
The impugned circular letter dated February 20, 1991 even if applicable to the Board employees would operate only prospectively and cover these employees who would be designated as senior clerks after the issuance of the said letter.
Petitioners having been designated with effect from a date prior thereto would not be governed by the impugned circular letter, and, therefore the auditors were not justified in raising objections which they did.
9.
It was then urged on behalf of the petitioners that the impugned circular letter took away their vested right and altered terms and conditions of their employment to their prejudice and that the same was in contravention of the statutory service rules which had by then come into force.
It was submitted that posts of Junior Assistants created by the impugned letter did not find mention in Appendix 'A' to the service rules referred to above.
Since I am holding that the impugned letter operated prospectively and did to apply to the case of the petitioner who had been designated as senior clerks prior to its issuance, it is not necessary for me to go into the other submissions made by counsel for the petitioner.
10.
In the result, the petitioner succeeds and the respondents are directed to grant the senior scale of Rs. 1200-2100/- to the petitioners and other clerks like them who had been designated as senior clerks w.e.f.
the dates prior to the issuance of impugned circular letter.
The respondents shall pay the arrears of pay within three months from the date of receipt of a copy of this order, failing which the petitioners shall be entitled to interest at the rate of 15 per cent per annum from the date when senior scale became due to them.
There will be no order as to costs.
Petition accepted.
