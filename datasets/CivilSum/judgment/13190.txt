Swatanter Kumar, J - In this petition under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India, the petitioners pray that the order dated 20.3.2001, Annexure P/5 to the petition, vide which the petitioners have been reverted from the post of Principals to that of the Lecturers, be quashed as it is against the rules and violative of the principles of natural justice.
2.
The petitioners were working as Lecturers and were appointed to the said posts in the year 1965-1966.
They were having good service record and were at seniority No. 395, 453, 299 respectively of the seniority list.
The promotion to the next post i.e.
Principal as per the Punjab Educational Service College Cadre Class-I Rules, 1976, was to be made on the basis of seniority-cum-merit.
As the petitioners had a good service record, they were promoted to the posts of Principals.
3.
The Government released a promotion policy dated 29122000 over and above the provisions of law and the rules framed for this purpose.
As per newly made policy the requisite benchmark on the basis of last five years records was altered and some stringent measures were taken for promotion and they were made effective retrospectively.
According to the petitioners, the vacancies of the Principals arose before the release of the new policy and the petitioners had been adjusted for nearly six months on the post of the Principals before release of the new policy.
These instructions were made effective retrospectively and the petitioners were reverted back to the posts of Lecturers and have not been promoted to the posts of Principals.
Aggrieved from this action of the respondents, the petitioners have filed this petition with a prayer that they be appointed to the said post, as per the vacancies existing at the relevant time.
The reversion order dated 20.3.2001 Annexure P.5 to the petition is also challenged before this Court.
4.
Upon notice, the respondents filed a detailed reply wherein the very maintainability of the writ petition was challenged and it was further stated that the petitioners were not eligible to be promoted as Principals either under the old criteria or under the new criteria.
However, it was stated that under the new criteria, petitioner No. 2 has already been promoted as Principal on the recommendation of DPC held on 2722001 while the others were not found to be fit for promotion in accordance with the criteria.
The petitioner were not reverted but stood adjusted as Lecturers as their so called promotion orders were also orders of mere adjustment depending upon their placement in the District.
It is submitted that the writ petition be dismissed.
5.
At the very outset, we may mention that in this petition we do not propose to discuss in any detail the validity of the criteria in question nor do we wish to comment upon the effect of Rule 10 of these instructions.
This is primarily for the reason that all the discussions would be academic keeping in view the facts and circumstances of this case.
6.
As far as petitioner No. 2 is concerned, the petition has become infructuous because he has already been promoted as Principal under the new criteria in furtherance to the minutes of the D.P.C.
held on 2722001.

7.
The above service profile would be any help to the petitioners only if they would satisfy any of the two prescribed criteria i.e. the old criteria and the criteria provided under the new policy.
The proposition of law relied upon by the petitioners can hardly be disputed.
It is well established that the vacancies which occur prior to the amendment of rules would be governed by the old rules and not by the amended rules.
Reference in this regard can be made to the judgments of the Hon'ble Supreme Court in the cases of YV Rangaiah and others v. J Sreenivasa Rao and others, 1983(1) S.L.R.
789, Dr. Rajinder Singh v. State of Punjab and others, 2001(2) SCT 815 (SC) : 2001(5) Supreme Court Cases 482 and Guneeta Chadha and others v. Union of India and others, 2001(1) S.L.R.
9 : 2001(1) SCT 1026 (P&H)(DB).
8.
On the above enunciated principles the petitioners would at best be entitled to be promoted on the existing vacancies prior to the amendment.
But this, the petitioners would be entitled to only if they fulfil the criteria provided under the old instructions.
The D.P.C.
was held on 8121999.
But even at that time the petitioners were required to have three very good reports, but the petitioner had only two very good reports to their credit and as such they did not satisfy the old criteria.
After coming into force of the new instructions, the petitioners were required to have 15 marks on the basis of the amended benchmark, but petitioner No. 1 only had 14 marks while petitioner No. 3 had 7 marks.
As such the petitioners do not satisfy any of the prescribed criteria and, therefore, they cannot lay their claim to promotion to the post of Principal as a matter of right.
The above factual position was not even disputed by the learned counsel for the petitioners before the Court.
9.
For the reasons afore-stated, we find no merit in this petition.
The same is hereby dismissed, leaving the parties to bear their own costs.
Petition dismissed.
