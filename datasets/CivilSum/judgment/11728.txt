THB Chalapathi, J - The state of Haryana is the appellant.
2.
The plaintiff-respondent filed a suit for declaration that he is entitled to be promoted as Forester with effect from 181978 and as Deputy Ranger w.e.f.
2.6.1990 i.e. from the dates his juniors were promoted with all consequential benefits.
3.
The trial Court vide its judgment dated 2681997 declared that the plaintiff is senior to Thambu Ram, Daya Nand and Satbir Singh who are defendants 5 to 7, but declined the relief that the plaintiff is entitled to be promoted to the post of Forester with effect from 181978 and Deputy Ranger from 161990.
Aggrieved by the rejection of the relief that he was not entitled to be promoted as claimed, the plaintiff filed an appeal to the Court of District Judge, Sonepat in Civil Appeal No. 64 of 1997.
The learned District Judge by his judgment dated 20111997 allowed the appeal of the plaintiff and declared that he is entitled to promotion as Forester w.e.f.
181978 and also as Deputy Ranger from 161990.
Aggrieved by the same, the State of Haryana preferred this appeal.
4.
There is no necessity to go into the facts of the case.
The appeal deserves to be allowed on the short ground of limitation.
Admittedly, the plaintiff was appointed as Forest Guard on 1911970.
He was promoted as Forester on 121982 after passing Forester Class Training during the year 1981-82.
According to him his juniors were promoted as Foresters on 181978 and those juniors have further been promoted as Deputy Rangers w.e.f.
161990.
Therefore, the plaintiff is claiming that he is entitled to be promoted as Forester from the date his juniors were promoted and the consequential promotion w.e.f.
161990.
The suit has been filed on 26101994.
The cause of action for the petitioner to claim promotion has arisen on 181978 when his juniors were promoted as Forester.
Further in the year 1981-82 only he passed the Forester Class Training.
The claim of the petitioner is barred by time since he filed the suit nearly 14 years after the promotion of his juniors as Foresters and 4 years after his juniors have been further promoted as Deputy Rangers.
I am of the view that the claim of the petitioner is hopelessly time barred.
In this connection, it is useful to refer to the decision of the Full Bench of this Court in Punjab State Electricity Board v. Ashok Kumar Sehgal, AIR 1990 P & H 117 : 2006(1) SCT.
674, wherein it was held that unexplained delay of 10 years assumes significance when we know and can conceive what would have transpired in that decade and how many innocent Line Superintendents would have become entitled to sit back and consider that their appointment and promotion effected long time ago would not be set aside after lapse of a number of years.
They further held that the neglect and delay and laches of the petitioners both are relevant to deny the relief.
5.
Further the relief of a declaration has to be sought for within 3 years from the date of accrual of the right.
Even assuming for the moment that the right has accrued to the petitioner when his juniors have further been promoted as Deputy Rangers in 1990, the suit having been filed four years thereafter is barred by time.
Reference may also be made to the decision for the Apex Court in State of Punjab and others v. Gurdev Singh and another, AIR 1992 SC 111 : 1991(3) SCT.
91, wherein their Lordships held that even if the order is void, it is liable to be set aside within a period of 3 years and Article 113 of the Limitation Act applies to such cases.
I am, therefore, of the opinion that the learned District Judge erred in law in setting aside the decree and judgment of the trial Court.
6.
Accordingly, I allow the appeal, set aside the decree and judgment of the learned District Judge and restore that of the trial Court.
However, there will be no order as to costs.
Appeal allowed.
