Swatanter Kumar, J - The petitioner has approached this Court under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India praying for issuance of an appropriate writ, order or direction for quashing the order dated 13.5.2002, Annexure P/2 to the writ petition, vide which the claim of the petitioner for regularisation of his services under the policy of the government and 7/1831996 has been rejected.
He further prays for issuance of a writ in the nature of Mandamus directing the respondents to consider the case of the petitioner for regularisation with all consequential benefits.
2.
Necessary facts are :- that the petitioner was appointed as Baildar on daily wages in April, 1989.
Services of the petitioner were terminated in June, 1996.
The petitioner raised an industrial dispute claiming that the termination of his services was illegal and arbitrary.
Reference was made by the appropriate government which culminated into passing of an award dated 322000 in favour of the workman, vide which he was reinstated in service with 20% back wages.
The said award is stated to have attained finality.
3.
The Government of Haryana framed a policy for regularisation of its daily wages employees on 7/1831996.
According to which, a daily wager, who has completed three years of service as on 3111996, who has to his credit 240 days of service in each year and his break in service was not in excess of one month in each year, was entitled to be considered for regularisation.
4.
The petitioner served upon the respondents a representation and legal notice for regularisation of his services, as the respondents failed to accede to the request of the petitioner, he was compelled to file a writ petition in this Court, registered as Civil Writ Petition No. 708 of 2002, which was disposed of vide order dated 2112002, directing the respondents to consider the request of the petitioner within three months, which was finally declined by the respondents, vide their letter dated 13.5.2002, Annexure P/2 to the writ petition, which has been impugned by the petitioner in the present writ petition.
5.
The facts are hardly in controversy.
6.
In the written statement filed by the respondents, it is stated that the impugned order has been passed in accordance with law and on the basis of the record and the petitioner does not fulfil the requisite conditions, as such, was not entitled to claim any relief.
Along with the reply, the respondents have annexed list of working days of the petitioner in different years, which have been projected in the muster roll, the extract is copy Annexure R/1.
7.
The claim of the petitioner has been rejected by the respondents, vide their letter dated 13.5.2002.
Relevant extract of the said order reads as under :-  "The Haryana Government vide Chief Secretary to Government Haryana again framed a policy to regularise the daily rated workers vide memo No. 6/38/95- 2GSI dated 7396 and 19396 and were not to be considered for regularisation who fulfil the following conditions :- i) who have completed three years of service on 31196 and they have worked for a minimum period of 240 days in each year.
ii) There break in service in any year is not more than 30 days.
iii) The worker should be present on 31196.
As per record verified, you have not completed 240 days in the year 1994-95 and break in service is more than 30 days.
As such, you are not fit for regularisation under this policy of the Govt." 8.
From the copy of the muster roll filed by the respondents as Annexure R/1 to their reply, it is clear that the petitioner had joined the services in May, 1989 and continued to serve till 1996.
In the year 1992, the petitioner had served for 236 days, in the year 1993, for 292 days and 1995, he served for 302 days, while during the intervening year, he had served for a number of days but there was break in service for more than 30 days in the years 1994, 1991, 1990.
The respondents, thus, cannot deny that the petitioner does not satisfy the condition of serving for a year in three years prior to commencement of the policy and had completed more than 240 days in each year of the three years.
9.
Learned counsel for the respondents contended that the three years have to be continuous and the conditions of the policy have to be strictly adhered to and satisfy before the claim of the petitioner for regularisation could be considered and accepted.
He has relied upon the judgment of the Full bench in the case of Tek Chand and others v. State of Haryana and others, 2002(1) SCT 308 (P&H) : 2001(3) Recent Services Judgments 604.
10.
We may notice that the Full Bench in the case of Tek Chand (supra) did not say that three years to be considered as continuous and immediately preceding the relevant date for regularisation i.e. 31st January, 1996.
On the contrary, a Division Bench of this Court in the case of Kavita Gandhi v. State of Haryana and others, 1997(4) Recent Service Judgment 760, clearly held as under :-  "10.
In view of the judgment of the Full Bench of this Court in Jagdish Lal and others (supra) it is abundantly clear that in order to become eligible for consideration for regularisation of the service under a particular notification of the State Government the prescribed years of completed service with reference to the cut-off date mentioned in the notification applies to such employees also who have completed those number of years of service prior to the cut off date and not necessarily, immediately preceding the said date.
In other words, if the employee has completed, with reference to the notification dated 731996/1831996, three years of completed service and was in service on cut off date i.e. 3111996, he would become entitled for regularisation of service provided he fulfilled the other conditions mentioned in the notification.
It is not necessary that the period of three years should be taken immediately preceding the cut-off date i.e. 3111996.
If the case of the petitioner is examined from this angle, she rendered more than three years completed service without any break prior to 3111996 and she was in service on the said date."
11.
In the years indicated above, the petitioner does not have break for more than 30 days in any of the years.
He has a break of more than 30 days in other words but admittedly he continued to be in service till 31st January, 1996.
Even the Full Bench in Tek Chand (supra) held that it was not necessary that the employee should be on duty on 31st January, 1996, and the break in service could be an impediment for regularisation of service of the employee only if the break is attributable to the employee and was not a result of compulsion caused by the employer.
Applying these principles to the present case, it is not the case of the respondents, as stated in the written statement, when the petitioner intentionally absented for more than 30 days.
In other words, it would be hardly relevant factor for determining the controversy in issue before us.
In the present case, we are of the considered opinion, that the order rejecting the request of the petitioner for regularisation of his services, suffers from an error even which is self explanatory from the record, which has been produced by the respondent themselves.
It would be irrelevant if the petitioner would not have completed 240 days in the year 1994-95, as he had already completed such number of days in 1992, 1993, and 1995, and in addition thereto even in other years.
The break of 30 days was not there in the service of the petitioner in the relevant years as discussed above.
12.
In view of the above discussion, the writ petition is allowed, the impugned order dated 13.5.2002, Annexure P/2 to the writ petition is quashed.
The respondents are directed to consider the case of the petitioner for regularisation and pass appropriate order and grant him the consequential relief, subject to the plea of limitation.
Parties are left to bear their own costs.
Petition allowed.
