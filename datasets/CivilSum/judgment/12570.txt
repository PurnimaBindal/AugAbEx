RS Mongia, J (Oral) - The relief claimed in this writ petition is that the petitioners are entitled to regularisation in service as Beldars as they have put in service much more than 24 days.
As an instance, experience certificates in the case of two petitioners namely Dalbir Singh and Sadhu Ram have also been attached.
The case of the respondents on the other hand is that the petitioners were just appointed as casual labourers on daily wages and they were required to work only when casual work was available and they were never appointed Beldars.
Disputed questions of facts have been raised by the respondents regarding the date of appointment, nature of appointment and nature of duties, the petitioners were performing and also number of days, the petitioners were working in a particular month or the total period they have put in as casual labourers.
2.
I am afraid that these disputed questions of facts cannot be decided under Article 226 of the Constitution of India as the findings would depend on evidence that may be led by both the sides.
The best course in these circumstances is that the matter should be decided by the Labour Court.While dismissing the writ petition, I could relegate the petitioners to their remedy under the Industrial Disputes Act and to raise an industrial dispute,but as this writ petition is pending since 1988 in the court, I do not adopt that course as it is not disputed on the part of the respondents that an industrial dispute does arise in this case.
Consequently, I direct the State Government to refer the dispute to the concerned Labour Court within a period of two months from the receipt of a copy of this order.It will be open to both the sides to raise all legal and factual issues before the Labour Court.
The writ petition is disposed of in the above terms.
No costs.
Order accordingly.
