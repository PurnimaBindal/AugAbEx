RS Mongia, J - Petitioners Shri HS Bakshi is a member of Punjab Superior Judicial Service in the State of Punjab.
He was appointed in the PCS (Judicial Branch) on 6th December, 1960 and was promoted in the selection grade of the Subordinate Judges, i.e. Rs. 1300-1500 on 9th April, 1973.
The State of Punjab promulgated Rules in 1979 known as Punjab Civil Services (Revised Scales of Pay) Rules, 1979 (hereinafter referred to as the Revised Pay Rules).
These Rules came into force with effect from 1st January, 1978.
2.
On 24th January, 1976, the petitioner had been promoted as Additional District & Sessions Judge in officiating capacity in the pay scale of Rs. 1200-2000.
From the facts mentioned above, it is evident that the petitioner on 1st January, 1978, was in service in dual capacity; he was holding the post of Selection Grade Sub Judge in substantive capacity, holding a lien against that post and was also working in officiating capacity as Additional District & Sessions Judge.
The Revised Pay Rules up graded the pay scales of all Punjab Government employees.
The selection grade of Rs. 1300-1500 of the Subordinate Judges was first replaced by the scale of Rs. 1775-2000 and subsequently in 1983 by the scale of Rs. 2000-2400 with effect from 1st January, 1978.
The time-scale of District & Sessions Judges (including Additional District & Sessions Judges) which was initially Rs. 1200-2000, was replaced by the scale of Rs. 1580-60-1700-75-2000-100-2400.
It may be observed here that the petitioner had elected to continue in the existing pay scale until 1st April, 1978 under Rules 4 and 5 of the Revised Pay Rules.
The petitioner's existing emoluments as Additional District & Sessions Judge in the pay scale of Rs. 1200-2000 as on 1st April, 1978 were Rs. 1720/- basic pay + 363 allowances etc. = 2083/-.
Respondent No. 2 fixed the petitioner's pay under the Revised Pay Rules in the pay scale of Rs. 1580-2400, as on 1st January, 1978 at Rs. 2100/- + Rs. 58 personal pay.
3.
By this petition, the petitioner challenges this pay fixation by respondent No. 2 on the ground that his pay has not been rightly fixed under the Revised Pay Rules read with some other Rules of Punjab Civil Services Rules, Volume 1, Part I (to which reference would be made hereinafter), as also on the ground that the pay of several officers junior to him and drawing less pay before the Revised Pay Rules came into force, had been fixed at a higher stage than fixed qua him.
4.
The learned counsel for the petitioner has submitted that the petitioner has not been given the benefit of subrule (2) of Rule 6 of the Revised Pay Rules and Rule 414(1) and (2) read with Rule 4.4.
(a)(i) of the Punjab CSR Volume I Part 1.
The respondents have admitted the fact that the officers juniors to the petitioner who were drawing lesser pay than him before the enforcement of the Pay Fixation Rules, had been allowed to be fixed at a higher stage than the petitioner.
In other words, the submission was that on the appointed day, ie 1st January, 1978, the persons who were getting lower pay prior to 1st January, 1978, had started getting the same pay as the petitioner.
The petitioner's counsel emphasised that had the petitioner not been promoted to the officiating post of Additional District & Sessions Judge, his pay would have been fixed at a higher level in the pay scale of Rs. 2000- 2400 relevant to his substantive post.
Rule 3(c) and 3(d) of the Revised Pay Rules define existing emoluments.
Rule 6 of these Rules provides for fixation of pay in the revised scale; whereas Rule 7 gives the date of increment in the revised scale.
These Rules are quoted below for ready reference :-  "3.
Definitions :- In these rules, unless there is anything repugnant in the subject or context.
(a).
--------------------------- (b).
--------------------------- (c) "existing scale" means the scale of pay of a Government Employee in respect of the post held by him in substantive or officiating capacity on the appointed day; (d) "existing emoluments" means aggregate of (i) basic pay of a Government employee in the existing scale on the appointed day; (ii) Dearness Allowance, Dearness Pay, Additional Relief, Additional Dearness Allowance sanctioned upto 12 monthly average consumer price index = 320 (1900 = 100) i.e. upto and including the instalment of Additional Dearness Allowances sanctioned with effect from the first day of January, 1978; (iii) ad hoc pay, if any, where such ad hoc pay is abolished with effect from the appointing day; and (iv) Special pay or part of special pay, if any which was granted in lieu of higher time scale and has been abolished as a separate component in the revised scale with effect from the appointed day.
Note :- Normal increment due and granted to any employee with effect from the first day of January, 1978 shall also be treated part of existing emoluments for the purpose of these rules.
6.
Fixation of pay in the revised scale :- (1) The initial pay of a Government employee in the revised scale shall, unless in any case it is otherwise directed, be fixed separately in respect of his substantive pay in the permanent post on which he holds a lien or would have held a lien had it not been suspended and in respect of his pay in the officiating post or posts, as the case may be, held by him in the following manner :  (i) Where a single existing scale has been replaced by a single revised scale, the pay in the revised scale in which a Government employee is placed shall be fixed at the stage next above the existing emoluments on the appointed day.
In case the benefit so accruing is less than the amount equal to the rate of increment at the relevant stage in the revised scale, the employee may be granted another increment in the revised scale of pay : Provided that, in all cases where the benefit still falls short of twenty rupees per month the employees be given further increment(s) in the revised scale so as to give him a minimum benefit of twenty rupees per month :  Provided further that where the revised pay fixed at such stage exceeds the existing emoluments by more than seventy-five rupees the pay shall be fixed at the highest stage in the revised scale at which the revised pay, so fixed does not exceed the existing emoluments by more than seventy-five rupees and the difference between the existing emoluments plus seventy-five rupees and the revised pay shall be allowed as personal pay to be absorbed in the pay at the time of next increment;  (ii) Where more than one existing scale has been replaced by a single revised scale, the initial pay in the revised scale shall be fixed in the manner indicated in clause (i), as if each of the existing scales had been singly replaced by the revised scales.
(iii) Where an existing scale has been replaced by two revised scales, initial pay of the person fitted in the lower or higher revised scale shall be fixed in a manner indicated in clause (i), as if the existing scale had been replaced by a single lower or higher revised scale, as the cases may be, provided that if the existing emoluments plus the benefit permissible in clause (i) or (ii) or (iii) work out to be less than the minimum of the revised scale, the pay shall be fixed at the minimum of the revised scale : Provided further that if the existing emoluments plus the benefit permissible in clause (i) or (ii) or (iii) exceeds the maximum of the revised scale, the pay shall be restricted to the maximum thereof, and the difference shall be treated as personal pay.
(iv) Where a Government employee is holding a permanent post and is officiating in a higher post and the scales of pay applicable to those two posts are merged into one scale, the pay shall be fixed under clause (ii) with reference to the officiating post only provided he has continuously officiated in that post for not less than one year and the pay so fixed, shall be treated as a substantive pay.
Where such a Government employee has not completed one year's continuous service in the higher officiating post on the appointed day, his pay in the revised scale shall be fixed separately with reference to his substantive pay and officiating pay in the existing scale and his pay in the revised scale as fixed with reference to the officiating pay shall be treated as substantive pay in that scale after rendering service for the period by which it fell short of one year on the appointed day, provided it is certified by the appointing authority that he would have continued to officiate in the higher officiating post during this period, had the revised scales not been introduced.
If, however, the appointing authority certifies that he would have reverted to the lower post during this period, his pay in the revised scale would from the date on which he would have reverted be regulated on the basis of the pay fixed on the appointed day with reference to his substantive pay in the lower post.
(v) Where two pay scales in the same line of promotion are replaced by a single revised scale, the revised pay of the Government employ working in the higher scale will not be fixed at a stage in the revised scale lower than that admissible to a Government employee working in the lower existing scale drawing basic pay at the same or lower rate as the employee working in the higher scale is drawing.
(2) If pay as fixed in higher officiating post under sub-rule (1) is equal to or lower than the pay as fixed in substantive post or a lower officiating post, officiating pay shall be refixed at the stage next above the substantive pay or the lower officiating pay, as the case may be.
7.
(1) Date of increment in the revised scale.- The next increment of a Government employee whose pay has been fixed in accordance with rule 6 shall be granted on the date on which he would have drawn his increment had he continued in the existing scale;  Provided that -  (i) Where the revised pay is fixed at the minimum of the time scale and on such fixation the revised pay exceeds the existing emoluments by more than rupees seventy-five, the next increment shall be granted on the date it falls due in the revised scale; (ii) The next increment shall be granted on the next succeeding day following the appointed day, to a Government employee whose pay fixed on the appointed day in the revised scale is at the same stage as the one fixed for another Government employee drawing pay at a lower stage than his in the same existing scale."
Rule 6(1) of the Revised Pay Rules, inter alia, provides that the initial pay of a Government employee be fixed separately in respect of his sub-stantive pay in the permanent post on which he holds a lien as well as in respect of his pay in the officiating post.
Sub-rule (2) of Rule 6, which provides that if the pay fixed in the higher officiating post of an employee is equal to or lower than his pay as fixed in the substantive post, his officiating pay shall be re-fixed at the stage next above the substantive pay.
It is evident from the reading of Rule 6(1) and 6(2) that where an employee held a lien against a permanent post but was officiating in a higher post on the appointed day, it was necessary to work out his pay relevant to substantive, post as well as to the officiating post.
5.
It is manifest that the pay of an employee so fixed as indicated above, cannot be lower than the pay of the employee junior to him who was drawing pay at lower stage than the stage of the senior employee.
It has, in fact, been admitted by the respondents, as observed above, that several officers junior to the petitioner as Sub Judges drawing pay at lower stages than the stage of the petitioner, in the existing scale of Rs. 1300-1500 before 1st January, 1978, when the Revised Pay Rules were enforced, has been fixed at higher stage than the petitioner and those juniors were drawing higher salary than him.
The petitioner cannot be made to suffer because on the relevant date ie 1st January, 1978, he happened to be officiating in the higher post as the Additional District & Sessions Judge.
In other words, can it be permitted that if the petitioner was not officiating against the post of Additional District & Sessions Judge, he would have got more pay than the one which was fixed in the officiating promoted rank.
In my view, it will be wholly arbitrary and violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India.
6.
There is no dispute that the pay fixation has to be worked out with reference to the appointed day, ie 1st January, 1978, as provided under the Revised Pay Rules, even if the appointed day by option falls subsequently.
This was so clarified by the Punjab Government vide letter No. 9/67/79-IFR, dated 21st December, 1979.
The petitioner's basic pay in the un-revised selection grade of Rs. 1300-1500 was Rs. 1300/- on 9th April, 1973 (the date on which he was promoted in the selection grade) and Rs. 1500 on 1st April, 1978 (this date 1.4.1978 instead of 111978 is being mentioned here because the petitioner had opted under Rules 4 and 5 to continue in the existing scale until 1.4.1978).
The existing emoluments on 1st April, 1978 were Rs. 1500/- + 100/- D.A.
+ Rs. 363 Additional D.A.
= Rs. 1963/-.
As on 1st January, 1978, he was entitled to the minimum of the revised selection grade of Rs. 2000-2400 i.e. Rs. 2000/- in his substantive rank of Sub Judge.
Now one of his juniors, Shri HL Garg as mentioned in the gradation list issued by this Court (Annexure P.2) who was appointed in the PCS (Judicial Branch) on 11th February, 1963, (as compared to the petitioner who was appointed on 6th December, 1960), was promoted in the selection grade on 11th February, 1975 (as compared to the petitioner, who was promoted to the selection grade on 9th April, 1973) and his existing emoluments on 1st January, 1978 were Rs. 863/- (as compared to the petitioner who was getting Rs. 1963), which was less than the petitioner.
Shri Garg's pay was also fixed at Rs. 2000/- per month on 1st January, 1978, which was the minimum of the revised selection grade.
Even persons who were junior to Shri Garg and were drawing lesser pay than Shri Garg, their pay was also fixed at Rs. 2000/- in revised pay scale as on 1st January, 1978 in the substantive rank of Sub Judges (Selection Grade).
7.
At this stage, proviso (ii) to Rule 7 above, as also notification No. 9/66/79-IFR, dated 13th March, 1980 by the Department of Finance, Government of Punjab, would come into play.
The above said Notification reads as under :-  "The President of India is pleased to decide that in such cases where the Government employee had reached the maximum of the existing scale subsequent to the 1st January, 1977, the next increment in the revised scale shall be granted on the completion of service for the full incremental period from the date on which he reached the maximum of the existing scale.
In the case of persons who had been at the maximum of the existing scale for more than a year as on 1st January, 1978 the next increment will be allowed on the 2nd January, 1978."
The pay of Shri Garg, who was drawing pay at a lower stage before the enforcement of the Pay.
Fixation Rules as compared to the Rules, has also been fixed at the same stage.
This entitles the petitioner to the next increment on 2nd January, 1978 in the substantive capacity i.e. Rs. 2100/- in the pay scale of Rs. 2000-2400.
As observed above, the petitioner had opted to continue in the existing scale until 1st April, 1978, the petitioner would have stagnated at the maximum of the unrevised selection grade - 1300-1500 for full one year.
Therefore, he would be entitled to have his pay fixed at Rs. 2200/- on that date, which is the date on which he opts to have the revised pay scale.
Thus, it is settled that had the petitioner not been promoted to officiate as Additional District & Sessions Judge, his pay would have been fixed at Rs. 2200/- as on 1st April, 1978 in the substantive rank.
In the officiating capacity, admittedly his pay was fixed at Rs. 2100/- ( + Rs. 58/- as personal pay) which is lower than his pay as would have been fixed in the substantive post.
This entitles the petitioner to have his pay re-fixed as Additional District & Sessions Judge in the time scale of Rs. 1580-2400.
Under Rule 4.14(i) and (ii) read with Rule 4.4(a)(i) and Rule 2.28 of the Punjab Civil Services Rules, Volume 1, Part I and Rule 6(2) of the Revised Pay Rules.
Rules 4.14(1), 4.14(2), 248 and 4.4(a)(i) are reproduced below :-  4.14(1) - Subject to the provisions of Rule 413 and 416 a Government employee who is appointed to officiate in a post shall draw the presumptive pay of that post.
4.14(2) - On an enhancement in the substantive pay, as a result of increment or otherwise, the pay of such Government employee shall be re-fixed under sub-rule (1) from the date of such enhancement as if he was appointed to officiate in that post on that date where such re-fixation is to his advantage.
248.- Presumptive pay of a post.- When used with reference to any particular Government employee, means the pay to which he would be entitled if he held the post substantively and were performing its duties, but it does not include special pay unless the Government employee performs or discharges the work or responsibility, on consideration of which the special pay was sanctioned.
4.4(a)(i).- (i) When appointment to the new post involves the assumption of duties or responsibilities of greater importance (interpreted for the purposes of rule 413) than those attaching to such permanent post he will draw as initial pay the stage of the time scale next above this substantive pay in respect of the old post.
Accordingly, the petitioner's pay in the officiating rank would be also above what he would have drawn in the substantive capacity.
In other words, his pay would be fixed at Rs. 2300/- as on 1st April, 1978 with the next increment to follow on 1st.
April, 1979 at Rs. 2400 and so on, as per Rules.
8.
The pay fixation of the petitioner at Rs. 2100/- (+ Rs. 58/- personal pay) on the basis of his existing emoluments in the officiating rank at Rs. 2083/- on 1st April, 1978, is even otherwise in gross violation of the mandate of Rule 6(1), first proviso, which provides that in all cases where by fixation of pay the benefit over the existing emoluments falls short of Rs. 20/- per month, the employee is to be given further increments in the revised scale so as to give him a minimum benefit of Rs. 20/- per month.
At the stage of Rs. 2100/- the benefit was only of Rs. 17/-.
Rule 6(1) of the Revised Pay Rules essentially forbids pay fixation at a lower stage than the existing emoluments.
Main Rule 6 and the first proviso set down the pay fixation formula complete in all respects prescribing the minimum and maximum limits of the benefit.
The second proviso of Rule 6 prescribes an additional ceiling to the benefit.
These two provisos have to be harmoniously construed.
9.
Construing both the provisos of Rule 6 harmoniously along with other Rules, quoted above, to the best advantage of the petitioner, on the basis of existing emoluments of Rs. 1963/- per month in the substantive rank (Rs. 1500/- + Rs. 463/-) the petitioner's pay should have been fixed at Rs. 2000/-.
Since the pay of one of his juniors Shri HL Garg who was getting lesser pay on 1st January, 1978 in the substantive rank, was also fixed at Rs. 2000/- the petitioner's pay would be raised to Rs. 2100/- on 2nd January, 1978, under proviso (ii) to Rule 7(2) of the Revised Pay Rules in the substantive rank.
Because of the stagnation at the maximum of the unrevised scale of Rs. 1300-1500 for full one year, the petitioner would be entitled to have his pay fixed at Rs. 2300/- on 1st April, 1978 (as the petitioner had opted a continue in the existing scale until 1st April, 1978).
Further, since as a matter of fact, the petitioner was officiating in the higher rank of Additional District & Sessions Judge on 1st April, 1978, he would be entitled to one further increment in the officiating rank and his pay would be Rs. 2300/- on 1st April, 1978.
Under Rule 414(1) and (2) read with Rule 4.4(a) (1) of the CSR Rules and Rules 6(2) of the revised Pay Rules.
10.
For the foregoing reasons, the writ petition is allowed and the pay as fixed by the respondents is hereby quashed.
The respondents are directed to refix the pay of the petitioner under "the Revised Pay Rules read with the other Rules, quoted above, first in the substantive rank and then in the officiating rank.
By the process indicated above, petitioner would be entitled to get Rs. 2300/- on 1st April, 1978, with the next increments on 1st April, 1979 and so on.
The petitioner would be entitled to the arrears as calculated in the above manner.
He shall be further entitled to interest of the rate of 12% per annum on the arrears so calculated from the date of their accrual to the date of payment.
These directions be carried out within three months from the receipt of this judgment.
There will be no order as to costs.
