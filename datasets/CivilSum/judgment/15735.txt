GS Singhvi, J - Feeling dissatisfied with the order dated 3121997 passed by the Central Administrative Tribunal, Chandigarh Bench dismissing OA No. 445/CH/1997 filed by him, the petitioner has invoked writ jurisdiction of this Court for issuance of a writ in the nature of certiorari to quash the order passed by the Tribunal as well as the order dated 1421997 issued by the Home Secretary, Chandigarh granting Senior/Selection Grade to his juniors in the cadre of Senior Librarians, namely, Smt. Kamlesh Kumar and S/Shri Harphool Singh (Retired), Jai Dev, Avtar Singh (Retired) and JD Verma (since dead) ignoring him.
2.
Perusal of the record shows that the petitioner was appointed as Assistant Librarian w.e.f.
161968 in Punjab Engineering College, Chandigarh.
At that time, he was having the qualification of Higher Secondary with certificate of Library Science.
During the course of service, he passed B Lib.
Science (1977) and M Lib Science (1979) and MA in Political Science (1985).
He was granted the pay scale of Rs. 700-1600 w.e.f.
17.4.1985.
He was also granted the benefit of revised pay scale of Rs. 2200-4000 w.e.f.
111986 but was not granted Selection Grade of Rs. 3700-5700 which was allowed to his juniors vide order Annexure P.6 dated 1421997 issued by the respondent No. 4 on the recommendations of the Screening Committee.
3.
Dr. Balram Gupta assailed the order passed by the respondent No. 4 by arguing that denial of Selection Grade to the petitioner is per se arbitrary and discriminatory and violative of the petitioner's fundamental right to equality in the matter of employment which is guaranteed to him under Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
Dr. Gupta submitted that the Screening Committee and the respondent No. 4 could not have denied Selection Grade to the petitioner on the basis of the adverse remarks allegedly recorded in the Annual Confidential Reports of the petitioner for the years 1990-91, 1991-92, 1992-93 because these reports fell out of the zone of consideration at the time the petitioner's case for grant of Selection Grade w.e.f.
111986 was considered.
He further submitted that the stale adverse entries recorded in the Annual Confidential Reports of the petitioner for the years 1971-72, 1978-79 and 1979-80 could not have been looked into for the purpose of assessing the suitability of the petitioner for grant of Selection Grade because these very entries must be deemed to have been considered insignificant by the competent authorities at the time of grant of upgraded pay scale to the petitioner w.e.f.
1.4.1980 and also when the petitioner was allowed to cross efficiency bar w.e.f.
111988.
Learned counsel submitted that the decision of the competent authority to allow the petitioner to cross efficiency bar w.e.f.
111988 is sufficient to prove that as on that date his record was considered satisfactory by the Departmental Promotion Committee.
Dr. Gupta dubbed the order of the Tribunal as wholly erroneous by pointing out that the consideration of the petitioner's case was adversely affected by irrelevant and extraneous material like the adverse remarks in his Annual Confidential Reports for the years 1971-72, 1978-79, 1979-80, 1990-91, 1991-92 and 1992-93.
Dr. Gupta also challenged the adverse entries made in the Annual Confidential Reports of the petitioner on the ground that the concerned authority did not follow the instructions issued by the Government of India for recording of Annual Confidential Reports.
4.
We have thoughtfully considered the submissions made by the learned counsel.
In our opinion, there is no merit in the claim made by the petitioner for grant of Selection Grade w.e.f.
111986.
As per the petitioner's own admission, he has earned adverse remarks in his Annual Confidential Reports in the following years :  1971-72  1978-79  1979-80  1980-81  1981-82  1982-83  1983-84  1990-91  1991-92  1992-93  His over all performance has been graded average in other Annual Confidential Reports.
His case was considered along with other employees for grant of Selection Grade by a Screening Committee consisting of Home Secretary, Chandigarh; Principal, Punjab Engineering College and Chairman of the Library.
The Committee found him unfit for grant of selection grade because of the adverse remarks entered in his Annual Confidential Reports.
5.
The argument of the learned counsel that the decision of the Screening Committee and the impugned order passed by the Tribunal are vitiated due to consideration of extraneous factors i.e. the adverse remarks contained in the Annual Confidential Reports relating to the years 1990-91, 1991-92 and 1992-93 appears attractive but cannot stand close scrutiny.
Reference to the adverse remarks of the later years has been made in the order of the Tribunal only in the context of the averments made in the reply filed on behalf of the official respondents.
However, these reports do not constitute the basis of Tribunal's conclusion that the decision of the respondents not to grant Selection Grade to the petitioner does not call for interference.
The Tribunal has, after examining the respective pleadings and record, held that the petitioner's challenge to the recommendations made by the Screening Committee merits rejection because he has failed to make out a case for interference with the order issued by the respondent No. 4 on the recommendations of the Screening Committee.
Therefore, it is not possible to agree with Dr. Gupta that the order passed by the Tribunal is legally erroneous.
6.
We are also convinced that the order dated 1421997 issued by the respondent No. 4 and the decision of the Tribunal rejecting the petitioner's claim do not call for interference by this Court only on the ground that the Screening Committee could not have taken into consideration the adverse reports of the later years.
Although, Dr. Gupta is right in his submission that the entries made in the Annual Confidential Reports of the petitioner pertaining to the period after 111986 were not relevant and germane to the field of consideration for judging his suitability for grant of Selection Grade w.e.f.
111986 but, in our opinion, the other adverse Annual Confidential Reports could legitimately be taken into consideration for forming a bona fide opinion that the petitioner was not fit for extending him the benefit of Selection Grade.
For the reasons best known to him, the petitioner did not challenge the adverse remarks recorded in his Annual Confidential Reports for the year 1978-79, 1980-81, 1982-83 and 1983-84 at the appropriate time.
Even before the Tribunal, he did not challenge these entries.
Therefore, the Screening Committee did not commit any error when it decided not to grant the benefit of Selection Grade to the petitioner by considering adverse entries pertaining to the years 1980-81, 1982-83 and 1983-84.
7.
The decision of the respondents to allow the petitioner to cross efficiency bar w.e.f.
111988 does not have any bearing on his claim for grant of Selection Grade w.e.f.
111986 because it has not been proved that while taking a decision to allow him to cross efficiency bar, the competent authority had considered the adverse remarks entered in the Annual Confidential Reports of the petitioner in respect of the years 1978-79, 1979- 80, 1980-81, 1981-82 and 1983-84.
Moreover, the petitioner has not placed before us any document to show that the criteria for grant of selection grade is similar to the one framed for allowing an employee to cross efficiency bar.
Thus, the action of the official respondents to allow the petitioner to cross efficiency bar w.e.f.
111988 cannot be made basis for grant of a declaration that denial of selection grade to him w.e.f.
111986 is vitiated by arbitrariness or is violative of the petitioner's fundamental right to equality guaranteed under Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
8.
Dr. Gupta made valiant effort to persuade us to examine the merits of some of the entries made in the Annual Confidential Reports of the petitioner by arguing that the same were manipulated by the Library Incharge, who was ill- disposed against the petitioner and who was interested in subverting his career.
However, we are not inclined to entertain his plea because neither in the application filed before the Tribunal nor is this petition, the adverse remarks entered in the Annual Confidential Reports of the petitioner have been challenged.
9.
No other point has been argued.
10.
For the reasons mentioned above, we hold that the order passed by the Tribunal does not suffer from any error of law.
We also hold that the order dated 1421997 issued by the respondent No. 4 is neither arbitrary nor violative of the petitioner's fundamental right to equality.
Hence, the writ petition is dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
