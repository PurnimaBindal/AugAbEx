GS Singhvi, J - In this second round in litigation, the petitioner has questioned the legality of Annexure P-17 by which respondent Nos. 3 to 7 have been assigned seniority above him in the cadre of Superintendents, Public Relations Department, Haryana.
Petitioner has prayed for quashing of Annexure P-17, and also for issue of a direction to respondent No. 1 to promote him as Deputy Director, Public Relations with effect from 1.7,1992 along with all consequential benefits.
2.
The petitioner had earlier filed Civil Writ Petition No. 3475 of 1992, which was allowed by this Court on 29.5.1992 (Annexure P-13) and a direction was given to respondent No.
I to prepare inter se seniority between the petitioner and respondents 3 to 7 afresh after giving opportunity of representation and personal hearing to all the affected persons.
In pursuance of the judgment of this court, respondent No. 1 issued show-cause notice (Annexure P-14) dated 1481992 and after considering the representations filed by the petitioner as well as respondents 3 to 5 and after hearing them, respondent No. 1 has issued the impugned order, In order to determine as to whether the petitioner is entitled to succeed in his challenge to the order (Annexure P-17) it is necessary to set out the facts relating to the services of the petitioner as well as respondents 3 to 7.
3.
Petitioner joined service as Clerk on 29.1.1959 in the Public Relations Department of the then State of Punjab.
He was promoted as Steno-typist on 1361960 and then as Junior Scale Stenographer with effect from 31.5.1961 (pay scale of Rs. 100-175).
Upon reorganisation of the State of Punjab and creation of the State of Haryana, the petitioner opted for the State of Haryana.
He was promoted as Senior Scale Stenographer (pay scale Rs. 116-225) with effect from 1111966 and was confirmed on that post with effect from 1111967.
He was appointed as Assistant w.e.f.
141969 vide Annexure P-1.
This post he held upto 8.7,1970.
With effect from 971970, the petitioner was appointed as Information Assistant.
After about two months, he was again posted as Assistant with effect from 1691970.
On this post he worked upto 2111971.
He was posted as Staff Reporter with effect from 28.1.1971.
He was regularly appointed as Staff Reporter on 591974 after selection by the Subordinate Services Selection Board.
He was promoted as Personal Assistant with effect from 10.12.1979 but was again posted as Staff Reporter on 2311980.
In between, he remained posted as Assistant from 7.12.1978 to 19.4.1979.
On 731980, Director, Public Relations, Haryana, issued order (Annexure P-4) promoting the petitioner and two others as Superintendents in the pay scale of Rs. 1,000-1,500.
Appointment of the petitioner as Superintendent was regularised by the Government vide order (Annexure P-6) dated 1871989 which was issued on the advice of the Haryana Public Service Commission.
Petitioner's promotion on the post of Superintendent was regularised with effect from 731980.
The post of Superintendent held by the petitioner and four others was declared as gazetted by order dated 2211986 (Annexure P-5).
In the seniority-lists of Superintendents issued vide Annexures P-7 and P-8, showing the position of the members of service as on 111988 and 3111989, the petitioner was placed at serial No. 2 and private respondents were placed down below.
After about two years of the issue of Annexure P-8, respondent No. 2 issued notice dated 3071991 to the petitioner intimating him that the Government has decided to refix his inter se seniority with respondents 3 to 7.
In Annexure P-10, the petitioner was placed below all the private respondents and he was called upon to file his objections.
After the petitioner had submitted his representation, the Government issued order (Annexure P-12) dated 2491991 and finalised the inter se seniority of the petitioner and respondents 3 to 7 placing all of them above the petitioner.
This order was challenged by the petitioner in Civil Writ Petition No. 3475 of 1992 and, as already mentioned above, the same was allowed by this court on 29.5.1992.
4.
Respondents 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7 joined service as Clerks in the Public Relations Department on 28121956, 15.5.1956, 1471957, 191960 and 191960 respectively.
They worked as Clerks till 30.10.1966 in the grade of Rs. 60-175.
Respondents 3, 4 and 5 were promoted as Assistants with effect from 1111966 in the pay scale of Rs. 116-250.
Respondents 6 and 7 were promoted as Assistants on 1141969 and 1551970 respectively.
Respondent No. 3 was further promoted as Deputy Superintendent and Superintendent with effect from 4111966 and 731980 respectively.
Respondent Nos. 4 to 7 were promoted as Deputy Superintendents on 12101977 and as Superintendents with effect from 8101980, 8101980, 1311981 and 971986 respectively.
Respondent No. 3 was promoted as Section Incharge before his promotion as Superintendent (non-gazetted) vide order (Annexure P-4).
He was promoted as Administrative Officer on 9111989.
Respondent No. 4 was promoted as Store Officer with effect from 1571991.
Similarly respondent No. 5 was also promoted as Store Officer with effect from 1571991.
5.
Petitioner has challenged order (Annexure P-17).
on the ground of arbitrariness and mala fides.
Petitioner has pleaded that his inter se seniority has been changed without any reason or rhyme because none of the private respondents except respondent No. 3 had made grievance against the seniority lists issued vide Annexures P-7 and P-8 and respondent No. 3 withdrew his representation vide Annexure P-9 dated 16101989 because the petitioner had also given up his claim for, the posts of Store Officer/Administrative Officer.
According to the petitioner, after 16101989 no material was available with respondent No. 1 to change his seniority vis-a-vis respondent Nos. 3 to 7.
Another plea of the petitioner is that on the basis of the date of his promotion as Superintendent, respondent Nos. 3 to 7 cannot be ranked senior to him because respondent Nos. 4 and 7 were promoted as Superintendents after his promotion and respondent No. 3 was below him in the order of promotion (Annexure P-4).
6.
In their reply, respondent Nos. 1 and 2 have stated that the petitioner was confirmed as Senior Scale Stenographer and as Staff Reporter and, therefore, he had no right to be promoted as Superintendent by being treated as Assistant.
Further case of the respondents is that the petitioner was promoted as Assistant with effect from 141969 on the basis of his seniority as Clerk and as he had actually worked as such from 141969 to 871970 and 1591970 to 21.7.1971, he cannot claim higher seniority vis-a-vis other Assistants under Rule 3(1) of the Punjab Civil Services (Promotion of Stenographers and Steno-typists) Rules, 1961.
Respondent Nos. 1 and 2 have also stated that the Government had turned down the claim of the petitioner for seniority above respondent Nos. 3 to 7 and for promotion as Superintendent.
However, respondent No. 2 did issue order dated 731980 in favour of the petitioner.
Against this promotion, respondent No. 5 had made representation dated 731980 which remained undecided for a sufficiently long time.
Respondents have pleaded that posts of Section Incharge and Deputy Superintendent are in the ministerial cadre whereas the post of Staff Reporter is not in the clerical line and as the petitioner was confirmed on the post of Staff Reporter, he did not retain lien in the clerical cadre and he was not even eligible to be promoted as Superintendent.
Further assertion of respondent Nos. 1 and 2 is that the post of Administrative Officer was always treated higher than the post of Public Relations Officer and respondent No. 3 was rightly treated as senior to the petitioner because he was holding the post carrying higher pay-scale than the post held by the petitioner.
Respondents have pleaded that the order (Annexure P-17) has been passed after giving full opportunity of hearing to the petitioner in accordance with the directions given by the High Court and, therefore, he can have no grievance against the impugned order.
7.
In their separate reply, respondent Nos. 4, 5 and 6 have stated that the petitioner was promoted as Assistant with effect from 141969 whereas they were promoted as Assistants with effect from 1111966 and, therefore, the petitioner has no right to be treated as senior to them.
These respondents have referred to a gradation-list of non-gazetted (Class-III) staff of the Public Relations Department (Annexure R-3/1) to show that they were placed above the petitioner.
Further case set up by the respondents is that the petitioner was not entitled to be promoted as Superintendent in preference to them because he was promoted as an Assistant much after the promotion of respondents 3 to 5.
They have referred to a note prepared by the Legal Remembrancer, Haryana, in support of their case that the petitioner did not fulfil the conditions of eligibility.
8.
After having heard the learned counsel for the parties and having perused the record as well as the relevant rules, I am of the opinion that the writ petition deserves to be allowed because respondent No. 2 has issued order (Annexure P-17) without proper application of the rules and without appreciation of the facts in a correct perspective.
9.
The facts that the petitioner was promoted as Senior Scale Stenographer with effect from 1111966 in the pay scale of Rs. 116-250 and that he was promoted as Superintendent with effect from 731980 vide order (Annexure P-4) are not in dispute.
Indeed, it is also not in dispute that vide order (Annexure P-6) dated 1871989 issued by the Government, the petitioner's promotion as Superintendent with effect from 731980 was regularised on the advice of the Haryana Public Service Commission.
Neither the order Annexure P-4 nor the order Annexure P-6 has been rescinded or modified.
It is, therefore, clear that since 731980, the petitioner is holding the post of Superintendent and his promotion on that post has been treated as regular one.
Respondent No. 3 was promoted as Superintendent on 731980 along with the petitioner but his name was placed below the petitioner in Annexure P-4 and no material has been placed on record to show that his promotion was subsequently regularised by the Government.
In so far as other respondents are concerned, they were promoted as Deputy Superintendents with effect from 12101977 and as Superintendents with effect from 8101980, 3011981 and 791986, respectively.
In view of the fact that respondents 4 to 7 were promoted as Superintendents after the petitioner, they could not be treated as senior to the petitioner in the cadre of Superintendents.
However in the impugned order, respondent No. 1 has treated respondent Nos. 4 to 7 senior to the petitioner without any reason or rhyme.
10.
The Punjab Public Relations Department (Class-III non-gazetted) Service Rules, 1958, on which learned counsel for both the parties have placed reliance, speaks of seniority from the date of substantive appointment.
Rule 11 of 1958 Rules which deal with seniority reads as under :-  "Rule II of 1958 Rules :  (i) The seniority inter se of the members of the Service holding the same class of posts shall be determined by the date of their substantive appointment to such post;  Provided that if two or more members are substantively appointed in the same class of posts on the same date, their seniority shall be determined as follows :  (a) a member recruited by promotion within the Service shall be senior to a member recruited otherwise;  (b) a member recruited by transfer from a department of Government other than the Punjab Public Relations Department shall be senior to a member recruited by direct appointment;  (c) in the case of members who are recruited by promotion according to the seniority in the appointment from which the members are promoted;  (d) in the case of members who are recruited by transfer from a cadre other than the Punjab Public Relations Department cadre, according to their seniority in the appointments previously held in the cadre;  (e) in the case of members who are recruited by transfer from different cadres other than the Punjab Public Relations Department cadre according to pay, preference being given to one who was drawing a higher rate of pay in his previous appointment and if the rate of pay drawn is the same, an older member shall be senior to a younger member; and  (f) in the case of members who are recruited by direct a appointment in accordance with the order merit determined by the Punjab Public Service Commission/Punjab Subordinate Services Selection Board, as the case may be.
(ii) The date of appointment orders all determine the initial seniority; provided that when more than one person is appointed on the same day, the seniority shall be in accordance with age."
11.
If in the light of this Rule, petitioner's claim is examined vis-a- vis respondent Nos. 4 to 7, there remains no doubt that on the basis of date of appointment/the date of substantive appointment, the petitioner has a right to be assigned seniority over and above respondent Nos. 4 to 7.
While deciding the seniority in pursuance of the directions given by the Court, respondent No. 1 has completely overlooked the fact that the petitioner had been appointed as Superintendent much prior to respondent Nos. 4 to 7.
Respondent No. 1 has also overlooked the provisions contained in Rule 11 of the 1958 Rules.
Moreover, the respondent No. 1 misdirected himself in considering the position obtaining prior to the petitioner's promotion as Superintendent.
Whether the petitioner was rightly promoted as Superintendent on.
731980 and whether regularisation of his promotion is correct or not were not the matters on which respondent No. 1 could make any adjudication because neither the promotion of the petitioner with effect from 731980 nor the regularisation of his promotion vide order (Annexure P-6) dated 1871989 was under challenge nor they were the subject-matter of review by the competent authority.
Therefore, respondent No.
I was clearly in error in declaring respondent Nos. 4 to 7 as senior to the petitioner in the cadre of Superintendents on the assumption that the petitioner was not eligible to be promoted as Superintendent w.e.f.
731980.
12.
In so far as the seniority of the petitioner vis-a-vis respondent No. 3 is concerned, the reasons assigned in the impugned order are more or less the same as have been assigned by respondent No. 1 for treating the other respondents senior to the petitioner and after having given my thoughtful consideration to the rival submissions on this aspect of the case, I am clearly of the opinion that respondent No.
I has gravely erred in placing respondent No. 3 above the petitioner.
Respondent No. 1 has completely overlooked the factual as well as legal position while treating respondent No. 3 as senior to the petitioner.
As on 1111966, the petitioner and the respondent No. 3 were holding the posts of Senior Scale Stenographer and Assistant respectively.
Both these posts carried the same payscale and even though the petitioner was subsequently appointed as Assistant with effect from 1.4,1969, his seniority as on 1111966 had to be determined keeping in view the provisions contained in the Punjab Civil Services (Promotion of Stenographers and Stenotypists) Rules, 1961, which were treated as applicable to the employees of the Haryana Government.
These Rules were, amended by the Governor of Haryana in exercise of his powers under proviso to Article 309 of the Constitution of India vide notifications dated 2561971 and 10.
11.
1978.
By the first notification, 1961 Rules were made applicable to the Stenographers and Steno-typists of all the departments of the Government of Haryana except those of the Haryana Civil Secretariat and the Punjab and Haryana High Court.
By the second notification, the existing Rule 3(1) was substituted.
The old Rule 3(1) provided that Stenographers and Steno-typists shall be eligible for promotion to the posts of Assistants, Head Assistants, Deputy Superintendents or Superintendents after one had worked as Assistant for a period of two years on some existing vacancy or by sharing the work of an Assistant.
Rule 3(2) provided that the seniority of a Stenographer, who successfully completes the period of two years training under sub-rule (1), vis-a-vis Assistants shall be determined by the dates of their continuous appointment against the posts of Stenographer or Assistant or if the dates of their appointment were to be the same, the one drawing higher pay than the other was to be treated senior.
If the rates of pay drawn by them were also the same, the older was to be treated senior to the younger one.
Under the substituted Rule 3(1), it has been provided that the period during which a Stenographer has performed the duties of an Assistant whether in addition to his own duties or otherwise will be taken into consideration while computing the period of his training as Assistant.
Rule 3(2) which relates to inter se seniority of Stenographers and Assistants has remained unchanged.
It is, therefore, clear that under the Rules of 1961 inter se seniority of Stenographers and Assistants had to be determined in terms of Rules 3(2) of the Rules of 1961.
However, no material has been produced before the Court by the petitioner or the respondents to show that their seniority has in fact been determined in accordance with the Rules of 1961.
1 may have made an adjudication on the issue of inter se seniority between the petitioner and respondent No. 3 but in the absence of complete material, I do not consider it proper to give any decision in this regard.
In my opinion, it should be just and proper to direct the respondent No. 1 to make a determination of inter se seniority between the petitioner and respondent No3 by taking into consideration their position as on 1111966 and also by taking into consideration the provisions of the Rules of 1961.
The respondent No. 1 shall also keep in view the fact that the petitioner and respondent No. 3 were promoted as Superintendents on temporary basis with effect from 731980 and vide Annexure P-6 promotion of the petitioner was regularised with effect from 1731980 itself.
13.
In the result, the writ petition is allowed.
Order (Annexure P-17) is declared illegal and is quashed.
It is declared that the petitioner is senior to respondent Nos. 4 to 7.
In regard to the inter se seniority of the petitioner and respondent No. 3, respondent No. 1 is directed to undertake a fresh exercise and determine their inter se seniority within four weeks.
While doing so, respondent No. 1 shall keep in mind their service particulars as well as relevant service Rules.
In case the petitioner is found to be senior to the respondent No. 3, appropriate action shall be taken by the Government in next three months to give retrospective promotion to the petitioner as Deputy Director along with all consequential benefits.
On the other hand, if the Government holds that respondent No. 3 is senior to the petitioner, no further action will be required to be taken.
Parties are left to bear their own costs.
Petition allowed.
