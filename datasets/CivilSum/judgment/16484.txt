Pranab Kumar Chattopadhyay, J - Petitioners herein have filed this writ application being aggrieved by the order dated 17th September, 2004 passed by the learned Central Administrative Tribunal, Kolkata Bench in OA No. 1036 of 2001 along with MA No. 614 of 2003, MA No. 486 of 2004 and C.P.C. No. 100 of 2003.
2.
On examination of the aforesaid order of the learned Central Administrative Tribunal it appears that the respondent/applicant filed an application before the said Central Administrative Tribunal, Kolkata Bench praying for certain reliefs and upon hearing both the parties, learned Tribunal on 14th September 2001, passed an interim order to the following effect :  "Having considered the submission made by the parties and having perused the transfer policy dated 31st of January, 1996 and the facts stated in the application, we direct that the status quo regarding transfer of the applicant from Calcutta shall be maintained till the next date.
The matter be listed for admission hearing on 18.10.2001."
3.
The petitioners herein, however, issued the transfer order to the respondent/applicant on 27th November, 2003 and relieved the said respondent from the duties w.e.f.
9th December, 2003 even though the aforesaid interim order passed by the learned Central Administrative Tribunal was in force.
4.
Being aggrieved by the aforesaid transfer order issued by the petitioners herein, a Contempt Petition was filed before the learned Tribunal on behalf of the respondent/applicant herein.
5.
It is not in dispute that the learned Tribunal by the order dated 14th September, 2001 directed that the status quo regarding transfer of the respondent/applicant from Calcutta shall be maintained and the petitioners herein transferred the respondent/applicant by issuing the transfer order on 27th November, 2003 even though the aforesaid interim order passed by the learned Tribunal was in force.
Upon hearing the learned Counsel of the respective parties, the learned Tribunal ultimately held that the transfer order passed by the petitioners herein on 27th November, 2003 was invalid and the learned Tribunal further held that the respondent/applicant was relieved in pursuance to the non est illegal order dated 27th November, 2003.
The relevant portion of the aforesaid order passed by the learned Tribunal on 17th September, 2004 is quoted hereunder :  13.
After counsel for the parties have been heard, we are of the view that after the Tribunal had passed the order dated 14.9.2001 directing the respondents to maintain status quo regarding transfer of the applicant from Calcutta and the said order was in force, any order passed in violation of the Tribunal's direction is non est.
The.
same cannot be given effect.
Hence the order passed by the respondents on 27112003 is invalid.
As the applicant was relieved in pursuance to the non est/illegal order dated 27112003, the same will have no effect on the continuity of service of the applicant.
By this illegal order dated 27112003 the applicant was prevented from working on the post he was holding and to which he was entitled to work in the light of the Tribunal's direction.
Hence the applicant shall be deemed to be on duty on the said post till he is allowed to join.
The whole intervening period shall be treated as on duty and the applicant shall get all the consequential benefits including the salary for the said period."
6.
In view of the aforesaid order passed by the learned Tribunal and in compliance with the subsequent order passed by this Hon'ble Court the applicant/respondent was allowed to joining service and all the consequential benefits were also granted to the said respondent/applicant excepting the salary for the intervening period when the applicant was relieved from duty pursuant to the order dated 27th November, 2003 till the said applicant was allowed to join i.e. for the period 9th December, 2003 to 21st September, 2004.
7.
The learned counsel of the petitioners submits that the respondent applicant herein is not entitled to receive any salary and other financial benefits for the period he did not discharge any duty.
The learned Counsel for the petitioners also submits that the respondent/applicant cannot be treated as on duty during the aforesaid period i.e. on and from 9th December, 2003 to 21st September, 2004 as during the aforesaid period, the said applicant admittedly did not discharge any duty.
8.
The learned counsel of the petitioners submits that following the principle of "no work no pay", the petitioners herein should not be directed to make any payment of salary and other allowances to the respondent/applicant during the aforesaid intervening period, i.e. on and from 9th December, 2003 to 21st, September 2004 when the said respondent/applicant did not discharge any duty.
9.
We are unable to accept the aforesaid contentions of the petitioners herein as the applicant was illegally relieved pursuant to the illegal/non est order dated 27th November, 2003.
10.
The learned Tribunal has rightly held that the aforesaid order dated 27th November, 2003 was non est/illegal and will not have any effect on the continuity of service of the respondent/applicant.
Since the respondent applicant was prevented from discharging his due duties and responsibilities by the petitioners herein pursuant to the aforesaid order dated 27th November, 2003 during the intervening period commencing on and from 9th December, 2003 to 21st September, 2004, the petitioners herein must treat the said respondent as on duty during the aforesaid period and shall also pay salary and other financial benefits for the said period.
11.
The learned Counsel for the petitioners cited the following decisions in support of his arguments :  (1) AIR 2006 SC 531 [Baldev Singh v. Union of India and Ors.]  (2) AIR 2007 SC 259 [Union of India and Anr. v. Tarsem Lal and Ors.]  The aforesaid decisions are not at all applicable in the facts and circumstances of the present case.
12.
For the aforementioned reasons, we are of the opinion that the learned Tribunal was justified in directing the petitioners herein to make payment of the salary and other allowances to the respondent/applicant for the intervening period i.e. on and from 9th December, 2003 to 21st September, 2004 treating the said applicant as on duty during the said period as the respondent/applicant was illegally prevented from discharging the duties by the petitioners herein during the said period pursuant to an illegal/non est order.
13.
We, therefore, approve the directions passed by the learned Tribunal in the impugned order dated 17th September, 2004 and refuse to interfere with the same.
14.
This writ petition thus stands dismissed being devoid of any merit.
There will be, however, no order as to costs.
Let Urgent Xerox certified copy of this judgment and order, if applied for, be given to the learned Advocates of the parties on usual undertaking.
Arunabha Basu, J - I agree.
Petition dismissed.
