SS Nijjar, J - This order will dispose of C.W.P.
No. 11923 of 1999 and C.W.P.
No. 4142 of 2000, as the questions of law and facts involved in these two petitions, are identical.
2.
In C.W.P.
No. 11923 of 1999, the petitioner seeks a direction to the respondents for grant of consequential benefits, such as Pension, Gratuity, Earned Leave for the period he spent on Extra-ordinary leave from 30111983 to 1481985.
He also seeks direction to the respondents to count the period of extra-ordinary leave from 30111983 to 1481985 towards promotion as Professor in Physics under the University Grants Commission Merit Promotion Scheme.
3.
In C.W.P.
No. 4142 of 2000, the petitioner seeks the fixation of pay and arrears of salary with effect from 111996 till 3112000.
He also seeks a direction to the respondents to pay arrears of gratuity, leave encashment and pension according to the basic pay of Rs. 20,000/- as on 3112000 in the revised University Grant Commission Pay-scale.
The Facts :  4.
The petitioner was appointed as Reader in Physics in the services of the University on 19101977.
This was preceded by the recommendations of the Selection Committee which were duly approved by the Executive Council of the University vide resolution No. 9 dated 15101977.
After one year and eight months, the petitioner's services were terminated in pursuance of resolution dated 20.6.1979 passed by the Executive Council.
The same became subject- matter of consideration by the Commission of Enquiry appointed by the Government of Haryana to enquire into the alleged acts of omissions and commission committed by the then Vice-Chancellor of the University-Shri Hardwari Lal.
In his report dated 3061980, Justice SS Dulat (Retd.)
who headed one-member Commission, observed that the Vice-Chancellor had not dealt with the petitioner's case fairly.
Similar observations were made by him in regard to other teachers whose services were terminated by the University.
The Government of Haryana accepted the report of the enquiry Commission which was also approved by the Haryana Legislative Assembly.
Thereafter, vide order dated 5101983 recorded on the file, the Vice-Chancellor of the University re-appointed petitioner as Reader in Physics.
On the same day, the Registrar of the University issued order for re-appointment of the petitioner as Reader in Physics in the pay-scale of Rs. 1200-1900/-.
5.
While the petitioner was out of service, Dr. Wahid U Malik, the then Vice-Chancellor, University of Kashmir recommended his case to Head of Physics Department, University of Zambia for assignment as Senior Lecturer.
After some correspondence, University Secretary of the University of Zambia, vide his letter dated 2791983, offered the post of Senior Lecturer in Physics to the petitioner.
On receipt of the aforementioned communication, the petitioner submitted application dated 2111983 to the University for grant of leave.
The same was sanctioned by the Executive Council of the University vide resolution No. 26 dated 21111983.
After completing his teaching assignment with the University of Zambia, the petitioner resumed duty in the University in August, 1985.
On 2991985, he submitted an application to the Vice- Chancellor of the University for grant of three increments by counting the period of extra-ordinary leave as duty period.
He repeated this request vide letter dated 24.5.1986.
His request was considered by the Executive Council of the University in its various meetings and finally it was decided to count the period of extra-ordinary leave towards annual increments.
The resolution passed by the Executive Council on 1871991 was reiterated in its meeting held on 731992.
The decision taken in that meeting was conveyed by the Registrar of the University to the Resident Assistant Examiner vide letter dated 2061992.
The Vice-Chancellor of the University also granted approval to the counting of extra-ordinary leave availed by the petitioner from 30111983 to 1481985 towards annual increments in terms of Clause 26(c) of the Leave Regulations.
This was conveyed to the petitioner by the Registrar of the University by his letter dated 621995.
6.
In the meantime, the Executive Council considered the issue relating to condonation of the break in services of the petitioner and two others, namely, Dr. SP Shukla and Dr. Satpal Singh Dahiya and vide resolution No. 106 passed in the meeting held on 2531991, it was decided to condone the break in the service of the petitioner and also grant him resultant benefits except financial benefits.
The Registrar of the University, vide his letter dated 1041991, conveyed the decision of the Executive Council to the petitioner.
However, the concerned authority of the University did not implement the decision of the Executive Council necessitating the filing of the C.W.P.
No. 5093 of 1999 by the petitioner which was allowed by a Division Bench of this Court vide order dated 2712000.
7.
The University challenged the aforesaid order by filing a petition for Special Leave to Appeal (Civil) No. 10849 of 2000.
The same was dismissed by the Supreme Court on 6112000.
8.
During the intervening period, the petitioner was confirmed on the post of Reader in Physics with effect from 2061986 vide Resolution No. 25 dated 2911987 of the Executive Council and his seniority was fixed vide the Registrar's letter dated 2951991 by taking into consideration his total service in that cadre.
He was also promoted as Professor in Physics w.e.f.
3131986.
9.
I have heard the petitioner in person and the counsel for the respondents at length and perused the record of the case.
10.
The petitioner filed CWP No. 7272 of 1998 claiming the same relief.
The writ petition was dismissed by the learned Single Judge.
Against the decision of the learned Single Judge, the petitioner had filed LPA No. 2 of 2002 which was also dismissed.
Against the aforesaid LPA, the petitioner filed Civil Appeal No. 1568 of 2004 arising out of S.L.P.
(C) No. 10941 of 2003, which was disposed of by the Supreme Court with a direction to the High Court to hear the appeal afresh and pass a reasoned order by taking into consideration all relevant aspects of the matter after giving opportunity of hearing to the parties.
11.
On remand, LPA No. 2 of 2002 has been decided by this Court on 29112004.
The petitioner had also filed CWP No. 5093 of 1999.
In the aforesaid writ petition, this Court again considered the question as to whether the petitioner is entitled to refixation of his pay by treating the period from 20.6.1979 to 4.11.1983 so as to include the increments that accrued to him during this period.
This writ petition was allowed by a Division Bench of this Court on 2712000 with the following observations :-  "The grievance now made before us is that even though the petitioner is about to retire in a couple of days the University has not so far re-fixed his pay during the break period by treating his service continuous.
In the written statement filed by the University, it is nowhere pleaded that the pay of the petitioner was re-fixed for the break period or that the increments to which he became entitled were paid to him.
It is averred in the reply that on his re-appointment the petitioner was granted pay which he was drawing before his services were terminated on 20.6.1979.
It is, thus, clear that the resolution passed by the Executive Council condoning the break in service has not been implemented in its entirety and the petitioner had not been granted the consequential benefits.
A bare reading of the resolution passed by the Executive Council makes it clear that the petitioner is entitled to have his service from 20.6.1979 to 4101983 counted and his pay re-fixed after calculating the increments which he would have earned during the said period.
This not having been done, we have no hesitation in allowing the writ petition which we hereby do and direct the University to re-fix the pay of the petitioner from 20.6.1979 till 4101983 treating him in service and giving him the benefit of annual increments which he would have earned during that period.
We further direct the University to re-fix his pay for the period after he was re-appointed till the date of his promotion as Professor and pay him the arrears of pay for that period.
For the sake of clarification we may mention that the petitioner would not be entitled to the arrears of pay for the period from 20.6.1979 to 4101983.
We may also observe that the petitioner will be entitled to all the consequential retiral benefits by treating him in service for the aforesaid break period.
He will also be entitled to gratuity and pension by treating him in service during that period.
The University is further directed to pay the arrears to the petitioner within a period of one month together with interest thereon @ 15% per annum.
The Vice-Chancellor of the University shall fix the responsibility of the officer concerned who was responsible for re-fixing the pay of the petitioner and the amount of interest paid by the University shall be recovered from that officer without debiting the amount to the account of the University.
Petitioner is entitled to have his costs which are assessed at Rs. 5,000/-.

The aforesaid observations make it abundantly clear that the claim put forward by the petitioner has already been allowed.
The judgment has become final and the respondents are duty bound to implement the same.
13.
In the judgment of LPA No. 2 of 2002, decided on 29112004, after considering the entire matter, the LPA Bench has observed as follows :-  "In our opinion, the appellant's case is almost indentical to that of Dr. Satpal Taneja and the learned Single Judge erred in declining relief to him.
There is no dispute between the parties that his case was considered by the Executive Council and the Vice-Chancellor of the University in accordance with amended sub-clause (c) of Clause 26 and it was decided to give him the benefit of increments by taking into consideration the period of extra- ordinary leave from 30111983 to 1481985 during which he worked as Lecturer in Physics in the University of Zamiba.
The resolution passed by the Executive Council and the order passed by the Vice-Chancellor for counting the period of extra- ordinary leave towards annual increments in terms of Clause 26(c) of the Leave Regulations have not been withdrawn, annulled or rescinded.
Therefore, there is no escape from the conclusion that the ratio of Dr. Satpal Taneja's case is applicable to the appellant's case and the decision taken by the University in 1998 which was conveyed to the appellant vide letter dated 15.4.1998 declaring his claim for grant of annual increments for the period from 30111983 to 1481985 as infructuous cannot but be termed as totally arbitrary, unconscionable and unjustified.
The learned Single Judge declined to apply the ratio of the order passed in Dr. Satpal Taneja's case (supra) to the appellant's case by observing that while the former had taken up the assignment in a foreign University as an employee of the University, the latter had taken up the assignment when he was not in the employment of the University.
In our opinion, the reason assigned by the learned Single Judge while distinguishing the appellant's case vis-a- vis that of Dr. Satpal Taneja's case is clearly erroneous.
In that case, the learned Single Judge held that notification dated 241998 cannot be operated with retrospective effect so as to deprive the petitioner of the benefit of annual increments payable to him in terms of Clause 26(c), as amended vide resolution No. 13 dated 28.11.1990 passed by the Executive Council.
The same principle ought to have been applied to the appellant's case because in his case also, the Executive Council had extended the benefit of amended Clause 26(c) of the Leave Regulations much before the issuance of notification dated 241998.
As a conrollary to this, we hold that the learned Single Judge committed a serious error by refusing to direct the University to implement the resolutions passed by the Executive Council and the order passed by the Vice-Chancellor for grant of annual increments to the appellant by taking into account the period of extra-ordinary leave.
We are further of the view that the learned Single Judge erred in rejecting the appellant's claim for annual increments in lieu of the period of extra- ordinary leave on the ground that he had not received invitation from the University of Zambia as envisaged by amended Clause 26(c) of the Leave Regulations.
While doing so, the learned Single Judge overlooked the fact that the Executive Council of the University which is an expert body had repeatedly examined the appellant's case and concluded that the same is covered by amended Clause 26(c).
The Executive Council must have looked into the papers including the offer of appointment given to the appellant by the University of Zambia vide letter dated 2791983.
Therefore, the learned Single Judge was not justified in taking a different view.
The issue may be considered from another angle.
The word `invitation' used in amended Clause 26(c) of the Leave Regulations consists of two parts.
The first part includes a direct offer sent by the host institution.
The second part includes any offer received in response to an application bio-data sent by the employee through the University.
The appellant's case is covered by the first part of the definition of the term "invitation" because he had received the offer of appointment from the University of Zambia, may be on the recommendation of Dr. Wahid U Malik, the then Vice-Chancellor of the University of Kashmir.
Therefore, we have no hesitation to hold that the Execution Council had rightly sanctioned annual increments to the appellant in lieu of the period of extra-ordinary leave.
In the result, the appeal is allowed.
The order of the learned Single Judge is set aside.
The writ petition filed by the appellant is allowed.
It is declared that notification dated 241998 cannot be applied to the appellant's case so as to deprive him of the benefit of the annual increments.
The respondent- University is directed to implement resolutions dated 1871991 and 731992 passed by the Executive Council and order dated 621995 passed by the Vice- Chancellor and give the benefit of annual increments to the appellant in lieu of the period of extra-ordinary leave from 30111983 to 1481985.
The monetary benefits payable to the appellant be calculated and the amount found due be paid to him within a period of three months from the ate of receipt/submission of the certified copy of this order.

The aforesaid observation of the LPA Bench makes it abundantly clear that the petitioner is entitled to the consequential benefits for the period of extra-ordinary leave from 30111983 to 1481985.
The petitioner is clearly entitled to be promoted with effect from 3131986, as entire period in dispute has been treated to be period spent on duty.
He was promoted on the post of Professor of Physics by order dated 1921991 in the pay-scale of Rs. 4500-150-5700-200-7300/-.
Subsequently, by order dated 28121991 the petitioner was ordered to be promoted with effect from 3131986 i.e. cut off date on which the petitioner became eligible for promotion.
It was made clear in the order that the promotion shall be for the limited purpose of pay fixation and no arrears shall be paid.
However, for further promotion under the Scheme, the period shall be counted from the cut off date of the eligibility year.
In view of the clarification dated 1881984 and the communication dated 28121991, there shall be no justification to deny the promotion to the petitioner on the post of Professor with effect from 3131986.
15.
In view of the judgments rendered by the Division Bench in CWP No. 5093 of 1999 and LPA No. 2 of 2002, the petitioner cannot be denied the relief of promotion with effect from 3131986.
16.
On 1881984, the University Grants Commission had issued the following directions to the Vice-Chancellor of the respondent-University :-  "UNIVERSITY GRANTS COMMISSION BAHADUR SHAH ZAFAR MARG NEW DELHI.
YD Sharma Deputy Secretary DO No. F1-7/83 (MP) Dated 18th August, 1984 Dear Vice-Chancellor The University Grants Commission at its meeting held on 7th July, 1984 on reference from a university considered the question of counting the period of study leave/extraordinary leave availed of by a teacher towards computation of eight years of continuous service in the cadre of promotion under the Merit Promotion Scheme.
The Commission agreed that the period of leave which entitles a teacher to earn annual increment would be counted towards computation of eight years of continuous service in the cadre of promotion under the Merit Promotion Scheme.
The above decision of the Commission may also be brought to the notice of colleges affiliated to the University.
With regards, Yours sincerely, (YD Sharma) Sh. Hardwari Lal Vice-Chancellor Maharshi Dayanand University Rohtak - 124001."
17.
These directions make it abundantly clear that the petitioner has to be granted the benefit of the period of leave towards computation of eight years of continuous service in the cadre for promotion under the Merit Promotion Scheme.
18.
The petitions are allowed in the aforesaid terms.
The respondents are directed to grant the entire consequential relief to the petitioner within a period of two months from the date of receipt of a certified copy of this order.
No costs.
Petitions allowed.
