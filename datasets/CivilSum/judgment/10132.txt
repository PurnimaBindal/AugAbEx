Surya Kant, J. (Oral) - Late Devinder Singh joined the Punjab State Warehousing Corporation (for short the Corporation) as Accounts Clerk on 181972 and was later on promoted as Assistant.
As per his correct date of birth, he was to retire on attaining the age of superannuation with effect from 3111984, however, vide an order dated 18.3.1981 the said Devinder Singh was retired from service retrospectively with effect from 3111981.
Devinder Singh raised an industrial dispute against the afore-mentioned action of the Corporation which was answered in his favour by the Labour Court, Chandigarh vide an award dated 981983 which was published in the Gazette on 1691983 (Annexure P-1) in terms whereof the order of his premature retirement was set aside and he was directed to be reinstated in service with full back wages and all others benefits.
It is not disputed that the award passed by the Labour Court, Chandigarh was challenged by the Corporation before this Court through a writ petition which was, however, dismissed.
The matter was thereafter taken to the Hon'ble Supreme Court but SLP moved by the Corporation was also dismissed.
The award thus attained finality.
2.
Unfortunately, Devinder Singh expired on 1721984.
His legal representatives, namely, petitioner No. 1 - widow of Devinder Singh, and petitioners No. 2 to 7 - his daughters and son, have now approached this Court through the present writ petition to command the Corporation to implement the award (Annexure P-1) and to release the payment of salary and other monetary benefits in terms thereof in their favour.
3.
An affidavit dated 982004 has been filed by the Managing Director of the Corporation to the following effect :-  "(1) That the husband of the petitioner Devinder Singh was employed in the service of the respondent Corporation as Assistant (HO).
He was retired from service on 31181.
He filed a reference before the Labour Court which was decided in his favour.
As per the award dated 1691983 he was entitled to the reinstatement in service with all consequential benefits.
At the time of retirement of Sh. Devinder Singh he was paid his retiral benefits.
In compliance of the award, the respondent corporation passed an order dated 1961986/3786 whereby it was ordered that Sh. Devinder Singh would be deemed to have retired from service on 31184 (AN) instead of 31181 (AN).
It was further ordered that he would be entitled for pay and allowances from 1.2.81 to 31184.
A copy of this order is annexed as Annexure R-1.
(2) That the said employee through his legal heirs was held to be entitled to Rs. 41,49650 on account of arrears of pay, bonous etc. which was duly paid.
Out of this Rs. 36,45250 which was paid to him vide cheque No. 54568 dated 20388.
As the employee had since expired on 1721984, this amount was received by his widow Sita Devi under receipt to the Corporation.
A copy of the receipt is appended as Annexure R-2.
The amount paid to him included Rs. 36,45250 as his retiral benefits, Rs. 1,920/- which had been deducted as CPF employers share and Rs. 3,124/- which had been deducted as income tax.
Furthermore an amount of Rs. 3,380/- was paid to him under the Gratuity Act, 1972 after the sanction for the same was accorded on 19.4.84.
Apart from this, Rs. 110/- was also paid to Smt. Sita Devi as refund of cash security.
Besides above, CPF, vide cheque No. 050979 dated 241181 (both share) amount to Rs. 7,11510 had also been released.
(3) That all the amounts due to Sh. Devinder Singh in pursuance of the award dated 1691983 have been duly released and paid to his widow Smt. Sita Devi the petitioner in the present case.
The petitioner has nowhere raised any objection to the amount which has been released to him.
She has accepted the amount without any demur or protest.
The claim of Smt. Sita devi has been finally settled by the respondent Corporation."
4.
Thus, according to the respondents, the award, Annexure P-1, has been implemented and whatever monetary benefits were due to the deceased Devinder Singh and/or his heirs, the same have been released.
5.
However, the afore-mentioned stand taken on behalf of the Corporation has been contested by the petitioners by way of an affidavit dated 2492004 of Rupinder Singh son of late Devinder Singh (petitioner No. 6).
In reference to the said affidavit, Shri Arora, learned counsel for the petitioners, contends that the following monetary benefits are yet to be released by the Corporation :  (a) There has been a considerable delay in payment of the arrears of pay amounting to Rs. 36,45250, therefore, the petitioners are entitled to interest @ 18% pa from the date of award till it was paid vide cheque dated 2931988.
(b) There is no receipt indicating the actual payment of Rs. 3,380/- to Smt. Sita Devi (petitioner) as arrears of gratuity though sanction order for the said payment dated 7.5.1984 (Annexure R-3) has been placed on record.
(c) Though there is an order dated 19111981 in favour of late Devinder Singh (copy appended as Annexure R-5) sanctioning the release of contributory provident fund (CPF) amounting to Rs. 7,115/-, there is nothing on record like receipt etc. to suggest that the actual payment of the said amount was ever made to the deceased.
(d) The deceased employee was retired retrospectively with effect from 3111981 vide an order dated 18.3.1981 and was treated on Earned Leave from 3111981 to 18.3.1981.
Since the deceased employee is deemed to be in service till he attained the age of superannuation on 3111984, the petitioners are entitled for the enacashment of Earned Leave of 46 days utilised towards regularisation of the afore-mentioned period.
6.
Shri Gurminder Singh, learned counsel for the Corporation, however, vehemently contests the claims raised by Shri Arora as according to him, all the due amounts have already been released and if at all something is left out, this Court, in exercise of its writ jurisdiction, is not a proper forum for resolving such disputed questions of fact.
According to him, there has been no inordinate delay in the release of arrears of pay as explained in the additional affidavit dated 26102004.
7.
Having heard the learned counsel for the parties and after perusing the record, I am of the view there appears to be some delay in the release of arrears of pay to which the deceased employee, namely, Devinder Singh was entitled to as per the award dated 1691983 (Annexure P-1).
Said Devinder Singh expired on 1721984.
There was, thus, sufficient time to release the arrears of pay in favour of the employee himself or in favour of his legal representatives after he had expired.
The only explanation which finds mention in the affidavit dated 26102004 is that the petitioners applied for a succession certificate before the civil Court which was granted to them on 31.1.1988, and which was forwarded by them to the Corporation on 25.2.1988, therefore, there was no delay on the part of the Corporation in releasing the arrears of pay.
I am, however, not impressed by the afore-mentioned submission.
The claim of the deceased employee regarding release of arrears of pay having attained finality and the amount having been calculated by the Corporation, it could be deposited in some bank to earn interest at the rate prescribed by the Reserve Bank of India.
The amount that belonged to the deceased employee remained with the Corporation till it was actually released on 2931988.
Consequently, I am of the view that the Corporation is liable to pay interest upon the arrears of Rs. 36,45250 @ 7% pa with effect from 1.1.1984 till the same was actually disbursed vide cheque dated 2931988.
8.
So far as the uncertainty regarding the payment of the arrears of gratuity and the CPF is concerned, the Corporation is directed to send attested photostat copies of the receipts and/or a certificate to the effect that the afore-mentioned amounts were actually released and paid to the deceased employee or any one of his legal representatives.
9.
With regard to the claim of arrears for encashment of earned leave of 46 days which the Corporation adjusted for regularising the period with effect from 3111981 to 18.3.1981, since in terms of the award dated 1691983 (Annexure P-1) deceased Devinder singh is deemed to have retired on 3111984, he was, therefore, in uninterrupted continuous service of the Corporation during these 46 days as well.
If the benefit of leave encashment has been granted to the deceased employee for the days which are less than the maximum prescribed period for the grant of such a benefit, namely, 240 days, then the Corporation is directed to reconsider the claim of leave encashment of the deceased employee so that the period of afore-mentioned 46 days or part thereof, is utilised towards encashment of maximum permissible days of earned leave.
The needful shall be done within a period of four months of the receipt of a certified copy of this order.
Disposed of accordingly.
Petition allowed.
