RL Anand, J - Earlier this writ petition was filed by Shri Devi Dayal Sharma, Laboratory Assistant, working in Sainik School, Kunjpura (Karnal), in the year 1998, but during the writ petition Shri Devi Dayal Sharma died and now this writ is being prosecuted by his L.Rs and they are making a prayer that the salary of the deceased may be re-fixed in the scale of Rs. 1200-2040 w.e.f.
111986.
2.
The case set up by the deceased was that he was appointed in respondent No. 2 school in the year 1964 as Laboratory Assistant in the grade of Rs. 100-2-125 and this grade was revised in the year 1978 and a new grade of Rs. 290-500 was given.
This grade was further revised to 330-350 with a corresponding selection grade of Rs. 530-610.
This was further revised to Rs. 1200-2040 w.e.f.
111986.
The grade of Rs. 290-500 was withdrawn by the respondent-authorities and a challenge was given to the action of the respondents in Civil Suit No. 340 of 1990.
The suit was decreed by the Senior Sub Judge, Karnal.
The School filed an appeal before the Additional District Judge, Karnal which was dismissed vide judgment and decree dated 1651996 and the judgment of the trial Court was affirmed.
The respondents also filed regular second appeal before the Hon'ble High Court which was also dismissed on 171997.
In short, the case set up by the petitioner was that he was entitled to the revised grade of Rs. 1200-2040 w.e.f.
111986, as the grade used to be given to the Laboratory Assistants in Kendriya Vidyalayas and the respondent authorities had adopted this grade for Laboratory Assistants working in Sainik Schools.
3.
Notice of the writ petition was given to the respondents.
The respondents have denied the allegations of the petitioner and the short stand taken by the respondents is that since the appointment of the petitioner as Laboratory Assistant in the year 1964 was not against a regular grade, therefore, the petitioner could not lay claim on the grade of Rs. 290-500 w.e.f.
1978, revised subsequently, nor he could have any legitimate claim for the grade of Rs. 1200-2040 w.e.f.
111986.
4.
I have heard Mr. KC Bhatia, Advocate on behalf of the petitioner, Mr. BS Bedi, Advocate on behalf of respondents No. 1 and 2 with their assistance have gone through the record of this case.
5.
Two litigations earlier started - one before the Hon'ble Supreme Court and another before the Civil Court.
All India Sainik School Employees Association filed a writ under Article 32 of the Constitution of India before the Hon'ble Supreme Court wherein a specific stand was taken that pay scales of Laboratory Assistants in Sainik Schools have been revised from Rs. 330-530 to the corresponding scale of Rs. 1200-2040 w.e.f.
111986 which are same in Kendriya Vidyalayas.
No change in the qualifications for the post of Laboratory Assistant has been made while revising their pay scales.
In view of this stand it has been proved that the Laboratory Assistants working in Sainik Schools were put at par for the purpose of pay as applicable to the Laboratory Assistants working in Kendriya Vidyalayas.
As stated earlier the grade of Rs. 290-500 was given to the deceased w.e.f.
111978.
For one reason or the other the respondent authorities withdrew this grade.
This action was not acceptable, as a result of which Shri Devi Dayal Sharma and one Shri Om Parkash Manglu filed a civil suit before the Civil Court challenging the action of the respondents for withdrawing of the scale of Rs. 290-500 and subsequent grades which were given to the post of Laboratory Assistant.
The defence of the defendants in the said suit was the same as now agitated by them.
It was pleaded by the respondents in the earlier suit that since the plaintiffs were not regularly appointed against the prescribed scales of pay, therefore, they were not entitled to the revised scale as per Clause 7 of the letter dated 26.5.1979.
This defence was rejected by the competent court of jurisdiction.
The defendants (now defendants) filed an appeal before the Court of Addl.
District Judge, Karnal and this very argument was raised which was again rejected vide para 10 of the judgment of the first Appellate Court and it will be appropriate on my part to reproduce the contents of said para as under:-  "10.
Devi Dayal plaintiff and Om Parkash plaintiff made their statements on oath before the Court.
Both have reiterated the stand taken by them in the plaint.
vide order dated 28.2.1990, the pay grades of the plaintiffs were sought to be revised in a manner so as to deny the benefits which have accrued to them on revision of pay.
It has not been denied by any side that the plaintiffs came into employment initially in the year 1962 when no such minimum qualification was required to be possessed.
In fact, there were no Rules and Regulations covering the service conditions of the Peons or the Laboratory Assistants or the Laboratory Attendants.
Of course, future recruitment of the posts could be amongst those candidates who had possessed the minimum qualification.
DW1 Durga Charan Sharma, Office Superintendent, Sainik School, Kunjpura stated that the bye-laws were framed for the first time in June, 1984 and prior to that the service conditions were governed by such rules which were embodied in a red book which came into existence in the year 1971.
The plaintiffs came into the employment of the defendants in the year 1962.
The pay scales were revised in the year 1979 and no condition was attached that the benefits of the revised pay scales would not be given to those candidates who had not passed their matriculation or acquired requisite qualification, the plaintiffs were appointed as Laboratory Assistants or Laboratory Attendants at various times on the basis of their experience, there was then no question of any minimum qualification for such posts.
A perusal of letter dated 22121989 Ex. D1 and of letter dated 26.5.1979 Ex. D2 go to show that all regularly appointed employees were entitled to opt for the revised pay scales irrespective of their qualification.
Simply alleging that the appointments of the plaintiffs were irregular is no ground at this stage of the case and they cannot be deprived of the benefit which has accrued to them on account of the revision of pay scales.
The appointments related back to the year 1962 and from time to time, the plaintiffs were appointed by the defendants Laboratory Assistants or Laboratory Attendants on the basis of their experience and as such it is now too late to say that the appointments of the plaintiffs were irregular.
The defendants cannot be allowed to take this plea on the principle of estoppel.
The service conditions of the plaintiffs cannot be changed to their detriment by alleging that the appointments of the plaintiffs were irregular because the plaintiffs were initially recruited by a person who was not competent to give appointments.
Taking all these facts into consideration, it cannot be said that the order dated 28.2.1990 which is based on the order dated 22121989 has been erroneously declared illegal by the Ld.
trial Court.
After all, the plaintiffs were to challenge the orders which were issued to them by the authorities concerned.
The relevant orders are dated 28.2.1990 and when they have been found to be wrong and illegal, the observations of the letter dated 22121989 (Ex. D1) regarding the refixation of pay automatically become irrelevant in the instant case.
Hence, the contention of the Ld.
counsel of the appellants that as the order dated 22121989 was not challenged, no relief should be given is without any merit.
I, therefore, find no justification to set aside the findings of the Ld.
trial Court as prayed for by the Ld.
counsel for the appellants."
6.
The respondents filed Regular Second Appeal No. 2570 of 1996 before the High Court which was again dismissed vide order dated 171997.
The order of the High Court can also be reproduced as follows:-  "I have heard learned counsel for the parties at the motion stage.
Letter dated December 29, 1984 addressed to Principal, Sainik School, Kunjpura, by the Board of Governors, New Delhi, was never intimated or communicated to the plaintiffs.
On the strength of this letter, certain benefits, which had been granted to the plaintiffs in the years 1962, 1963 and 1978, were withdrawn by the Sainik School vide order dated 28.2.1990, and before passing this order, the plaintiffs were neither associated nor were afforded an opportunity of being heard, what to talk of serving a show cause notice of the proposed action sought to be taken against them.
The order passed by the Sainik School, undisputedly affects the civil rights of the plaintiffs and an order passed without hearing them was apparently violative of the principles of natural justice.
The plaintiffs were at least entitled to an opportunity of being heard before the order affecting their service was passed.
This is what precisely the courts below have held.
In that view of the matter I see no ground to interfere.
Dismissed."
7.
The reading of the discussion of the first Appellate Court and the High Court would give rise to the two conclusions - firstly, that the civil Court did not accept the stand of the respondents that Shri Devi Dayal Sharma was not appointed against a regular scale or that the scale of Rs. 100-125 was not the prescribed scale and secondly, the action of the respondent authorities in withdrawing the scale was also not approved on the ground of principles of natural justice.
8.
The learned counsel appearing on behalf of the respondents No. 1 and 2 invited my attention to the letter dated 26.5.1979 (Annexure P-21) clause 7, which runs as follows:-  "7.
All regularly appointed employees i.e. those who have been regularly appointed against a prescribed scale of pay, are eligible to opt for the revised pay scales for their respective posts, irrespective of their qualifications.
Further recruitment to the posts, however, must be from amongst those candidates who possesses the minimum qualifications prescribed."
The respondents cannot take the benefit of this clause at this juncture once this very plea has been rejected by the Civil Court.
Moreover, though the grade of Rs. 100-125 was not a prescribed grade but nevertheless it cannot be said that it was not a regular grade.
Further, the deceased was given a regular grade of Rs. 290-500.
It was a prescribed grade for the post of Laboratory Assistant.
In this view of the matter, I amd of the opinion that there was no justification on the part of the respondent authorities to deprive the petitioner of the grade of Rs. 1200-2040 w.e.f.
111986 when the Laboratory Assistants working in Sainik Schools were put at par in the matter of pay and allowances with the scale made applicable to Kendriya Vidyalayas.
9.
Resultantly, this writ petition is hereby allowed and a declaration is given in favour of the L.Rs.
of deceased petitioner that the deceased was entitled to the revised grade of Rs. 1200-2040 w.e.f.
111986 along with corresponding selection grade and consequential grades which were made applicable from time to time to the post of Laboratory Assistant.
The arrears of 38 months before the date of the institution of the writ petition be calculated and released with further benefits within three months from today, failing which the respondents shall pay interest @12% per annum.
The pension, gratuity, revised pension etc. shall also be calculated in the light of the orders passed above.
There shall be no order as to costs.
Copy Dasti.
Petition allowed.
