Jawahar Lal Gupta, J. (Oral) - The two petitioners herein impugn the selection of respondent Nos. 3 to 12 as Restorers/Library Attendants at the Panjab University.
A few facts may be noticed.
2.
The Panjab University advertised 10 posts of Library Attendants/Restorers in different teaching departments.
Class `C' employees working in the University Library/Office/Departments holding the prescribed qualification and experience were eligible to compete.
The process of selection included a written test and a practical test.
The petitioners claim that in spite of the fact that they had a better merit than some of the selected candidates who did not even fulfil the requisite qualifications, respondent Nos. 3 to 12 have been selected, while the petitioners have been rejected.
3.

It has been further pointed out that a Committee consisting of 14 senior officers of the University was constituted by the Syndicate for conducting the interviews and selecting the candidates.
Out of the fourteen persons, 11 persons including Prof. SK Trehan and Prof. SP Narula, attended the proceedings of the selection committee.
The committee selected respondent Nos. 3 to 12 and further found that three posts, one each in Panjab University Library department of Physics and department of Sociology could not be filled up because no suitable candidates could be found.
It has also been pointed out that the guidelines for the selection of the candidates were issued by the Syndicate in its meeting held on June 30, 1989.
The entire procedure, as prescribed by the Syndicate, was followed by the Committee, as has been mentioned in the written statement.
With regard to the allegation that respondent No. 3 did not fulfilled CWP No. 15073 of 1989 which was disposed of by a Division Bench on March 8, 1990.
In this petition, an undertaking had been given that Mr. Amar Chand would be interviewed for the post of Library Attendant/Restorer.
Similarly, with regard to respondent No. 5, it has been mentioned that he is a Graduate and is working as a Library Restorer since May 30, 1988.
5.
I have heard Mr. CM Chopra, learned counsel for the petitioners.
He submits that in pursuance to the illegal selection, the University has already appointed respondent Nos. 3 to 12 the post of Library Attendants/Restorers.
He submits that the selection is vitiated as respondent Nos. 3, 5 and 9 do not possess the requisite qualification of experience.
6.
A perusal of the chart given above shows that respondent Nos. 3, 5 and 9 possess the qualification of intermediate, BA and BAI respectively.
They obviously possess qualification higher than the minimum prescribed in the advertisement, viz Matric or under-matric.
Furthermore, they have secured much higher position than the two petitioners, both of whom are not even matriculate.
In fact, petitioner No. 1 has failed in his matriculation examination while petitioner No. 2 has passed only the 6th Standard examination.
Still further, as has been explained by the respondents in the written statement with regard to respondent No. 3 an undertaking had been given to this Court in a writ petition filed by him and he was considered for selection in pursuance to that undertaking.
The position with regard to respondent No. 5, who admittedly is a Graduate and respondent No. 9 who has passed the BAI examination being similar to that in case of respondent No. 3, though they had not filed any writ petition, the University called them also for interview.
Keeping in view the fact that the qualifications of these petitioners are much higher than those of the two petitioners as also the fact that they had been considered suitable by a properly constituted selection committee, I am not inclined to interfere with their selection and appointment.
7.
Even otherwise, it has been categorically averred in the written statement that three post were ordered to be readvertised as none of the remaining candidates was considered suitable.
In such a situation, no useful purpose would be served by setting aside the selection of respondent Nos. 3, 5 and 9 especially when their higher academic qualifications provided a sufficient ground for granting the relaxation of a short period in the matter of experience.
Even otherwise, the selection is only for the posts of Library Attendants/Restorers.
8.
Learned counsel for the petitioners then contends that the marks ear- marked for interview are excessive, i.e. 20 out of 100 marks have been allotted for interview alone.
He further points out that each of the petitioners have been given zero marks for interview while the respondents have been given 10 marks each.
Even if 10 marks are allotted to each of the petitioners, they would still remain below the respondents in order of merit.
Consequently, the marks allotted for interview have not materially affected the result so far as the petitioners are concerned.
Accordingly I find no merit in this petition.
It is dismissed.
However, in the circumstances of the case, the parties are left to bear their own Costs.
