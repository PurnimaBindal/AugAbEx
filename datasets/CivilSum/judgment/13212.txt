Mehtab S Gill, J - The petitioner has prayed that a writ in the nature of certiorari be issued quashing orders dated August 3, 2000 (Annexures P-8 and P-13) and circular letter dated December 31, 1997 (Annexure P-14).
He has further prayed to issue a writ in the nature of mandamus directing the respondents to reimburse his medical bills (Annexures P-4 to P-7 and P-10 to P-12) amounting to Rs. 75,546/- along with interest at the rate of 18% per annum from the dates of submission of these bills.
2.
The petitioner has alleged in his petition that he is suffering from chronic sugar and blood sugar diseases since the year 1978 and had been getting treatment from various Civil Hospitals.
On January 14, 1994 he fell ill and was removed to Civil Hospital, Ferozepur, where he was treated as an out-door patient, but in spite of various tests conducted, doctors attending on him could not diagnose the disease.
He was referred to Guru Gobind Singh Medical College and Hospital, Faridkot, where the disease was diagnosed as an acute renal failure and he was put in dialysis.
As this facility was not available in Faridkot Hospital, he was referred to Dayanand Medical College and Hospital, Ludhiana on January 25, 1994.
The doctors of Dayanand Medical College and Hospital, Ludhiana diagnosed him and declared that his both kidneys were damaged.
He was further advised to go to Madras for kidney transplantation.
He went to Willingdon Hospital at Madras on February 22, 1994 and had a kidney transplantation done.
He was discharged from the said Hospital on February 26, 1994, but he had to remain at Madras for some time for post-operation treatment/observation/check up as advised by the doctors.
Vide letter dated June 23, 1994 (Annexure P-1), he was declared fit to go home and resume his normal work subject to continue taking the prescribed medicines.
A letter dated June 23, 1994 (Annexure P-2) was sent by the Willingdon Hospital, Madras to Dr. JS Sandhu, Consultant Nephorologist of Dayanand Medical College, Ludhiana, informing him to take care of post- transplant, which was being done on the petitioner as an out-door patient.
The petitioner joined his duties as District Revenue Officer, Ferozepur on July 1, 1994 and the follow-up treatment was being done by Dayanand Medical College and Hospital, Ludhiana.
Vide letter dated August 21, 1997, the District Medical Board, Muktsar scrutinised his case on August 21, 1997 and he was issued a chronic disease certificate being a case of kidney/renal failure.
3.
The petitioner has further averred that he submitted his bills of the abovesaid operation for reimbursement, but the same were not cleared for the reason that before he went to Madras, he had not obtained the requisite recommendations and certificate from the Chief Medical Officer two months prior to getting the said treatment.
He, therefore, filed Civil Writ Petition No. 17770 of 1995, which was allowed by this Court on January 31, 1996.
His medical bills were reimbursed.
His medical bills for the medicines purchased for follow-up treatment were also being continuously reimbursed.
It has further been averred that he is getting the same type of treatment as an out- door patient as he had been getting as an in-door patient.
It has been further averred that the bills duly certified by the competent Medical Authorities for the period from March 4, 1996 to March 6, 1996 for Rs. 4,191/-; for the period from January 1, 1998 to June, 1998 for Rs. 8,114/- and for the period from June 1, 1998 to January 31, 1998 for Rs. 14,280/- and for the period from March, 1999 to August, 1999 for Rs. 17,066/- (Annexures P-4 to P-7) have been rejected by the respondents on the ground that these bills are of the treatment taken as an out-door patient.
A copy of the order dated August 3, 2000 vide which the said bills were rejected is attached with the petition as Annexure P-8.
It has been further averred that the wife of the petitioner, Smt. Balbir Kaur, has also suffered from Cancer in her uterus and she was operated upon on November 16, 1998 from the Apollo Hospital, New Delhi.
The bills were reimbursed.
Vide order dated September 28/29, 1999, she was advised for regular follow-up treatment from Mohan Dai Oswal Cancer Treatment and Research Foundation, Ludhiana.
The bills of the wife of the petitioner, i.e., Balbir Kaur were also rejected vide order dated August 3, 2000 (Annexure P-13).
These bills are for the period from September, 1998 to December, 1998 for Rs. 13,287/-; for the period from September, 1999 to March, 2000 for Rs. 13,284/- and for the period from December, 1999 to April, 2000 for Rs. 5,324/- (Annexures P-10 to P-12).
4.
Notice of motion was issued.
5.
Respondents filed written statement.
6.
A preliminary objection taken by the respondents was that the medical treatment of the petitioner and his wife Balbir Kaur was that of as an out- door patient.
As per instructions contained in circular letter No. 5/122/97- IFP/11179 dated December 31, 1997 (Annexure P-14), the Punjab Government has granted fixed medical allowance of Rs. 250/- per month to all eligible employees/pensioners on uniform basis with effect from January 1, 1998 without any option.
It has been mentioned in the letter that there shall be no reimbursement for outdoor treatment at all with effect from and after January 1, 1998.
Thus, the claims of the petitioner and his wife were denied for the reimbursement of the medical bills.
Complicated chronic diseases which have been identified are listed in letter No. 12/69/98-5HB/V/21329 dated September 1, 2000 (Annexure R-1), attached with the reply of the respondents.
The perusal of this letter dated September 1, 2000 shows that the Punjab Government has permitted medical re-imbursement to its employees/pensioners in respect of expenses incurred by them as an out-door patient if the disease from which such employees/pensioners are suffering is covered in the list `A' of Annexure R-1.
7.
I have heard the learned Counsel for the petitioner and the respondents.
8.
Learned counsel for the respondents has drawn my attention to the aforesaid letter issued by the Punjab Government dated December 31, 1997 vide which medical reimbursement for outdoor treatment shall not be given and thus, his contention is that the bills of the petitioner and his wife were rightly rejected as those pertained to the period subsequent to January 1, 1998.
9.
Learned counsel for the petitioner has stated that after post-transplant operation, purchasing of the medicines whether being an indoor patient or out-door patient is a continuous process to heal the disease/cure which the petitioner and his wife were facing.
He drew my attention to Annexure `A' of Annexure R-1 wherein the chronic diseases have been mentioned.
The same are reproduced as under :-  "List of complicated chronic diseases in which O.P.D.
Treatment and Pre- Indoor and Post-Indoor Treatment will be re-imburseable.
1.
Chronic Renal Failure.
2.
Cancer/Malignancies of different organs.
3.
Heart failure and Cardiomyopathy (Myocardial Infarction) and Anginapectoris.
4.
Thalisemias.
5.
Degenerative Disorders of Nervous Systems and other organs.
6.
Organs Transplants.
7.
Congential Disorders.
8.
Cirrosis.
9.
Coelics Disease.
10.
Immunological disorders leading to disability.
11.
Chronic Respiratory Failure.
12.
Chronic Pelvic Infection.
13.
Spinal Injuries.
It is clear from Annexure `A' of Annexure R-1 that failure of kidneys and cancer/Malignancies of different organs are the chronic diseases.
10.
The learned Single Judge of this Court in Civil Writ Petition No. 4525 of 2000, tilted Kuldip Singh Virk v. State of Punjab and others, 2001(4) SCT 469 (P&H) has held that the chronic patient is entitled to reimburse the medical bills even if he is an out-door patient.
However, my attention was drawn to a decision of a Division Bench judgment of this court rendered in Ravi Kant v. State of Haryana, 1998(4) SCT 206, wherein it was held that "even if an employee is entitled to only fixed medical allowance per month for out-door treatment, it is for the employer to keep him in good health, in a fit state of body and mind so that he is able to discharge his duties....." If the employee cannot live without the daily medicines prescribed to him, such treatment has to be viewed as in- door treatment.
The follow up treatment after indoor treatment/operation has to be tagged on to the indoor treatment."
11.
The facts of the present case are squarely covered by these two judgments of this court, as cited above and this writ petition is, thus, allowed.
I have no hesitation in quashing the orders dated August 3, 2000 (Annexures P-8 and P-13) vide which the respondents have rejected the claims of the petitioner and his wife on account of expenses incurred by them for their follow-up treatment.
Respondents are directed to reimburse the medical bills of the petitioner (Annexures P-4 to P-7 and P-10 to P-12) amounting to Rs. 75,546/- as per instructions dated 192000 Annexure R-1.
Respondents are further directed to pay interest at the rate of 6% per annum from the date of submission of these bills till the final payment.
Petition allowed.
