Jawahar Lal Gupta, J. (Oral) - The petitioner entered service as a Temporary Engineer in the Punjab Public Works Department Irrigation Branch in the year 1957.
In January 1971, he was .promoted to the post of Executive Engineer which he joined on February 3, 1971.
As an Executive Engineer he was working in the pay scale of Rs. 750-50-1000-50-1300.
He was due to cross the efficiency bar w.e.f.
February 1, 1977.
His case was kept pending till June 26, 1982 when the Government passed five orders by which he was stopped at the efficiency bar w.e.f.
February 1, 1977 onwards.
As a result, he was not granted the increments in the revised scale of pay of Rs. 1200-1860.
The petitioner's representation before the authorities having borne no fruit and he having proceeded on voluntary retirement w.e.f.
February 3, 1983 the petitioner has come up in this petition.
The petitioner claims that the orders stopping him at the efficiency bar having been passed without the grant of any opportunity whatsoever deserve to be quashed.
He further claims the refixation of pay in the revised scale as also the grant of the scale of Rs. 1400-2100 w.e.f.
February 1, 1981 and the selection grade of Rs. 2000-2300.
2.
On behalf of the respondents it is claimed that the petitioner has been rightly held up at the efficiency bar w.e.f.
February 1, 1977 to February 1, 1981 as his record was not found to be upto the mark.
It is further claimed that his pay has been rightly fixed at Rs. 1560/- in the pay scale of Rs. 1200-50-1500-60-1860 vide order dated March 17, 1981.
On this premises, the petitioner's claim is sought to be contested.
It has not been denied that no opportunity was given to the petitioner before passing of the impugned orders dated June 26, 1982.
3.
I have heard Mr. BN Sharma, for the petitioner and Mr. Jaswant Singh for the respondents.
4.
It is no doubt correct that the stoppage of a person at the stage of crossing the efficiency bar is not a punishment.
In fact, a specific provision in this behalf was made by introduction of an explanation in the Punjab Civil Service (Punishment and Appeal) Rules 1952 by notification dated October 31, 1974.
It is also correct that JV Gupta, J In Haryana Khadi and Village Industries Board v. Krishan Gopal Taneja, 1985(2) SLR 121 took the view that opportunity of hearing was not required when a person is stopped at the efficiency bar.
However, I find that, in a subsequent decision in OP Gupta v. Union of India and ors., 1987(5) S.L.R.
288 it was observed that while passing a prejudicial order in terms of FR 25 to deprive the Government servant of his increments above the stage of crossing the efficiency bar retrospectively after his retirement, the Government has the duty to hear the concerned government servant before the order is made against him.
Following this view in RN Arora v. State of Haryana & Ors., 1990(1) S.L.R.
703 and in Hari Singh v. State of Haryana & Ors., 1991(3) SLR 670, the orders stopping the civil servant at the stage of crossing the efficiency bar have been quashed.
5.
In the present case, the Government decided the matter regarding the crossing of efficiency bar only, vide its order dated June 26, 1982 and refused to grant the increments which had fallen due from February 1, 1977 onwards.
It may be that the Government is entitled to stop a person at the efficiency bar if he is not considered suitable therefor.
However, no explanation has been given as to why the petitioner's case for crossing the efficiency bar was not considered at the appropriate time every year.
Normally, the case should be examined six months prior to the date on which the efficiency bar falls due, so that the decision is available to the employee at the appropriate time.
If he is found unsuitable for crossing the efficiency bar in a particular year, he can improve himself during the ensuing years.
In the present case, year after year the Government had failed to do the needful.
In the year 1982, five orders were passed with one stroke of pen.
To say the least, the action was most unfair and arbitrary.
Superior authority, which was trying to assess the suitability and efficiency of a subordinate officer, was itself lacking in efficient performance of its duties.
6.
Accordingly, the impugned orders are quashed.
The respondents shall decide the matter fresh in accordance with law and after giving him opportunity.
In the circumstances of the case, the petitioner shall be entitled to his costs which are assessed at Rs. 2,000/-.
