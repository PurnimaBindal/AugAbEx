The case of the petitioners is that they have been put to a financial loss per month from the period 1.4.1979 to 30.4.1991 of various amounts as have been detailed in para 7 of the petition.

Petitioners have challenged the letter/order dated 171089) Annexure P-4) directing the application of instructions dated 3101985 (Annexure P-2) in regard to house rent to police personnel and memo.
dated 23,491 (Annexure P6), directing payment of house rent to police personnel at pre-revised scales with effect from 141991.
The petitioners have also challenged the letter vide which recovery of the excess house rent allowance paid to the petitioners has been ordered.
The main case as projected through pleadings as also canvassed by Noubet Singh Panwar, learned counsel of the petitioners is that the action is unsustainable in law inasmuch as instructions Annexure P-1 regarding payment house rent to police personnel, have not been yet revoked and that the impugned action offends principles of natural justice because no opportunity of showing cause was given to the petitioners before deciding to apply instructions dated 31085.
This petition has been contested by the respondents I to 5 and written statement has been filed by Shri BS Sandhu, IPS, Superintendent of Police, Kaithal and by way of Preliminary Objections, it is pleaded that the orders dated 31085 and dated 171089 have been challenged in 1991 and therefore, there is inordinate delay in filing the present petition.
It is also pleaded that the petitioners have no cause of action since they drew more House Rent Allowance under the provisions of letter dated 271179 to which they were not entitled.
The case of the respondents is that the revisions of pay scale of Haryana Police Personnel were sanctioned, with effect from 1.4.1979 and as per para 4 of the said letter, Compensatory Allowance 10% of the basic pay in addition to House.
(sic) Therefore, the employees draw the Compensatory Allowance @ 10% and House Rent Allowance @ 10% on the revised pay scales as per Annexure P-1.
It is further pleaded that vide letter dated 3101985, it was decided by the Government that such Government employees, who were entitled to rent free accommodation but have not been provided with Govt. accommodation may be granted House Rent Allowance @ 5%, 7-1/2% and 10% of their pay (subject to the pay range, in the revised pay scale) as laid down in para 1 of Finance Department letter dated 671981.
The House Rent Allowance on slab basis was admissible to them according to the pay range and the place of posting as sanctioned vide Haryana Government letter dated 1471980 or actual rent whichever is less.
These orders were to take effect from 1.1.80 but if any excess payment becomes recoverable from any Government servant on that account for the period from 111980 to the date of issuance of letter that shall be considered to be waived off.
The aforesaid letter was applicable to the police department as well but could not be implemented since correspondence with the Government was made to seek the clarification.
The government issued a letter dated 171089 in which the Government has emphasised that the letter dated 3101985 is also applicable to Police Department and recovery of excess amount paid be also made.
It is the case of he respondents that vide letter aforesaid, implementation of letter dated 271179 was impliedly revoked.
The action of the Government is defended by pleading that the same is legal and justified.
Mr. Naubat Singh appearing for the petitioners has mainly canvassed before this Court that in view of the fact that letter/Instructions Annexure P-1 dated 27111979 have neither been specifically or impliedly revoked, the petitioners are entitled to keep on getting the House Rent Allowance as also House Compensatory Allowance.
6.
Mr. DR Trikha, D.A.G.
Haryana appearing for the State however opposed the point raised by Mr. Naubat Singh and contends that instructions issued in 1985 would result into implied revocations of order dated 27111979.
After hearing the counsel for the parties, I am of the considered view that insofar as the first contention of the petitioners is concerned, there is no substance in the same.
Primarily the house rent allowance is only a concession and not an established right of an individual.
Sanction and extent of house rent, enhancement or reduction thereof depends upon exigencies of the service and various circumstances prevailing at different times.
If there has been a reduction on account of the revision of pay scales which allowance as referred to above was only a concession, no ground can be made on that count.
In sofar as the contention of Mr. Naubat Singh, that there has been no withdrawal or revocation of Order Annexure P-1 is concerned suffice it to say that instructions/letter issued in 1985 would certainly amount to impliedly repealing Annexure P-1.
It was not necessary in the facts and circumstances of the case to specifically say that instructions Annexure P-1 shall no more hold the field.
The instructions of 1979 and 1989 cover the same field and to extent the earlier one conflict with the latter, the earlier one shall automatically be held to be inapplicable.
Mr. Naubat Singh has then contended that in any case there was absolutely no satisfaction (justifcation) so as to recover the arrears inasmuch as the petitioner were getting the house rent allowance under orders issued by the competent authority.
Mr. Trikha however contends that vide Annexure P -6, the Director General of Police, Haryana had informed all heads of Police Offices located in Haryana as to in what manner the police personnel have to get house rant allowance and that being so the petitioners were aware of their actual entitlement and thus cannot successfully contend that the recovery of excess amount should not be made from them.
I, however tend to agree with the learned counsel appearing for the petitioners and do not find any merit in objection raised by Mr. Trikha.
Admittedly, the petitioners were getting 10% of their basic pay as house rent allowance vide orders dated 27111979 which were made effective from 1.4.1979.
It is nobody's case that authority that passed order Annexure P- I was incompetent to do so.
Once the petitioners were getting the house rent allowance under valid orders which orders ofcourse were modified, clarification of which was done in 1991 then in that situation to recover the amount, as such, paid in excess to the petitioners would be wholly unjustified and arbitrary.
It requires to be said that even by letter Annexure P-2, the employees who have been paid in excess, no recovery was to be made from them and the same was waived off.
7.
In view of what has been stated above, the petition meets partial success.
Whereas applicability of revised House Rent Allowance is held valid, the recovery of excess amount from the petitioners is held to be invalid and the orders passed on that-behalf are struck down.
In view of effects that the petition has met with partial success, there shall be no orders as to costs.
