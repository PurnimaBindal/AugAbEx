MM Kumar, J - This petition filed under Article 226 of the Constitution by the Storekeepers (Class-III) working in Industrial Training Institute in Punjab, prays for issuance of a writ in the nature of mandamus directing respondents to implement the decision of the State Government taken on the basis of recommendation made by the Second Pay Commission and to grant the Storekeepers the higher pay scale equivalent to that of Senior Clerks.
Further prayer has been made for grant of consequential benefits in the form of arrears of salary by revising their pay scale to Rs. 400-660 and Rs. 510-800 w.e.f.
111978.
A writ of certiorari has also been sought for quashing order dated 30101986, whereby the pay scale of Rs. 510-800 granted to the respondents has been withdrawn.
2.
Brief facts of the case, necessary for the disposal of this petition are that the petitioners joined the Department of Technical Education and Industrial Training on various dates which have been given in the seniority list (Annexure P-1).
All the petitioners were working as Storekeeper at the time of recruitment.
The post of Storekeeper used to carry the pay scale of Rs. 109-200 which was equivalent to the pay scale of Assistant and the pay scale for the post of Clerk was Rs. 60-175.
This shows that the post of Storekeeper was superior than that of Clerk.
In 1978, the Storekeepers were drawing the pay scale of Rs. 120-250 and the Clerks were placed in the pay scale of Rs. 110-250.
The pay scale of Accountant was that of the Storekeeper i.e. Rs. 120-250.
The Second Pay Commission recommended the revised pay scale of Rs. 400-600 in the case of Storekeeper and Rs. 400-600 for the post of Clerk.
The recommendations made by the Second Pay Commission alleged to have been accepted by the Government and implemented in all the departments except the Department of Technical Education and Industrial Training (respondent No. 2).
In accordance with the decision of the State Government circulated on 25101979, it was clarified that the post of Sub Divisional Clerks, Accounts Clerk and Storekeepers in various departments which were in the un-revised scale of Rs. 110-250 were to be encadred with the post of Clerks.
The specialised posts were to be manned by Senior Clerks.
A copy of the aforementioned decision has been placed on record as Ex.P-2.
On 2221980, a circular letter was issued to all the departments intimating revision of scale of pay of various categories of posts that had been sanctioned by the President of India (Annexure P-3).
The post of Storekeeper has been mentioned at Sr. No. 21 and 171.
In the column for revised pay scale of remarks, it is specifically stated that these posts were to be included in the cadre stength of Clerks.
Accordingly, all the petitioners, except petitioner No. 10 Joginder Singh and petitioner No. 7 Sukhdev Singh were given the revised pay scale of Senior Clerk.
i.e. Rs. 510-800 on 27.11.1982 w.e.f.
111978.
A copy of the order is placed on record as Annexure P-4.
It has further been clarified that 68 posts of Clerks working in the department were given the grade of Senior Clerk w.e.f.
111978 as is evident from the office order dated 2891982 (P- 5).
It has further been asserted by placing reliance on order Ex. P-7 to P-9 dated 1531980, 581985 and 591980 respectively that the pay scale in respect of Storekeepers by designating them as Senior Clerks were implemented and all of them were given scale of Rs. 510-800.
However, the respondent, instead of implanting the order Annexures P-2 and P-3 in respect of the Storekeeper/Senior Clerks of the Department of Technical Education and Industrial Training withdrew the benefit of higher grades which was given to them vide order dated 30101986 (P-10).
Despite representation made, the pay scale has not been restored and the petitioner has approached this Court by raising the aforementioned grievance.
3.
In the reply, the stand taken by respondent is that the office order dated 25101979 (Annexure P2) was applicable only to those who were drawing pay scale of Rs. 110-250 whereas the petitioners were drawing the pay scale of Rs. 120-250.
It has further been asserted that the duties and qualifications of the two posts of Storekeepers and Clerks are entirely different.
A Storekeeper is required to be matriculate with one year experience of handling in engineering sources, posting of stock-books ledgers, good hand writing and knowledge/qualification upto matriculation or its equivalent standard; both in Hindi and Punjabi.
The qualification for the post of Clerks are matric/higher secondary with knowledge of typewriting and knowledge of Hindi and Punjabi upto the matric standard.
It has further been asserted that there has never been a merger of the two posts and cadres continue to be separate identity in respect of cadre and seniority.
They are governed by different conditions of service and pay scale.
4.
Mr. GS Bajwa, learned counsel for the petitioner has argued that once the pay scale of Rs. 510-800 has been granted to those who were drawing the pay scale of Rs. 110-250 i.e. Clerks, there is no reason for the respondent No. 2 to deny the application of decision Annexure P-2 dated 25.7.1979 to the cadre of Storekeepers who were drawing pay scale of Rs. 120-250.
Learned counsel has also placed reliance on various office orders i.e. Annexures P-7 to P-9 dated 1531980, 581985, 591980 respectively to argue that in all other departments, the revised pay scale has been granted to the Storekeepers except to the petitioners who are working in the Technical Education and Industrial Training Department.
In support of his submission, he has made a reference to the judgment of this Court in the case of Gurmeet Ram and others v. State of Punjab and others, C.W.P.
No. 18/4 of 1981, decided on 191981.
In that case, this Court has noticed the fact of grant of higher scale of Rs. 510-800 even to the Storekeepers although the petitioners were Sub Division Clerks in that petition.
5.
Ms. Swati Gupta, learned State counsel has argued that it is an age old principle that once the duties, qualifications and functions of two posts are different, then no parity can be claimed for the purpose of grant of pay scale.
She has placed reliance on the averments made in paras 5, 6 and 7 of the written statement and argued that there has never been a merger of the post of Store-keeper with that of Clerk.
She has claimed that in the absence of such a merger, no parity in the pay scale can be claimed by the petitioner.
It has further been emphasised by referring to P-3 that the qualifications are entirely different and, therefore, the pay scale have to be different.
6.
After hearing the learned counsel for the parties and perusing the record, I am of the considered view that this petition deserves to be allowed because the pay scale of Storekeeper has always been in higher than those of Clerks.
In the pre-revised era, the Clerk used to draw a pay scale of Rs. 60-175 and the Storekeeper was given Rs. 106-200.
Whatever might have happened in the subsequent revision, by no stretch of imagination, it can be assumed that the Storekeeper would start drawing pay lesser than those of Clerks.
There is nothing on the record to show that after revision of pay scale in 1978, there is any drastic change in the nature of duties of the Storekeeper which they used to perform before the revision was effected on 25101979 w.e.f.
111978.
In somewhat similar situation, their Lordships of the Supreme Court had an occasion to consider the aforementioned proposition in the case of Employees of Tannery and Footwear Corporation of India Ltd and another v. Union of India and others, 1991 SCT 339 (SC) : AIR 1991 SC 1367.
In that case also, the pay scales of employees for one cadre were not revised on account of some error made by the Pay Commission although in the pre-revised era, these employees used to draw higher pay scale.
It was not disputed that in 1970, the pay-scales of employees of four categories in two corporations were identical.
Their duties were also the same which have continued to be same.
The parity was disturbed at the time of two revisions in 1977 and 1982.
7.
In the present case also, the Storekeepers were drawing pay scale of Rs. 106-200 in the pre-revised era and the Clerk used to draw Rs. 60-175.
However, the situation now is that the clerks are drawing a pay scale of Rs. 510-800 and the petitioners have been granted Rs. 400-660.
Such an anomaly is writ large and cannot be justified in the absence of any explanation tendered by the respondent department that there is drastic change in the duties of the Storekeeper or their qualifications.
8.
Moreover, a perusal of orders Annexure P-7, P-8 and P-9 dated 1531980, 581985 and 591980 would show that revised pay scale has been implemented in respect of storekeepers in the department of Health and Family Welfare anal.
P.W.D.
(IB).
The government instructions dated 18/25101979 (P-2) has been implemented.
There is no reason to deny the same benefit to the petitioners, who are employees of the Industrial Training Institute.
9.
In Purshotam Lal v Union of India, 1973(1) SLR 633, a Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court did not accept the exclusion of one category of employee for grant of benefits of revised scales of pay.
Rejecting such an argument, their Lordships observed in paras 14 and 15 as under :  "14.
Mr. Dhebar contends that it was for the Government to accept the recommendations of the Pay Commission and while doing so to determine which categories of employees should be taken to have been included in the terms of reference.
We are unable to appreciate this point.
Either the Government has made reference in respect of all Government employees or it has not.
But if it has made a reference in respect of a Government employees and it accepts the recommendations it is bound to implement the recommendations in respect of all Government employees, if it does not implement the report regarding some employees only it commits a breach of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
This is what the Government has done as far as these petitioners are concerned.
15.
The learned counsel next contends that there has been great delay in bringing this petition and we should not exercise our discretion.
There has been some delay but on the facts of this case we are of the opinion that there has not been undue delay, especially as in his letter dated March 23, 1967 the President, Forest Research Institute and Colleges said that the points were examined and if necessary, the Ministry would be consulted."
10.
Therefore, there was no legal justification for withdrawing the revised pay scale of Rs. 510-800 to the petitioners w.e.f.
111978.
11.
For the reasons stated above, this petition succeeds.
The impugned order (Annexure P-10) dated 30101986 is hereby quashed.
Respondents are directed to grant the petitioner pay scale of Rs. 510-800 w.e.f.
111978 by restoring the order Annexure P-4.
Let their pay scales be re-fixed and the arrears be paid to the petitioners within a period of two months from today.
The petitioners shall also be entitled to consequential benefits in the form of enhanced retiral benefits in case any one of them has superannuated.
The petitioners shall be entitled to the cost of this petition which is determined at Rs. 10,000/-.
Petition allowed.
