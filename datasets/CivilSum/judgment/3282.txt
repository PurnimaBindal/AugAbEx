GR Majithia, J - The petitioner, who is working as a Junior Engineer in P.W.D.
(Public Health Branch), Haryana, has sought a mandate to the respondents to grant him selection Grade of Rs. 1640-2900 with effect from January 1, 1986, i.e. the date from which the said scale was granted to the persons junior to him, in this petition under Articles 226/277 of the Constitution of India.
2.
The petitioner, after his selection as Junior Engineer by the Subordinate Services Selection Board, Haryana, was appointed as such by the respondents on August 26, 1973.
He was confirmed as Junior Engineer in the year 1980.
He was allowed revised scale of pay in terms of the Haryana Civil Services (Revised Scale of Pay) Rules, 1980 (for short, the Rules).
Total strength of Junior Engineers as on January 1, 1986 was 512 posts, against which 256 posts, i.e. 50% of the total strength, were earmarked for selection grade i.e. higher grade.
The petitioner was fully eligible for grant of selection grade but was erroneously declined the same.
He filed representation, which was also declined.
3.
In the written statement filed on behalf of the respondents, it is stated that in terms of the Government Instructions contained in letter No. 2.10.85 2 GSI dated May 9, 1985, only those officials are eligible for the benefits of promotional grade who have obtained at least 70% or more reports of `Good' or `better' categories during the last 10 years.
The case of the petitioner for grant of promotional/selection grade w.e.f.
January 1, 1986 was considered with the other officials, i.e.
Junior Engineers.
The petitioner did not earn 70% good reports during the last 10 years, i.e. 1976-77 to 1985-86 and as such the promotional/selection grade of Rs. 1640-2900 was not granted to him.

The writ petition came up for hearing before our learned brother NK Sodhi, J It was urged before him that average reports which had not been conveyed to the petitioner could not be treated as adverse reports and must, therefore, be held as good reports for the purpose of grant of promotional scale/selection grade.
The reports for the year 1977-78, 1978-79, 1979-80 and for the period 4.9.80 to 31.3.81 although categorised as average reports except for the period 1480 to 3980, which was not available, were not conveyed to the petitioner and could not be treated as adverse reports.
In support of this submission, reliance was placed on a Single Bench decision of this Court in Jugal Kishore v. State Transport Controller, Punjab, Chandigarh, 1990(2) R.S.J 116.
The learned Judge has, in turn, relied upon a Division Bench decision of this Court in C.W.P.
No. 15225 of 1989 (Harish Chander Bhatia v. State of Haryana and others), decided on January 23, 1990.
The learned Judge expressed doubt about the correctness of these judgments and desired that the case be referred to a larger Bench for decision.
It is how the matter has been placed before us.
6.
Indisputably, the policy decision regarding the promotion of officers/officials to the higher posts contained in Haryana Govt. letter No. 931-IGS-72/10308 dated April 13, 1972, was revised by circular letter No. 2/10/85-2GSI, dated May 9, 1985.
In the letter dated April 13, 1972, it was, inter alia, provided that the officials who had earned 50% or more good or better than average reports during the last ten years of service should alone be considered eligible for promotion to a higher post.
The letter dated May 9, 1985 reads as under :  "I am directed to invite your attention to the instructions contained in Haryana Government letter No. 931-IGS-72/10308, dated 13.4.72 on the subject noted above, wherein it was provided inter alia that the officials who have earned 50% or more good or better than average reports during the last ten years of service should alone be considered eligible for promotion to a higher post.
2.
The matter has been considered further by the State Government and it has been decided that in future only such officials/officer(s) who have obtained at least 70% or more reports of `Good' or better categories during the last 10 years should be considered eligible for promotion to a higher post.
However, as before, greater weightage shall be given to the reports earned on the higher post from which further promotion is being considered."
The policy decision contained in circular letter No. 2/10/85-2GST, dated May 9, 1985 was revised by the Haryana Government vide letter No. 2/39-89- 2GST, dated July 1, 1991 and the same reads thus :-  "I have been directed to invite your attention to circular letter No. 2/10/85-2GST dated 9.5.85 whereby the eligibility for promotion was prescribed from 50% to 70% good record and to state that the Government has re-examined the issue on the basis of decisions of the High Court in the petitions filed against the implementation of said instructions.
After due consideration, it has been decided that the instructions dated 9.5.85 shall be deemed to have been implemented with immediate effect according to which while screening the 10 years confidential reports for the purpose of examining the case of promotions, 50% of the reports upto the year 1984-85 and thereafter 70% good or better reports are essential.
These instructions may be brought to the notice of all the officials and officers."
The revised policy decision envisaged that while screening the 10 years' confidential reports for the purpose of examining the case of promotion, 50% of the reports upto 1984-85 and thereafter 70% good or better reports of the official are to be taken into consideration.
The policy decision dated July 1, 1991 was not produced with the original writ petition, but it was placed on record with the review application No. 160-A of 1991 filed in CWP No. 2734 of 1991.
7.
The petitioner's case for grant of Selection Grade ought to have been considered in the light of the policy decision contained in circular letter No. 2/10/85-2GST, dated May 9, 1985, as revised by letter No. 2/39-89-2GST, dated July 1, 1991.
The categorical stand of the respondents is that the case of the petitioner for grant of Selection Grade was considered only in the light of the instructions contained in letter No. 2/10/85-2GST, dated May 9, 1985.
The respondents were in error in not reconsidering the petitioner's case for grant of Selection Grade in the light of the revised instructions dated July 1, 1991.
A conjoint reading of the instructions dated May 9, 1985 and July 1, 1991 indicates that for the purpose of examining the case of an official for promotion, annual confidential reports for the last 10 years have to be screened and the concerned official must have obtained 50% reports of good or better categories upto the year 1984-85 and thereafter 70% good or better reports.
A persual of the summary of the annual confidential reports of the petitioner for the last 10 years, i.e. 1976-77 to 1985-86, as given in the written statement indicates that the petitioner has obtained 60% `Good' reports and as such he was entitled to the grant of Selection Grade.
It would have been appreciated if the State has offered to reconsider the case of the petitioner for grant of Selection Grade/Promotional Scale in the light of the revised instructions dated, July 1, 1991.
The petitioner fulfils the conditions for grant of Selection Grade and as such is entitled to the same.
8.
The other question which survives for consideration is : whether the average confidential report not communicated to the concerned officer/official has to be treated as `good' report for the purpose of promotional scale/selection grade.
In Jugal Kishore's case (supra), the learned Judge, relying upon a Single Bench judgment of this court in Smt. Shanti Devi v. The State of Haryana and others, 1989(3) S.L.R.
284, and an unreported judgment of a Division Bench of this Court in Harish Chander Bhatia's case (supra) held that the uncommunicated average reports cannot be considered as adverse reports and are to be treated as `Good' reports.
The uncommunicated average reports cannot be treated as `good' reports.
These could not be ruled out of consideration while determining the eligibility for promotion in the light of the instructions dated May 9, 1985 and July 1, 1991.
There is no basis for the conclusion that the uncommunicated average reports can be treated as 'good' reports for the purpose of promotion or grant of selection grade.
The State Government, while screening the case of an officer/official for grant of promotional scale/selection grade, has to consider the entries in the annual confidential reports/character rolls, both favourable and adverse.
The Review Committee invariably is composed of high and responsible officers and it is not expected that they will take into consideration uncommunicated remarks.
They would be conscious that these were not communicated to the Government servant and that he was not given an opportunity to explain or rebut the same.
The uncomminicated average entry in the confidential report will never be made the basis for exclusion of an officer/official for consideration for a promotional post.
The average reports are not necessarily adverse reports.
These will be considered along with the other reports for determining the eligibility for grant of promotion.
If, on the basis of the remaining reports, the Government servant fulfils the eligible criterion, he will be allowed the promotional benefits.
The judgment rendered by a Division Bench of this Court comprising of GC Mital, J (as he then was) and Amarjeet Chaudhary, J in Harish Chander Bhatia's case (supra) reads as under :-  "We have gone through the policy decision laid down by the State Government in Annexure P-1 under which higher grade is to be given to 50% of the senior-most Junior Engineers.
The petitioner has not been given the higher grade of Rs. 1640-2900 although seven of his juniors have been given the aforesaid grade.
The reason for not giving the higher grade to the petitioner is that out of the last ten reports, three are `average' and two reports have not been written and he has only five `good' reports.
For the reports not available with the department, the petitioner cannot be deemed to be adverse.
It is not disputed that no report was ever served on the petitioner as `adverse report'.
Therefore, even the three average reports cannot be taken as `adverse reports'.
In this view of the matter, the basis for not giving the higher grade to the petitioner is knocked out.
For the reasons recorded above, we allow this writ petition and direct the respondents to release the grade of Rs. 1640-2900 to the petitioner from the date it was given to the persons junior to him.
The arrears be paid to the petitioner within three months from today."
A reading of this judgment suggests that the learned Judges were only considering the instructions contained in circular letter No. 931-IGS-72/10308, dated April 13, 1972, wherein it was, inter alia, provided that the officials who had earned 50% or more good or better than average reports during the last 10 days of service should alone be considered eligible for promotion to a higher post.
The Bench had no occasion to consider the instructions dated May 9, 1985 and July 1, 1991, reproduced supra.
The Bench was right in saying that the average report cannot be treated as adverse report.
On the basis of the remaining entries in the confidential reports, the Bench found that the petitioner in that case was entitled to the higher grade since he fulfilled the conditions mentioned in letter No. 931-IGS-72/10308, dated April 13, 1972.
The Bench didn't observe that the uncommunicated average reports would be treated as good reports or adverse reports.
The learned single Judge in Jugal Kishore's case (supra) inferred from the ratio of the Division Bench decision in Harish Chander Bhatia's case (supra) that the uncommunicated `average' reports were to be treated as good reports.
The conclusion drawn by the learned single Judge is not referable to the ratio of the judgment in Harish Chander Bhatia's case (supra).
There is no basis for arriving at a conclusion that the uncommunicated `average' reports can be treated as `good' reports.
The efficacy of the average in the confidential reports is not wiped off.
This can neither be treated as good report nor as bad report.
Looking from any angle, the view taken by the learned single Judge in Jugal Kishore's case (supra) cannot be sustained and the same is overruled.
9.
Even otherwise, the view taken by the learned single Judge in Jugal Kishore's case (supra) is not a good law in view of the recent judgment of the apex Court reported as Baikuntha Nath Das and another v. Chief District Medical Officer, Baripada and another, A.I.R. 1992 SC 1020 : 1992(2) SCT 92 (SC).
In that case, the apex Court was examining the question whether it is permissible to the Government to order compulsory retirement of a Government servant on the basis of the material which includes uncommunicated adverse remarks.
After examining the ratio of the earlier judgments concerning Government servants compulsorily retired, the Bench observed thus :-  The following principles emerge from the above discussion :  (i) An order of compulsory retirement is not a punishment.
It implies no stigma nor any suggestion of misbehaviour.
(ii) The order has to be passed by the Government on forming the opinion that it is in the public interest to retire a government servant compulsorily.
The order is passed on the subjective satisfaction of the Government.
(iii) Principles of natural justice have no place in the context of an order of compulsory retirement.
This does not mean that judicial scrutiny is excluded altogether.
While the High Court or this Court would not examine the matter as an appellate court, they may interfere if they are satisfied that the order is passed (a) mala fide, or (b) that it is based on no evidence, or (c) that it is arbitrary in the sense that no reasonable person would form the requisite opinion on the given material in short; if it is found to be a perverse order.
(iv) The Government (or the Review Committee as the case may be) shall have to consider the entire record of service before taking a decision in the matter of course attaching more importance to record of and performance during the later years.
The record to be so considered would naturally include the entries in the confidential records/character rolls, both favourable and adverse.
If a government servant is promoted to a higher post notwithstanding the adverse remarks, such remarks lose their sting, more so, if the promotion is based upon merit (selection) and not upon seniority.
(v) An order of compulsory retirement is not liable to be quashed by a Court merely on the showing that while passing it uncommunicated adverse remarks were also taken into consideration.
That circumstance by itself cannot be a basis for interference.
Interference is permissible only on the grounds mentioned in (iii) above.
This object has been discussed in paras 29 to 31 above."
These observations were made while examining the case of a government servant to order compulsory retirement.
These observations would apply with equal force for examining a case of a Government servant for promotion.
For the reasons stated above, the petition succeeds and it is directed that the petitioner should be granted the selection grade from the date of his entitlement.
There will be no order as to costs.
Petition Allowed.
