Swatanter Kumar, J - By this judgment, we propose to dispose of above two writ petitions being CWP Nos. 17152 of 2001 and 9661 of 2001 as they arise from the same award dated 2222000.
2.
Punjab State Electricity Board has filed C.W.P.
No. 17152 of 2001 mainly on the ground that the learned Presiding Officer, Industrial Tribunal, Punjab, Chandigarh has fell in error of law as well as has not appreciated the evidence in its correct perspective.
Resultantly, the finding recorded by the Industrial Tribunal granting the relief of payment of arrears on the basis of the revised pay scale to the workman, is erroneous.
In C.W.P.
No. 9661 of 2001 filed by the workman, a prayer has been made for issuance of directions to the respondents to implement the award against the State Electricity Board.
3.
The facts giving rise to the present petitions can be summarised.
4.
The workman was appointed as a bus conductor on work-charged basis in the category of conductor/cleaner on 25.6.1987.
As there was surplus staff in the Electricity Board, the services of the petitioner were dispensed with, but was re-employed, on regular basis as a bus conductor w.e.f.
12.2.1992.
The workman was receiving less salary than other persons working in the same cadre.
As such, he requested for removal of pay anomaly based on the strength of circular dated 21.2.1980 issued by the Board on 20.1.1999.
The claim of the petitioner was not accepted and as such, on the request of the workman, reference was made to the Industrial Tribunal.
The claim petition was filed by the workman to which the Management filed a detailed reply.
Parties led evidence.
Vide award dated 2222000, the claim of the workman was accepted.
This award has been assailed in the writ petitions afore-noticed by the parties.
5.
The learned Industrial Tribunal while considering the merits of the claim raised by the workman and concluding in favour of the workman, held as under :  "The said anomaly was compounded grouping the post of `cleaner' of a bus with that of bus conductor, even though cleaner was in the scale of 75-90 prior to 111978 and scale of that post was revised to Rs. 300-430 w.e.f.
111978; whereas the post of bus conductor was in the pre-revised scale was Rs. 86-140; and it was revised to 325-495 as would be evident from Annexure "C" of the circular letter of the Board dated 21.2.1980.
In view of the post of bus conductor being in the higher grade and nature and duty of that post being manifestly higher qualitatively it could not be downgraded to the level of the post of cleaner while revising the scales of pay w.e.f.
111986.
Bus conductor was grouped with cleaner as mentioned in Item No. 11-A of Circular letter dated 2341992 (ExtM/2) issued by the Board and in complete disregard of the aforesaid factual position, the pre- existing scale of post of bus conductor was shown to be Rs. 325-495 despite its having been revised to Rs. 400-600.
The post at Sr. Nos. 1 to 17 in the circular letter Ext M/2 were in the pre-revised scale of Rs. 325-492 and all these posts were placed in the uniform scale of Rs. 810-1560; whereas the posts at Sr.Nos.
18 to 32 in the pre-revised scale of Rs. 400-600 were placed in the scale of Rs. 950-1800 with a initial start of Rs. 1000/-.
The workman was entitled to be placed in that category of the posts to have the benefit of Rs. 950-1800 with initial start of Rs. 1000/- in view of his already being placed in the pre-revised scale of Rs. 400-600.
It was obviously on that basis that the respondents admitted in their reply about having recommended to the Board that the representation of the workman for being placed in the scale of Rs. 950-1800 may be accepted.
In para 2 of the reply, the respondents have specifically stated that the management of PSEB had allowed the basic scale of Rs. 400-600 to a conductor w.e.f.
111978 and in para 6 did not deny the averment about the recommendations having been made for the acceptance of the representation of the workman and simply stated that the matter was entrusted to the Pay Revision Committee.
It has also not been specifically stated if that Committee has taken a final decision rejecting the recommendation.
Ex.W/9 is the forwarding letter of the SE UBDC, Malikpur to the Secretary Finance PSEB, Patiala mentioning therein that Satnam Singh Conductor who was drawing pay in the scale of Rs. 810-1440 had represented for being allowed the scale of Rs. 950-1800 and that according to the circular letter of the Board dated 21.2.1980 the pre-revised scale of the conductor was Rs. 110-250 which had been revised to Rs. 400-600 and that on the said basis he is entitled to be placed in the scale of Rs. 950-1800 and, therefore, request was made for allowing him that scale clarifying that he is a matriculate and photocopy of certification is being forwarded.
It is also not disputed that the workman Satnam Singh is having conductor's licence and he is fully qualified for the post of conductor, like the conductors of the Irrigation Department of the Punjab Government who have been placed in the scale of Rs. 950-1800 with initial start of Rs. 1000/-".
6.
The Court further noticed that Subhash Chander Jaryal, the sole witness appearing on behalf of the Management, corroborated the testimony of the workman that one Shri Karamjit Singh who was employed as a conductor in the office of SE (O&M), Ropar, was drawing the pay scale of Rs. 950-1800.
However, the above recorded findings are challenged by the learned counsel appearing on behalf of the Board mainly on the following grounds :  (i) The Industrial Tribunal could not have applied the concept of "equal pay for equal work" in the facts and circumstances of the case; (ii) The circular dated 28th April, 1989 was never adopted or accepted by the Board which is an autonomous body and, as such, the workman cannot be granted benefit thereof; and  (iii) Different 16 posts were clubbed by the expert body as skilled workers in terms of the Circular and the recommendation of the Committee and the Board was not accepted.
This being so, the Tribunal could not equate the posts on the face of experts' body recommendation.
7.
We find substance in the submissions raised on behalf of the Board in this petition.
The Board had issued two circulars dated 21st February, 1980 and 23rd April, 1992.
While benefits were granted to the employees under the circular of 1980.
The revision of the pay scales was granted and posts were clubbed together by the Board vide its circular dated 23rd April, 1992.
On the recommendation of the Second Pay Anomaly Committee, 16 posts specified in the said circular were granted the revised pay scale of Rs. 830-1560.
It is not disputed before us that the workman has been granted the benefit of these circulars.
The circular dated 28th April, 1989 (Annexure P-3) which was issued by the State of Punjab relating to the pay scale of bus conductors was never adopted by the Board.
In fact, that is not even the case of the workman.
The Board is an autonomous body and is controlled by the resolutions passed by its Board.
The terms and conditions of employment will be controlled by the Board and circulars issued by the Government per se would not be applicable to the Board in regard to terms and conditions of employees of the Board.
The petitioner had joined the service in the year 1992 and was given the benefit of the two circulars issued by the Board.
In this view of the matter, the learned Industrial Tribunal was not justified in granting relief to the workman as per letter of the government.
8.
The learned Tribunal has further erred in deciding whether the posts of conductors should have been grouped by the Committee and the Board with other posts or not, particularly when there was no direct evidence on this aspect of the matter and it was not a matter in issue.
Merely that the conductors of the Irrigation Department of the Government of Punjab had been placed in the pay scale of Rs. 950-1800 with initial start of Rs. 1000/-, would not be a ground for equating the conductors of the Board with those scales.
The case of the conductors was recommended by a Committee but was not accepted by the Board and it clubbed 16 posts under the head of skilled workers in the same category.
Such policy decision would primarily fall within the domain of authorities concerned and Court would not normally interfere in such matters until and unless an element of discrimination or arbitrariness was patent on the face of the record.
According to the respondents, the basic scales were allowed on the recommendation of the second Pay Anomaly Committee which keeping in view of the category of job based on academic qualifications, job evaluation and responsibilities attached to the post founded on its expertise re-designated as skilled workers, cleaners/conductors/helpers and placed them in the same scale.
There is no evidence on record before us to come to a conclusion that the authorities had acted arbitrarily and without any basis before them.
For the reasons afore-stated, we allow this petition, set aside the award dated 22nd February, 2000 passed by the Industrial Tribunal, Punjab, Chandigarh and answer the reference against tthe workman.
Civil Writ Petition No. 9661 of 2001 has become infructuous and no further orders are called for.
The writ petitions are accordingly disposed of with no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
