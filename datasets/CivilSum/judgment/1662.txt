GS Singhvi, J (Oral) - In this petition the petitioner has prayed for quashing an order Annexure P.5 by which respondent No. 3 Gurnam Singh was promoted as Deputy Director, Food and Supplies with a further prayer that the petitioner be declared to be senior to respondent Nos. 2 and 3 in the Cadre of District Food Supplies Controllers and a direction be issued to the respondents to promote the petitioner with effect from the date of promotion of his juniors.
2.
Though the petition involves determination of a very short issue, the case has a chequered history and before dealing with the contentions of the learned counsel appearing for the parties it is necessary to make a reference to some of the facts.
3.
The petitioner joined service as Assistant Food and Supplies Officer on 21121962.
He was promoted as District Food & Supplies Officer on 1741970 and then as District Food & Supplies Controller on 23101974.
Respondent Nos. 2 and 3 are ex-Emergency Commissioned Officers.
They joined service as District Food and Supplies Officers on 671971 and 1.12.1971 respectively by direct recruitment on the recommendations of Punjab Public Service Commission.
A seniority list of the District Food & Supplies Officers was circulated by the Government of Punjab vide Annexure P2 dated 1121972.
In that list respondent Nos. 2 and 3 were shown at Sr. Nos. 15 and 17 respectively, whereas the petitioner was shown at Sr. No. 25.
4.
Against the seniority list dated 1121972, the petitioner and some other persons filed writ petitions before this Court.
CWP No. 3912 of 1972 filed by the petitioner was heard alongwith C.W.P.
No. 786 of 1974.
By a common order this Court allowed the writ petitions on 211978 and quashed the seniority list Annexure P2.
Against the judgment of the learned Single Judge, LPA was filed by the State Government which was registered as LPA No. 127 of 1978.
The same was dismissed as withdrawn on 731978.
Another LPA was filed by SBS Virk and others in respect of C.W.P.
No. 786 of 1974 filed by Madan Gopal Garg.
This LPA No. 41 of 1978 was allowed by a Division Bench on 1121982 (reported as SBS Virk and others v. JS Bagga and another, 1982(2) S.L.R.
The Division Bench set aside the order passed by the learned Single Judge on 211978.
5.
In so far as respondent Nos. 2 and 3 are concerned, they did not file any appeal against the order dated 211978 passed by a learned Single Judge.
It is given out by the petitioner that after the judgment of the learned Single Judge, which was in his favour fresh seniority list Annexure P.3 was issued wherein the petitioner was placed at Sr. No. 22 and respondent Nos. 2 and 3 were placed at Sr. Nos. 26 and 27 respectively.
Yet another tentative seniority list was issued by the Government on 6.1.1981 showing the position of the employees as on 8.5.1980 and in that list also the name of the petitioner has been shown above the private respondents.
Petitioner has pleaded that notwithstanding his higher seniority position, respondent No. 3 has been promoted as Deputy Director vide order dated 29121981 and since a person junior to him has been promoted, his right of equality before law in the matter of employment has been infringed.
6.
Some more facts of which it is necessary to take notice are that respondent No. 2 filed C.W.P.
No. 3002 of 1978 claiming benefit of military service in the matter of seniority.
That writ petition was dismissed in default on 3031981.
Respondent No. 3 filed CWP No. 2993 of 1978.
In that petition Madan Gopal Garg was impleaded as one of the respondents.
Writ petition of respondent No. 3 was allowed by a learned Single Judge vide his judgment dated 3181981.
Against this judgment the State of Punjab filed LPA No. 1043 of 1981.
The same was dismissed by a Division Bench on 2591982.
Madan Gopal Garg also filed LPA No. 974 of 1981 against the order of the learned Single Judge dated 3181981.
That too was dismissed by a Division Bench on 2311982.
Madan Gopal Garg took the matter to the Supreme Court in SLP No. 9499 of 1981.
The Supreme Court did not interfere with the order passed by the High Court but directed that benefit of military service should be given to the writ petitioner in accordance with the judgment of the apex Court in Randhir Singh Dhull v. SS Bhambri, (1981)2 SCC 338.
7.
During the course of hearing Shri Nijjar, Senior Advocate appearing for respondent No. 3 has produced before the Court a photostat copy of the order dated 10/2011986 issued by the Government of Punjab assigning seniority to respondent No. 3 Gurnam Singh by granting him the benefit of military service in accordance with the order dated 3181981 passed by the learned Single Judge read with the order passed by the apex Court on dated 2631982.
8.
The principal argument of Dr. Gupta is that the order passed by the learned Single Judge on 211978 in C.W.P.
No. 3912 of 1972 filed by the petitioner wherein question is no more open to be examined afresh by the Court in this petition.
9.
After having given my thoughtful consideration to the rival contentions in the light of the facts which have been placed on record and the various orders passed by this Court, I am of the considered opinion that the petitioner is not entitled to any relief in this petition and the writ petition deserves to be dismissed.
10.
No doubt the writ petition No. 3912 of 1972 filed by the petitioner was allowed by a learned Single Judge on 211978 and qua respondent Nos. 2 and 3 that judgment became final because they did not file any appeal against the order of the learned Single Judge.
However, it is not possible to ignore the fact that against the decision in C.W.P.
No. 786 of 1974 which was decided alongwith CWP Nos. 3912 of 1972 filed by the petitioner, LPA No. 41 of 1978 was filed by the other private respondents and the same was allowed by a Division Bench on 1121982, Thus, in effect the order passed by the learned Single Judge on 211978 was reversed by the Division Bench.
This had the logical effect of restoring the seniority list Annexure P2 which was upset by the order of the learned Single Judge.
Therefore, it is not possible to accept the contention of Dr. Gupta that the petitioner should continue to enjoy seniority over and above respondent Nos. 2 and 3 notwithstanding the judgment of the Division Bench dated 1121992.
Once the Division Bench has set aside the order of the learned Single Judge the benefit, if any, derived by the petitioner on the strength of the order of the learned Single Judge would be deemed to have been lost to the petitioner.
11.
Even if for a moment it is assumed that the petitioner could claim semblance of some right on the basis of the order passed by the learned Single Judge respondent Nos. 2 and 3 were impleaded as parties has become final qua them because they did not challenge the judgment of the learned Single Judge by filing a LPA and, therefore, these respondents could not have been treated as senior to the petitioner.
Learned counsel argued that the judgment of the learned Single Judge dated 211978 must operate as res judicata qua respondent Nos. 2 and 3 and the mere fact that on a separate petition filed by respondent No. 3 the Court has directed the grant of benefit of military service to him cannot be a ground for placing respondent Nos. 2 and 3 above the petitioner in the cadre of District Food & Supplies Officers or District Food & Supplies Controllers.
Dr. Gupta argued that petitioner Charan Pal Singh was not a party to C.W.P.
No. 2993 of 1978 and, therefore, the order passed by the learned Single Judge allowing that writ petition or the order passed by the Division Bench dismissing the LPA's filed by the State Government or by private respondents as well as the dismissal of SLP by the Supreme Court cannot operate to the prejudice of the petitioner Charan Pal Singh.
Learned Assistant Advocate-General and Shri Nijjar have argued that in the face of the unequivocal direction given by a learned Single Judge on 3181981 in CWP No. 2993 of 1978 which has been upheld upto the Supreme Court, the respondent State of Punjab has no option but to assign seniority to the respondent Nos. 2 and 3 by giving them the benefit of military service and in any case with the reversal of the order passed by the learned Single Judge on 211978 the petitioner's right to claim seniority over and above the private respondents stands obliterated.
Learned counsel argued that the objection regarding constructive res judicata was examined by the learned Single Judge who allowed CWP No. 2993 of 1978 and, therefore, that on 211978, in my opinion, with the acceptance of C.W.P.
No. 2993 of 1978 by this Court and grant of benefit of military service to respondent No. 3 for the purpose of assignment of seniority to him in the cadre of District Food & Supplies Officers, the Government was under a mandatory obligation to place respondent No. 3 above the petitioner.
It is not in dispute that if respondent No. 3 or any similarly situated person is given the benefit of past military service he will have to be ranked as senior to the petitioner.
The order dated 3181981 passed by the learned Single Judge in the writ petition filed by respondent No. 3 has become final with the dismissal of L.R.A.
by the Division Bench and SLP by the Supreme Court.
Therefore, respondent No. 3 and other similarly situated persons have got a legal right to be assigned seniority by computation of their military service.
The mere fact that the petitioner was not a party to the writ petition filed by respondent No. 3 will not in any manner affect the right of respondent No. 3 to be assigned seniority on the strength of the order passed by this Court on 3181981.
In fact the petitioner was not a necessary party to C.W.P.
No. 2993 of 1978.
Petitioner in that writ petition (respondent No. 3 herein) was not having any lis qua the petitioner in the matter of seniority.
He was claiming his right to be assigned seniority on the basis of his past military service in terms of the rules framed under the Constitution of India.
It is not the case of the petitioner that he is also one of the persons recruited in the quota of ex-military personnel.
Therefore, the petitioner could hardly have any say in the matter of assignment of seniority to respondent No. 3 on the basis of his past military service.
This is sufficient to dispose of the claim of the petitioner that he is not bound by the direction given by the learned Single Judge on 3181981 in the writ petition filed by respondent No. 3.
12.
A similar objection regarding the impact or the order of the Court by which benefit of military service could be given to a person who had joined service as an ex-military personnel was rejected by this Court in C.W.P.
No. 6003 of 1974 - Pritam Chand v. State of Punjab to which a reference has been made in para 7 of the reply filed by respondent State of Punjab.
In my opinion the Government has not committed any illegality by treating respondent Nos. 2 and 3 senior to the petitioner in the cadre of District Food and Supplies Officers.
13.
Yet another reason for not giving relief to the petitioner is that in effect and in substance the petitioner is seeking to challenge the order passed by a co-ordinate Bench of this Court on 3181981 in exercise of its jurisdiction under Article 226 of the Constitution of India.
Every High Court is a Court of record by virtue of Article 215 of the Constitution of India and an order passed by the High Court in exercise of its jurisdiction under Article 226 cannot be made subject-matter of a petition filed under Article 226 in the same Court.
The only course open to an aggrieved party against an order of Single Judge of the High Court is to file LPA where such one LPA is provided under the rules regulating the procedure or to file a petition before the Supreme Court under Article 136 of the Constitution.
Similarly against an order of the Division Bench the only mode of challenge available to an aggrieved party is to file a SLP under Article 136 of the Constitution of India.
Order passed by the High Court under Article 226 cannot be challenged directly in a writ petition filed under Articles 32 or 226 of the Constitution of India.
This conclusion of mine is fortified by a Seven Judges Bench Judgment of the Supreme Court in Naresh Shridhar Mirajkar v. State of Maharashtra and another, AIR 1967 SC 1.
For the reasons recorded above, the writ petition is held to be without merit and is, therefore dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
