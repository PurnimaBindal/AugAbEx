MR Agnihotri, J - The petitioner was appointed as Constable in the Haryana Police on 6th January, 1989.
He continued to serve as such and performed the duties of various assignments right upto 18th January, 1992, when he was discharged from service in terms of Rule 1221 of the Punjab Police Rules, 1934, vide the following report No. 12, dated 18th January, 1992 :-  "Time 15.32 hours.
Reported that order No. 414-20/AC-1 dated 3192 from the Superintendent of Police, Railways, Ambala Cantt.
was received by hand through Constable Siya Ram No. 846 according to which Constable Ved Parkash No. 861 was found to be inefficient Police Officer and he has been discharged from the services in terms of Rules PPR 1221.
He has gone on patrolling duty in Trains No. 363 Down, 480 Up, 479 Down, 366 UP to Delhi, alongwith Constable Manfoor No. 29.
Upon his return, the concerned constable will be informed about the decision.
Sd/- Ram Chand,  GRP Palwal (SEAL)."
Accordingly, he was relieved from his duties on 18th January, 1992, afternoon, on the authority of the discharge order passed by the Superintendent of Police, Haryana, on 3rd January, 1992.
The petitioner has challenged the aforesaid impugned order on the ground that as he had been appointed on 6th January, 1989, he completed these years regular service on 5th January, 1992, his discharge from service on 18th January, 1992, that is, after three years' service was illegal, being contrary to the provisions of Rule 1221 of the Punjab Police Rules, 1934.
2.
In the written statement, it has been pleaded that since the order of discharge had passed on 3rd January, 1992, it did not matter if the same was received by the petitioner on 18th January, 1992.
The order of discharge was duly despatched to the petitioner through S.H.O./G.R.P.S.
Faridabad, on 3rd January, 1992.
3.
After hearing the learned counsel for the petitioner, we find that the impugned action is wholly contrary to the mandatory provisions of Rule 1221 of the Punjab Police Rules, 1934, framed under Sections 7 and 12 of the Police Act, 1986 Rule 1221 reads as under :-  "12.21 - A constable who is found unlikely to prove an efficient Police Officer may be discharged by the Superintendent at any time within three years of enrollment.
There shall be no appeal against an order of discharge under this rule."
This rule has been the subject-matter of interpretation by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in the case The Superintendent of Police, Ludhiana and another v. Dwarka Das, 1979(1) SLR 299, wherein their Lordships have authoritatively held that the power of discharge can be exercised only within a period of three years and not after the expiry of three years.
According to the said judgment under Rule 1221 after the expiry of the period of three years, for that will attract Article 311 of the Constitution Therefore, the petitioner could be discharged from services under Rule 1221 ibid only with in a period of three years, fiat is, before subsequent thereto, the services of the petitioner were sought to be terminated, then the object could be achieved only by resorting to the procedure of disciplinary proceedings against the petitioner.
According to the plain dictionary meaning the expression "within" when used in regard to the limit of a period of time, it means, "before the end of after not more than and at some time during."
Obviously, right upto 18th January, 1992, the petitioner continued to discharged the duties of his office, as is clear from the impugned report dated 18th January, 1992, itself by which order the respondents wanted to communicate the order of discharge dated 3rd January, 1992 to the petitioner.
The plea raised by the respondents that for bringing about the termination of service of the petitioner, the discharge order should be deemed to have taken effect on 3rd January, 1992, itself, when it was passed and not on 18th January, 1992 when it was actually communicated, it wholly untenable in law in view of the authoritative pronouncement of the Hon'ble Supreme Court in State of Punjab v. Amar Singh Harika, AIR 1966 Supreme Court 1313 and State of Punjab v. Khemi Ram, 1969 SLR 833 : AIR 1970 SC 214 wherein it was held that the order of termination of services of an employee would not become effective unless it was published and communicated to the employee concerned.
Consequently, we allow this petition and quash the impugned order dated 3rd January, 1992, as communicated to the petitioner on 18th January, 1992, by which the petitioner was discharged from service.
The petitioner shall be deemed to have continued in service and shall also be entitled to all the arrears of salary and allowances etc. to which he would have been entitled, had the impugned orders not been passed.
Petition allowed.
