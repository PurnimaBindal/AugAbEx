RL Anand, J - Shri JC Sikka has filed the present writ petition under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India against the respondents and it has been prayed by the petitioner that suitable directions be issued to the respondents for the release of the salary and other retiral benefits.
2.
The case set up by the petitioner is that he joined the Punjab Film & News Corporation as Manager on 961980.
His services are governed by Punjab Civil Services (Punishment and Appeals) Rules, 1970.
He was served with a charge- sheet and inquiry was conducted.
The petitioner was placed under suspension and ultimately his services were terminated on 1721988.
He filed a civil suit challenging the order of termination, and the suit was decreed by the trial Court on 29.5.1993.
The Corporation had filed the first appeal in the Court of Additional District Judge, Chandigarh which was dismissed on 391996.
The Corporation then filed RSA before the Hon'ble High Court bearing No. 883 of 1997 and it was also dismissed on 1451997 and the judgments of the trial Court as well as the first appellate Court were confirmed.
It is alleged by the petitioner that despite judgments in his favour he was not allowed to join the duties nor he was paid the arrears.
Resultantly, he filed the Contempt Petition No. 1017 of 1999 which was disposed of on 5101999.
In pursuance of the order of the Civil Courts, the Corporation paid the arrears of pay including interest up to 30.8.1999 when a cheque was issued in favour of the petitioner.
However, the petitioner was not reinstated into service nor any work was assigned to him.
The petitioner gave the joining report in the Office of the Managing Director-cum-Chairmnan of the corporation on 6/7101999.
On 30111999 the petitioner was served with an office order and he was asked that his case for appointment on Class III post is being considered and for this purpose the certificates regarding educational qualification and experience were required and the petitioner was told to submit the said certificates.
The petitioner supplied the requisite information on 30121999 and stated that his date of birth is 6.3.1943.
He made a mention that he passed the Matriculation Examination and got 449 marks out of 850 marks.
He also gave his affidavit that his date of birth is 661948.
On 1052000, the petitioner was served with another memo whereby he was again told to supply the certified copies of educational qualifications as well as experience.
On 2052000, the petitioner approached his counsel and replied the said memo reiterating the information which the supplied earlier.
3.
The main grievance of the petitioner is that though he has been reinstated into service vide order dated 1101999 and that he also given the joining report on 6101999 but no amount of salary is being released to him.
With this background, the petitioner has come to this Court and has sought the directions of this Court against the respondents.
4.
A joint written statement has been filed on behalf of respondents No. 1 to 4.
According to the respondents, the petitioner, in fact, has remained absent from duty since 15111999 onwards and, therefore the present writ is not maintainable.
It is also the stand of the respondents that all the dues amounting to Rs. 6,33,607/- have been paid to the petitioner, vide cheque No. 979539, on 6101999.
Apart from this, a sum of Rs. 95,358/- represents the employers and employees share towards the provident fund of the petitioner.
As such the petitioner has been paid the consequential benefits and with the payment of the said sum nothing more is payable to the petitioner.
It is also the stand of the respondents the petitioner has not furnished the necessary certificates of educational qualifications nor he has furnished the proof of his age.
At any rate the petitioner has attained the age of superannuation of 58 years on 3062001, therefore, he is not to be considered for appointment in the Government service.
5.
It is also stated by the respondents that petitioner only worked in the corporation for five days ie 28101999, 29101999, 3111999, 4.11.1999 and 5111999.
Thereafter, the petitioner became absent and as such he is not entitled to the relief.
6.
A rejoinder was filed by the petitioner is which he reiterated his allegations made in the petitioner by denying those of the written statement.
7.
It was pleaded by the petitioner in the replication that respondents are blowing hot and cold in the same breath.
The petitioner was made shuttle cock when he was asked to bring this document or that document.
The petitioner thus never remained absent for duty.
He was always ready and willing to perform his duties.
On the other hand, the respondents did not allow him even to sit in the office on the plea that Corporation had already closed its office and that there was no work for the petitioner.
8.
I have heard the learned counsel for the parties and with their assistance have gone through the record of the case.
9.
Less said the better in this writ petition.
On going through the pleadings of this case, I am of the considered opinion that respondents are adopting the delaying tactics right from the day when the suit of the petitioner was decreed by the Civil Court.
Then the Corporation filed the first appeal and then the second, appeal and when both the litigations were lost by the respondents, it started creating miseries for the petitioner as a result of whcih the petitioner had to file contempt petition.
Under the threat of the contempt petition the respondents became little bit wiser when they issued the office order on 1101999 regarding reinstatement of the petitioner and the petitioner joined the service of the Corporation on 6101999.
Thereafter the petitioner was left with his lot again on account of the conduct of the officer/official of the Corporation.
It did not lie in their mouth once they have reinstated the petitioner to withhold his salary with effect from 191999 up to 3062001.
The frontal stand of respondents is the petitioner remained absent and he attended his duty only for five days.
If that is so, which debarred the respondent authority not to take departmental action against the petitioner.
It is suggested at the bar by the learned counsel for the petitioner that up to 3062001 no departmental proceeding was ever initiated or even contemplated by the respondent authorities.
In such a situation I have to say that the defence offered by the respondents is totally untenable.
The present writ petition is in the nature of execution.
When the petitioner has become successful in the Civil Court as well in the High Court, it was mandatory on the part of the respondent authority to implement the orders of the Court.
Resultantly, this writ is allowed against the respondents and directions are given to the respondents to release the salary of the petitioner within one month from today with effect from 191999 to 3062001.
In the event of not making the payment the petitioner shall also be entitled to interest at the rate of 12% on the due amount.
Directions are also given to the respondents to examine the case of the petitioner for the release of the retiral benefits according to service rules as applicable to the petitioner.
There shall be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
