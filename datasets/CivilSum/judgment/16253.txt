MS Liberhan and HS Brar, JJ.
- The only contention raised by the learned counsel for the petitioner is that the services of respondent No. 2 were never terminated.
A persual of the impugned award also shows that the services of respondent No. 2 were never terminated.
On appreciation of the evidence, the Labour Court came to the conclusion that it was the petitioner-Committee who did not permit respondent No. 2 to perform his duties, and consequently granted him the necessary relief.
Once the counsel for the petitioner concedes that the services of respondent No. 2 were never terminated, the question of paying back wages does not arise at all.
These are then the running wages to which respondent No. 2 was entitled to.
Either his services were deemed to have been terminated which indisputably is not the case here because neither any notice was served nor any enquiry was held and no order terminating the services of respondent No. 2 was passed.
In the absence of any order terminating the services of respondent No. 2, he continued to be in service.
2.
The learned counsel has raised another contention that since the services of respondent No. 2 were never terminated, therefore the reference is bad in law.
Without expressing any opinion on the merits or demerits of this contention of the learned counsel, in view of the undisputed facts and the observations made above, we do not find it a fit case where interference is called for in exercise of writ jurisdiction.
The writ petition is dismissed with costs.
Costs Rs. 1,000/-.
Petition dismissed.
