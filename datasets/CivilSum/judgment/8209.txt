SS Sudhalkar, J (Oral) - This writ petition has been filed by the petitioner (hereinafter referred to as the "workman") challenging the award of the Labour Court dated 3151999 vide which his prayer for reinstatement was declined.
According to the petitioner, he had joined service on 5.2.1992 with respondent No. 1 as Beldar and worked upto February, 1995.
In the written statement before the Labour Court, copy of which has been produced atannexure P/3, the statement of the workman regarding the period of his service is denied.
It is contended that he has worked for 59 days only.
It is stated in the written statement that he had worked with effect from 151995 to 28.6.95 i.e. for 59 days.
The Labour Court considered the evidence.
However, it was of the opinion that the concerned muster rolls have been placed on record by the management during the course of examination of WW-1.
The Labour Court also found that in the written statement there was mistake in the mention of the year and the year 1995 is wrongly written instead of 1992.
Regarding the muster rolls the Labour Court held that they are from 1.5.1992 to 3091992 and 30.10.1992 to 31.12.1992 and from 121993 to 30.4.1993 which shows that the workman had worked for 31 days in the month of May, 1992 and 28 days in the month of June, 1992 and thereafter his name does not find mention in the muster rolls.
It was also observed by the Labour Court that the workman did not get summoned any other record to show that he had worked from 551992 to February, 1995 whereas the documentary evidence from May, 1992 to April, 1993 shows that the workman has worked only for 59 days and, therefore, the Labour Court has held that the workman has not worked for 240 days.
2.
The petitioner's case is that he has worked from 551992 upto February, 1995.
The Labour Court has observed that there is a mistake in the mention of year in the written statement filed by respondent No. 1 and it should be 1992 instead of 1995.
Be that as it may, the workman has come out with a specific case of his having worked for a particular period and respondent No. 1 was contesting the case.
The respondent who was having best evidence should have produced the same, for that particular period, which is not done.
The Labour Court has held that the petitioner did not get summoned the record.
However, as per law laid down by the Supreme Court in the case of Gopal Krishanji Ketkar v. Mohamed Haji Latif and others, A.I.R. 1968 S.C. 1413, any party who is in possession of the best evidence should produce the same and if any party does not produce the same, adverse inference can be drawn against that party.
The principle of that case is directly applied on the facts and circumstances of the present case and instead of drawing adverse inference against respondent No. 1, the Labour Court has drawn inference against the petitioner.
We, therefore, follow the principle laid down in the above cited case and after following the principle, we find that respondent No. 1 who was in possession of the best evidence, has not produced the same and, therefore, adverse inference can be drawn against respondent No. 1.
This being the position we accept the say of the petitioner regarding his date of joining and date of termination of his services.
In view of the above, the workman has worked for more than 240 days with respondent No. 1.
3.
Mr. Takkar, learned counsel for respondent No. 1 has stated that copies of muster rolls have been produced in this Court.
However, this will not discharge the burden on the respondent because of the muster rolls were produced before the Labour Court, evidence could have been led by the petitioner in support of his case and the respondent's witness could have been cross-examined and the court could have come to decision whether the said documentary evidence could be relied upon or not.
Therefore, copies of the muster rolls produced in this court do not help respondent No. 1.
4.
So far as back wages are concerned Mrs. Rathore, learned counsel for the petitioner has been fair enough to consider that 25 per cent of the back wages may be deducted.
Accordingly, the petitioner shall be entitled to 75% back wages from the date of demand notice.
5.
In the result, this petition is allowed.
The award of the Labour Court is set aside and the petitioner is ordered to be reinstated with continuity of service.
However, he shall be entitled to 75 per cent back wages from the date of demand notice.
Petition allowed.
