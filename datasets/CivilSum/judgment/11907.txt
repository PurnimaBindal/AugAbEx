MS Liberhan, J. (Oral) - This order will dispose of Civil Writ Petition Nos. 15610, 15260 of 1989, 5654, 5678, 8242, 8923, 9272, 9622 of 1990 and 3574, 17396, 17398 and 18974 of 1991 as common questions of law and facts have been raised in these writ petitions.
2.
The brief facts are that the respondent was appointed as chowkidar with effect from 2441985 and continued working as such till his services were terminated on 15th May, 1988.
The respondent challenged his termination inter alia contending that he had worked for more than 240 days; the provisions of Section 25(f) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 have not been complied with, therefore, his termination is illegal.
3.
Vide the impugned award, the Labour Court, vide its order dated 2891989, on a appreciation of evidence concluded that the respondent had worked for more than 240 days and the provisions of Section 25(f) of the Industrial Court Act, 1947 have not been complied with.
Consequently, this termination was held to be illegal.
Since there was no evidence that the respondent was employed during the period of his termination, the respondent was reinstated with back wages.
4.
Learned counsel for the petitioner thus challenged the finding in respect of working of the respondent for more than 240 days but was unable to satisfy this Court that this finding of fact is erroneous in any manner whatsoever.
There is no error in the appreciation of evidence worth mentioned here.
So far as the legal preposition is concerned that the termination of a person without complying with the provisions of Section 25(f) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 is void, is well settled.
There is no second opinion on this principles of law, precedently or otherwise.
5.
Learned counsel for the petitioner vehemently argued that the respondent was not entitled for the back wages.
The service of the respondent were terminated in the year 1988, as stated above, and the respondent was directed to be reinstated vide award dated 2891989 (Annexure R.1).
There is no date of employment for a short period, the operation of the impugned award with respect to the recovery of back wages was stayed.
No special circumstances have been pointed out in the course of arguments nor were raised before the authorities below to deny the back wages to the workman-respondent.
There is no evidence on the record to show that the respondent was gainfully employed during his forced idleness.
6.
Keeping in view of the law laid down in Hari Palace, Ambala City v. Presiding Officer, Labour Court, 1979 Punjab Law Reporter 720, ordinarily, a workman whose service has been illegally terminated would be entitled to full back wages.
7.
No other point has been urged before us.
8.
In view of the observations made above, there is no force in all the aforesaid writ petitions and the same are dismissed leaving parties to bear their own costs.
Petition dismissed.
