Hemant Gupta, J - This order shall dispose of COCP Nos. 109, 348, 349, 111, 113, 114, 115, 116, 117, 118, 119, 120 and 121, all of 2004, as the same have arisen from a common judgment.
2.
Petitioner is a Class IV employee of the Punjab Government.
Being matriculate he was denied promotion in Class III, although his juniors were promoted.
He filed a writ petition which was allowed by this Court on 612003, wherein it was found that the petitioner was entitled for promotion from Class IV to Class III from the date, the persons junior to him had been promoted.
It was further ordered that it the petitioner was found eligible to be promoted, his promotion shall be released within a period of 3 months from the date of the order and that the petitioner shall also be entitled to all consequential benefits.
3.
Earlier the petitioner filed a Contempt Petition before this Court which came up for hearing on December 1, 2003.
The rule was discharged in view of undertaking of the respondents that the petitioner shall be allowed to join on the promoted post within one month from thereof and that decision regarding consequential benefits shall also be taken within the aforesaid period.
The petitioner has invoked the jurisdiction of the Contempt Court again, pointing out that the consequential benefits have not been paid to the petitioner.
In pursuance of the notice issued, a reply has been filed.
Along with the reply an order dated 712004 had been appended, wherein the arrears of ante-dated notional promotion has been denied to the petitioner by relying upon the judgment of the Hon'ble Supreme Court in State of Haryana v. OP Gupta, 1996(2) SCT 294 (SC) : 1996(2) RSJ 194.
4.
Learned counsel for the petitioner has vehemently argued before this Court that the writ petition was allowed by this Court on 612003 and the petitioner was found entitled to all consequential benefits.
The consequential benefits include the arrears of pay from the date of notional promotion i.e. from the date juniors to the petitioner were promoted.
For the said proposition, learned counsel for the petitioner has relied upon Oriental Insurance Co Ltd v. TS Sastry, 2003(4) SCT 916, Swaran Singh v. State of Punjab and others, 1997(1) SCT 405; Sudershan Kumar v. State of Haryana, 1997(2) SCT 278 and Avtar Singh v. State of Haryana and another, 1998(4) SCT 40 (P&H) : 1998(1) RSJ 317.
5.
The respondents have denied the arrears of salary from the date of notional promotion relying on an earlier judgment of the Supreme Court reported as 1996(2) RSJ 196, OP Gupta's case (supra).
Whether the said judgment shall be applicable in the case of the petitioner or the judgments relied upon by the petitioner aforesaid, is a question which is required to be decided in appropriate proceedings but not in contempt proceedings.
In TS Sastry's case (supra), relied upon by the petitioner, the earlier judgment of the Supreme Court in OP Gupta's case was not brought to the notice of the Court.
Therefore, denial of arrears of salary from the date of ante-dated notional promotion cannot be sufficient to hold that the respondents have wilfully and intentionally disobeyed the directions of this Court dated 612003.
Consequently, the rule is discharged.
The petitioner is at liberty to establish his claim of arrears of salary from the date of ante-dated notional promotion in appropriate proceedings.
Order accordingly.
