JS Khehar, J (Oral) - The petitioner Gurbax Singh was dismissed from the service of the Police Department by an order dated 11.12.1992.
The aforesaid order was impugned by the petitioner by filing CWP No. 16282 of 1992.
In the aforesaid writ petition, an interim order was passed in favour of the petitioner, whereby the operation of the order of dismissal dated 11.12.1992 was stayed.
In the aforesaid circumstances, the petitioner continued to render service with the respondents till 3111993.
The tenure of employment of the petitioner came to an end on 3111993 on account of the fact that he came to attain the age of superannuation on the aforesaid date.
Prior to the aforesaid date of retirement, the petitioner was convicted by the Sessions Judge, Faridkot, by an order dated 31101992, under Section 218 of the Indian Penal Code.
On account of the petitioner's conviction by the aforesaid order dated 31101992, despite the fact that the petitioner had attained the age of retirement, he was not paid any retiral benefits.
2.
The petitioner preferred an appeal before this Court, wherein the impugned the order of his conviction dated 31101992 was set aside.
Learned counsel for the parties are agreed that the order of conviction dated 3110,1992, passed by the Sessions Judge, Faridkot, was set aside by this Court on 12101998 and that the petitioner was acquitted.
3.
Having successfully assailed the order, on the basis of which he had been denied of his retiral benefits, the petitioner then started to demand his retiral benefits.
Since the same were not being released to him he approached this Court by filing CWP No. 5370 of 2000, which was disposed of by an order dated 452001, requiring the respondents to decide the representation filed by the petitioner claiming his retiral benefits.
It would, however, be pertinent to mention that all the retiral benefits due to the petitioner were released to the petitioner in the years 1999 and 2000.
The claim of the petitioner for demand of the retiral benefits, therefore, stood satisfied.
By an order dated 282001, the representation of the petitioner was disposed of.
He was held not entitled to any interest on account of the delayed payment of retiral benefits.
4.
Through the instant writ petition, the solitary claim raised is for interest on delayed payment of retiral benefits.
Insofar as the instant issue is concerned, the stance adopted by the respondents is, that retiral benefits were not released to the petitioner on account of his conviction.
His claim was taken up for consideration for release of retiral benefits as soon as he was acquitted by this Court on 12101998.
It is vehemently contended on behalf of the learned counsel for the respondents that there is no material on the record of this case, nor any factual averment in the pleadings of the instant case, depicting any lapse or negligence or delay at the hands of the respondents.
In view of the above, it is the vehement contention of the learned counsel for the respondents, that the petitioner is not entitled to any interest on account of delayed payment of retiral benefits.
5.
Insofar as the petitioner's claim for interest on account of delayed payment of retiral benefits from 121993 till the amount was eventually paid in 1999-2000, is concerned, the petitioner shall have to satisfy; firstly, whether the denial of retiral benefits after the conviction of the petitioner by the Sessions Judge, Faridkot, on 31101992 was unreasonable and unjustified ?
And secondly, if the answer to the aforesaid query is in the negative, whether the petitioner is entitled to interest from the date of his retirement i.e. to be counted with effect from 121993 till the eventual payment was made to him.
If the answer to the aforesaid query is in the negative, whether the petitioner would be entitled to interest from the date of his acquittal by this Court on 12101998.
6.
Insofar the aforesaid two queries are concerned, the stance adopted by the respondents in the written statement is that the action taken by the respondents in depriving the petitioner or retiral benefits emerges from Rule 2.2.
(a) of the Punjab Civil Services Rules, Volume II.
It reveals that the Government has a right to withhold or withdraw the payment of pension to an individual who is convicted of a serious crime or is guilty of grave misconduct.
In the aforesaid circumstances, the employer has the authority to deprive the petitioner of his retiral benefits even after he had attained the age of superannuation on 3111993.
The fact that the petitioner was convicted on 31101992 and further that the aforesaid order of conviction was operational till 12101998, has not been disputed by the learned counsel for the petitioner.
It is, therefore, apparent that the action of depriving the petitioner of his pensionary benefits after his having attained the age of superannuation on 3111993 could not have been assailed till the petitioner's acquittal on 12101998.
In view of the above, it is natural to conclude that the non-release of retiral benefits till the actual acquittal of the petitioner on 12101998, cannot be stated to be unjustified.
7.
In answer to the queries raised above, it is hereby concluded that it was open to the respondents to deprive the petitioner of his pensionary benefits after his conviction, and further, that the petitioner's entitlement to pensionary as well as other retiral benefits emerged only after his acquittal by this Court on 12101998.
Accordingly, the claim of the petitioner for interest on delayed payment of retiral benefits is to be adjudicated only with reference to the date of his acquittal i.e. 12101998.
Learned counsel for the petitioner acknowledges the fact that most of the retiral benefits were released to the petitioner on 16.3.1999 and the remaining retiral benefits were released to the petitioner during the years 1999 and 2000.
While adjudicating upon the claim of the petitioner demanding interest on delayed retiral benefits, it is imperative to conclude that there has been no inordinate and unexplained delay at the hands of the respondents for the payment of retiral benefits after the petitioner's acquittal on 12101998.
Since it is apparent from the factual position narrated in the foregoing paragraphs that most of the retiral benefits were released to the petitioner on 16.3.1999, it is clear that most of the retiral benefits were paid to the petitioner within six months of the date of the petitioner's acquittal on 12101998.
As such, there is hardly any justification on the basis of which this Court can return a finding that there was inordinate and unexplained delay in the release of retiral benefits to the petitioner.
The pleadings of the case also do not contain any express allegation on the aforesaid issue.
In view of the above, I find no merit in the claim of the petitioner for payment of interest on delayed payment of retiral benefits.
Petition dismissed.
