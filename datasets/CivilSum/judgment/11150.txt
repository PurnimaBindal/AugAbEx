GR Majithia, J - The plaintiff-appellant has come up in regular second appeal against the judgment and decree of the first appellate Court partly modifying those of the trial Judge and dismissing his suit in toto.
2.
The facts -  The plaintiff-appellant (hereinafter the plaintiff sought declaration with consequential relief of injunction on the allegations that he was appointed as an Apprentice Clerk sponsored by Employment Exchange, Jind, by defendant-respondent No. 2, (hereinafter the contesting defendant) from September 14, 1982 though the formal order was released on September 19, 1983; that he joined his duties and has been working as Clerk in the office of the contesting defendant though he is being shown as a Helper in the record which is fake and fictitious; that he was also shown as Ticket Verifier from October 4, 1983 and thereafter as Helper from November 1, 1983 to April 30, 1984; and that in fact he has been continuously working as such in the office of the contesting defendant right from September 14, 1982 but his pay for the period from September 14, 1983 to October 3, 1983, May 1, 1984 to May 30, 1984 and June 1, 1984 to September 30, 1984 was not released without any reason and he was entitled to receive the same.
He had completed more than 240 days by June 1984 and had become entitled to be regularised as Clerk in view of the instructions issued by the State Government.
3.
The contesting defendant, apart from taking preliminary objections, on merit pleaded that though the plaintiff was appointed as a Clerk apprentice, yet he was relieved of the said post vide order dated September 19,1983, because he had failed to pass apprenticeship examination; that thereafter the plaintiff has worked as Helper and not as Clerk because the appointment of a Clerk was within the purview of the Subordinate Service Selection Board, Haryana and that the plaintiff was not entitled to be paid the wages for which he had not worked under the defendants.
4.
From the pleadings of the parties, following issues were framed by the trial Judges :-  1) Whether the plaintiff is entitled to be treated as a regular clerk and entitled to full pay and allowances attached to the said post with effect from 41083 onwards to date; if so, to what effect?
2) Whether the plaintiff was relieved from duty as apprentice clerk vide order No. 11931/ECW dated 19983 in accordance with the initial appointment order No. 2337-41/ECW dated 1982 ?
3) Whether the civil court has no jurisdiction to try the present suit?
4) Whether the suit is bad for want of requisite notice under Sections 80 CPC ?
5) Whether the suit is not properly valued for the purposes of court fee ?
6) Whether the suit does not lie against the defendant No. 2, if so, to what effect?
7) Relief.
5.
The trial Judge disposed of issues No. 1 an 2 together.
Issue No. 2 was decided in favour of the plaintiff, under issue No. 1, it was held that the plaintiff was not entitled to be treated as regular clerk but he was entitled to pay as Clerk for the months of March/April and August, 1987; he was also entitled to the salary as Helper from September 14, 1983 to October 3, 1983, May 1, 1984 to May 30, 1984 and June 1, 1984 to September 30, 1984; issue No. 3 was found in favour of the plaintiff; issue No. 4 was found against the defendants and issue No. 5 and 6 were taken up together and answered against the defendants.
On ultimate analysis, the suit of the plaintiff was decreed in the light of the finding recorded under issue No. 1.
6.
The parties feeling aggrieved against the judgment and decree of the trial Judge preferred two separate appeals, which were disposed of by the First appellate Court by a common judgment.
The first appellate Court, on appraisal of the evidence, came to the conclusion that the plaintiff was appointed as an apprentice Clerk but was relieved of the said post on September 19, 1983 since he had failed to pass the Apprenticeship Examination.
It also came to the conclusion that the plaintiff worked as Ticket Verifier from October 4, 1983 to October 31, 1983 and then as Helper from October 1, 1983 to April 30, 1983 and paid for the same job.
It also held that the plaintiff was not entitled to be regularised in service as a Clerk.
The claim of the plaintiff for pay as Helper from September 14,1983 to October 3,1983; May 1, 1984 to May 30, 1984 and June 1, 1984 to September 30, 1984 was declined by it on the ground,that it was barred by time.
In coming to this conclusion, it relied upon the statement that he was not paid the last pay on September 30, 1984.
The suit was filed by the plaintiff on May 17,1988 and it was obviously barred by time.
In the ultimate analysis, the appeal filed by the contesting defendant was allowed and that of the plaintiff was dismissed.
7.
Before me, learned counsel for the appellant again stressed that the plaintiff was appointed as a Clerk on regular basis and he was entitled to have his services regularised.
The learned counsel conceded that no order of appointment was issued by the competent authority appointing the plaintiff as a Clerk.
His case is that he had been working as such in the Haryana Roadways Depot and thus was entitled to payment of salary.
To establish that the plaintiff was working as Clerk, he referred to slip, Ex. PH, issued by Raj Singh, Clerk, indicating that the plaintiff had been working as a Clerk.
The first appellate Court, for sound reasons, rejected this document and after appraisal of the evidence came to the conclusion that the plaintiff had failed to prove that he had been actually working as a Clerk and his claim for salary for the months of March, April 1, 1987 was negatived.
The counsel did not urge that the finding recorded, by the first appellate Court that the claim of the plaintiff for wages as Helper for this period was beyond limitation was wrong.
The appeal is devoid of any merit.
8.
For the reasons stated above, the appeal fails and is dismissed, but with no order as to costs.
Appeal dismissed.
