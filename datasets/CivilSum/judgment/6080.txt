MM Kumar, J - This order shall dispose of two writ petitions bearing C.W.P.
No. 1687 of 2002 and 1906 of 2002 as common questions of facts and law are involved.
However, the facts are taken from C.W.P.
No. 1687 of 2002 unless stated otherwise.
2.
The petitioner has approached this Court by filing petition under Article 226 of the Constitution for issuance of a writ of certiorari for quashing order dated 2692001 (P-6).
It has further been prayed that the respondents may be directed to grant disability pension consisting of service element and disability element for 20% disability to the petitioner from the date of discontinuance of the disability pension for life.
An alternate prayer has also been made to the effect that in case the percentage of disability of the petitioner was assessed at less than 20% than he may be granted service element w.e.f.
2421962 in accordance with Regulation 186(1&2) read with Regulation 183 of the Pension Regulations for the Army, 1961.
It has also been prayed that the petitioner be brought before the Re-Survey Medical Board in accordance with Army Order 152 of 1973, which provides for the Re-Survey Medical Board in case there is a substantial increase in the disability percentage of the individual.
3.
The petitioner was enrolled in the Indian Army on 10.10.1951 after having been found medically fit in the medical category of `AYE'.
He was sent for training in the allotted trade B.E.G.
Roorkee.
After completion of training he was allocated to the Unit in the year 1952-53.
The petitioner was placed in an area which was infested with `PULMONARY TUBERCULOSIS' in the year 1953.
Unfortunately, he fell sick and suffered from the disease of `Pulmonary Tuberculosis' and was admitted to Military Hospital in April, 1953.
The disease was held to be attributable to military service.
It is appropriate to mention here that at the relevant point of time the disease of `Polumonary Tuberculosis' was considered as a deadly disease.
Despite treatment the petitioner could not improve and he was sought before the Medical Board.
On the recommendations of the Medical Board, the petitioner was boarded out from Military Service on 151953 after rendering 1 year 6 months and 22 days service.
He was brought to the lower medical category `EEE' with 100% disability at the time when he was invalidated out of the Military Service.
As the disease of the petitioner was attributable to Military Service, therefore, he was granted disability pension consisting of service element and disability element for 100% disability, from time to time i.e. 251953 to 661953, 1231955 to 661956, 761956 to 821957 and 9.2.1957 to 3061957.
The petitioner was granted disability pension consisting of above mentioned two elements upto 2321962.
The pension was reduced depending on the gradual reduction of disability to 80%, 60%, 40% and 20%.
On 2311962 again Medical Board was held and the disability of the petitioner was described as static at the rate of 20%.
However, the Chief Controller of Defence Accounts (Pension) (for brevity, ` the CCDA') reduced the disability to less than 20% and stopped the disability pension of the petitioner w.e.f.
2421962 in respect of disability element as well as the service element of pension.
The proceedings of the Medical Board were not supplied to the petitioner on the ground that the same were confidential documents.
Thereafter the petitioner has made several representations to the respondent authorities requesting for Re-Survey Medical Board.
It has further been claimed that the position of the petitioner further deteriorated and he is taking treatment from Singla Hospital, 167-A, Kitchlu Nagar, Ludhiana.
A medical certificate dated 2422000 from the said hospital has been attached as Annexure P-2, certifying that his disability is 40%.
4.
When the representations of the petitioner were not considered and replied, he served a justice demand notice, dated 682001 (P-5) through his counsel demanding continuance of disability pension consisting of service as well as disability element w.e.f.
2421962 onwards along with 18% interest and to further bring him before the Re-Survey Medical Board as his percentage of disability had increased.
Respondent No. 4 sent a reply vide letter dated 2692001 (P-6) mentioning therein that the claim of the petitioner was sent to Principal Controller of Defense Accounts (Pensions), Allahabad (for brevity, `the PCDA'), which was rejected by assessing his disability at less than 20% final.
The petitioner had not since been granted service element for life.
It was discounted w.e.f.
2421962 consequent upon rejection of disability element.
The order of the PCDA was communicated to the petitioner vide office letter No. 1417529/47-R/PD/R, dated 1751962.
The reply further pointed out that the petitioner did not prefer any appeal nor any petition on receipt of the decision of the PCDA, therefore, no action for grant of any type of pension could be taken.
5.
A sketch of brief facts may also be noticed from Civil Writ Petition No. 1906 of 2002.
That petitioner was enrolled in the Royal Indian Army on 15.8.1942 where he served upto 961948 and thereafter discharged from the service.
He was again enrolled in the Army on 671948 after medical examination in the medical category `AYE'.
On account of service at High Altitude and in adverse climatic conditions he suffered from the disease of Osteo-Arthritis of Sacro-Iliac Joint Low Backache.
He was downgraded to the medical category `CEE'.
On 631950, the Medical Board recommended his invalidation from service in the medical category `CEE(P)' with 20% disability and he was granted pension from 731950 consisting of both the elements (service and disability), which continued upto 2941956.
In May, 1956 he was brought before the Re-Survey Medical Board and his disability was assessed at 20%.
His claim was forwarded to the PCDA, which had rejected the report of Re-Survey Medical Board by assessing his disability at less than 20%, vide their letter No. G3/CA-57/57/76171/RA, dated 1791957.
The petitioner preferred appeal, which was rejected on 1461960.
He again filed appeals on 2111961 and 20111962, which were also rejected on 18121961 and 2771964 respectively.
In this manner, the disability pension of the petitioner in C.W.P.
No. 1906 of 2002 was discontinued w.e.f.
3041956 under Regulation 375 read with Regulation 318 of the Pension Regulations for the Army, 1961.
6.
In the written statement, the respondents have admitted the factual position in relation to enrolment of the petitioner in the Army, his invalidation from service due to medical category of `EEE' due to disease `Pulmonary Tuberculosis' grant of disability pension w.e.f.
251953 to 2321962 and discontinuation of the same w.e.f.
2421962.
For stopping the service element of pension to the petitioner the respondents have taken the stand that the qualifying service of the petitioner was less than 10 years, therefore, as per existing Rules/Orders, he is not eligible for service element of disability pension for life as the same is admissible only for the period to which the disability element is admissible to an individual.
It has been further stated that in January, 1962 the petitioner was brought before Re-Survey Medical Board and his disability pension claim was sent to the PCDA, which they have rejected by assessing his invalid disability at less than 20%, vide order/letter 7/8.05.1962 (R-1).
The further stand of the respondents is that neither the petitioner had preferred any appeal nor any petition after discontinuation of disability pension w.e.f.
2421962 and he had not sent any intimation regarding substantial increase of disability prior to the legal notice which was received after a period of 39 years.

8.
Mr. Surjit Singh, learned counsel for the respondents has vehemently argued that in C.W.P.
1687 of 2002 no appeal has was filed and the claim made by the petitioner is highly belated.
He has pressed the plea of delay and laches in the other petition also.
According to the learned counsel the Re-Survey Medical Board has shown that there was continuous improvement in the medical condition and, therefore, the PCDA reached a logical conclusion, which should not be interfered with by this Court.
9.
Having heard the learned counsel for the parties at a considerable length and after perusing the documents placed on record, I an of the view that the short issue involved in both the petitions is that whether the CCDA or PCDA could sit in judgment over the opinion of the experts in medical line.
It is admitted position that the disability of the petitioners in both petitions was certified to be 20% by the Re-Survey Medical Board.
However, the PCDA has rejected the disability pension by assessing the invalid disability at less than 20%.
The aforementioned issue is squarely covered by the judgment of the Hon'ble Supreme Court in the case of Ex. Sapper Mohinder Singh v. Union of India, Civil Appeal No. 164 of 1993, decided on 1411993 (supra), wherein it has been held as under :-  "From the above narrated facts and the stand taken by the parties before us, the controversy that falls for determination by us in a very narrow compass viz. whether the Chief Controller of Defence Accounts (Pension) has any jurisdiction to sit over the opinion of the experts (Medical Board) while dealing with the case of grant of disability pension, in regard to the percentage of the disability pension, or not.
In the present case, it is nowhere stated that the petitioner was subjected to any higher Medical Board before the Chief Controller of Defence Accounts (Pension) decided to decline the disability pension to the petitioner.
We are unable to see as to how the accounts branch dealing with the pension can sit over the judgment of the experts in the medical line without making any reference to a detained or higher Medical Board which can be constituted under the relevant instructions and rules by the Director General of Army Medical Core".
The aforementioned view has been followed by Division Benches of this Court in the case of Satpal Singh v. Union of India and others, (C.W.P.
No. 15445 of 2003, decided on 2692005; Ex-Sappar Ujagar Singh v. Union of India and others, C.W.P.
No. 17688 of 1996, decided on 9101997 (supra), and in a recent judgment in the case of Ex-Subedar Jasmail Singh v. Union of India and others, 2006(2) Law Herald 1480.
10.
I, respectfully follow the view taken by the Hon'ble Supreme Court as well as this Court and hold that the CCDA/PCDA cannot sit in judgment over the opinion expressed by Re-Survey Medical Board.
Accordingly, applying the principle laid down by the aforementioned judgments, these petitions deserve to be allowed.
11.
During the course of arguments, learned counsel for the respondents have raised the issue of delay in filing petitions after a long gap of more than 40 years.
This argument is not tenable, inasmuch as, it is a settled position of law that the right to receive pension of any kind is a recurring cause of action and it accrues every month.
In this regard, the judgments relied upon by the learned counsel for the petitioner, as detailed in the earlier part of the judgment are fully applicable in the instant petitions.
12.
In view of above, both the writ petitions are allowed.
A direction is issued to the respondents to assess the disability pension of the petitioner in each case by keeping in view that they had suffered 20% disability for life, which is referable to service, with effect from the dates disability pension has been discontinued.
It shall include both disability element and service element of pension.
However, the arrears are confined to 38 months preceding the date of filing of petitions, which are 24012002 (C.W.P.
No. 1687 of 2002) and 29.01.2002 (C.W.P.
No. 1906 of 2002).
The arrears shall be paid within a period of two months from today.
The petitioners in both cases shall continue to get disability pension for life.
The petitions disposed of in the above terms.
Petitions allowed.
