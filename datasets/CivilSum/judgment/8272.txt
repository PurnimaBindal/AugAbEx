SD Gundewar, J - Rule.
Rule made returnable forthwith by consent.
2.
Heard Shri BP Dharmadhikari, the learned Counsel for the petitioner and Shri Jagdale, learned Counsel for respondent No. 1.
3.
The respondent No. 1 was in the employment of the petitioner as Conductor.
On 2241987, while he was on duty as a Conductor in ST Bus plying on Gaji- Wardha route, the said Bus came to be checked by the checking staff of the petitioner.
At that time 235 passengers were found present in the said ST Bus.
However, the respondent No. 1 had issued tickets of lesser denomination to 95 passengers, who were travelling from Bhankheda to Wardha, though he had recovered the full fare from them.
So also, it was found that two passengers travelling from Bhankheda to Wardha were travelling without tickets.
The charge-sheet was, therefore, issued by the respondent No. 1 on 1061987 and he was dismissed on 7101987 after the charges were proved in the Departmental Enquiry held against him.
4.
On 411988, the respondent No. 1 filed Complaint ULP No. 7/88 before the Labour Court challenging his dismissal.
On 3011992, the Labour Court allowed the Complaint ordering his reinstatement without backwages.
On 30th March, 1992, the petitioner challenged the aforesaid order passed by the Labour Court in Revision No. 110/92 before the Industrial Court and the learned Member of the Industrial Court granted interim stay which was confirmed on 20.11.1992.
The respondent No. 1 challenged the aforesaid order of granting stay before this Court by filing Writ Petition No. 362/93, which came to be dismissed on 641993.
On 2871993 after hearing the parties, the Industrial Court dismissed the Revision filed by the petitioner.
Thereafter, on 25101993 the respondent No. 1 came to be reinstated by the petitioner in service.
The respondent No. 1 then filed Complaint ULP No. 235/94 on 1141994 claiming backwages for the period from 30th Jan., 1992 to 25th Oct., 1993 with interest at the rate of 9%.
The Industrial Court allowed the said Complaint on 28.8.1998 and granted backwages with 9% interest to the respondent No. 1.
Being aggrieved by the said order, the petitioner has filed the present petition.
5.
Shri BP Dharmadhikari, the learned Counsel for the petitioner submitted that the learned Member of the Industrial Court erred in granting backwages to the respondent No. 1 from 3011992 till 25101993 as during the said period the respondent No. 1 was not on duty because of the interim stay granted by the Industrial Court which was afterwards confirmed by the said Court and the Writ Petition filed before this Court by the respondent No. 1 against the said order came to be dismissed on 641993.
6.
Shri Jagdale, the learned Counsel for the respondent No. 1, relying upon the decision in the case of The General Secretary, B.E.ST Workers' Union v. The General Manager, B.E.ST Undertaking, 1997(1) Cur LR 898 and IB Inamdar v. The B.E.ST Undertaking, 1996(1) Cur LR 831, submitted that on 3011992, the Labour Court allowed the Complaint filed by the respondent No. 1, but without backwages.
According to him, this order came to be stayed by the Industrial Court on 30th March, 1992 which was confirmed on 20.11.1992 and the Writ Petition, filed by the respondent No. 1 in this Court, was dismissed on 641993.
According to him, the Industrial Court dismissed the Revision filed by the petitioner on 2871993 and on 25101993 the respondent No. 1 came to be reinstated by the petitioner.
So according to him, from 30th of January, 1992 till 25th Oct., 1993, the respondent was kept out of service for no fault of his and merely because the petitioner obtained an order of stay from the Industrial Court.
It is also submitted by him that had such stay not been obtained by the petitioner, the respondent No. 1 would have worked during the said period and earned his wages.
7.
In view of the aforesaid submissions made by the learned counsel for the respondent No. 1, I see no reason as to why the respondent No. 1 should be deprived of the said wages, which he could not earn doing the aforesaid period on account of stay obtained by the petitioner.
8.
In the result, the writ petition stands dismissed.
Rule is discharged accordingly, with no order as to costs.
Petition dismissed.
