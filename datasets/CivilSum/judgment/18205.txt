MMS Bedi, J - The petitioner has retired on 342004 as Assistant Security Commissioner, Railway Protection Force, Northern Railways, Varanasi and settled at Chandigarh.
He has filed this petition under Article 226 of the Constitution seeking quashing of order dated 18.5.2005 (Annexure P-4) passed by Chief Health Director, respondent No. 2 refusing to reimburse the medical claim of the petitioner for the medical treatment taken by him during the period from 312003 to 1212003.
2.
As per the impugned order the petitioner has been denied the medical reimbursement on the ground that the scheme of Retired Employees Liberalised Health Scheme-1997 (for short `RELHS-1997') was opened till 3132002 and the petitioner had deposited Rs. 10,475/- vide CR No. 516448 dated 2272002 of his own and obtained medical card without adhering to the policy guidelines, as such the Medical I Card (RELHS) is invalid.
3.
The plea taken by the respondents in the written statement filed pursuant to the notice of motion issued is that the petitioner had wrongly obtained RELHS medical card after his retirement whereas as per RELHS-1997 the petitioner was required to apply atleast three months prior to the date of his retirement.
As per RELHS-1997, the medical card (Annexure P-1) is, therefore, invalid.
It is averred in the written statement that the scheme was reopened for pre 1997 retirees vide Annexure P-5 and again on 28.1.2005 uptill 31.12.2005.
The petitioner was advised by respondent No. 4 to deposit the invalid medical card and get a new medical card made under this scheme vide letter dated 2552005 (Annexure R-4/6).
The petitioner has undertaken the treatment in 2003, as such he cannot avail the medical reimbursement under the Scheme (Annexure P-6).
4.
Learned counsel for the petitioner has submitted that the petitioner had been issued the medical card dated 182002 (Annexure P-1) after he having contributed the requisite amount.
No fraud has been played by the petitioner to obtain the said card.
Therefore, the impugned order denying the petitioner his lawful claim of medical reimbursement is illegal and arbitrary.
5.
Mrs. Abha Rathore, learned counsel for the respondents has opposed the claim of the petitioner by contending that the petitioner has obtained an invalid card after the period permissible to him under the scheme, as such he is not eligible for reimbursement.
The RELHS medical card cannot be utilised by the petitioner to obtain the benefit of medical reimbursement.
6.
We have thoughtfully considered the submissions made the learned counsel for the parties and are of the view that this petition deserves to succeed.
It is undisputed that the RELHS-1997 had been framed for the benefit of the retired railway employees to provide them full medical facilities as admissible to the serving employees in respect of medical treatment, investigations, diet and reimbursement of claims for treatment in Government or recognized non-railway hospitals.
The contribution for joining RELHS-1997 was equivalent to the last month's basic pay of the employee at the time of retirement for those who opted to join the scheme.
The persons who joined the RELHS after 111996 were required to pay the difference of one time contribution on account of introduction of fourth pay commission's revised pay scale w.e.f.
111996.
As per para 5 of the scheme the mode of joining the scheme is that the employees are required to give their option to join the RELHS-1997 atleast three months prior to their date of retirement.
The option given once is to be treated as final.
It is specifically mentioned that no further chance would be given subsequent to retirement.
The members of previous scheme i.e.
Retired Railway Employees Contributory Health Scheme (RRECHS) were permitted to have the option to switch over to RELHS-97 by making payment as required under the scheme.
It is not disputed that the scheme was again reopened for pre 1997 retirees and again on 28.1.2005 up till 3122005 and the petitioner has been advised vide letter dated 2552005 (Annexure R-4/6) to get a new medical card made under the scheme.
It is an admitted fact that the petitioner had been made a member of the scheme by issuing him a RELHS medical card dated 182002 (Annexure P-1).
7.
The petitioner had deposited a sum of Rs. 10,475/- ie the basic pay vide CR No. 516448 dated 2272002 and had undergone Coronary Angioplasty at FORTIS Hospital, Mohali.
He had to pay a bill of Rs. 2,32,269/- on account of treatment of Coronary Angioplasty.
He was discharged on 1212003.
8.
Once the respondents have accepted the contribution of the petitioner under the RELHS-1997 after his retirement, and was issued the medical card (Annexure P-1), the principle of estoppel would come into play disentitling the respondents to later on turn around to refuse to acknowledge the petitioner as a member of the RELHS-1997.
So far as the bar of exercising option beyond the period of atleast three months prior to the date of his retirement is concerned, the same is not intended to be an absolute bar because subsequently vide letter dated 2552005 (Annexure P4/6) the petitioner has been advised to deposit back the identity card and get the new medical card made as at present the scheme is open.
9.
The limitation prescribed in Rule 5 to give option to join RELHS-1997 atleast three months prior to the date of retirement does not appear to be mandatory but it is only directory keeping in view the fact that the scheme framed for retired employees is a piece of welfare and social legislation which requires to be construed liberally so as to grant benefit to the retired employees.
The respondents are now estopped from extending the benefit of RELHS-1997 to the petitioner after having made him a member of the scheme by accepting the contribution from him and having issued him the medical card (Annexure P-1).
The offer of the respondents vide letter dated 2552005 advising the petitioner to get a new medical card made, as the scheme is open, indicates that the condition of giving option atleast three months prior to the date of retirement stands waived off.
10.
In view of the above discussion, we are of the opinion that the petitioner has been wrongfully denied the benefit of medical reimbursement.
Therefore, the writ petition is allowed and the impugned order dated 18.5.2005 (Annexure P-4) is hereby quashed.
The respondents are directed to process the medical bill of the petitioner in the sum of Rs. 2,32,629/-, reimburse him the amount permissible to him as per the medical rules and pay the amount to him within a period of four months from the date of receipt of the copy of this order.
Petition allowed.
