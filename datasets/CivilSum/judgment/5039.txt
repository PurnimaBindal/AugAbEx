SS Nijjar, J. (Oral) - With the consent of counsel for the parties, the writ petition is taken up for final disposal at the motion stage itself.
2.
The petitioners are working as Tubewell Operators (Jr.
Technician) in the Punjab State Tubewell Corporation Limited (hereinafter referred to as "the Corporation").
The conditions of service of the petitioners are governed by the rules which are applicable to the employees of the Irrigation Department, Punjab who are holding corresponding posts.
In fact, the Corporation is under the overall control of Secretary of Government of Punjab Department of Irrigation, Punjab, Chandigarh.
The State of Punjab constituted the 3rd Pay Commission for making recommendations regarding the revision of pay scales and other service conditions of the Punjab Government employees.
On the basis of the recommendations of the 3rd Pay Commission, the pay scales of the employees of the Corporation were revised w.e.f.
111986 along with the employees of the Punjab Government.
Chapter 26 of the 3rd Pay Commission deals with skilled and semi-skilled staff.
It was recommended that the skilled and semi-skilled staff be placed in the revised scales, according to their qualifications.
The skilled and semi-skilled staff were divided into 5 categories, namely, Junior Technicians (Tubewell) Operators (in the case of respondent-Corporation), Technician Grade IV, Technical Grade III, Technician Grade II and Technician Grade I.
It was also recommended that the aforesaid posts shall be filled by way of direct recruitment as well as by promotion.
The recommendations were accepted by the State Government by letter dated 26101988 (Annexure P-1).
The State Government directed all the Heads of Departments to implement the recommendations by making necessary amendments in the departmental rules.
Consequently, Punjab Civil Services (Revised Pay) Rules, 1988 (hereinafter referred to as "the Revised Pay Rules") were notified by the State Government on 991988.
These rules came into force w.e.f.
111986.
Rule 8 of the aforesaid Rules provided as under :-  "8.
Fixation of Pay on Promotion : Notwithstanding anything contained in these rules, in case of promotion to a higher post effected after the date of publication of this notification in Punjab Government Gazette the benefit of minimum two increments shall be given while fixing the pay in the scale of the higher post.
The next increment in the scale of the higher post shall be allowed after the completion of twelve months' qualifying service in that scale."

Technician) in the pay scale of Rs. 950-1800 were promoted as Technician Grade IV in the pay scale of Rs. 1025-1800 on different dates and were later on promoted to the higher post of Technician Grade III on different dates in the pay scale of Rs. 1200-2100.
Generally speaking, the petitioners were appointed as Tubewell Operators on different dates in 1975 and 1976.
They were promoted as Technician Grade IV, on 111986, 111987, 111988, 141988 and 111989.
They were further promoted as Technician Grade III on 111987, 111989, 121989, 111990, 151990, 111991, 111992 and 111993.
The petitioners were also granted benefit of one increment on promotion from Tubewell Operators to Technician Grade IV and one increment on promotion from Technician Grade IV to Grade III.
The petitioners claim that they were, in fact, entitled to two increments on each promotion i.e. from Tubewell Operator (Jr.
Technician) to Technician Grade IV and Technician Grade III.
One GS Grewal, retired Junior Technician filed CWP No. 15523 of 1990 in this Court praying that his pay be fixed in accordance with Rule 8 of the Revised Pay Rules.
He was compelled to file the aforesaid writ petition as the Punjab Government had issued instructions directing that the two increments in case of promotion to the higher post would only be granted to promotions made after 1391988.
Since GS Grewal had been promoted as Chief Engineer on 611988, he had been denied the benefit of Rule 8 (supra).
The aforesaid writ petition was allowed on 16111992.
The aforesaid judgment is reported in 1993(1) SLR 640.
It was held that there cannot be any discrimination between persons who were promoted w.e.f.
111986 to 1391988 and those promoted after 1391988.
The relevant observations made by this Court in the aforesaid judgment dated 16111992 are as under :-  "Instead of striking down Rule 8 of the Rules in my opinion, it should be read down so as to give the benefit to all the employees who are promoted after the coming into force of the rules, irrespective of their dates of promotion whether they be before or after the date of promulgation."
In view of the aforesaid observations, Punjab Government issued instructions on 1651996 (Annexure P-5) wherein it was clarified that in view of Rule 8 (supra), every person who is promoted w.e.f.
2111986 will be granted two increments on promotion to the higher posts.
The petitioners, therefore, claim the benefits of these instructions also.
These instructions, according to the petitioners, are binding on the Corporation.
In fact the Corporation endorsed the instructions to all the Superintending Engineers for information and necessary action, on 2211997.
However, subsequently, the State Government took a decision to abolish the post of Technician Grade IV and merged the same with the post of Technician Grade III.
Henceforth, Junior Technicians in the pay scale of Rs. 950-1800 would be entitled to be promoted to the post of Technician Grade III in the pay scale of Rs. 1200-2100.
In view of the decision of the State Government, the respondent-Corporation issued order dated 28101998 (Annexure P-6) abolishing the post of Technician Grade IV and merging the same with the post of Technician Grade III.
On the basis of the aforesaid merger, the Divisional Engineer, Tubewell and Stores Division, P.S.T.C.
Ltd., Ludhiana (respondent No. 3) issued letter dated 2141999 (Annexure P-7) directing to effect recoveries from the employees at the rate of Rs. 1500/- pm of the amount allegedly over-paid.
The petitioners and their Union protested that the order (Annexure P-7) is wholly illegal.
However, respondent No. 3 again issued an order on 29.4.1999 (Annexure P-8) reiterating that the initial order was correct and the recoveries be effected.
Faced with this situation, the petitioners approached this Court by way of filing CWP No. 8268 of 1999 challenging the order dated 2141999 and the letter dated 29.4.1999.
The writ petition was allowed by a Division Bench of this Court on 222000 and order dated 2141999 and the letter dated 29.4.1999 were quashed.
While allowing the aforesaid writ petition, following observations were made by the Division Bench of this Court :-  "In view of the above conclusion and the fact that respondent Nos. 2 and 3 and the officers subordinate to them did not give action-oriented notice and opportunity of hearing to the petitioners before issuing direction for recovery from their salaries, we have no hesitation to hold that the impugned recoveries are null and void and on that ground alone, Annexures P-7 and P-9 are liable to be quashed.
Ordered accordingly.
The writ petition is allowed in the manner indicated above."
In view of the aforesaid observations of the Division Bench, thereafter, the respondents issued show-cause notice to the petitioners on 2042001 (Annexure P-10), seeking the explanation from the petitioners as to why recoveries be not effected.
Before taking the final decision on the matter, respondent No. 3 again started effecting recoveries from the pay of the petitioners.
The petitioners, therefore, again approached this Court by filing CWP No. 7508 of 2002.
The aforesaid writ petition was disposed of by this Court on 14.5.2002 with the observations that no recoveries be effected by reducing the pay without passing a final order.
Again the respondents passed some more orders ordering recovery from the petitioners on 2762002.
These orders were again challenged by the petitioners by filing CWP No. 17296 of 2002.
This writ petition was also disposed of by a Division Bench of this Court by order dated 28.10.2002 (Annexure P-12) and the respondents were directed to treat the writ petition as a representation to the show-cause notice and to pass fresh orders, after taking into consideration the pleas raised by the petitioners.
In view of the orders passed by the Division Bench, the respondents have passed the impugned order dated 2412003 (Annexure P-13), again ordering recoveries from the pay of the petitioners.
In the impugned order, the justification given by the respondents is that since by order dated 15.6.1990, the post of Technician Grade IV has been merged with that of Technician Grade III, by abolishing the same, the benefit of promotion which was granted to the petitioners, is liable to be withdrawn and the recovery is justified, as per the rules.
The petitioners have filed the present writ petition under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India, challenging the impugned order (Annexure P-13).
6.
The respondents have filed a written statement.
There is no denial of the facts as narrated above, by the respondents.
However, it is stated that being placed in the category of Junior Technician Grade IV, the petitioners cannot claim to have been promoted.
They were designated as Technician Grade IV to avoid direct recruitment, as the Corporation was not in a position to make direct recruitment upto certain percentage which had been indicated in the 3rd Pay Commission report.
Since the petitioners had not been promoted, Rule 8 (supra) would not be applicable.
The judgment of the High Court rendered in CWP No. 15523 of 1990 would not be applicable as there was no promotion involved.
Even otherwise, the petitioners cannot be permitted to challenge the decision of the Corporation as the post of Technician Grade IV had been abolished and merged into the post of Technician Grade II on the demands made by the Union of the employees.
Since the petitioners had only been placed in the pay scale of Junior Technician Grade IV on redesignation, they would not be entitled to one increment on such placement/redesignation.
Therefore, the pay having been erroneously fixed, has now been rightly reduced and the recoveries, therefore, have been ordered to be effected in accordance with law.
7.
We have heard the learned counsel for the parties at length and perused the paper-book.
8.
Mr. Chopra, learned Sr.
Counsel appearing for the petitioner has submitted that the petitioners having been promoted as Technician Grade IV and subsequently as Technician Grade III were entitled to a jump of two increments on each promotion.
The petitioners had been wrongly given only one increment on each promotion.
This, according to the learned Sr.
Counsel, was contrary to the mandate of Rule 8 which stipulated that benefit of minimum two increments shall be given while fixing the pay in the scale of higher post.
He further submits that on abolition of the post of Junior Technician Grade IV, factually, promotion of the petitioners on the aforesaid post, cannot be wiped out.
The petitioners had been actually promoted.
Increments had been actually granted.
Now the aforesaid benefits cannot be withdrawn.
Even if it is accepted that the payment has been erroneously made, the same cannot be permitted to be withdrawn with retrospective effect.
9.
Learned counsel for the respondents has submitted that the petitioners are misconstruing the designation on the post of Technician Grade IV as a promotion.
According to the learned counsel, the order dated 1771996 (Annexure P-3) clearly indicates that Tubewell Operators from Sr. Nos. 1 to 5 on the seniority list as it existed on 111986 had been placed in the scale as well as the designation w.e.f.
111986 as indicated against their names.
The order nowhere states that these employees had been promoted from the post of Tubewell Operators to the post of Technician Grade IV.
The decision of the Board of Directors had been taken, after it had been referred to a Standing Committee for working out the modalities for the implementation of Chapter 26 in consultation with the various Unions of Operational staff.
Therefore, the petitioners cannot now be permitted to say that they are entitled to two increments on each promotion, in accordance with Rule 8 (supra).
10.
We have considered the submissions made by the learned counsel for the parties.
We are of the opinion that the plea of the respondents with regard to estoppel is liable to be rejected.
There can be no estoppel against law.
Even otherwise, the Division Bench of this Court in its order dated 222000 passed in CWP No. 8268 of 1999 has clearly held that the petitioners cannot be non- suited by invoking the doctrine of promissory estoppel or principle underlying Section 18 of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.
The respondents have not challenged the aforesaid judgment of the Division Bench of this Court, by filing any appeal in the Supreme Court.
Therefore, the respondents cannot be permitted to raise this argument.
We are also unable to accept the argument of Mr. Sethi that the petitioners were not promoted from the post of Tubewell Operator to that of Technician Grade IV.
At the time when the petitioners were placed in the higher grade, the post of Technician Grade IV was very much in existence.
The petitioners were granted one additional increment.
If the post was not promotional, there was no question of granting the additional increment to the petitioners.
Under Rule 8 (supra), the petitioners would have been entitled to four increments.
This, in fact, is the law laid down by this Court in the case of GS Grewal (supra).
It was held that since the petitioner therein had been promoted to the post of Chief Engineer, he is entitled to the grant of two increments.
Once the petitioners had been promoted from the post of Tubewell Operator to the post of Technician Grade IV, they would have been entitled to grant of two increments.
Under Rule 8, if a promotion is made, the employee is entitled to two increments.
Even if the argument of the respondents is now accepted that the petitioners were not entitled to the grant of one increment on designation as Technician Grade IV, it would practically make no difference.
It is accepted by both the sides that the post of Technician Grade IV has been merged with the post of Technician Grade III.
Mere use of the word "abolition" would not have the effect of wiping out the promotion which was practically given to the petitioners from the post of Tubewell Operator to that of Technician Grade IV.
Furthermore, since the post had merged into the post of Technician Grade III, the petitioners would be entitled to be re-designated as Technician Grade III, subject to the availability of the posts.
On such designation/promotion, the petitioners would be entitled to two increments under Rule 8 of the Revised Pay Rules, reproduced above.
Therefore, the claim of the respondents that the petitioners have been overpaid is without any basis, under any circumstances.
In view of the above, we allow the writ petition, quash the impugned order dated 2412003 (Annexure P-13) and direct the respondents that no recovery shall be made from the pay of the petitioners.
We further direct that their pay shall not be reduced and it shall be calculated on the basis of the increments already granted for future also.
No costs.
Petition allowed.
