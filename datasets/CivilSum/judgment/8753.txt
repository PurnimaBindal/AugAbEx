M. Jagannadha Rao, CJ.
- This appeal has been preferred by the Union of India against the judgment of the learned Single Judge dated 10.10.1995 in Civil Writ Petition No. 771 of 1991, allowing the writ petition and quashing the show cause notice dated 1551989 issued under Section 19 of the Army Act read with Rule 14 of the Army Rules and also quashing the impugned order of dismissal dated 13111990.
The learned Single Judge also directed payment of full back wages and all other consequential benefits to the respondent/writ petitioner from the date he was removed from service till the date he retired on superannuation and also to give him pensionary and other retiral benefits, in accordance with law.
2.
This appeal was filed in December 1995.
During the pendency of this appeal the Supreme Court has decided a case, similar on facts, viz. Major Radha Krishan v. Union of India and others, JT 1996(3) SC 650 : 1996(2) SCT 475(SC).
Learned counsel for the respondent (writ petitioner) submits that in view of the said judgment of the Supreme Court, wherein the Apex Court has upheld the Division Bench judgment of this court in Lt.Col.
(TS) HC Dhingra v. Union of India and another), 1988(2) Delhi Lawyer (DB) 109. this appeal is liable to be dismissed.
3.
On the other hand learned counsel for the appellant has relied upon the decisions of the Supreme Court in Sr. Gouranga Chakraborty v. State of Tripura and another, AIR 1989 SC 1321 ; Union of India and others v. Ram Phal, 1996(2) SCT 638(SC) : AIR 1996 SC 1500 and Chief of Army Staff and others v. Major Dharam Pal Kukrety, AIR 1985 SC 703, to contended that powers under Section 122 of the Act and those under Rule 14 of the Army Rules are independent powers.
It is further submitted that inasmuch as the powers are independent, the power under rule 14(2) can be exercised by the competent authority even after the expiry of the three years from the date of commission of offence.
In the instant case the alleged incident, which forms the subject matter of the charge-sheet, took place on 591983 and under Section 122 of the Army Act no trial by court martial of any person subject to the Army Act for any offence shall be commenced after the expiry of the period of three years from the date of the incident.
In other words, no court martial could have been initiated on or after 591986.
But appellant contends that even so, power of dismissal under Rule 14 read with Section 19 can be independently exercised, even though the time for commencement of a court-martial proceeding has expired on the date when action is taken under Rule 14.
4.
In the present case the alleged incident took place on 591983 and, subsequently, an enquiry was conducted by a duly constituted Court of Enquiry and a tentative charge-sheet was issued to the petitioner on 271984.
Summary evidence was recorded on 1941985.
Show cause notice was issued to the petitioner on 3151986 and petitioner sent his reply on 161986.
On 21111986 additional summary evidence was to the recorded.
It is at that stage that notice was issued to the respondent/writ petitioner on 1551989 for dismissal of the petitioner under Army Rule 14.
Petitioner sent his reply on 1281989 and, ultimately, an order of dismissal was issued on 13111990.
5.
Learned counsel for the petitioner contended before the learned Single Judge that once the three-year period from the date of the alleged incident, i.e. 591983, had expired on 591986 the power under Rule 14 could not be exercised on the plea that it was inexpedient or impracticable for conducting the court martial.
Rule 14(2) of the Army Rules reads as under :  ``14.
Termination of Service by the Central Government on account of misconduct : (1) xx xx xx xx (2) When after considering the reports on an officer's misconduct, the Central Government or the Chief of Army Staff is satisfied that the trial of the Officer by a Court martial is inexpedient or impracticable but is of the opinion that the further retension of the said officer in the service is undesirable, the Chief of the Army Staff shall so inform the officer together with all reports adverse to him and he shall be called upon to submit in writing, his explanation and defence.
Provided that the Chief of the Army Staff may withhold from disclosure any such report or portion thereof if, in his opinion, its disclosure is not in the public interest of the security of the State.
In the event of the explanation of the officer being considered unsatisfactory by the Chief of the Army Staff, or when so directed by the Central Government, the case shall be submitted to the Central Government with the officer's defence and the recommendation of the Chief of the Army Staff as to the termination in the manner specified in Sub-rule (4).''
As per the decisions of this Court in HC Dhingra's case power under rule 14(2) could not be exercised on the ground that it was inexpedient or impracticable to conduct a court martial three years after the date of the alleged offence.
The said view of this Court has, as already stated, been now accepted by the Supreme Court in Major Radha Krishan v. Union of India and others (supra), decided on 2531996.
The said judgment is declaratory of the law.
The impugned show cause notice dated 1551989 as well as dismissal order dated 13111990 must, therefore, be held to be illegal and without jurisdiction.
We are, therefore, in clear agreement with the learned Single Judge that Rule 14 could not have been invoked.
6.
However, we may point out that while dealing with the scope of proviso to Rule 14(2) the learned Single Judge has made certain observations as to circumstances in which the power under rule 14 could be invoked.
The learned Single Judge has noted that the said Rule 14(2) ``envisages that if the security of the State or public interest are involved'' then the rule comes into play.
We may here note that the main part of Rule 14(2) deals with termination of service without a court martial if the authority is satisfied that it is inexpedient or impracticable to conduct such an inquiry.
The proviso to Rule 14(2) does not deal with the power under Rule 14(2) but with the circumstances under which, while giving show cause notice under Rule 14(2), the authority could withhold certain information in public interest or security of the State.
To the extent, the learned Single Judge held that the proviso restricted the scope of the power under the main part of Rule 14(2), the said observations are left open because no such situation relevant to the proviso has arisen in this case.
7.
No doubt learned counsel for the appellant has submitted before us that in view of certain other decisions of the Supreme Court, to which we have already referred the power under Rule 14 is an independent power unconnected with Section 122 of the Army Act.
We are, however, not inclined to go into this aspect inasmuch as the decision in Major Radha Krishan v. Union of India and others (supra), is directly on the point in issue in this appeal.
8.
The appeal is in dismissed.
The appellant will comply with the judgment of the learned Single Judge within four weeks from today.
Appeal dismissed.
