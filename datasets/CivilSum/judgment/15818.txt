Usha Mehra, J - The petitioner was appointed as Junior Engineer in the Delhi Development Authority (in short DDA) in October 1969.
He was promoted to the rank of Assistant Engineer and subsequently in November, 1980 as Executive Engineer.
He officiated as Executive Engineer DD-II (now ND 7) till 3rd February, 1983.
He handed over the charge of the said Division DD-II to Shri MC Behl on 4th February, 1983.
On Ist/6th November, 1990 petitioner was served with the charge sheet pertaining to the construction of 480 LIG houses at Pocket `N' (Poorvi) at Pitam Pura, Delhi.
In the said chargesheet it was alleged that on 14th February, 1981 Quality Control Cell of DDA inspected the site and found certain irregularities in the construction of those houses.
The Technical Examiner of the CTE Organisation again examined the work and matter was referred to the Director Vigilance by the Chief Technical Examiner for investigation on 30th November, 1983.
Certain lapses and irregularities in the execution of work were alleged.
Another chargesheet was issued on 26th December, 1990 in respect of construction of 936 Janta Houses at Pocket `B' (Poorvi) Pitam Pura, Delhi.
Third chargesheet was issued on 25th June, 1993 regarding the development of the land for Zone H-5 (Pt) Pitam Pura SH Sewerage in Phase-II (Balance Work).
The allegation was that in the work which was carried out by the petitioner in the year 1982-83 some irregularities were found and that the work was found to be substandard on quality control inspection.
Yet another chargesheet was issued to the petitioner on 8th July, 1997 pertaining to the work done in the year 1980-83 relating to the development of land near Wazirpur Village for residential colony, Phase-III.
Because of these chargesheets and the pendency of the departmental proceedings, the Departmental Promotion Committee (in short DPC) which met in November, 1990, again in September, 1993 and December, 1993 respectively considered the case of the petitioner for promotion but kept the result in sealed cover.
The charges levelled against him and depriving him of the promotion to the post of Superintending Engineer pertains to the period of 1980-83.
The charge sheets regarding the alleged irregularities have been issued after almost ten years.
The Inquiry Officers appointed in each of the cases have not proceeded in any of the cases till date thereby depriving the petitioner of his chances of promotion.
Since the charge sheets have been issued after a lapse of 7 to 10 years and the Inquiry Officers have not proceeded with the enquiry till date, hence the charge sheets are liable to be quashed and consequently he be promoted to the rank of Superintending Engineer which promotion was illegally deprived.
2.
Respondent tried to explain away the delay by submitting that the first chargesheet which was issued on 1st/6th November, 1990 could not have been issued earlier because the record relating to that work including the measurement books were not available.
The measurement books were lying with one Mr. UP Sharma.
He was under suspension and absconding.
Police report was lodged against him.
The record was partly received in April-May, 1986.
Thereafter the case was investigated and handed over to the Superintending Engineer for comments.
The Superintending Engineer gave his comments in December, 1987.
The Vice Chairman after considering the entire case ordered major penalty proceedings and also referred the case to the Chief Vigilance Commission (in short CVC) for advice.
The advice from CVC was received on 19th July, 1989.
Accordingly chargesheet was issued on 1st/6th November, 1990.
The Inquiry Officer was appointed in October, 1991.
Since that Inquiry Officer was not available another Inquiry Officer was appointed in November, 1992 and was subsequently changed in July, 1993.
The enquiry is pending.
3.
As regards second chargesheet the respondent tried to explain the delay by saying that the Vice Chairman noted a news item in Indian Express that some houses in Pitam Pura were inspected and the mater had been referred to the CBRI for advice.
The said CBRI gave damaging report about the foundation of the walls.
On reading that report, the Vice Chairman got the matter investigated through the Engineer Member, DDA.
Thereafter one Shri K.K.
Sood and Shri Joginder Singh alongwith others were placed under suspension on 9th July, 1984.
The work was got inspected on 19th August, 1984.
Measurements were taken on 22nd August, 1984 in the presence of Chief Engineer (Quality Control).
The Chief Engineer (North Zone) found that the secured advance of Rs. 1490 lakhs had been paid to the contractor and certain shortages were there in the material.
Accordingly a memo was issued to the petitioner on 30th August, 1981.
The Superintending Engineer submitted his detailed report in November, 1984 identifying the name and defaulting officers.
Accordingly on 13th December, 1984 another memo was issued to the petitioner.
Petitioner submitted his reply on 20th December, 1974.
On the representation of the petitioner his suspension was revoked.
He was reinstated on 15th January, 1985 alongwith others.
The Vice Chairman after considering the details of the investigation ordered in October, 1987 to initiate major penalty proceedings against the petitioner.
It was only then that the charge sheet dated 26th December, 1990 was issued.
4.
Third chargesheet was issued on 25th June, 1993.
This pertained to the work of sewerage line which was inspected by the Lt Governor.
The Lt.
Governor noticed the defective work and found that sewerage lines were not functioning properly.
On the basis of that note of the Lt.
Governor the Chief Engineer (Quality Control) conducted an enquiry in May, 1986 and submitted his report in July, 1986.
He asked the Vigilance Department of DDA to seize the measurement books.
The Vigilance Department conducted a detailed inspection.
On the basis of that inspection report, the Vice Chairman ordered for major penalty proceedings against the petitioner on 26th May, 1988.
It was thereafter that chargesheet was issued on 25th June, 1993.
Inquiry Officer was appointed on 1st March, 1993 but proceedings could not proceed.
5.
Similarly, fourth chargesheet was issued on 28th September, 1997 regarding the work of development of land near Wazirpur village.
The work was raided by the Anti Corruption Branch, Delhi Administration.
The said Department, thereafter requested the Chief Technical Examiner to inspect the work technically to know about the quality of work.
The work was technically examined by Chief Technical Examiner on 3rd February, 1986, 4th February, 1986 and 13th February, 1986.
He submitted his report on 28th February, 1986.
Since the documents were seized by the Anti Corruption Branch who inspite of repeated requests did not return the documents till February, 1996, hence the chargesheet could not be issued earlier than July, 1997.
It has also been submitted by the respondent that the petitioner's case was considered for promotion to the post of Superintending Engineer by the DPC held on 28th November, 1990.
The DPC after taking into account the ACRs, vigilance clearance status and other requirements of the post ordered that the recommendations of the DPC in respect of the petitioner be kept in sealed cover.
Similarly the DPC held on 8th September, 1993 and on 17th December, 1993 directed that the recommendations be kept in seal cover.
The DPC which was held on 2nd February, 1994 could not consider the case of the petitioner because there was only one post and the petitioner did not find place in the zone of consideration.
Similar decision was taken by the DPC held on 19th April, 1994.
Petitioner was lastly considered by DPC on 26th July, 1996 and it was found that he was unfit for promotion because of the vigilance and departmental proceedings pending against him.
6.
I have heard Mr. Rakesh K Khanna for the petitioner and Mrs. Anusuya Salwan for the respondent/DDA.
Admittedly the cases covered by all the four chargesheets pertain to the period 1980-83.
Petitioner was made Incharge of DD-II Division in November, 1980.
He handed over the charge of that Division on 3rd February, 1983.
It is also a fact on record that work of 480 LIG Houses at Pocket `N' (Poorvi) at Pitam Pura, Delhi were got inspected by the Technical Examiner of CTE on 16th November, 1983.
Matter was referred by CTE to the Vigilance Department of DDA on 30th November, 1983.
Similarly, with regard to the work of 936 Janta Houses at Pocket `V' (Poorvi) Pitam Pura work was got inspected by the Chief Engineer (Quality Control) on 1st June, 1984 and 5th June, 1984.
With regard to development of land for Zone H-5 (Pt.) Pitam Pura work was inspected on 4th October, 1985 by the Chief Engineer (Quality Control), who submitted his report on 30th April, 1986.
As regards the work of development of land near Wazirpur Village the work was inspected by the Technical Examiner of CTE He submitted his report in February, 1986.
In the first case the chargesheet was issued on 1st/6th November, 1990, in the second case on 26th December, 1990, in the third case on 25th June, 1993 and in the fourth case on 28th July, 1997.
It is also a fact on record that against the first chargesheet Inquiry Officer was appointed on 10th October, 1991, who was changed on 30th November, 1992 and finally changed on 6th July, 1993.
Departmental proceedings have not progressed.
Similarly of the second change, the Inquiry Officer was appointed on 1st March, 1993, who was changed on 3rd May, 1995, again changed on 20th June, 1996.
The departmental proceedings have not progressed at all.
So far as the third case is concerned, the Inquiry Officer was appointed on 14th November, 1994 and till date no progress made.
In the fourth case though the charge sheet was issued on 28th July, 1997 but till date Inquiry Officer has not been appointed.
7.
The above facts go to show that the DDA took the matter casually.
After the inspection of the site in 1983-84 the DDA did not take any step to issue chargesheet or start departmental proceedings.
In fact no cogent or plausible explanation has been offered by the respondent to justify the delay in each of the case.
For example in the first case the inspection by the Technical examiner of CTE took place on 16th November, 1983.
The mater was referred to the Vigilance Department of DDA on 30th November, 1983.
From November, 1983 till November, 1990 no action was taken.
The effort by the respondent to explain the delay by saying that the documents were taken away by one Mr. UP Sharma and hence inspection of the record could not be given appears to be a lame excise.
The respondent has admitted that the record was received by it in April-May, 1986.
Then what prevented it from initiating action in April/May, 1986 itself.
Why it had to wait till November, 1990 for that there is no explanation except saying that Superintending Engineer gave his comments in December, 1987 and Vigilance clearance from CVC was received in July, 1989.
From 1983 to 1986 there is no explanation except to say that the record was with Mr. UP Sharma.
From April-May, 1986 to December, 1987 delay remain unexplained except saying that the Superintending Engineer did not give his comments when the matter was sent to CVC there is no whisper of the same.
Similarly from December, 1987 to July, 1989 why the matter was kept pending with the Vice Chairman and why the Vigilance.
clearance from CVC was not obtained earlier.
Even otherwise CVC clearance was received in July, 1989 and yet the chargesheet was not issued till November, 1990.
For this also there is no explanation.
This shows that the respondent was not vigilant in pursuing the matter.
If the charges were so serious then the respondent ought to have been vigilant enough to initiate departmental proceedings forthwith rather than daily-dallying the action for nearly seven years.
The matter did not end in November, 1990 when chargesheet was issued.
But thereafter also the DDA has been slacking in pursuing the departmental proceedings.
For this delay again there is no justification nor any reasonable explanation offered.
In October, 1991 Inquiry Officer was appointed who was changed thrice thus till date the departmental proceedings have not commenced.
Why three Inquiry Officers had to be changed resulting in further delay for that there is no explanation.
Similarly with regard to the second charge sheet, the quality control inspection took place on 19th August, 1984.
Thereafter there is no explanation as to why the departmental proceedings could not be initiated against the petitioner immediately.
No explanation worth the name has been given.
Accordingl to respondent the Vice Chairman after considering all the details and the investigation ordered in October, 1987 to initiate major penalty proceedings whereas the chargesheet was issued in December, 1990.
This shows the respondent was not interested in initiating action against the petitioner.
Moreover, three Inquiry Officers have changed from March, 1993 to June, 1996 and the departmental proceedings have yet not started.
Similarly for the delay in issuing the third chargesheet no plausible explanation has been given by the respondent.
As per respondent's own showing the Chief Engineer (Quality Control) inspected the work on 4th October, 1985.
He submitted his report on 30th April, 1986.
From 30th April, 1986 till June, 1993 no explanation worth the name has been rendered by the respondent to justify the delay in initiating departmental proceedings.
As per the respondent's own showing the Vice Chairman ordered major penalty proceedings on 26th May, 1988 but from 26th May, 1988 till 25th June, 1993 no action was taken nor any justified cause shown for this inordinate delay.
Similarly regarding the work of Village Wazirpur the same was inspected by the Technical Examiner of CTE in February, 1986 but no action on the basis of that report was taken till July, 1997.
The respondent's explanation that the documents were taken away by the Anti Corruption Department is belied from the reading of chargesheet dated 28th July, 1997.
In the statement of charges enclosed with the chargesheet the respondent admitted that the final report of the Anti Corruption Department was received 2nd December, 1989.
In view of this admission it does not lie in the month of the respondent now to say that since the matter was pending with the Anti Corruption Department, therefore, chargesheet could not be issued earlier.
Having received the final report of the Anti Corruption Department in December, 1989 there was no justification nor any offered as to why the chargesheet was issued in July, 1997.
Moreover, no explanation has been given why departmental proceedings have not progressed in each of the three cases and in the last case no Inquiry Officer has till date been appointed.
8.
From the facts narrated and discussed above, it becomes clear that the Technical Examiner inspected the work of the petitioner in November, 1983 in the first case, in June, 1984 in the second case and April, 1986 in the third case and February, 1986 in the fourth case.
The Vice Chairman ordered action to be taken in 1986 and 1988 respectively.
Still the respondent did not take any action till November, 1990 in the first case, December, 1990 in the second case, June, 1993 in the third case and July, 1997 in the fourth case.
One fails to understand what prompted the DDA in deferring the departmental proceedings against the petitioner for the allegedly defective work executed under the supervision of the petitioner.
If now the respondent/DDA is permitted to initiate or proceed with the enquiry it would tantamount to depriving the petitioner his right of defence.
Because by this time most of the records will not be available.
Once the evidence is lost it would cause great prejudice to the delinquent to establish his case.
There was in fact no need for the respondent/DDA to wait for such a long time particularly when the report of Quality Control and the Vigilance Department report as well as the report of the Chief Engineer (Quality Control) against the sub-standard work executed in the year 1980-83 had been received by the DDA from 1983 to 1986.
If the matter was serious then the departmental proceedings should have been initiated straight way.
This would have given chance to the petitioner to defend himself.
At that time the evidence would have been intact and either party could have used the same to prove his case.
But after a lapse of ten years or more the evidence at site must have changed.
If now the enquiry is allowed to proceed it would cause great prejudice to the petitioner.
It in fact violated his right to defence.
9.
It is a settled principle of law that unexplained delay vitiates the departmental proceedings.
Explanation which the DDA tried to give as given in the counter affidavit is self-contradictory.
In fact explanation given by the DDA in the counter affidavit cannot be called even a plausible explanation.
Supreme Court in the case of State of Madhya Pradesh v. Bani Singh, AIR 1990 SC 1308 observed that in the absence of any explanation or plausible explanation the impugned order cannot be sustained.
As already pointed out above there is no justification for the inordinate delay of 7 to 10 years in each of the cases in initiating departmental proceedings and then not pursuing the same coupled with the fact of changing the Inquiry Officers three times without just cause at its own whims and fancies, this court has no option but to quash the charge sheets bearing No. F.4(62)83-Vig./8000/8043 dated 1/6.11.1990, F.23(43)84/Vig./9540 dated 26.12.1990, F.26(384)86-Vig.
dated 25.6.1993 and F.22(11)96/Vig./9/Vol.III/8744 dated 2871997.
Order accordingly.
The writ is allowed.
The impugned charge sheets are hereby quashed.
Consequently departmental proceedings are also quashed.
10.
Before parting I must mention that the DPC recommendations were kept in seal covers.
I ordered for the opening of the same.
The DPC which met on 28th November, 1990 graded the petitioner "Good" but stated "Unfit for promotion".
Declaring him unfit is contradictory in term, having graded him "Good" there was no justification to say him "unfit" except that the members of the DPC had in mind the vigilance cases against the petitioner.
The DPC after considering the ACRs, the work and other criteria for promotion graded him good.
Having graded him good, there was no justification to declare him unfit for promotion.
It is in this backdrop directions are given to the respondent to reconsider the case of the petitioner for promotion for the period November, 1990 when the petitioner was graded "Good".
If need be a special DPC may be constituted to consider his case for the period November, 1990 within four weeks.
With these observations, the petition stands disposed of.
Writ allowed.
