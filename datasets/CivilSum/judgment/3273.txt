IS Mathur, J - This petition is directed against the order of compulsory retirement, dated 711992 (Annexure-1 to the writ petition).
2.
The petitioner was appointed on the post of Routine Grade Clerk in 1955.
He was confirmed on that post on 921972.
He was promoted on the post of Senior Clerk with effect from 30.6.1982.
He was awarded one special adverse entry for the work relating to the year 1971 (Annexure 2 to the writ petition).
3.
The petitioner claims that he was never awarded any adverse entry besides the one special adverse entry awarded on 2551987 (Annexure 2 to the petition) which related to issue of a wrong Parwana on 20.12.1971.
It is alleged by the petitioner that he has consistently been getting good confidential reports, both before and after the date of the said special adverse entry and that the order of compulsory retirement has been passed without application of mind.
It is also alleged by the petitioner that the order has not been passed in bona fide exercise of power and that it is mala fide and arbitrary.
4.
The opposite parties were directed to produce the relevant record and the same has been produced.
5.
I have heard learned counsel for the petitioner and the learned standing counsel who agree that the petition may be disposed of finally.
6.
In Brij Behari Lal Agarwal v. Hon'ble High Court of Madhya Pradesh and others, AIR 1981 Supreme Court 594 the Supreme Court has observed thus :  "What we would like to add is that when considering the question of compulsory retirement, while it is no doubt desirable to make an overall assessment of the Government servant on record, more than ordinary value should be attached to the confidential reports pertaining to the years immediately preceding such consideration."
In Brij Behari Lal Agarwal's case the petitioner was compulsorily retired by order, dated 28th September, 1979.
The High Court took into account the confidential reports relating to the period prior to 1966.
It was held that such remote entries should not have been taken into consideration.
7.
In Baikutha Nath Das and another v. Chief District Medical Officer, Baripada and another, AIR 1992 Supreme Court 1020 : 1992(2) SCT 92 (SC), the Supreme Court referred to and reiterated its earlier decision in Brij Lal Agarwal's case and observed as follows in paragraph 21 of the report :  "This Court has taken the view in certain cases that while taking a decision to retire a Government servant under Rule 56(j) more importance should be attached to the confidential records of the later years and that much importance should not be attached to the record relating to earlier years or to the early years of service."
The same view has been taken by the Supreme Court in Baldev Raj Chadha v. Union of India and others, AIR 1981 Supreme Court 70 and Amar Kant Chaudhary v. State of Bihar, AIR 1984 Supreme Court 531.
In Baldev Raj Chadha's case the Supreme Court has observed that entries of five years should be seen in considering the question of compulsory retirement while in Brij Behari Lal Agarwal's case it has been observed that entries of ten years may be seen.
8.
Thus the clear legal position is that, in considerating the matter of compulsory retirement, the entries of later years, that is of five years or at best of ten years should be given more importance and the entries of the earlier years should not be given much importance.
9.
In the present case a perusal of the report of the screening committee and the service record of the petitioner indicates that there is only one adverse entry in the nature of the censure entry given on 2551987.
This special adverse entry relates to the issue of a written Parwana in a case under Section 229-B of the UP Zamindari Abolition and Land Reforms Act on 20.12.1971.
Even though this entry has been given in 1987 it relates to the year 1971, which is obviously a period beyond ten years.
It will further appear that the concerned authorities themselves did not take this entry seriously because the petitioner was first suspended but was reinstated in the year 1981 itself.
Thereafter the petitioner was not visited with any adverse action.
Since no major punishment was awarded to the petitioner, it is evident that the matter covered by this entry related to mere negligence and not of deliberate misconduct.
10.
There is no adverse entry in the character roll of the petitioner for the period after 1971.
During the last ten years ie from 1982-83 till the date of order of retirement (711992), the petitioner has obtained satisfactory reports in 1982-83, 1983-84.
In 1984-85 he has been placed in category "good" (UTTAM).
In 1985-86, 1986-87, 1987-88, 1988-89, 1989-90 and 1990-91 the petitioner's work has been appreciated.
In 1985-86 it is mentioned in the confidential entry that the petitioner is experienced and hard working official who has good knowledge of rules and court work and he performed his work with fact.
In 1986-87 also the confidential entry records him to be an experienced, hardworking and factul worker who has taken special interest in the disposal of old cases with honesty.
His work and conduct towards officers was noted to be good, and it is also mentioned that he had good knowledge of the rules.
In 1987-88 again he has been described as hard working, factul and experienced official who has done his work conscientiously.
He is reported to be having good knowledge of rules and his conduct has been good.
In 1988-89 his work and conduct has been found satisfactory.
In 1989-90 again it is noted that he is an experienced, hard working and factul worker who performed the duties of his post successfully.
He has been categorised in 'good' category.
In the 1st entry before the impugned order for the year 1990-91 also the petitioner has been noted to be an experienced, hard working, factful and disciplined official who has full knowledge of the Rules and Regulations.
It is specifically mentioned that he disposes of the audit objection, pension matters, administrative proceedings conscientiously and his work is good and he has been categories as 'good' (UTTAM) official.
It will be seen that except in the year 1982-83, 1983-84 and 1988-89 where the petitioner has been given 'satisfactory' entry the other entries from 1985-86 to 1990-91 are good and in all these entries petitioner's work has been praised and he is noted to be hard working, experienced and factual official.
These entries would indicate that the work of the petitioner had consistently been satisfactory good and there was no question of deterioration in his work warranting compulsory retirement.
11.
Since the entries for all the aforesaid period of ten years have been mostly good and petitioner's work was generally appreciated, even in entries of 1989-90 and 1990-91 immediately prior to the impugned order, and there are no adverse entries, the impugned order must be held to be arbitrary and violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
Accordingly, the order cannot be sustained.
12.
The petition is accordingly allowed.
The order of compulsory retirement, dated 711991 (Annexure I to the writ petition), is quashed.
The petitioner shall be deemed to be continuing in service till date and until the date of his superannuation unless his services are otherwise terminated in accordance with law.
The petitioner shall be paid the entire arrears of salary up-to-date within a period of three months from the date a certified copy of this judgment is produced before the opposite parties.
Petition allowed.
