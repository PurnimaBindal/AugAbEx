Hemant Gupta, J - The challenge in the present writ petition is to the order dated November 18, 2002 (Annexure P-11) whereby the representation of the petitioner for counting continuous ad hoc/temporary service, followed by regular service towards qualifying service for pensionary benefits was rejected.
2.
The petitioner was appointed as Clerk in the Punjab Forest Department on July 28, 1966 through employment exchange.
He continued to work on temporary basis as Clerk/Typist upto January 22, 1969 with notional break of one day.
The details of such ad hoc service are as under :-  Date of office order Period of ad hoc service 30-7-1966 1-8-1966 to 30-9-1966 10-10-1966 Extended upto 31-10-1966 08-11-1966 2-11-1966 to 28-2-1967 04-04-1967 2-3-1967 to 30-4-1967 16-05-1967 Extended upto 30-06-1967 14-09-1967 2-9-1967 to 31-10-1967 13-11-1967 Extended upto 31-1-1968 12-02-1968 2-2-1968 to 1-5-1968 09-05-1968 Extended upto 31-7-1968 02-08-1968 2-8-1968 to 31-10-1968 3.
The services of the petitioner were regularised on the recommendations of the Punjab Subordinate Services Selection Board on January 20, 1969 when she was still working on ad hoc basis.
The petitioner retired on attaining the age of superannuation w.e.f.
April 30, 1999.
However, the services rendered by the petitioner as ad hoc/temporary w.e.f.
July 28, 1966 to January 20, 1969 was not treated as qualifying service for the purposes of calculating the pensionary benefits.
The petitioner has relied upon instructions dated October 26, 1995 which contemplate that the provisions of Rule 3.17(A) as well as Rule 423 of the Punjab Civil Services Rules, Volume II.
The petitioner has also relied upon ad hoc/temporary service on full time basis followed by regular appointment is to be counted for pensionary benefits.
The representation of the petitioner claiming that such ad hoc/temporary service be counted towards qualifying service was rejected by the State Government with one line order without giving any reason.
4.
The State Government has filed reply wherein it was stated that condition (b) of Rule 423 of the Punjab Civil Services Rules, Volume-II (for short "the Rules") is not satisfied by the petitioner inasmuch as service preceding the interruption was less than five years and, therefore, the petitioner is not entitled to treat her ad hoc service towards qualifying service.
5.
It may be stated at the outset that the reliance placed by the respondents is on unamended Rule 423 of the Rules which contemplates that interruption in service either in between two spells of permanent or temporary service or between two spell of temporary service and permanent service may be condoned subject to the conditions mentioned therein.
However, such rule as reproduced by the respondents is in the written statement was amended vide notification dated 26-09-1985.
In fact, the petitioner has reproduced the substituted Rule 423 of the Rules in the writ petition but the State Government chose to rely upon a rule which was substituted.
The claim of the petitioner was rejected on the ground that the petitioner is not eligible for pension as the service preceding the interruption was less than five years on the basis of unamended rule.
6.
The question whether ad hoc service prior to appointment on regular basis is liable to be counted was raised before a Division Bench of this Court in the case titled Hazura Singh v. State of Punjab and others, 2004(1) S.C.T.
695, (Civil Writ Petition No. 19732 of 2001) decided on January 14, 2003.
It has been held therein that the period of ad hoc temporary service prior to regularisation of the service is liable to be counted for the purpose of gratuity and pension if the break in service is for a reason beyond the control of the employee.
7.
Rule 423 of the Rules as amended reads as under:-  "In the absence of a specific indication to the contrary in the service record, an interruption between two spells of service rendered under the State Government shall be treated as automatically condoned, and the pre- interruption service shall be treated as qualifying service for pension purposes, except where the interruption has been caused by resignation, dismissal or removal from service or due to participation in a strike, but the period of interruption itself shall, under no circumstances, be reckoned as qualifying service for pension purposes."
A perusal of the aforesaid rule shows that an interruption between two spells of service rendered under the State Government is to be treated as automatically condoned except where the interruption has been caused by resignation, dismissal or removal from service of due to participation in a strike.
The interruption in the case of the petitioner is neither on account of resignation, dismissal or removal from service or due to participation in a strike.
8.
In view of the above provisions as explained by a Division Bench of this Court in the judgment rendered on January 14, 2003 in Hazura Singh's case (supra), ad hoc service rendered by the petitioner prior to her appointment on regular basis is to be counted as qualifying service for pensionary benefits.
There was no wilful absence from duty by the petitioner prior to her appointment on regular basis.
The breaks in service were unilateral act of the State Government.
9.
Consequently, impugned order dated November, 18, 2002, is set aside.
The State Government is directed to count the days of actual ad hoc service of the petitioner towards her qualifying service for the purposes of pensionary benefits.
Petition allowed.
