MM Aggarwal, J - Petitioners in this petition had prayed for issuance of a direction to respondent Nos. 1 and 2 i.e. State of Haryana to retain them in service and consider their case for regularisation of their services.
2.
The case of the petitioners is that they had worked on daily wages under the respondents-State authority as Beldars (Class-IV) from the year 1974 to 2691995 except notional breaks.
Their work and conduct had been satisfactory during the period of their service.
They had been doing their duty from 800 AM to 500 PM with short lunch break and were being paid wages ranging from Rs. 42/- to Rs. 65/- per day.
They had been making repeated requests and representations to the respondents-State authority for regularisation of their services as Beldars.
The last representations had been made on 211995 Annexures P-2, P-3, P-4 and P-5.
3.
The State Government issued instructions vide letter No. 6-4-90-2 GSI dated 2751993 (Annexure P-1) for regularisation of the services of casual daily wagers.
However, the petitioners' services were not regularised.
They filed Civil Writ Petition No. 8625 of 1995 in which an order was made on 1871995 for consideration of the case of the petitioners and for taking a decision.
However, respondent No. 2 denied the claim of the petitioners vide letter Annexures P-8, P-9, P-10 and P-11 and they were removed from service on 2691995 by non-speaking order in contravention of the instructions issued on 2751993.
It is also pleaded that their removal is also in violation of the statutory provisions of Section 25-F of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.
It is also pleaded that they had neither been given one month's notice nor retrenchment compensation was paid to them.
It is further pleaded that the petitioners have been deprived of their legal rights of getting their services regularised without any reason.
They have been discriminated as services of similarly situated Beldars i.e. respondents No. 3 and 4, who had joined service in 1980, had been regularised by the respondents-State authority.
4.
In the written statement filed on behalf of respondents No. 1 and 2, it has been pleaded that petitioners No. 1 to 3 had worked from the year 1976 whereas petitioner No. 4 had worked from the year 1979.
It is pleaded that they had worked up to 2691995.
It is further pleaded that the daily wagers were engaged on a fixed rate as prescribed by the Government as per rules.
They were not subject to any disciplinary action and no formal appointment letter is required to be issued.
They are appointed as and when there is work.
They were not engaged when there was no work.
It is pleaded that they had no right of regularisation particularly in view of the instructions of the Government issued vide Notification dated 1151994.
With this written statement, the respondents had attached Annexures R-I to R-IV showing the period starting from 1988-89 to 1995-96 month-wise as to in how many days in a particular year these petitioners had been engaged.
5.
It was further stated that respondents No. 3 and 4 fulfilled the conditions for regularisation of their services as per the instructions dated 2751993 and 1151994 and, therefore, their services were regularised.
6.
Th instructions (Annexure P-1) of the Government of Haryana issued vide order dated 2751993 regarding regularisation of work-charged/casual/daily rated employees, reads as under :  "The matter regarding the regularisation of the work- charged/casual/daily rated employees was engaging the attention of the Govt. for some time past.
After careful consideration it has now been decided that the services of the employees of these categories should be regularised as under : Work Charged Employees The work-charged employees who have completed five or more years of continuous service as on 21st March, 1993, and were in service on 31st March, 1993, shall be regularised.
On regularisation, these employees shall be liable for transfer in the State of Haryana on any project/work.
Casual/Daily Rated Employees The casual and daily rated employees who have completed five years service on 31st March, 1993 and were in service on 31st March, 1993, shall be regularised.
On regularisation, they shall be put in the time scale of pay applicable to the lowest Group "D" cadre in the Govt and they would be entitled to all other allowances and benefits available to regular Govt.
Servants of the corresponding grade.
2.
Necessary action regarding regularisation of the services of such employees working in your department should be taken within 15 days in consultation with the Finance Department, if necessary, under intimation to Government along with the number of employees so regularised."
7.
Further clarifying the instructions dated 2751993, the instructions dated 1151994 (Annexure R-5) reads as under :  "(v) That the work and conduct of such employees shall be over all of good category and no disciplinary proceedings are pending against them; and  (vi) that the employees possessed the prescribed qualification for the post at the time of their appointment on adhoc basis.
Daily Rated Employees (i) the Government had issued instructions dated 27th May, 1993, for regularisation of services of all the work-charged, casual and daily wager employees who had completed five years service on 31st March, 1993.
In the reference of these instructions the regularised employees were entitled to the lowest Group D sale of pay and all other allowances and benefits available to regular Government employees.
But it has come to the notice of Government that in some departments wagers are also working on Class III posts such as Clerks, Steno- typists and Drivers etc.
The question whether such daily wagers should be regularised in Group D or Group C Scale has been engaging the attention of Government for some time past and after careful consideration it has now been decided that daily wagers who had completed five years service on Class III posts on 31st March, 1993 and were in service on 31st March, 1993, should be regularised against their respective class III posts provided they fulfil the requisite qualifications and were originally appointed on class III posts and the posts are available.
If the posts are not available they should be not created (sic) from the Finance Department or they should be regularised in Group D scale on compassionate ground like other daily wagers.
2.
Some departments have also sought clarifications about the maximum period of break in five years service.
The Govt. has decided that the daily wages employees who had worked for five years and have worked for a minimum period of 240 days in each year should be regularised but the break in service in any year should not be more than one month at a time.
The seniority of the ad hoc daily wages employees so regularised viz. the class III employees should be determined w.e.f.
31st March, 1993.
The inter se seniority of such ad hoc or daily wages class III employees shall be determined in accordance with the date of their joining the posts on adhoc or daily wages basis.
If the date of joining the post(s) on ad hoc or daily wages by such ad hoc or daily wages employees was the same, then an older employee shall rank senior to an employee younger in age.
If the date of appointment of the direct recruit and the date of regularisation of ad hoc or daily wages employees are the same, the direct recruit shall be senior."
8.
The High Court vide order dated 1871995 in CWP No. 8625 of 1995 had directed the Superintending Engineer, PWD (B&R), Circle-20, Ambala Cantt to decide the representation of petitioners dated 2111995.
Annexures P-2 to P-5 are the representations of the petitioners whereas Annexures P-8 to P-11 are the letters written by Sub-Divisional Engineer, Provincial Sub-Division No. 3, Ambala Cantt.
to the petitioners informing them that they had not completed five years continued service as on 3131993 and that they had not completed 240 days in a year without break and, therefore, their services could not be regularised.
9.
Counsel for the petitioners had relied on a Division Bench decision of this Court reported as Anand Kumar v. Haryana Urban Development Authority etc., 1995(1) SCT 677 (P&H) : 1995(1) Recent Services Judgments 230 and argued that when there is break in service or interruption in service due to action of the employer, it could not be treated as break for the purposes of circulars issued by the Government of Haryana.
It was further held that administrative instructions cannot have retrospective effect and that the petitioners had a right of regularisation in view of the circular dated 2751993 and argued that the instructions dated 18.3.1994 had been struck down vide this judgment and as such the petitioners are entitled to regularisation of their services.
Counsel for the petitioners further relied on a judgment of the Hon'ble Supreme Court reported as State of Haryana v. Piara Singh, 1992(3) SCT 201 (SC) : 1992(3) RSJ 235.
10.
As per instructions dated 2751997 (Annexure P-I) the services of casual and daily rated employees who had completed five years' service as on 3131993 and were in service on 3131993, were to be regularised.
11.
Annexure R-I shows that Ramesh Chand petitioner had worked for the following period :  1035 days in 1988-89 1275 days in 1989-90 1975 days in 1990-91 231 days in 1991-92 165 days in 1992-93 and in the month of March, 1993 he had worked only for nine days.
12.
Annexure R-II shows that petitioner No. 2 Lal Chand had worked for the following period :  1515 days in 1988-89 1315 days in 1989-90 242.5 days in 1990-91 1745 days in 1991-92 1615 days in 1992-93 and in the month of March, 1993 he had worked only for 10 days.
13.
Annexure R-III shows that petitioner No. 3 Parkash had worked for the following period :  05 days in 1988-89 46 days in 1989-90 43 days in 1990-91 38 days in 1991-92 885 days in 1992-93 and in the month of March, 1993 he had worked only for 10 days.
14.
Annexure R-IV shows that petitioner No. 4 Ramji Lal had worked for the following period :  35 days in 1988-89 865 days in 1989-90 216 days in 1990-91 1435 days in 1991-92 154 days in 1992-93 and in the month of March, 1993 he had worked only for 10 days.
15.
The subsequent instructions dated 18.3.1994, (Annexure R-5 with the written statement) should be taken to be clarificatory of the instructions dated 2751993 where clarification has been given that the daily wager employees who had worked for five years and have worked for a minimum period of 240 days in each year should be regularised but the break in service in any year should not be more than one month at a time.
In Anand Kumar's case (supra) letters for regularisation of services had been issued to the petitioners after considering their case and finding them to be eligible for regularisation of services but then those letters were withdrawn in view of the instructions dated 18.3.1994.
The facts of that case are entirely different and instructions dated 18.3.1994 were not quashed whereas only order dated 2761994 withdrawing the order of appointments had been set aside.
That letter dated 2761994 had been issued after about 8-9 month of the date of regularisation of services of those petitioners.
Under these circumstances, the ratio laid down in Anand Kumar's case (supra) is not applicable to the facts of this case.
16.
From the judgment of the Hon'ble Apex Court in Piara Singh's case (supra), it would come out that it is applicable to `workmen' as defined in the Industrial Disputes Act.
The statements (Annexures R-I to R-IV), regarding the number of days the petitioners had worked in each year prior to 1992-93, would show that they were being called as and when required for a very few days in a month.
The instructions dated 2751993 could be applicable to those persons who were in service as on 3131993 and who had completed five years' service on 3131993.
From the statements Annexures R-I to R-IV it cannot at all be said that the petitioners had completed five years' service as on 3131993.
They had just worked for about 9-10 days in full one month of March, 1993 and cannot be said to be in service as on 3131993.
17.
As far as respondents No. 3 and 4 are concerned, it has been pleaded in the written statement filed on behalf of respondents No. 1 and 2 that these respondents (No. 3 and 4) fulfilled the criteria laid down in instructions dated 2751993 and, therefore, their services were regularised.
This was not controverted by the petitioners by filing replication.
Under these circumstances, the action of respondents No. 1 and 2 in not regularising the services of the petitioners cannot be called arbitrary or discriminatory and violative of the instructions issued.
This writ petition being without merit is dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
