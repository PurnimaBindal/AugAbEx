JS Khehar, J - The petitioner was inducted into the service of the respondents as an instructor.
He was promoted to the post of Group Instructor with effect from 111983 by an order dated 1181972.
Onward promotion from the post of Group Instructor is to the post of Principal.
By an order dated 3011981 (Annexure P-6), a person junior to the petitioner, namely, respondent No. 4 was promoted to the post of Principal ignoring the claim of the petitioner.
Through the instant writ petition, the petitioner claims promotion to the post of Principal with effect from the date his junior was promoted as such.
2.
The solitary contention of the learned counsel for the petitioner is that the statutory rules regulating promotion to the post of Principal delineate the bench-mark for promotion as 50% good or above reports.
It is the vehement contention of the learned counsel for the petitioner that on the appointed date when persons junior to the petitioner were promoted to the post of Principal, the petitioner satisfied the aforesaid bench-mark and should have been promoted.
The non-consideration of the claim of the petitioner when persons junior to him were promoted as Principal, is, therefore, according to the learned counsel for the petitioner, an act of absolute arbitrariness at the hands of the respondents.

A perusal of the aforesaid affidavit reveals that as on the cut off date, out of the 10 Annual Confidential Reports, the petitioner was assessed as "average" in 8 and "good" in one and "very good" in one.
In view of the aforesaid status of the Annual Confidential Reports earned by the petitioner, it is the vehement contention of the learned counsel for the respondents, that the petitioner did not satisfy the bench-mark for promotion to the post of Principal when respondent No. 4 was promoted as such on 3011981.
5.
In order to controvert the petitioner's service record depicted in the affidavit dated 1712005, learned counsel for the petitioner vehemently contends, that the petitioner while holding the post of Group Instructor was required to cross the efficiency bar with effect from 151981.
He was, however, prevented from doing so by an order dated 2911982.
Yet again the petitioner was not allowed to cross the efficiency bar with effect from 151981 vide order dated 1841983.
The orders dated 2911982 and 1841983, referred to above, were impugned by the petitioner by filing a civil suit at Narnaul.
The aforesaid civil suit which was filed on 3081983 was dismissed by the trial Court on 19.3.1988.
Dissatisfied with the order passed by the trial Court, the petitioner preferred an appeal.
On 12.2.1990, the District Judge, Narnaul, decreed the suit filed by the petitioner, whereupon, he was allowed to cross the efficiency bar with effect from 151981, by the authorities.
6.
On the basis of the decision rendered by the District Judge in his order dated 12.2.1990, learned counsel for the petitioner vehemently contends that the petitioner should also be allowed promotion to the post of Principal, with effect from 3011981.
The reason for the aforesaid, according to the learned counsel for the petitioner, is that the bench-mark for crossing the efficiency is exactly the same as the bench-mark for promotion from the post of Group Instructor to the post of Principal.
Since the petitioner was held entitled to cross the efficiency bar by the decision rendered by the District Judge, Narnaul, dated 12.2.1990 with effect from 151981, the petitioner should be deemed to have satisfied the bench-mark laid down for promotion to the post of Principal as on 3011981.
In view of the determination rendered by the District Judge, Narnaul, in his order dated 12.2.1990; the service record of the petitioner reflected in the affidavit dated 1712005, which has been relied upon by the respondents to depict the unsuitability of the petitioner is wholly misconceived.
7.
In some substance, there seems to be justification in the contention of the learned counsel for the petitioner, inasmuch as, the petitioner having been found eligible to cross the efficiency bar with effect from 151981 on the basis of the same criteria which is applicable for promotion from the post of Group Instructor to the post of Principal, he should also be entitled to promotion to the post of Principal with effect from 3011981.
Despite the merits the claim raised by the petitioner, it is the vehement contention of the learned counsel for the respondents that it was not now open to the petitioner to impugn the order dated 3011981.
At the time when the instant writ petition was filed on 2711992 even a civil suit could not have been filed by him, in view of the law of limitation.
It is the vehement contention of the learned counsel for the respondents that the period of limitation so as to impugn the order dated 3011981 would expire after the lapse of 3 years therefrom.
9.
I find substance in the instant contention of the learned counsel for the respondents.
It was not open to the petitioner to impugn the action of the respondents in promoting a person junior to the petitioner on 3011981 more than a decade after the aforesaid promotion.
Even a civil suit at that juncture was impermissible under law.
In order to overcome the aforesaid objection, the solitary contention at the hands of the petitioner, is that the petitioner while establishing his claim for release of efficiency bar must be deemed to have established his right for the first time for promotion to the post of Principal.
The aforesaid contention would have been justified if the claim of the petitioner for promotion had arisen on a date subsequent to the date when the claim of the petitioner for crossing the efficiency bar arose.
In such an eventuality, the petitioner could have waited for the outcome of the said determination, and could have validly applied the said determination in his claim for promotion to the post of Principal.
In the instant writ petition, however, the claim of the petitioner for promotion is with effect from 3011981 i.e. a date preceding the date on which the petitioner was to cross his efficiency bar.
The petitioner should have ordinarily challenged the impugned order dated 3011981 (Annexure P-6), before challenging the action of the respondents in denying him the right to cross the efficiency bar with effect from 151981.
It is, therefore, not possible for this Court to reject the objection raised by the learned counsel for the petitioner based on the law of limitation.
10.
In view of the above, it is not necessary for this Court to evaluate the merits of the claim of the petitioner for promotion to the post of Principal, since the claim of the petitioner for promotion was raised after the expiry of the period of limitation.
It would be unfair to the learned counsel for the petitioner if reference is not made to the decision rendered by the Calcutta High Court in Manik Lal Pathak v. State of West Bengal, 1992(1) SCT 284, which was brought to the notice of this Court.
The decision rendered in the aforesaid case is not applicable to the instant case as the issue of limitation was not adjudicated upon therein.
Dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
