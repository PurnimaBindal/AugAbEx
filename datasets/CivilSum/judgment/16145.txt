AP Chowdhri, J - This order will dispose of Civil Writ Petitions 15531 and 15537 of 1992 raising common question of fact and law.
2.
The appropriate government rejected notice of demand made by the workman in these two cases by order dated November 4, 1991.
The Additional Labour Commissioner, however, sent letter dated October 7, 1992, to be petitioner at its Bombay address asking the petitioner to appear before him on October 12, 1992, in connection with the matter whether reference to the Labour Court is to be made or not.
The letter was received on October 12, 1992, at Bombay and the petitioner appeared before the Additional Labour Commissioner on 14th or 15th October, 1992 when the petitioner was informed that decision had already been taken and the same would be conveyed to the petitioner in doe course.
Ultimately, it was learnt that the appropriate government had decided to make a reference and the formal letter issued in this behalf was dated October 23, 1992.
In other words, even though notice was given to the petitioner to be heard before taking a decision whether reference has to be made or not the reference was made without hearing the petitioner; obviously on account of the delay in transit in the delivery of the letter.
3.
After going through the petition and the written statement and hearing learned counsel for both the parties, we are of the view that it would be in the interest of justice if the petitioner is afforded a reasonable opportunity of being heard before taking a decision whether reference is to be made or not in accordance with law.
We accordingly set aside the reference impugned in this case and direct the parties to appear before the Additional Labour Commissioner, Punjab, on August 3, 1993.
The Additional Labour Commissioner shall re-enter the matter against its original number, afford an opportunity of being heard to the parties and decide the matter afresh according to law.
Both the writ petitions are disposed of in these terms.
