V Gopala Gowda, J - The petitioner was working as Headmaster of an educational institution run by Sri Siddartha Education Society.
There was some dispute between the petitioner and the management which culminated in a compromise in R S A No. 427/86 before this Court.
After his retirement on 3061989 the pensionary benefits had been fixed to the petitioner.
While doing so, the 2nd respondent-Commissioner for Public Instruction has passed the impugned order at Annexure-D, dated 24-10-2000 as under :  "Since the petitioner has not worked from 8-9-78 to 2-9-86 (7 years 11 months 24 days) and since he has also given an undertaking before the Hon'ble High Court in RSA No. 1427/86 that he will not claim any backwages for this period and since Rule 244-A of KCSR has no provision to treat non-duty period in excess of 3 years as qualifying service, the petitioner is entitled to count the period from 8-9-78 to 2-9-86 for the purpose of pensionary benefits.
Hence the plea is rejected."
The Government also confirmed the above order by dismissing the appeal filed by the petitioner in Appeal No. 26/2001 by its order at Annexure-E, dated 15-4-2002.
Petitioner is seeking to quash the aforesaid two orders and is seeking a direction to respondents 1 to 3 to grant the pensionary benefits for the period 8-9-1978 to 12-8-1986 with interest at 18% per annum.
2.
In the memo filed in R.S.A.
No. 427/86 before this Court, the petitioner had undertaken as under :  "(3) I will not claim any backwages from 1978 September up to August 12th, 1986, subject to the condition that there must be continuity of service".
It is clear from the above that petitioner has given up only the backwages for the aforementioned period with a condition that there should be continuity of service.
As long as continuity of service is there, he is entitled to pensionary benefit also for the said period.
In the letter at Annexure-B issued to the Deputy Director for Public Instructions from the office of Commissioner for Public Instruction, the annual increments have been accorded to the petitioner.
It is also stated that the period from 8-9-1978 to 2-9-1986 should be treated as service condition period.
Hence, it cannot be said that petitioner was not in service during the said period.
The order at Annexure-D, which is extracted above, is contrary to the undertaking given by the petitioner before this Court and the letter at Annexure-B The view expressed in Annexure-D that there is no provision in Rule 244-A of the Karnataka Civil Services Rules to treat non-duty period in excess of 3 years as qualifying service, is wholly untenable.
In fact the said Rule has no application as it deals with the time passed on all kinds of leave.
When continuity of service is given to the petitioner, the same shall be treated as `qualifying service' for the purpose of granting the pensionary benefits.
Otherwise, the very purpose and object of giving 'continuity of service' without wages would be defeated.
Even the Government also failed to take note of this aspect of the matter while considering the appeal preferred by the petitioner.
In the circumstances, the impugned orders are liable to be quashed.
3.
Accordingly, the writ petition is allowed and the impugned orders are quashed.
The respondents are directed to extend the pensionary benefits to the petitioner for the period 8-9-1978 to 2-9-1986 without any interest, within three months from the date of receipt of a copy of this order, failing which the petitioner is entitled for interest at the rate of 12% on the arrears of pension amount due from the respondents.
Petition allowed.
