Jawahar Lal Gupta, J. (Oral) - The petitioners were working as Electricians in the department of Transport.
The post of Electrician was in the pay scale of Rs. 120-250/-.
The Punjab Government promulgated the rules called the Punjab Civil Services (Revised Scales of Pay) Rules, 1979.
A copy of the extract from the notification is at Annexure P-1 with the writ petition.
The existing scale of pay of the post of Electrician was wrongly shown as Rs. 140-300.
A revised scale of Rs. 450-800 was sanctioned with effect from January 1, 1978.
The petitioners' pay was fixed in the scale of Rs. 460-800 with effect from January 1, 1978.
Certain arrears were also paid.
Subsequently, it was noticed that the actual pay scale of the post of Electrician was Rs. 120-250 and not Rs. 140-300.
The revised pay scale for the post in the scale of Rs. 120-250 was Rs. 400-660 and not Rs. 450-800.
Accordingly, the Directorate vide its letter dated June 16, 1981 ordered that the pay scale may be corrected.
The General Managers of the Punjab Roadways were advised to refix the pay of the Electrician.
It was further directed that ``they should recover the amount of over-payment already made in easy instalments''.
Aggrieved by this order, the petitioners filed the present writ petition on July 21, 1981.
They inter alia contended, that the pay scale sanctioned by statutory rules could not be denied by issuing an executive order.
While the petition was pending, the State Government issued the notification dated October 5, 1981 by which a retrospective amendments was made in the Rules and the pay scale of the Electricians was fixed at Rs. 400-660 with effect from January 1, 1978.
In view of the issue of this notification, the petitioners filed an amended petition.
A copy of the notification has been produced as Annexure P-3 with the amended writ petition.
The petitioners pray that this notification be quashed.
2.
A written statement has been filed on behalf of the respondents.
It has been pointed out that the original notification was wrongly issued on the assumption that the post of Electrician was in the pay scale of Rs. 140-300.
Since this assumption was wrong, the consequential revision was also wrong.
The mistake has been rectified by the impugned notification.
3.
Counsel for the parties have been heard.
4.
Mr. PS Patwalia, learned counsel for the petitioners has submitted that the Pay Commission had recommended the grant of a scale of Rs. 450-800 to the petitioners.
This recommendation had been accepted by the State Government.
This having been done, the respondents are not entitled to retrospectively reduce the pay scale of the Electrician from Rs. 450-800 to Rs. 400-660.
Secondly, the learned counsel has submitted that the payment already made to the petitioners cannot be recovered especially when a period of almost 15 years has already elapsed.
The claim made on behalf of the petitioners has been controverted by Mr. Dhillon, learned counsel for the respondents.
5.
It is not disputed that the petitioners were actually working as Electricians in the pay scale of Rs. 120-250.
Consequently, it is apparent that the 1979 Rules were based on a factually wrong assumption that they were in the scale of Rs. 140-300.
They were, thus, given the revised scale of Rs. 450-800 on a wrong assumption.
Nothing has been produced by the petitioners to show that the Pay Commission which had been constituted by the State Government had actually recommended the grant of a pay scale of Rs. 450-800 to the cadre of Electricians in the Transport Department.
In this situation, it is apparent that the State Government had granted the scale of Rs. 450- 800 to the petitioners on an apparently wrong assumption.
This mistake having come to notice, the State Government has retrospectively amended the rules and placed the posts of Electricians in the scale of Rs. 400-660.
It cannot be said that the rule is ultra vires or that the State Government was not entitled to correct the mistake.
It is well settled that a mistake can always be rectified.
To that extent, the contention raised by the learned counsel cannot be sustained.
6.
In so far as the petitioners' grievance with regard to the order of recovery is concerned, it deserves notice that they had not misrepresented any fact.
They had been granted the pay in strict conformity with the notification issued by the State Government.
Still further, it is the undisputed position that the petitioners are at a very low rank in the service.
If orders for recovery are now implemented, they will be put to considerable hardship.
In this situation, while negating the challenge of the petitioners to the validity of the notification dated October 5, 1981, it is held that the respondents should not be permitted to recover the excess payment which had been made to the petitioners prior to the issue of the notification.
A similar view was taken by their Lordships of the Supreme Court in Sahib Ram v. State of Haryana, 1995(1) SCT 668 SC.
7.
Accordingly, in view of the above, the writ petition is disposed of with a direction that the respondents shall not be entitled to make any recovery from the petitioners for the salary already paid to them prior to the issue of the notification dated October 5, 1981.
Otherwise the pay of the petitioner shall be fixed on the assumption that they were in the scale of Rs. 400-660 with effect from January 1, 1978.
In the circumstances, there will be no order as to costs.
