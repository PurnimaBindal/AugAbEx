Jawahar Lal Gupta, J. (Oral) - The petitioner seeks a two-fold relief.
Firstly, he prays for the issue of a writ in the nature of mandamus directing the respondents to grant him the selection grade of Rs. 1200-1700 w.e.f.
January 1, 1978 and secondly he claims that the respondents be directed to include his post of Organizer (Games and Sports) in the Punjab Education Service Class-II.
He further prays for the grant of consequential reliefs like fixation of seniority and further promotion.
A few facts may be noticed.
2.
The petitioner was appointed as a Clerk on October 28, 1952.
On September 25, 1959 he was promoted as an Assistant.
He was confirmed as such with effect from April 1, 1966.
On June 6, 1975, the State Government sanctioned the creation of "one post of Organiser (Games and Sports) on the scale of Rs.
400-800."
The sanction for this post was granted till February 29, 1976.
Vide order dated June 25, 1975, the petitioner was appointed "as Organiser (Games and Sports) in the scale of Rs. 400-800 on six months basis or till the availability of a candidate selected by the Punjab Public Service Commission ....." The petitioner continued to hold this post.
Ultimately, vide order dated April 21, 1986, a copy of which has been produced as Annexure P-5 with the writ petition, the petitioner's services were regularised.
3.
As already noticed above, the pay scale of the post of Organiser (Games and Sports) was initially fixed at Rs. 400-800.
Subsequently, this pay scale was revised and raised to Rs. 825-1580 with effect from January 1, 1978.
After the receipt of the recommendation of the IIIrd Pay Commission, the pay scale of this post was further raised to Rs. 2,000-3,500.
These scales were actually granted to the petitioner.
However, the petitioner claims that he should have been placed in the scale of Rs. 1200-1700 with effect from January 1, 1978 and Rs. 2400-4,000 with effect from January 1, 1986.
4.
The respondents have filed a written statement controverting the claim made by the petitioner.
It has been inter-alia averred that the petitioner was only a member of the Ministerial Cadre of the Directorate of Education and was governed by the Clerical Service Rules (office of the Director of Public Instructions, Punjab as amended upto 1.11.1968) and that he was not a member of the Class-II Service so as to be entitled to the reliefs claimed by him.
It has been further averred that the petitioner was not entitled to the grant of selection grade and that the case of the officers to whom the grade had been actually granted was different from that of the petitioner.
It has also been pointed out that the petitioner's case regarding the grant of selection grade was referred to the IIIrd Pay Commission, but his claim could not be accepted as the "Punjab Govt. had awarded the Master scale to avoid stagnation w.e.f.
111986."
On these premises, the claim made in this writ petition has been controverted by the respondents.
5.
I have heard Mr. VP Sharma, learned counsel for the petitioner and Mr. Randeep Singh Surjewala for the respondents.
Mr. Sharma has made a two-fold submission.
Firstly, it has been contended that the action of the respondents in not granting the selection grade to the petitioner is discriminatory and violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India inasmuch as two officers, who were holding the posts of Statistical Officer and Technical Officer-cum-Craftsman respectively have been granted the selection grade of Rs. 1200-1700 while it has been denied to the petitioner.
Secondly, it has been argued that a number of ex-cadre posts which were originally in the scale of Rs. 825-1580 have now been placed in the scale of Rs. 2400-4,000 with effect from January 1, 1986.
However, so far as the post of Sports Organiser is concerned, the pay scale of Rs. 2,000-3500 only has been granted.
The petitioner submits that even this is violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India.
Mr. Sharma has further contended that the post in question had continued for a long time and there is no reason to justify its being treated as an ex-cadre post.
He claims that the State Government was bound to include it in the cadre of Punjab Education Service Class-II.
6.
The claim made on behalf of the petitioner has been controverted by Mr. Surjewala, appearing for the respondents.
Besides contending that the action of the respondents is not violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India, he has also submitted that the petition suffers from the vice of laches and should be dismissed on that ground alone.

This notification is taken on record as "Mark A".
A perusal of this notification shows that various posts like those of Film Librarian, Sports Organiser, Statistical Officer etc. were described as Ex-Cadre posts in the pay scale of Rs. 825-1580 which was revised and raised to Rs. 2,000-3500.
It further appears that the post of Technical Lecturer was initially in the pay scale of Rs. 825-1700, which was revised and raised to Rs. 2100-3700.
So far as the Subject Experts Vocation in the office of the Director of Public Instructions (Schools) is concerned, it carried the pay scale of Rs. 825-1580 Rs. 1200-1700, but was fixed in the revised scale of pay of Rs. 2,000-3500.
Similar is the position with regard to the post of Technical Lecturer (Vocation).
The revision of pay scale has been sanctioned by the competent authority on the recommendation of the Pay Commission after the matter had been considered by the appropriate authorities in the Government.
There is no uniform rule that if two posts have been initially placed in an identical scale of pay, the Government is bound to place them in identical scale even at the time of the revision of pay scales.
A number of factors such as qualifications prescribed for the post, the duties attached thereto, the level of responsibility etc. have to be taken into consideration before the pay scale for a particular post is sanctioned.
If on consideration of relevant factors, the competent authority comes to the conclusion that the two posts carry different qualifications or duties and as such should be placed in different scales of pay, it cannot be said that the action suffers from the vice of discrimination.
Mr. Sharma, however, relies on the observations of a learned Single Judge of this Court in Kirpal Jeet v. The State of Punjab and another, 1987(4) SLR 594, to contend that an identical scale had to be provided even at the time of revision.
A perusal of this judgment shows that the learned Single Judge had found as a fact that "in the present case the petitioner is in a much better position.
She possesses better qualifications than those possessed by the Lecturers (Junior Scale), who have been placed in the higher pay scale.
She has also been entrusted, throughout her career, with duties of purely educational nature involving the imparting of instructions at various levels.
The mere fact that she has to work in the office of the Director, Public Instruction, Punjab, instead of the educational institutions, is no ground to deny the same pay scale to her which she would have got had she been required to work in the educational institutions."
It was in view of this factual position that the claim of Kirpal Jeet was upheld.
Such is not even the suggestion in the present case.
It has not been shown that the duties attached to different posts or the qualifications prescribed therefore are identical.
In such a situation, it cannot be said that the action of the respondents is violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India.
9.
As for the grant of selection grade, learned counsel for the petitioner has placed firm reliance on the letter dated March 3, 1980 issued by the State Government, a copy of which has been produced as Annexure P.8 with the writ petition.
A perusal of this letter shows that the Government had decided "to lay down" certain "guidelines for the provision of selection grades in the cadre of the various Services ..." This was a letter addressed to the Heads of the Departments and these guidelines were laid down for their consideration.
This letter does not confer an indefeasible right on any individual to claim the grant of selection grade.
If the respective heads of the Departments on consideration of these guidelines felt that certain posts deserved to be placed in the selection grade, they were competent to do so.
In so far as the ex-cadre posts were concerned, the competent authority decided to grant selection grade for two out of a number of posts.
The grant of selection grade to the incumbents of those posts has not been challenged.
These posts were in all respects separate and different from the one held by the petitioner.
If the appropriate authority has considered it just and fair to sanction the grant of selection grade for the post of Statistical Officer or for the post of Technical Officer-cum-Craftsman, it cannot be said that it was bound to do so even in respect of the other posts.
No rule or law can be said to have been violated by the action of the respondents in not granting a selection grade for the posts of Sports Organiser.
Consequently, even this claim cannot be sustained.
10.
Lastly, it was contended that the post of Organiser (Games and Sports) has continued to exist since June, 1975 and the action of the Government in not including this post in the Service called 'The Punjab Education Service Class-II' is unfair and illegal.
It is the admitted position that the Punjab Education Service was initially constituted by the Rules called 'The Punjab Education Service Class-II Rules, 1934.'
These rules were subsequently repealed when the rules called 'The Punjab Educational Service (School & Inspection) (Class II) Rules, 1976' were promulgated on May 27, 1976.
Even though the rules constituting Class-II Service were promulgated on May 27, 1976, the post of Organiser (Games and Sports) was not included in the Cadre.
Similar is the position with regard to the number of other ex-cadre posts.
It appears that even while issuing the notification dated February 17, 1989, the post in question has been described as that of Sports Organiser.
It has been treated as an Ex-cadre post.
Inclusion of a post within a particular Service is the prerogative of the competent authority.
If it feels that a particular post should not be included in a particular Service, its action cannot be interfered with unless it is found that the action is malafide or violative of a rule or law.
The factual position in the present case is that at least six posts have been treated as Ex-cadre posts.
The one held by the petitioner is in no way different from the other posts.
It has also not been shown that there are any prescribed qualifications for the post of Organiser (Games and Sports) or that its duties are a kin to those which are required to be performed by the members of the Class-II Service.
In this situation, it cannot be said that the action of the respondents is either arbitrary or unfair.
12.
In view of the above, I find no merit in this writ petition.
It is accordingly, dismissed.
In the circumstances of the case, there will be no order as to costs.
Petition dismissed.
