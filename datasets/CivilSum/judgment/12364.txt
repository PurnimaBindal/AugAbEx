J S Khehar, J - The Health Department, Haryana, started the Rural Malaria Scheme, under which the Health Department sprays DDT for a period of four and half months every year.
Accordingly every year, staff is recruited for the period during which DDT spraying is undertaken.
2.
Some writ petitions were filed in this Court alleging that appointments under the Rural Malaria Scheme were being made on a pick and choose basis.
A direction was sought for the preparation of a scheme for recruitment of employees whereby security of service would be assured to the existing employees and appointment on a pick and choose basis would be stopped.
This Court vide its order dated 951990, passed in CWP Nos. 9631 and 9633 of 1988, directed the authorities to prepare a regular scheme for employment of persons for the enforcement of the Rural Malaria Scheme.
In obedience to the direction issued by this Court, the Director General, Health Services, Haryana, formulated a scheme for spray operations in the State of Haryana dated 751991.
A copy of the said scheme has been appended to the writ petition as Annexure P.11.
3.
The petitioner was appointed for the first time as Swasthya Sahayak for spraying DDT under the Rural Malaria Scheme in 1980.
It is admitted by the respondents that the petitioner has been engaged for seasonal spraying during the years 1980, 1983 to 1987 and 1990 to 1992.
4.
On 1661993, the petitioner was issued an offer of appointment to the post of Class IV (SSK) in the scale of Rs. 750-12-870-EB-14-940 with allowances as were admissible to employees for the Haryana Government.
A perusal of the offer of appointment would show that on acceptance of the offer of appointment, the petitioner would be subject to, Government Servants Conduct Rules, 1966, and his conditions of service would be governed by the Punjab Civil Services Rules as applicable to the State of Haryana.
It was also stipulated that the petitioner would remain under probation for a period of 2 years, which could be extended for one more year.
The petitioner accepted the aforesaid offer of appointment and started to draw salary in the scale referred to above.
In terms of the letter of appointment dated 1661993, the petitioner was addressed an office letter dated 27.7.1995 informing him that he had been permitted to cross the period of probation with effect from 17.6.1995.
In other words it was declared that the petitioner had completed successfully the period of probation.
It seems that the scale of the pay of the petitioner as referred to in his letter of appointment came to be revised to Rs. 2550-55-2660-EB-60-3200.
By an office order dated 28.9.1998, the petitioner was permitted to cross the Efficiency Bar in the aforesaid pay scale at the stage of Rs. 2660/-, with effect from 161998, thus raising the basic pay of the petitioner from Rs. 2660/- to Rs. 2720/- with effect from 161998.
Cumulative effect of the order of appointment, clearing the period of his probation, and being permitted to cross the Efficiency Bar, is that the engagement of the petitioner for all intents and purposes was on a regular basis and also that his performance was satisfactory.
5.
The grievance of the petitioner arose on issuance of an office order dated 20111998, on the basis of a representation filed by one Sukhbir Singh, whereby the petitioner's services have been terminated by giving him one month's notice.
The aforesaid order dated 20111998 has been impugned in the instant writ petition.
6.
The first and foremost submission of the learned counsel for the petitioner is that the impugned order draws various conclusions against the petitioner, as for instance that the offer of appointment dated 1661993 was not made in conformity with the terms of the scheme dated 751991.
It is also concluded by the authorities that the representationist Sukhbir Singh is senior to the petitioner.
All the said conclusions had been drawn behind the back of the petitioner without seeking his comments.
7.
It was next contended that the authorities made the aforesaid conclusions while deciding a representation made by Sukhbir Singh, Swasthya Sahayak.
The grievance of the petitioner is that the representation made by the said Sukhbir Singh was never made available to the petitioner for his comments.
He was accordingly not afforded any opportunity to assert his own superior position before recording the conclusion that he was junior to Sukhbir Singh.
8.
On the assertions made above, the petitioner impugns the order dated 20111998 vide which his services have been terminated asserting that the action of the authorities is in violation of the rules of natural justice.
The case set up is, that no order adversely affecting the interests of the petitioner can be passed without following the rules of natural justice.
9.
It is undoubtedly true that the petitioner is liable to be adversely affected by the order dated 20111998.
It is also true that before passing the aforesaid order, the petitioner had not been afforded an opportunity of hearing.
It is not disputed that the impugned order dated 20111998 came to be passed as a consequence of a representation made by Sukhbir Singh.
The representation made by the said Sukhbir Singh was admittedly not supplied to the petitioner nor was he given an opportunity to refute his claim.
10.
In fact, the claim of the petitioner is that he had been inducted into service consequent upon his name having been sponsored by the District Employment Exchange, Sonepat, on the basis of letter dated 1661993.
His employment had no nexus with the scheme dated 751991.
According to the learned counsel for the petitioner, after being appointed in 1993, as stated above, the petitioner was permitted to cross the period of probation with effect from 17.6.1995, he was thereafter permitted to cross the efficiency-bar with effect from 161998.
On the basis of these facts the learned counsel for the petitioner asserted that Sukhbir Singh who had been appointed under the scheme could not be placed in the same seniority list as the petitioner who was a regular employee.
Besides the aforesaid claims an alternative plea was also raised and it was asserted that the petitioner was appointed as Swasthya Sahayak under the Rural Malaria Scheme for the first time in 1980, whereas the said Sukhbir Singh was appointed in the same capacity many years later.
Consequently, the petitioner must be deemed to be senior to the said Sukhbir Singh even under the scheme dated 751991.
It is not necessary for this Court to examine the niceties of the claim of the petitioner at this stage.
It is by now a well settled proposition of law that an employee is entitled to a reasonable opportunity before any adverse action is taken against him.
It is clear from the narration of facts and events recorded above that the petitioner was neither communicated the representation made by Sukhbir Singh nor he was afforded any opportunity before conclusions were finally drawn against him on the basis of the said representation.
In the aforesaid circumstances, the impugned order dated 20111998 must be deemed to have been passed arbitrarily and in violation of the rules of natural justice.
The impugned order is, therefore, clearly violative of Article 14 of the Consttitution of India, the same is accordingly liable to be quashed.
11.
For the reasons recorded above, this writ petition is allowed and the impugned order dated 20111998 is quashed.
12.
In the order dated 16121998, passed by this Court on the first date of hearing, the authorities were directed to maintain status quo regarding the petitioner's service.
If the petitioner had continued to render service in spite of the impugned order, he would naturally be entitled to his pay and allowances for the aforesaid period.
If not, since the impugned order dated 20111998 has been quashed, he would be entitled to be continued in service along with all monetary benefits.
The same shall be released to the petitioner within one month of the date of the receipt of the instant order in the office of the respondents.
It is, however, clarified that the authorities shall be competent to take such action in furtherance of the representation made by Sukhbir Singh as they consider appropriate, but before doing so, they shall comply with the rules of natural justice.
Needless to mention that the authorities shall duly take into consideration the assertions made by the petitioner by passing a speaking order.
Ordered Accordingly.
