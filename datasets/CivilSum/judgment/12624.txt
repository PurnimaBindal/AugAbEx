JV Gupta, CJ - The petitioner had appeared in 10 + 1 Examination held by the Punjab School Education Board, SAS Nagar (Mohali), in March, 1989, but failed.
She appeared in the supplementary examination held in September, 1989 and was declared pass.
It is alleged by the petitioner that she submitted her forms to the respondent-Board for appearing in 10 + 2 Examination which was to be held in March, 1990.
It is further averred by the petitioner that in the said examination from she had clearly disclosed that she had passed 10 + 1 examination in September, 1989.
Since she had appeared in the said examination as a private candidate, her form had been duly attested by the Principal, Gandhi Higher Secondary School, Ahmedgarh.
It is the case of the petitioner that her examination form was accepted and she was issued Roll No. Card sometimes in March, 1990.
She appeared in 10 + 2 examination under Roll No. 878364.
2.
The petitioner goes on to say in the writ petition that the respondent-Board had declared the result of 10 + 2 examination on 5th July, 1990 and against her Roll No. it was mentioned cancelled" meaning thereby that the candidature of the petitioner had been cancelled.
The petitioner was verbally told that since there was less than one year gap between her passing 10+1 examination and appearing in 10+2 examination, she was ineligible to appear in 10+2 examination according to the Regulations of the Board.
3.
The petitioner approached this court by way of present writ petition for the issuance of a director to the respondent to declare her result of 10+2 examination.
4.
Written statement has been filed on behalf of respondent, in which it has been stated that in fact the supplementary examination which was held in September, 1989 was only for Re-appear/Compartment candidates in some papers and not for those who had failed in the entire 10+1 examination earlier.
However, a special concession was given to the failed candidates to appear in September, 1989 but it was made clear in the press that those candidates who had passed the 10 + 1 examination in September, 1989, would not be eligible for appearing in 10 + 2 examination in March, 1990.
Further, it has been stated that the Regulations of the Board also prode that there should be a gap of one year between the passing of 10 + 1 examination and appearing for 10 + 2 examination.
5.
We have heard the learned counsel for the parties at length.
There is no denying the fact that the petitioner had mentioned in her examination form for appearing in 10 + 2 examination that she had passed 10 + 1 examination in September, 1989.
She had not concealed any fact from the Board.
The Board had permitted the petitioner to take her examination.
Now the Board cannot turn round that the petitioner being ineligible to sit in the 10 + 2 examination her candidature could be cancelled.
The Board is estopped by its own conduct.
A similar matter had come up before the Apex Court in Shri Krishan v. The Kurukshetra University, Kurukshetra, A.I.R. 1976 S.C. 376 and recently in Santan Gauda v. Berhampur University and others, AIR 1990 Supreme Court, 1075.
That being so the petitioner's candidature could not be cancelled.
6.
In all fairness to the learned counsel for the respondent-Board, we may also deal with the point that according to regulations, the petitioner was ineligible to appeared the gap between the passing of the 10+1 examination and appearing in 10 + 2 examination was less than one year.
This very point arose before a Division Bench of this Court in LPA No. 426 of 1987 (The Punjab School Education Board v. Harinder Kaur), decided on 18th December, 1989, wherein it was held that under the above mentioned circumstances, a candidate who had passed in the supplementary examination would be eligible to appear in the next annual examination in the higher class.
The learned counsel further submitted that this view of the Letters Patent Bench ran counter to the view expressed by another Division Bench in Anjana Gulati 'minor' v. The Punjab School Education Board Civil Writ Petition 4863 of 1987, decided on 15th March, 1988.
Suffice it to say that Anjana Gulati's case (supra) was duly considered in Harinder Kaur's case (supra).
In view of what has been stated above, the writ petition is allowed and the respondent-Board is directed to declare forthwith the result of the petitioner of 10 + 2 examination which she took in March, 1990.
No costs.
Petition allowed.
