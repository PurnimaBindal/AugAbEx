The impugned order supersedes all the previous orders and refixes the dates of promotion and confirmation as under:-  S No.  Name with place Date of promotion as Sr. Assistant Date of confirmation as Sr. Asst.
1.
Abdul Rashid Sofi Accounts Section 1351987 1351989.
2.
Hamidullah Bhat Accounts Section -do- -do- Thus these officials by virtue of impugned order are deemed to have been promoted as Senior Assistants on 1351987 and have been confirmed in the scale of Junior Assistants from 1351989.
2.
Prior to this the respondent-University vide Order No. (Promotion-SA)Adm/88 dated Jan 30, 1988, the two petitioners had been promoted as Senior Assistants in the scale of Rs. 1200-2040.
The order dated 30.1.1988 was further superseded by two orders, one passed on 251988 vide No. F (Promotion- SA)Adm/88 and the other passed on 24121988 vide No. (Promotion-SA)Adm/88.
Vide these orders the petitioners were treated to have been promoted as Senior Assistants with effect from 651987.
The impugned order superseded all the earlier orders of promotion and treats the petitioners to have been promoted with effect from 1351987 instead of 651987.
The petitioners figured higher in the order dated 27.4.1984 appointing them as Junior Assistants.
Respondents No. 5 & 6 have been promoted as Senior Assistants on 651987.
Thus they have stolen a march over the petitioners and have become senior to the petitioners.
3.
The petitioners are aggrieved of the order dated 2821996 on the grounds that the petitioners were not heard before changing their seniority position and the petitioners were, with an oblique motive, not allowed to join their posts on 151987 when they returned after having availed of their study leave.
They joined back their duties on 151987 after remaining on leave w.e.f.
29.10.1986.
The petitioners, according to the official respondents, have reported for duty on 451987, however, till 1351987 they were not allowed to join on the plea that their leave cases had not been settled.
They were allowed to join on 1351987 and treated on extraordinary leave for a period of 196 days.
4.
An order, promoting 34 Junior Assistants, including respondents No. 45 & 6, as Senior Assistants was passed on 651987.
The petitioners' case is that on 651987 they had admittedly presented themselves for duty and they were due for promotion alongwith others with effect from 651987 only.
5.
I have heard the learned counsel for the parties.
6.
Mr. Q Lone appearing for the respondents-University has vehemently argued that petitioners were wrongly promoted with effect from 651987 and the seniority of the petitioners was wrongly fixed.
Once this error was to be rectified it could be done without adhering to the rules of natural justice.
7.
As far as denial to the petitioners' right of promotion is concerned they have relied upon Rule 40 of the Civil Service Regulations.
According to them, the petitioners were on long leave and they had no claim to a substantive promotion.
Rule 40, for the sake of convenience may be reproduced as under:-  "An Officer on long leave has no claim to substantive promotion.
Such promotion may, however, be given, but the promotion has effect on the date Officer returns to duty."
Even if we go strictly by rule 40 supra, it contemplates that an Officer an long leave cannot claim substantive promotion as of right, it, however, leaves a discretion with the employer to promote such officer but such promotion shall have effect from the date on which the officer returns to duty.
8.
In the instant case the petitioners had, admittedly, returned to resume their duties with effect from 451987.
They claim to have joined back for duty with effect from 151987, however, what is admitted from the record produced before me by Mr. Lone is that they had physically presented themselves on 451987 with an application to allow them to join with effect from 151987.
9.
Be that as it may, it goes without saying that on 451987 the petitioners had duly reported for resuming their duties.
It is pertinent to mention here that their service was intact.
The petitioners had not been removed from service although their joining reports had been received on 451987.
That makes all he difference.
Had they been allowed to join even on 451987, the embargo created by Rule 40 supra would not at all apply.
10.
The case of the petitioners has not been considered with positively.
The delay in allowing the petitioners to join their posts was caused by the respondent-University in not allowing the petitioners to join on the date they presented themselves or they returned for duty.
11.
We thus revert to the second limb of the argument.
12.
A question arises whether or not the violation of rules of natural justice in the present case vitiates the order impugned.
13.
Mr. Lone has referred to a judgment passed by a Bench of this Court in case titled Kulbhushan Singh v. Chief Engineer and Ors, 1977 All India Service Law Journal 231(J&K).
This Court in this judgment relied upon a Full Bench judgment of Patna High Court reported in AIR 1970 Patna 397 and a Supreme Court judgment reported in 1975(2) SCC 818.
In the Patna High Court judgment it was held that the Government in exercise of its executive powers could revoke its decision if it believed the decision to be incorrect.
Their Lordships held that a Public servant who might have taken undue benefit under a wrong order has no right of being heard if the Government proposes to undo the wrong.
It was also held that fixing of seniority and its alteration are purely administrative matters and rules of natural justice have no application.
In the Supreme Court case also the same view was upheld and it was maintained that disturbing seniority on the basis of record available and without hearing the parties likely to be effected was a administrative function and was justified without adhering to rules of natural justice.
In the same judgment this Court referred to a case titled Bachittar Singh v. State of Punjab and Ors, reported in 1973(1) SLR page 863 also.
14.
I have considered this judgment and also those relied upon by his Lordships Dr. AS Anand, J (as his Lordships then was).
15.
In Patna case there was preponderous evidence that the earlier decision taken by the Government was incorrect.
Same was the case before the Punjab High Court.
Their Lordships of the Supreme Court in 1975(2) SCC 818, were equally sure that the record available before the Government was sufficient enough to justify that the earlier seniority had been fixed on some mistaken information.
16.
A question in this matter rises as to what was the record before the University on basis of which they could come to a conclusion that the earlier seniority was erroneously fixed.
There is absolutely no record.
On the other hand the respondent-University is basing its claim on a fiction of law tried to be created under rule 40 supra.
The University decision does not at all answer as to why were the petitioners not allowed to join their duties with effect from 451987 or 151987 whatsoever.
Respondents did not allow the petitioners to resume their duties from 451987.
Had they done so, the petitioners would be promoted with effect from 651987.
Doubts arise with respect to the design behind such omission.
It will be worthwhile to mention that the zerox copy of the record produced before me shows that at all levels respondents have been obsessed and haunted by an opinion tendered by some Financial Advisor who happens to be one of the top bureacrats of the State.
17.
Universities are the power houses of our beacon-light.
They are held by courts in highest of esteem.
They are expected to uphold law.
They should not be hijacked by whims of a State Officer unconnected with law.
I have found on record some cogent legal opinions also but as against the opinion of the Financial Advisor the legal advice seems to have been given a total gobye.
It is here that the error was committed.
18.
The respondent-University has proceeded on a fictitious assumption that an error had been committed while fixing the earlier seniority of the petitioners.
After all, what was the basis for them to come to the conclusion that such fixation was erroneous ?
Nobody has tried to apply his mind upon this aspect of the case.
19.
I find that the order impugned did not rectify any error but on the other hand itself committed an error by dislodging the petitioners from an advantageous position they had gained earlier, clandestinely deprived them of their promotion and jeopardised one of their legal rights.
20.
That being so, I quash the order impugned.
The result being that the earlier orders of promotion passed in favour of the petitioners will be intact.
The petitioners will be treated to have been promoted from 651987.
The inter se seniority position of petitioners and respondents No. 5 & 6 will be relegated back.
21.
No order as to costs.
Order accordingly
