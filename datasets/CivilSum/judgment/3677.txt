Adarsh Kumar Goel, J - The respondent filed a suit for declaration to the effect that order of discharge from service dated 351990 w.e.f.
30.4.1990 was void as no opportunity of hearing was given to him.
2.
The appellants contested the suit stating that the respondent-plaintiff was appointed as a temporary constable on 30111989 and he was discharged from service under Rule 1221 of the Punjab Police Rules, 1934 (for short, the Rules) on an assessment that he was not likely to prove an efficient police officer.
Such an order could be passed within three years of enrollment without any opportunity.
As regards the contention that the order was arbitrary, it was observed that the plaintiff had been convicted on 29.9.1981 for an offence under the provisions of the Punjab Excise Act, 1914, though he was released on probation.
3.
The trial Court decreed the suit.
It was observed that on the basis of date of birth given in plaintiff's application for appointment, the accused was only ten years, nine months and eight days of age on the date of the alleged offence dated 2311980 and his trial was without jurisdiction, the same having proceeded before an ordinary magistrate.
It may be noted that under Section 27 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, (for short; the Code), any person below the age of 16 could be tried only by a court of Chief Judicial Magistrate or by a court specially empowered under the Children Act, 1960.
4.
On appeal, the low er appellate court affirmed the decree.
5.
Learned counsel for the State submitted that following substantial questions of law arise for consideration :-  (i) Whether order of discharge simpliciter, under Rule 1221 of the Rules, of a temporary constable could be held to be punitive ?
(ii) Whether the employer could take into account the fact that the employee did not disclose his conviction prior to appointment and also the factum of such conviction, even if such an employee was granted benefit of probation ?
6.
Learned counsel for the respondent, on the other hand, submitted that question of law was whether an order of discharge could be passed when an employee is recruited after consideration of his antecedents and that too without any show-cause notice.
7.
After hearing learned counsel for the parties, I am of the view that a substantial question of law does arise for consideration as to whether order of discharge simpliciter passed against a temporary constable under Rule 1221 of the Rules could be held to be arbitrary or stigmatic on admitted facts ?
8.
Learned counsel for the appellants relied upon a decision of this court in Punjab State and another v. Anil Kumar, 2002(3) SCT 339 (P&H) : 2002(5) SLR 634 and a decision of the Apex Court in State of Punjab and others v. Bhagwan Singh, 2002(1) SCT 253 (SC) : JT 2001 (Suppl.
1) SC 7.
Both the said decisions are on interpretation of Rule 1221 of the Rules.
Reliance was also placed on decision of the Apex Court in Krishnadevaraya Education Trust and another v. LA Balakrishna, 2001(1) SCT 784 (SC) : AIR 2001 SC 625; Pavanendra Narayan Verma v. Sanjay Gandhi PGI of Medical Sciences and another, 2002(2) SCT 358 (SC) : JT 2001(9) SC 420 and HF Sangati v. R.G.
High Court of Karnataka and others, 2001(2) SCT 333 (SC) : AIR 2001 SC 1148.
It was also pointed out that Ex. D.1, judgment of conviction of the plaintiff and the file dealing with conviction of the plaintiff, which is also attached with the record of the trial Court, show that the plaintiff gave his age as 24 years in his statement under Section 313 of the Code, while in his application for recruitment, he has given his date of birth as 1969 and assumption made by the courts below that conviction of the plaintiff was void as his age was below 16 years at the time of conviction, was not correct.
9.
Learned counsel for the respondent-plaintiff relied upon decision of the Apex Court in Prithipal Singh v. State of Punjab and others, 2001(1) SCT 459 (SC) : 2000(3) RSJ 736, which is also on the interpretation of Rule 1221 apart from decision of this court in Punjab State and another v. Resham Singh, 1999(2) SCT 636 (P&H) : 1999(2) RSJ 455; Punjab State and others v. Paramjit Singh, 2002(3) RSJ 114 and Ex-Constable Jhirmal Singh v. State of Punjab and others, 2001(4) SCT 506 (P&H) : 2001(3) RSJ 463, which are also on the interpretation of the said rule.
It was also submitted that antecedents of the plaintiff having been checked up at the time of recruitment, his conviction for a minor offence could not have been taken into account for holding that he would not be an efficient police officer and in any case, opportunity of hearing ought to have been given before passing of the order.
10.
Question to be considered is whether discharge simpliciter of a temporary employee can be held to be stigmatic so as to require an opportunity of hearing being given and whether order of discharge can be held to be arbitrary.
11.
This question has been repeatedly considered.
In Pavanendra Narayan Verma (supra), after reviewing the earlier case-law, the Apex Court observed "courts continue to struggle with semantically indistinguishable concepts like `motive' and `foundation' and terminations founded on a probationer's misconduct have been held to be illegal while terminations motivated by the probationer's misconduct have been upheld.
The decisions are legion and it is an impossible task to find a clear path through the jungle of precedents."
It was then concluded that to hold an order to be punitive, three factors should be present :- (a) a full scale formal enquiry (b) into allegations involving moral turpitude or misconduct and (c) which culminated in a finding of guilt.
If all the three factors are present, the termination has to be held to be punitive.
Conversely if any one of the three factors is missing, the termination has to be upheld.
The said decision has been followed in a recent decision in Mathew P Thomas v. Kerala State Civil Supply Corporation Ltd and others, 2003(1) SCT 1051 (SC) : 2003(3) SCC 263.
On the interpretation of the same rule i.e. Rule 1221 of the Punjab Police Rules, 1934, the matter came up before the Apex Court in Bhagwan Singh (supra).
Allowing the appeal of the State of Punjab holding an order of termination to be punitive, it was held that when a probationer is discharged during probation and for that purpose, assessment of his work is done, the same cannot amount to stigma.
Same is the view expressed in Anil Kumar, Krishnandevarya Education Trust and HF Sangati (supra).
12.
In the decisions relied upon on behalf of the plaintiff, order of discharge was not order simpliciter.
In the present case, the respondent was appointed as a temporary employee, which is clear from order of appointment in service file of the plaintiff Ex. P-1, which is as under :-  "Candidate Sukhdev Singh s/o Kuldeep Singh r/o Talwandi Goraya, PS DB Nanak, Tehsil Batala, District Police Batala, Caste Jat who had been declared medically fit by Civil Surgeon Gurdaspur and his character and antecedents have also been found good is hereby enrolled as temporary constable w.e.f.
30111989 AN in the pay scale of Rs. 950-25-1200/30-1560/40-1800/- with initial start Rs. 1,000/- P.M. against the vacancy of Constable Increased Strength under action Plan No. _____.
He is allotted constabulary No. 2776/BR of this district."
Order of discharge is as under :- "Recruit constable Sukhdev Singh No. 2776 of this district is quite unsuitable to be retained in police force and is not likely to become a good police officer.
He is, therefore, discharged from the police force w.e.f.
30.4.1990 A.N.
under Rule 1221 of Punjab Police Rule Volume-II.
Issue orders in OB and all concerned to note for necessary action.
Sd/- SK Goel, IPS Sr Supdt.
of Police, Batala."
Attested Sd/- Head Clerk.
13.
In view of the decisions referred to above, it cannot be held that an order of discharge simpliciter is stigmatic and would be void for want of giving an opportunity to the respondent.
It is also not possible to hold that the impugned order was passed arbitrarily taking into account irrelevant order of conviction.
The respondent had given his age as 24 years during his trial.
In any case, the fact that he had been convicted, could not be held to be an irrelevant fact.
For the above reasons, this appeal is allowed.
Decree of the courts below is set aside and the suit of the plaintiff-respondent is dismissed.
There will be no order as to costs.
Appeal allowed.
