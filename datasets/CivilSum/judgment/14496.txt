RS Mongia, J - Though only Misc.
Application No. 12963 of 1994 was fixed for hearing today, but with the consent of the parties, I have heard them on merits of the writ petition as I propose to dispose of the writ petition as well.
2.
The undisputed facts are that the petitioner was working as a Draughtsman Gr.I since November 15, 1966 in the General Reserve Engineer Force (in short 'GREF').
The petitioner is claiming that by virtue of the decision of the Ministry of Finance, dated September 11, 1987, copy at Annexure P-8, read with the Ministry of Defence Order dated August 4, 1988, copy at Annexure P8/1, the petitioner's unrevised pay scale of Rs. 205-280 prior to January 1, 1973 be revised to Rs. 425-700 with effect from January 1, 1973.
It may be observed here that in the concluding paragraph of the order of the Ministry of Defence dated September 11, 1987, it was decided as under :-  "The question of extension of benefit of the judgment of the Supreme Court to the similarly placed Draughtsmen in other Ministries/Departments of India has been under consideration of the Government.
President is now pleased to decide that the D/man as were in the pay scale of Rs. 205-280 prior to 111973 and were placed in the scale of Rs. 330-560 based on the recommendations of the Third Pay Commission as referred to in para 1 above, may be given the scale of Rs. 425-700 notionally from 1173 and actually from 1987."
3.
Later on, vide order dated August 4, 1988 of Ministry of Defence, it was noticed that on a petition filed by a section of employees of the office of Chief Engineer, Jalandhar Zone, Jalandhar Cantt., the Central Administrative Tribunal, Chandigarh Bench in its judgment delivered on May 14, 1987 in the case of Shri Surinder Singh and others versus The Union of India directed to fix the pay of the applicants in that case in the pay scale of Rs. 425-700 from the date of their promotion as Draughtsman Grade-I The question that arose was to extend the benefit of above said judgment to other Draughtsmen, similarly placed.
It was decided that the said pay scale of Rs. 425-700 be given from the date of promotion or from January 1, 1973, whichever was later.
4.
The first relief that is being claimed by the petitioner is that in accordance with the abovesaid decisions, the petitioner is entitled to the pay scale of Rs. 425-700 w.e.f.
January 1, 1973 as he stood already promoted to Draughtsman Grade-I much prior to that date, as noticed above.
Learned counsel for the respondents is not disputing that the petitioner is entitled to the abovesaid relief.
5.
Some CPWD employees raised a dispute regarding revision of their pay scale which was referred to the Arbitration Board of CPWD and an award dated November 10, 1980 followed, by which it was held that the CPWD Draughtsmen Grade-I, who were earlier in the pay scale of Rs. 425-700, are entitled to the pay scale of Rs. 550-750.
6.
Some similarly situated employees, like the petitioner, raised the demand that their pay scale should also be revised to Rs. 550-750 and ultimately Draughtsmen Grade-I of MES took the matter before the Central Administrative Tribunal, Calcutta Bench which decided that Draughtsmen Grade-I of MES were entitled to the pay scale of Rs. 550-750 w.e.f.
November 1, 1983 or from the date of their promotion to Draughtsman Grade-I, whichever was later.
7.
Learned counsel for the petitioner further contended that some other employees of the MES to whom the benefit of the earlier judgment of the Central Administrative Tribunal, Calcutta, was not given also approached the Central Administrative Tribunal, Delhi and by following the Judgment of the Calcutta Bench, the Delhi Bench also awarded the same relief to those MES employees as had been awarded to the Draughtsmen Grade-I of the MES by the Calcutta Bench of the Central Administrative Tribunal.
He further contends that, in fact, the Government of India in the Ministry of Finance had also decided on October 19, 1994, copy at Annexure R3 to reply to the CM Application, that the benefit which had been given to the Draughtsmen Grades I, II and III working in the CPWD on the basis of the Award of Board of Arbitration, was extended irrespective of their recruitment qualification in all the Government of India offices.
For ready reference, the order of the Ministry of Finance, Government of India, dated October 19, 1994 may be quoted, which reads as under :-  "The undersigned is directed to refer to this Department's O.M.
No. F.5(59)- E.III/82 dated 13384 on the subject mentioned above and to say that a committee of the National Counsel (JCM) was set up to consider the request of the staff side that the following scales of pay, allowed to the Draughtsmen Grade I, II and III working in CPWD on the basis of the Award of Board of Arbitration may be extended to Draughtsmen Grade-I, II and III, irrespective of their recruitment qualification, in all Government of India offices :-  Original Scale Revised scale on the basis of the Award.
Draughtsmen Grade I 525-700 550-750.
Draughtsmen Grade II 330-560 425-700.
Draughtsmen Grade III 260-430 330-560.
2.
The President is now pleased to decide that the Draughtsmen Grade I, II and III in offices/departments of the Government of India other than in CPWD may also be placed in the scales of pay mentioned above subject to the following :-  (a) Minimum period of service for placement from the post carrying scale of Rs. 975-1540 to Rs. 1200-2040 (pre-revised Rs. 260-430 of Rs. 330-560).
7 years.
(b) Minimum period of service for placement from the post carrying scale of Rs. 1200-2040 to Rs. 1400-2300 (pre-revised Rs. 330-560 to Rs. 425-700).
5 years.
(c) Minimum period of service for placement from the post carrying scale of Rs. 1400-2300 to Rs. 1600-2660 (pre-revised Rs. 425-700 to Rs. 550-750).
4 years.
3.
Once the Draughtsmen are placed in the regular scales, further promotions would be made against available vacancies in higher grade and in accordance with the normal eligibility criteria laid down in the recruitment rules.
4.
The benefit of this revision of scales of pay would be given with effect from 13582 notionally and actually from 1.11.1983."
8.
Learned counsel for the petitioner contended that in spite of the decision having been taken by the Ministry of Finance, the above benefit is not being extended to the petitioner.
However, learned counsel for the respondents, says that the implementation of the above order on the Defence side, like the Ministry under which the petitioner is working, has been kept in abeyance and would be implemented after getting some information.
This case has been adjourned from time to time to enable the learned counsel for the respondents to apprise this Court as to what decision has been taken by the Government in the matter.
Learned counsel for the respondents says that the matter is still under consideration.
9.
Learned counsel for the petitioner submitted that there is no reason as to why the decision already taken on October 19, 1994 be not implemented in the case of the petitioner and be kept in abeyance as the Ministry of Defence in obedience to the orders of the Central Administrative Tribunal, Calcutta Bench had already implemented that decision in case of Draughtsmen of the MES.
Not only that, similarly situated persons in the CPWD on the basis of the Award of the Board of Arbitration have also been given the benefit of the revision of pay scale and it was on the basis of that award that the Government of India in the Ministry of Finance had taken the decision on October 19, 1994.
10.
Having taken the decision, as referred to above, I see no reason why it should not be implemented, even though it may cause some financial implication.
The non-implementation thereof would result in discrimination against the petitioner.
11.
For the foregoing reasons, I allow this writ petition and direct the respondents to fix the pay of the petitioner in the pay scale of Rs. 425-700 with effect from 111973 in accordance with the decision of the Government of India dated 1191987 (Annexure P.8) read with Govt. of India decision dated 481988 (Annexure P8/1).
Further, with effect from November 1, 1983, the pay be fixed in accordance with the decision dated October 19, 1994, referred to above.
Let these directions be carried out within two months of the receipt of a copy of this order either from this Court or a certified copy thereof from the petitioner.
After the calculations are made, as observed above, the arrears be released to the heirs of the petitioner (as the petitioner died during the pendency of the writ petition on May 10, 1995) within a period of six weeks thereafter.
No costs.
Petition allowed.
