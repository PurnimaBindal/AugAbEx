Sat Pal, J - This petition has been preferred against the order dated 3rd October, 1997, passed by Additional District Judge (II) Kurukshetra.
By this order, the learned Additional District Judge has modified the earlier order of stay dated 14th August, 1997, and has directed the Regional Engineering College, Kurukshetra (appellant herein) to accommodate Jagar Singh and Joginder Singh on two seats.
2.
Briefly stated, the facts of the case are that Jagar Singh, Joginder Singh Sandhu and Dharam Pal filed a suit for declaration to the effect that they were entitled to be admitted to BE (Mechanical Engineering) course for the year 1994-1995 in the Regional Engineering College, Kurukshetra and defendants Nos. 2 to 14 were not entitled to be admitted in the said course with consequential relief for mandatory injunction directing the defendant College to admit the plaintiffs to BE (Mechanical Engineering) course.
Vide judgment dated 31st March, 1997, the learned Additional Civil Judge (Senior Division) Kurukshetra decreed the suit of the plaintiffs and held that the admission of defendant Nos. 2 and 6 to the course of BE (Mechanical Engineering) was liable to be cancelled and that the plaintiffs Nos. 1 and 2 (Jagar Singh and Joginder Singh Sandhu) being next in the merit were entitled for the admission to the said course.
By this judgment, defendant college was directed to cancel the admission of defendants Nos. 2 and 6 and grant admission to plaintiffs Nos. 1 and 2 in the year 1997-1998.
The defendant college was further directed to keep two seats out of the seats meant for sponsored-in-service candidates reserved for plaintiffs Nos. 1 and 2.
3.
The defendant/petitioner-College filed appeal before Additional District Judge, Kurukshetra against the judgment dated 31st March, 1997 passed by the learned trial Court.
The plea taken by the petitioner/college before the learned lower appellate Court was that the learned trial Court could not order reservation of two seats for the plaintiffs Nos. 1 and 2 for the session 1997-1998 in lieu of the seats to which the plaintiffs were entitled during the academic year 1994-1995.
It may be relevant to note here that vide ex parte order dated 14th August, 1997, the learned Additional District Judge Kurukshetra ordered the stay of the operation of the judgment and decree dated 31.3.1997 passed by the learned trial Court pending the decision in the appeal.
After hearing the learned counsel for the parties, the aforesaid order dated 1481997 was, however, modified and the interim stay granted on 1481997 was vacated vide the impugned order dated 3rd September, 1997.
4.
Mr. Virk, the learned counsel appearing on behalf of the petitioner submitted that operation of the judgment and decree dated 31.3.1997 passed by the learned trial Court was stayed by the learned lower appellate Court on 1481997 and the learned lower appellate Court was wrong to vacate the interim stay granted on 1481997 vide impugned order dated 3101997 particularly when the appeal is still pending before the learned lower appellate Court.
He submitted that in terms of the law laid down by the Supreme Court as well as by this court, it was not appropriate for the learned lower appellate Court to direct the petitioner/college to admit the respondents by way of interim orders.
In support of his submission, the learned counsel placed reliance on two judgments of the Supreme Court in Home Secretary, UT Chandigarh v. Darshjeet Singh Grewal, JT 1993(4) SC 387, State of UP v. KM Ramona Perhar, JT 1994(5) SC 632 and a judgment of this Court in Punjabi University, Patiala v. Lal Jeet Singh, 1996(2) PLR 450.
5.
The learned counsel further submitted that respondent Jagar Singh who had applied afresh for the year 1997-1998, has already been admitted to the BE (Mechanical Engineering) Course in September, 1998 and as such no relief is being sought with regard to his admission.
The learned counsel further submittted that so far as respondent Joginder Singh Sandhu is concerned, he cannot be given admission at this stage.
He submitted that course started in September, 1998 and the first semester of the course has been completed, examination has also been held and the second semester had already started.
He submitted that even all the seats for the year 1997-1998 already stood filled up and there is no vacant seat for granting admission to the respondent Joginder Singh Sandhu for the year 1997-1998.
In support of this submission, he placed reliance on a judgment of the Supreme Court in State of Punjab v. Renuka Singla, 1994(3) RSJ 589 : 1994(1) SCT.
636 and a Division Bench of this Court in Priya Kapoor v. State of Punjab, 1995(4) RSJ 58 : 1995(4) SCT.
846.
6.
Mr. Balhara, the learned counsel appearing on behalf of the respondents submitted that the suit of the respondents Jagar Singh and Joginder Singh Sandhu had already been decreed by the learned trial Court and as such the petitioner/college was required to keep two seats out of the seats meant for sponsored-in-service candidates for the year 1997-1998 in terms of the judgment and decree of the learned trial Court.
He, therefore, contended that since the respondent/college itself was at fault by not keeping the two seats in terms of the judgment and decree passed by the learned trial Court, the petitioner/college should be given now direction to admit respondent Joginder Singh Sandhu also even by creating additional seat.
In support of his submission, the learned counsel placed reliance on two judgments of this Court in Randhir Singh v. Chandigarh Administration, 1992(8) SLR 113 and Ranbir Singh v. Punjab University, 1992(2) SLR 397.
7.
I have given my thoughtful consideration to the submissions made by the learned counsel for the parties and have perused the records.
From the facts on record, it is clear that the petitioner/college was directed to grant admission to respondents Jagar Singh and Joginder Singh Sandhu to BE (Mechanical Engineering) course for the year 1997-1998 vide judgment and decree dated 31.3.1997 passed by the learned trial Court.
By the said judgment, the petitioner/college was further directed to keep two seats out of the seats meant for sponsored-in-service candidates reserved for the said respondents.
Since respondent Jagar Singh has already been admitted to the course on the basis of his fresh application for admission, the relief granted to him by the learned lower appellate court is no more relevant for the purpose of deciding this revision petition.
8.
From the facts on record, it is also clear that the operation of the judgment and decree dated 31.3.1997 passed by the learned trial Court was stayed by the learned Lower appellate Court vide order dated 1481997 and the said order dated 1481997 was, however, vacated by the learned lower appellate Court vide impugned order dated 3101997.
The operation of the impugned order dated 3101997 was, however, stayed by this Court on 13101997 in the present revision petition.
The impugned order dated 3101997 is again an interim order passed by the learned lower appellate Court as the appeal filed by the petitioner/college is still pending before the learned lower appellate Court.
As held by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in the case of Home Secretary UT Chandigarh (supra) it is not appropriate for the Courts to direct the college to admit a person by way of interim orders and such orders should be made at the final stage.
In view of the law laid down by the apex Court in the said case, I am of the opinion that the impugned order dateed 3101997 passed by the learned lower appellate Court cannot be legally sustained.
9.
As stated herein above, the course for the year 1997-1998 started in September 1997 and the first semester of the course has already been completed and even the second semester has already started.
The learned counsel for the petitioner/college has also submitted that there is no vacant seat in the course for the year 1997-1998 for granting admission to the respondent Joginder Singh Sandhu.
admittedly BE (Mechanical Engineering) course is a prefessional course and for such a course, it is not appropriate to create an additional seat particularly when the first semester of the course has already been completed and the second semester has started.
In this connnection it will be relevant to reproduce the following observation of the apex Court in the case of Renuka Singla (supra):  "It cannot be disputed that technical education, including medical education, requires infrastructure to cope with the requirement of giving proper education to the students, who are admitted.
Taking into consideration the infrastructure, equipment, staff, the limit of the number of admissions is fixed either by the Medical Council of India or Dental Council of India.
The High Court cannot disturb that balance between the capacity of the institution and number of admission, on "compassionate ground."
The High Court should be conscious of the fact that in this process they are affecting the education of the students who have already been admitted, against the fixed seats, after a very tough competitive examination.
According to us, there does not appear to be any justification on the part of the High Court, in the present case, to direct admission of respondent No. 1 on "compassionate ground" and to issue a fiat to create an additional seat which amounts to a direction to violate Section 10-A and Section 10-B(3) of the Dentists Act referred to above."
10.
In the case of Priya Kapoor (supra) a Division Bench of this Court held that the petitioner could not be permitted to join the course of BDS (which is again a professional course) after the expiry of more than six months as on date.
11.
For the reasons recorded hereinabove, the petition is allowed and the impugned order dated 3101997 passed by the learned lower appellate court is set aside.
Keeping in view the facts and circumstances of the case, I however direct the learned lower appellate Court to dispose of the appeal expeditiously preferably within three months from the date of receipt of this order.
12.
The parties are, however, left to bear their own costs.
Petition allowed.
