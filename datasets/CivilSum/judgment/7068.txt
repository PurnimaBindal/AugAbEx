MM Kumar, J - This petition filed under Article 226 of the Constitution prays for issuance of a writ in the nature of mandamus directing the respondents to continue the disability pension consisting of both the service element and disability element w.e.f.
781952 onward for 30% disability along with all other consequential relief with interest at the rate of 18% pa.  2.
Few facts may first be noticed.
The petitioner was enrolled in the Indian Army on 17101941.
While in service, the petitioner was wounded in the active J&K Operation in 1948 as a bullet hit him on 11.7.1948 and he was admitted in the Military Hospital.
He suffered the injury described as "LEFT FOREARM EFFECT OF".
On 2371949, the petitioner was invalidated out of the military service after rendering 7 years and 9 months service from 17101941 to 2371949.
The disability of the petitioner was assessed at 30% permanent for life by the Medical Board, which was attributable and aggravated to military service.
He was granted disability pension consisting of service element and disability element from 2471949 to 681952.
Thereafter the petitioner was again brought before the Resurvey Medical Board where his disability percentage was assessed to be static at 30% but the CCDD(P) accepted the disability to be less than 20%.
Thereafter his disability pension was stopped.
The Record Office of the respondents vide letter dated 5111999 (P-2) admitted that the petitioner was wounded in J&K Operation in 1948 with 30% disability.
The petitioner was given ex gratia grant of Rs. one lac in the year 2000.
It is claimed that the petitioner made repeated representations with regard to substantial increase in percentage of disability and he was brought before the Resurvey Medical Board on 592001 in Military Hospital, Jalandhar Cantt., where he remained admitted from 3182001 to 792001 and his injury was declared as permanent in nature.
On 372001, the respondent intimated the petitioner that his percentage of disability was assessed at less than 20% on 1091954 by the Resurvey Medical Board and therefore he is not entitled to the disability pension.
The petitioner thereafter made representation dated 1292001, served Notice of demand dated 152002 and a reminder dated 1282001 for grant of disability pension consisting of both the elements.
The respondents on 292002 replied to the legal notice and intimated that the CDA(P) in consultation with the Medical Advisor (Pension) had assessed the disability to be less than 20% for life and hence, the petitioner is not eligible and entitled to the disability pension at all.
On 1192002, the Record Office of the respondents addressed a letter to the Chief CDA(P) intimating that the service element is admissible to the petitioner, which was granted to him vide PC No. 139 II, dated 2571949 and was required to be paid to the petitioner for whole of his life.
Thereafter, the petitioner again represented to the respondents on 1692002 and 23192002, however, the respondents have failed to grant disability pension consisting of both the elements to the petitioner w.e.f.
781952 and onwards.
3.
This petition was admitted on 17.2.2002.
However, even after lapse of more than three years, no written statement has been filed by the respondents controverting the assertions made in the petition.
4.
At the hearing, learned counsel for the petitioner confined the claim of the petitioner only for the grant of service element of pension w.e.f.
781952 and onward for life.
5.
Mr. BS Sehgal, learned counsel for the petitioner has argued that in accordance with Regulation 186(1)(2) of the Pension Regulations for Army, 1961 (Part I and II) the petitioner is entitled to payment of service element of pension which was rightly paid from 2471949 to 681952.
Learned counsel has submitted that after the petitioner has been re-assessed by the Re-survey Medical Board and his disability has been reduced to less than 20 per cent then the disability element of pension may be liable to be discontinued as per the provisions of Regulation 173 but under the garb of discontinuation of disability element of pension, the respondents have even discontinued the service element of pension after 781952 which is admissible to the petitioner under Regulation 186(1)(2) of the Regulations for life.
Learned counsel has placed reliance on a Division Bench judgment of this Court in the case of Amarjit Singh v. Union of India and others, CWP No. 12311 of 1996, decided on 2721997 and a Single Bench judgment of this Court in the case of Nek Singh v. Union of India and others, CWP No. 1717 of 1998, decided on 1072000 which has been upheld by this Court in the case of Union of India v. Nek Singh, LPA No. 42 of 2001, decided on 2792001.
6.
On the basis of the aforementioned judgments, Mr. Sehgal, learned counsel for the petitioner, has argued that if the disability element of pension is not to be granted on account of resurvey medical report reducing the disability to less than 20 per cent, it may result into discontinuation of disability element of pension under Regulation 173 but under the garb of discontinuation of disability element of pension, the right of the petitioner to claim service element of pension cannot be defeated.
In that regard, learned counsel has made a reference to Regulation 183 which postulates two elements of disability pension namely service element and disability element.
He has also referred to Regulation 173 read with Regulation 186 and argued that once a person is re-assessed at below 20 per cent then he may cease to avail disability element of pension from such a date but he will continue to draw service element of disability pension for life.
7.
Learned counsel for the petitioner has emphasised that once it is established that the disability of the petitioner is attributable and aggravated to the military service, it cannot be, by any stretch of imagination held that the petitioner is not entitled for grant of service element of disability pension.
He has further asserted that once the petitioner was granted service element of disability pension from 2471949 to 681952, the same cannot be discontinued thereafter on account of the fact that disability of the petitioner was assessed less than 20% or by asserting that the petitioner is lacking 10 years of qualifying service.
Learned counsel has mainly placed reliance on a Division Bench judgment of this Court in the case of Amarjit Singh (supra) wherein while interpreting Rules 173 and 186(1) of the Pension Regulations for the Army, 1961, under similar circumstances the Division Bench allowed the writ petition while granting service element of pension till death of the ex-serviceman.
8.
Learned counsel for the respondents has not been able to point out any distinguishing factor in Amarjit Singh's case (supra) so as to make it inapplicable to the facts of the present case.
However, learned counsel has insisted that this petition is hopelessly barred by limitation as the same has been filed after more than 40 years.
According to the learned counsel, disability element of pension has been claimed w.e.f.
781952.
9.
Mr. Sehgal, learned counsel for the petitioner has submitted that in such matters the question of delay would not be material and it cannot be used to defeat the rights of the petitioner.
In that regard, he has placed reliance on a Division Bench judgment of this Court in the case of Sardara Singh v. Union of India, 1992(6) SLR 683 wherein the petition of an army personnel for granting him disability pension was allowed by ignoring the delay of 40 years.
He has then referred to a judgment of the Supreme Court in the case of SR Bharnala v. Union of India, AIR 1997 SC 27 : 1996(4) S.C.T.
573 and argued that bar of limitation cannot be raised by the respondents.
10.
After hearing learned counsel for the parties, perusing the record and judgments cited by the learned counsel for the petitioner, I am of the considered opinion that the instant petition deserves to be allowed.
I find that the controversy in this case is squarely covered by the Division Bench judgment of this Court in the case of Amarjit Singh (supra).
The observations of the Division Bench in Amarjit Singh's case (supra) read as under :-  "Rule 173 of the rules states that unless otherwise specifically provided, disability pension consisting of service element and disability element may be granted to an individual who is invalidated out of service on account of a disability and it is assessed at 20% or over.
Rule 186(1) states that in (an ?)
individual who is invalided out of service with a disability when assessed at below 20% shall be entitled to service element only.
In 1986 when petitioner's disability was assessed below 20% he ceased to have any right to get disability element of pension but is entitled to service element only.
As stated earlier, that disability element of pension was Rs. 25/- and the service element of pension paid to him was Rs. 13/- and 5 annas.
As per the present orders issued by Central Government & minimum service pension payable to an ex-military personal is Rs. 375/-.
Since the service element of pension is to continue till death of the ex-serviceman we are of the view that petitioner should be paid the minimum service element of the pension, namely Rs. 375/- from the date on which he was found disentitled to get the disability element of pension.
In this view respondents are to disburse pension (Service element) to the petitioner at the rate of Rs. 375/- from 531986.
Arrears due till date must be quantified and disbursed within three months from the date of receipt of a copy of this judgment.
In case the arrears are not disbursed within the said period of three months, the entire arrears will carry interest at the rate of 12% from the date of expiry of three months till the date of payment.
Respondents should regularly pay pension to the petitioner at the rate of Rs. 375/- every month without any break."
11.
It is also appropriate to mention here that against the aforementioned judgment the respondents filed a Special Leave Petition bearing SLP(C) No. 7400 of 1997 before Hon'ble the Supreme Court, which was dismissed on 2291997, upholding the judgment of the Division Bench of this Court.
I also find merit in the argument raised by learned counsel for the petitioner that once the service element for the period from 2471949 to 681952 has been granted to the petitioner the same cannot be denied to him for the remaining period by asserting that he lacks 10 years of qualifying service.
12.
The argument of the learned State counsel raising the bar of limitation does not require any detailed consideration in view of the judgment of Hon'ble Supreme Court in the case of SR Bharnala v. Union of India, AIR 1997 SC 27 debarring the respondent-State to plead the bar of limitation after committing default.
In other words, the respondents cannot be permitted to take advantage of their own wrong.
It has further been held by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in the case of SK Mastan Bee v. General Manager, South Central Railway and another, 2003(1) SCT 367 (SC) : 2003(1) SCC 183 that where the petitioner is illiterate person with meagre resources then despite delay the writ petition for arrears of family pension would be maintainable.
In the present case, the petitioner is Sepoy who is not literate.
Therefore, I respectfully follow the views of the Hon'ble Supreme Court in the cases of SR Bharnala and SK Mastan Bee's case (supra) and over-rule the objection with regard to delay.
It is thus evident that this petition deserves to be allowed.
13.
The petitioner is entitled to be paid service element of pension as per the rates fixed by the Central Government from time to time, from the date it was discontinued till life.
Therefore, respondent is directed to pay the petitioner service element of pension from 781952 and onward for life.
The respondents shall calculate the due arrears of the petitioner till date and disburse the same to him within three months from the date of receipt of a certified copy of this judgment.
In case the arrears are not disbursed within the said period of three months, the entire arrears will carry interest at the rate of 12% from the date of expiry of three months till the date of payment.
The respondents are also directed to regularly pay service element of disability pension to the petitioner every month without any break for whole of his life.
The writ petition is allowed in the above terms with no orders as to costs.
Petition allowed.
