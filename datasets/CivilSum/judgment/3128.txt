H S Bedi, J. (Oral) - This petition shall dispose of CWP Nos. 17184 of 1996 and 1332 of 1995; the facts are being taken from the former.
2.
The petitioner, Raj Pal Singh, joined the Haryana Police as an Assistant Sub Inspector on 15th July, 1974, after having been selected by the Subordinate Services Selection Board.
He was promoted as Inspector in January, 1986.
The petitioner was thereafter directly appointed as Deputy Superintendent of Police w.e.f.
8th February, 1988, as a special case, on account of his outstanding performance as a Police Officer and the selection was duly approved by the Haryana Public Service Commission.
He was confirmed as a DSP on 8th February, 1990 and was granted the senior time scale w.e.f.
8th February, 1993.
It is the petitioner's case that as of today he is at the 7th position in seniority of D.S.Ps.
in the State of Haryana as per the gradation list circulated on 1st October, 1995, and, as such, was in the zone of consideration for induction into the Indian Police Service.
Respondent Nos. 20 and 22 Rajbir Singh and Baljit Singh had joined service as Assistant Sub-Inspectors in Hisar Range.
They filed a representation before the Deputy Inspector General of Police, Hisar Range, seeking confirmation as ASIs.
from an earlier date and this Officer vide his order dated 25th February, 1991 (Annexure P-22) ordered their notional date of confirmation as Sub-Inspectors w.e.f.
10th November, 1977, on the premise that one Sajjan Singh, a Sub- Inspector in Gurgaon Range, and junior to these two officers, had been confirmed from that date.
Consequent to the order Annexure P-22 these two respondents were brought on promotion list `G' w.e.f.
28th October, 1987 and given a deemed date of promotion as D.S.Ps on provisional basis w.e.f.
16th November, 1987.
The State Government thereafter issued order dated 15th March, 1994 (Annexure P.23) wherein it was directed that the inter se seniority of respondents No. 20 and 22 as D.S.Ps.
would be below Sat Pal and above the petitioner Raj Pal Singh.
Several other D.S.Ps.
taking benefit of the order Annexure P-22 also got the dates of confirmation as Sub-Inspectors ante dated to 10th November, 1977.
It appears that the State Government in the meanwhile concluded that the order Annexure P-22 was erroneous and a show cause notice Annexure P-24 dated 28th June, 1995 was issued to respondent Nos. 20 and 22 and the other D.S.Ps.
as to why the benefit given to them should not be withdrawn.
In this notice it was spelt out that as on 10th November, 1977 no posts of Sub-Inspectors were available against which respondent Nos. 20, 22 and the others could have been confirmed and that as the seniority of the Sub-Inspectors was to be maintained range-wise, their confirmation in a different range i.e. in Gurgaon Range and giving them ante dated promotion on the ground that one Sajjan Singh who Sub Inspector in Gurgaon Range and was junior to them had been confirmed, was wholly unjustified.
While the matter was still under consideration, Jaswant Singh Lamba and some of the other respondents in the petition filed C.W.P.
No. 4272 of 1995 claiming that as they were senior to respondent Nos. 20 and 22, they were entitled to confirmation from an earlier date as well.
The matter came up for hearing before a Division Bench of this Court on 23rd November, 1995 and the petition was disposed of with the following observations :-  "After hearing the learned counsel for the parties at length, we direct the Secretary, Home Department, Haryana Government, Civil Secretariat Chandigarh to treat this writ petition as a representation on behalf of the petitioners qua their grievances and decide the same after hearing the petitioners and other affected persons by passing a speaking order within four months from the receipt of copy of this order.
Copy of the order be supplied Dasti to the counsel for the parties on usual costs.
The writ petition stands disposed of."

The D.I.G.
in consequence passed the order Annexure P-25 dated 20.4.1996.

11th February, 1980 and were accordingly promoted as Inspectors on 5th April, 1980 and as a consequence of these orders, these respondents would rank senior to the petitioner as D.S.Ps.

18th August, 1978, and as Inspectors w.e.f.
5th April, 1980 to the private respondents and that this order was wholly unjustified for the reason that on 18th August, 1978 there was no vacant post of Sub-Inspectors in the range concerned against which they could have been confirmed.

4.

5.

18th August, 1978 and in the resultant vacancies some of the private respondents herein could be deemed to have been confirmed w.e.f.
18th August, 1978.
The learned counsel accordingly contended that on the day in question i.e. 18th August, 1978, vacancies did exist against which the private respondents had been confirmed and, as such, the order of the Director General of Police was fully justified.

8.

No. 4277 of 1995 (Annexure P.29) the specific stand taken was that the confirmation of respondent Nos. 20 and 22 wef.
10th November, 1977, was un-sustainable as there was no permanent vacancy available on that day against which they could be confirmed.
9.
As against this Mr. J S Khehar while conceding that as on 10111977, posts were admittedly not available has, however, referred to Annexures R-8/2 and R-8/3 to contend that as on 1881978, 89 and seven posts, respectively, had become available against which the respondents could have been confirmed and as such the orders impugned were absolutely correct on law and facts.
10.
I have considered the arguments of the learned counsel for the parties and gone through the records of the case.
It is the conceded position that on 10111977 no post was available against which the private respondents could have been confirmed.
This is evident not only from the contentions that have been raised before this Court but even from the facts stated in the show cause notice Annexure P-24, the written statement filed in C.W.P.
No. 4272 of 1995 (Annexure P.29) and in the replies filed by the private respondents in the present proceedings as well.
The question arises as to whether additional posts had been created or had become available subsequently vide Annexures R- 8/2 and R-8/3.

14.
The Director General of Police vide this order confirmed seven ex- servicemen without the availability of substantive posts, as according to him, this was permissible and in the resultant vacancies ordered the confirmation of six other persons w.e.f.
1881978.
The underlined portions of the Annexure makes this apparent.
It is significant that in Hisar Range even this little camouflage was not resorted to and the D.G.P.
ordered the confirmation of nine persons w.e.f.
1881978 by deemingly transferring them from Ambala to Hisar Range against the substantive posts available in Hisar Range.
Mr. Patwalia has also challenged this procedure by arguing that a transfer of Sub-Inspectors from one range to another and the deeming exercise were wholly unauthorised as well and in this connection has placed reliance on Rule 121 of the Punjab Police Rules, 1934 (as applicable to Haryana) to contend that Sub-Inspector/Inspectors were to be borne on range rolls and their promotions were also to be made range-wise as envisaged under Rule 1310 and that it was the D.I.G.
of the range who could under Rule 1415 in limited circumstances arrange the exchange of Sub-Inspectors of the same range by mutual agreement.
According to the learned counsel, the direction given to the DIG Ambala Range in Annexure R-8/3 was wholly without authority of law.
15.
As against this Mr. Khehar has also placed reliance on Rule 134 which stipulates that officiating promotions to the rank of Inspectors would ordinarily be made by the DIG of the range but if the flow of promotion was un-evenly distributed amongst the ranges, the DGP could make suitable transfers of Sub-Inspectors on the promotion list from one range to another and, as such, the order Annexure R-8/3 was fully authorised by the Rules.
16.
I have considered this aspect as well and find that the petitioner's case has merit.
It will be evident from a reading of the Rules referred to above that the seniority of the N.G.Os.
i.e. up to the rank Sub-Inspector is maintained range-wise.
Under Rule 1415 a limited power has been given to the DIGs to make a transfer from one range to another in the case of Sub- Inspectors by mutual agreement between them and in no other case.
The direction issued by the DGP, Haryana in Annexure R-8/3 that some of the Sub Inspectors would be deemed to be notionally allocated to district Hisar was, to my mind, wholly unjustified.
The reliance of Mr. Khehar on Rule 134 to justify the action of the DGP is also un-acceptable for the reason that it is nobody's case that there was any un-even flow of Sub Inspectors with regard to promotion within the ranges.
17.
It has also been argued by Mr. Patwalia that while disposing of C.W.P.
No. 4272 of 1995, this Court in its order dated 23rd November, 1995, had directed that the Home Secretary would pass appropriate and speaking orders after hearing the petitioners and others likely to be affected and as the petitioners were persons who were seriously affected, they were entitled to a hearing.
It has accordingly been highlighted that though the Home Secretary had taken no action, yet the DGP had made the orders dated 31101996 (Annexure P-26) and 25.10.1996 (Annexure R-8/3) to the serious prejudice of the petitioners and that too without hearing them.
18.
As against this, the learned counsel for the respondents has pointed out that the dispute in CWP No. 4272 of 1995 was inter se the petitioners and the respondents therein with regard to their confirmation in the rank of Sub- Inspectors and the petitioners who were directly recruited as DSPs were, therefore, not affected by the orders aforesaid and as the State Government had taken no decision after the order of this Court dated 23rd November, 1995, their seniority as DSPs had not been changed so far.
The argument is that it would be appropriate if the matter was left in the hands of the Home Secretary for decision.
19.
After hearing the learned counsel for the parties on this aspect as well, I find that the stand of the petitioner's counsel has merit.
To my mind the dispute inter se the petitioners and the respondents in this writ petition would have a vital bearing on the fate of the petitioners in the future and in relation to their induction into Indian Police Service.
If the private respondents herein were given ante-dated confirmation as has been so done by the official respondents w.e.f.
1881978 it would have a direct bearing on the seniority of the petitioners who would be rendered junior to the respondents.
It is, therefore, evident that a chain reaction would ensue and the rippling effect would, as a consequence, unsettle the petitioners' seniority.
I am, therefore, of the opinion that the petitioners herein were persons who would be deeply affected with regard to the dispute between the parties in CWP No. 4272 of 1995.
There is yet another reason which dictates that it would be appropriate that this Court should decide the matter herein and now.
The contents of Annexure R-8/3 dated 25.10.1996 may be looked at once again.
The last two lines read as under :-  "Order may be issued accordingly and a copy of the order be sent in this office by tomorrow."
20.
This order Annexure R-8/3 has already been held to be without justification but the petitioners' counsel has referred to certain additional factors which give an indication of the anxiety on the part of the higher echelons of the department to help the private respondents, as the DIG had passed the consequential orders on 4.11.1996 whereas the DGP without waiting for any report from the DIG concerned, made the order Annexure P-26 on 31101996.
Moreover, as the State Government has taken a categorical stand that the private respondents were entitled to confirmation w.e.f.
18th October, 1978, it would be inappropriate to accept the respondent's contention and refer the matter to the Home Secretary, for what would be a foregone conclusion.
21.
There is yet another reason as to why interference is called for the present writ petition which flows from the discussion just above.
It will be seen that the private respondents had been confirmed in the years 1980 and 1983.
The first order in favour of respondents No. 20 and 22 giving them confirmation w.e.f.
10th November, 1977 as Sub Inspectors was made on 25th February, 1991, and the consequential order giving them promotion as DSPs.
w.e.f.
16th November, 1987 (Annexure P-23) was made on 15th March, 1994.
The other respondents got their ante dated confirmations as Sub-Inspectors subsequent to these dates.
It is the case of the private respondents that they had been repeatedly representing to the authorities seeking confirmation from earlier dates in the rank of Sub-Inspectors over all these years, but the fact remains that the entire matter was raked up only on account of the order passed by the DIG Haryana, Hisar Range, (Annexure P-22) dated 2221991.
It is, therefore, evident that the private respondents had not cared to agitate the matter seriously and had accepted their dates of confirmation in the rank of Sub-Inspectors for almost a decade.
Mr Patwalia has, therefore, urged that the action of the respondents in upsetting the seniority after so many years had taken away a right that had vested in the petitioners as by upsetting the dates of confirmations of the private respondents, their seniority inter se would be disturbed to their great prejudice.
He has pointed out that this position was unacceptable in the light of Rabindra Nath Bose and others v. Union of India and others, AIR 1970 SC 470 in which it had been observed as under :-  "Each person ought to be entitled to sit back and consider that his appointment and promotion effected a long time ago would not be set aside after a lapse of a number of years.
It will be neither just nor equitable to deprive persons who have been promoted many years ago, of the rights that have accrued to them regarding their rank and seniority by purporting to conduct a review of promotions after the lapse of many years."
In KR Mudgal and others v. EP Singh and others, 1986(4) SCC 531 the following observations were made :-  "A government servant who is appointed to any post ordinarily should at least after a period of 3 or 4 years of his appointment be allowed to attend to the duties attached to his post peacefully and without any sense of insecurity."
22.
Likewise, reliance has also been placed on another decision of the Supreme Court in Malcon Lawrence Cecil D'Souza v Union of India, AIR 1975 SC 1269.
The ratio of the various judgments cited by Mr. Patwalia can be culled out by observing that the matters with regard to seniority and the like must be agitated within a reasonable period and if a party seeking a benefit chooses to keep quiet for a number of years no relief is due to such a party.
To my mind, the ratio of these judgments would be applicable equally to an administrative authority as well as to a proceeding in Court.
The Director General of Police was, therefore, wholly in error in choosing to ante date the dates of confirmations of the private respondents in the rank of Sub Inspectors after a lapse of a decade or more to the serious prejudice of the petitioners.
23.
I am, therefore, of the opinion that the present petitions must succeed.
They are accordingly allowed; resultantly the orders Annexures P-26 and R-8/3 qua the petitioners are quashed.
Mr. Patwalia has then stated that as the rights of the petitioners have been safeguarded by the present order he would have no objection if the inter se dispute qua the promotee Sub-Inspectors in CWP No. 4272 of 1995 is allowed to be resolved in terms of the order dated 23rd November, 1995.
The learned counsel for the respondents also accepts this proposal.
It is further clarified that any order that the Home Secretary may pass would not affect the seniority of the petitioners qua respondents No. 3 to 22 as DSPs.
There will, however, be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
