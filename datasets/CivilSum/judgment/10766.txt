SS Nijjar, J - In this writ petition under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India, the prayer of the petitioner is to count for retiral benefits the service of 31 years and 6 months rendered by him in the State of Haryana.
2.
The petitioner joined as Lecturer in Physics.
Regional Engg.
College, Kurukshetra, on 1121965.
Thereafter, on 9101967, he went to join as Research Assistant in the Department of Physics, University of Birmingham, England.
He came back to India and joined as Lecturer in Physics, Panjab University Post Graduate Regional Centre, Rohtak, on 571970.
On October 1, 1973, he joined as Assistant Director, Haryana Police Forensic Science Laboratory, Madhuban (Karnal).
He joined as Reader in Physics, MD University, Rohtak, on 19101977.
After one year and eight months, the petitioner's services were terminated in pursuance of resolution dated 2061979 passed by the Executive Council.
The Government of Haryana appointed Justice SS Dulat as Commission of Enquiry to enquire into the termination of petitioner's services.
The Commission was set up on 1781979 and submitted its report on 3061980.
On the basis of the report of the Dulat Commission, the petitioner was reinstated in service.
He rejoined the University on his initial post of Reader in Physics on 5101983.
The petitioner went on two years leave to work as Senior Lecturer in Physics in the University of Zambia, Lusaka (South Africa), on 29.11.1983.
He came back and joined the University on 1481985.
There was a dispute as to whether the period spent by the petitioner in Zambia shall be treated as a break in service.
However, the Executive Council of the University in its decision on 2531991 resolved that the break in service of the petitioner is condoned with all the resultant benefits of continuous service and that his original position is restored, except the financial benefits.
The aforesaid decision was communicated to the petitioner by the Registrar vide his letter No. ET-2-91/8413 dated 10491.
By Resolution dated 2531991, the original seniority of the petitioner w.e.f.
his initial date of appointment was restored in the Department of Physics.
Subsequently, his position as Professor was also restored by order dated 2311993.
The petitioner had been promoted as Professor of Physics w.e.f.
3131986.
Communication to this effect was sent to the petitioner by the Deputy Registrar on 28121991 (Annexure P-17).
Thus, it becomes apparent that the petitioner has been granted the benefit of continuity of service from the initial appointment, excepting for the financial benefits i.e. payment of arrears of salary.
On 1641998, the petitioner submitted an application that he be granted benefit of service rendered in other institutions before joining the respondent-University.
The respondent-University approved the Pension Scheme and introduced the same in the University w.e.f.
141995 vide Resolution No. 35 passed in the meeting of the Executive Council held on 1161998.
By Circular No. Endst.
No. Pen/F-I/98/3013-3112 dated 2071998, all the teachers were asked to give their option upto 19101998.
The petitioner duly submitted the option for the pension scheme vide letter No. Phy-1160 dated 2181998 (Annexure P-20).
The petitioner is also claiming an additional three years benefit in service according to Clause 17 of a Notification issued by the University Grants Commission dated 24121998.
In the aforesaid Notification in Clause 17, it was provided as under :-  "1700 SUPERANNUATION BENEFITS 1710 The benefit in service upto a maximum of 3 years should be provided for the teachers who have acquired PhD degree at the time of entry.
so that almost all teachers get full retirement benefits which are available after 33 years of service, subject to the overall age of superannuation.
1720 Other conditions with respect to Superannuation Benefits may be given as per Central/State Government Rules."
The aforesaid Notification was accepted by the respondent-University.
Thus, the petitioner claims that he is entitled to the benefit of three years additional service.
The application submitted by the petitioner on 3131998 was forwarded to the University by the Head of the Physics Department on 641998.
Thereafter on 641999, the petitioner submitted another application to the Registrar of the respondent-University to count the service rendered by the petitioner in the State of Haryana towards retirement benefits i.e. gratuity and pension etc.
This application was also forwarded by the Head of Physics Department.
Since the respondent-University failed to take any action, the petitioner has filed the present writ petition.
3.
The respondents have filed written statement.
It is stated that the petitioner remained out of University service from 2061979 to 4101983 as his services had been terminated.
He also remained on extraordinary leave (without pay) from 30.11.1983 to 1481985.
Therefore, he is not entitled to any benefit for the aforesaid periods.
Thereafter, the respondents have given the details of various writ petitions the petitioner had filed.
It is submitted that the petitioner is deliberately splitting up the cause of action and unnecessarily harassing the respondents.
The petitioner could have claimed the present relief in CWP No. 7272 of 1998.
The petitioner had also filed CWP Nos. 5093 of 1999 and 7179 of 1999.
If a cause of action enables a person to ask for a larger and wider relief, than that to which he limits his claim, he cannot afterwards seek to sue for the balance relief by independent proceedings.
It is stated that the writ petition is liable to be dismissed being vexatious and frivolus litigation with, compensatory costs under Sections 35/35-A of the CPC.
The respondents also plead that the writ petition is highly belated and suffers from delay and laches.
The petitioner is claiming benefit of the service rendered by him from 1962 to 1977.
Even otherwise, under Clause 7 of the Pension Rules, the service rendered by the petitioner in the State Government or government aided private college prior to joining the University, cannot be counted as qualifying service for pensionary benefits.
The qualifying service is defined under Clause 4(i) of MD University Employees' Pension Scheme/Rules which includes all service interrupted or continuous and paid by the University and for which University share is contributed towards pension fund.
It is also provided that the period of break shall be omitted when working out qualifying service.
Thus, the petitioner is not entitled for the benefit of service rendered by him outside the respondent-University.
The petitioner was re-appointed in service on 5101983.
The decision to re-appoint the petitioner was approved by the Executive Council vide Resolution No. 26 in its meeting held on 6101983.
The petitioner had filed CWP No. 2143 of 1979 challenging the order of termination.
The writ petition was, however, dismissed.
The petitioner filed LPA No. 6868 of 1983 which was also dismissed.
Thereafter, the petitioner moved SLP No. 15043 of 1983.
During the pendency of the SLP, the petitioner approached the respondents for taking him back in the service on humanitarian grounds.
Therefore, he was offered a fresh appointment on 5101983 by protecting his pay and as such he had re-joined without any reservation.
The re-appointment of the petitioner was also approved by the Executive Council vide Resolution No. 26 in its meeting held on 6101983 which is attached as Annexure R-2.
The copy of the re-appointment letter issued to the petitioner has been attached as Annexure R-3.
4.
From the above, it becomes apparent that the petitioner was not entitled to the benefit of service.
The respondents have also denied the claim of the petitioner under Clause 1710 of the Notification issued by the UGC under the title "Superannuation Benefits".
It is stated that while considering the recommendation of the UGC, Clause 17 has been substituted by paragraph 19.
In this paragraph, the point relating to the benefit of service for maximum three years for teachers holding PhD degree has been deleted.
Even otherwise, the petitioner had joined service as Lecturer in Physics, Regional Engg.
College, Kurukshetra on 1121965 whereas he completed his PhD during the year 1971.
5.
I have considered the submissions made by the petitioner in person and perused the written statement filed by the respondents.
6.
It is not disputed that the petitioner had retired on superannuation w.e.f.
3112000.
From a perusal of the facts narrated above, it also becomes apparent that the petitioner has been in continuous service in the University since 19101977.
7.
As noticed in the respective pleadings of the parties, the services of the petitioner were terminated pursuant to the resolution dated 2061979.
The Dulat Commission had concluded that the petitioner has not been treated fairly.
These observations of the Commission have been noticed in various judgments including the judgment rendered by this Bench (SS Nijjar in C.W.P.
No. 11923 of 1999 and CWP No. 4142 of 2002, decided on 23.3.2005).
During the pendency of the various proceedings, the respondents had undertaken to re- appoint the teachers whose services have been terminated, namely, Dr. SP Shukla and Mr. Satpal Dahiya and the petitioner.
A persual of the order passed by the Vice-Chancellor on 5101983 on the representation of the petitioner would show that the petitioner was allowed to re-join on the same terms and conditions which were offered to Dr. SP Shukla and Mr. Satpal Dahiya.
On the same date, the petitioner was given the order of re-appointment on the post of Reader.
The order categorically states that the petitioner is re-appointed on the post of Reader in the Pay Scale of Rs. 1200-50-1300-60-1900/-.
It was also provided that he will be entitled to receive basic pay which he was drawing at the time of termination of services i.e. Rs. 1250/- P.M.
He was also granted usual benefits and allowances as per the University Rules.
He was put on probation for a period of one year in the first instance.
He was informed that his conduct would be specially watched because he was one of those who under the leadership of Dr. BS Dahiya created great trouble for the University after the petitioner and some others were not confirmed in the service of the University.
The aforesaid appointment was approved by the Executive Council in its meeting held on 6101983 vide resolution No. 26.
The aforesaid sequence of events would make it abundantly clear that the petitioner is entitled to continuity of service from the date his services were terminated and reappointment on 5101983.
In fact, this matter is no longer res integra.
The matter has been considered by a Division Bench of this Court in CWP No. 5093 of 1999, decided on 2712000.
It has been held that the period between 2061979 to 4101983 would be treated as the period spent on duty.
The petitioner was only denied monetary benefits.
Even with regard to the period of Extra-ordinary Leave of the petitioner from 30.11.1983 to 1481985, a Division Bench of this Court in its judgment rendered in L.P.A. No. 2 of 2002, has held that the benefit of annual increment shall be granted to the petitioner for the period of Extra-ordinary leave from 30.11.1983 to 1481985.
8.
In view of the above, I am of the considered opinion that the objection raised by the respondents with regard to the break in service of the petitioner from 2061979 to 4101983, is wholly misconceived.
The petitioner is clearly entitled to the benefit of the aforesaid service for retiral benefits.
9.
The claim of the petitioner with regard to gratuity would have to be considered under the provisions contained in MD University Calendar, Vol.
I, 1995.
The relevant provision is as under :-  "Gratuity : 41(1)(a) : Gratuity shall be payable to an employee on the termination of his employment after he has rendered to the University continuous service for not less than 5 years - (i) on his superannuation; or (ii) on his retirement or resignation; or XXX XXX XXX" 10.
Pension is to be granted to the employees of the respondent-University under the Maharshi Dayanand University Employees Pension Scheme, 1977.
This scheme is applicable to all the employees who were in service on 141995.
Qualifying service for the purposes of pension is defined under Clause 4 as follows :-  "4.
QUALIFYING SERVICE (i) All service interrupted or continuous paid by the University and for which University share is contributed towards Pension fund shall be treated as qualifying service.
The period of break shall be omitted while working out aggregate service."
11.
The Executive Council had accepted the Pension Scheme w.e.f.
141995 vide Resolution No. 35 in its meeting held on 1161998.
During the pendency of the writ petition, the petitioner has moved CM No. 5898 of 2004 and placed on record two Notifications issued by the State of Haryana dated 24122001 (Annexure P-32) and dated 662002 (Annexure P-33).
On the basis of these Notifications, the petitioner submits that the service rendered by the petitioner from 1965 to 1977 has to be added to the total service of the petitioner for the purposes of grant of gratuity as well as pension.
A perusal of notification dated 24122001 shows that the Haryana Government has agreed for counting of service rendered by the employees of the respondent- University, Kurukshetra University and MD University as qualifying service for the purpose of pension on the following conditions :-  "1.
The service rendered by the said employees in these institutions is without any break and is continuous."
In view of the discussion above, it becomes apparent that the petitioner is entitled to count the entire service rendered by him in different institutions for pension and gratuity.
12.
The claim of the petitioner for additional three years benefit of service is based on Clause-17 of the Notification issued by the UGC on 24121998.
A perusal of the aforesaid clause of the notification shows that the benefit in service, upto a maximum of three years, is to be provided to the teachers who had acquired degree of PhD at the time of entry into service.
The aforesaid provision has been clearly made to enable the teachers to get full retirement benefits as are admissible on rendering 33 years of service.
The notification issued by the UGC has been accepted by the University.
The respondents, however, pleaded that the State Governments have been empowered to make changes in the pay scale and date of implementation of UGC pay scales vide Clause-3 of the letter of the Government of India, Ministry of Human Resources Development (Department of Education), bearing No. F.1-22/97- U.1 dated 27th July, 1998.
A perusal of Clause-3 of the aforesaid letter, however, shows that it gives discretion to State Government to introduce scales of pay different from those mentioned in the scheme.
It has no relevance to the recommendations with regard to the benefit of additional three years of service under Clause-17 of the UGC notification.
The recommendations of the UGC once accepted have to be implemented uniformally.
This view of mine will find support from the observations made by the Supreme Court in the case of Purshottam Lal and others v. Union of India and another, AIR 1973 Supreme Court 1088.
In paragraph 15, the Supreme Court has laid down the law as follows :-  "Mr. Dhebar contends that it was for the Government to accept the recommendations of the Pay Commission and while doing so to determine which categories of employees should be taken to have been included in the terms of reference.
We are unable to appreciate this point.
Either the Government has made reference in respect of all Government employees or it has not.
But if it has made a reference in respect of all Government employees and it accepts the recommendations it is bound to implement the recommendations in respect of all Government employees.
If it does not implement the report regarding some employees only it commits a breach of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
This is what the Government has done as far as these petitioners are concerned."
These observations, in my opinion, are fully applicable to the facts and circumstances of this case.
Once the recommendation is accepted by the University, the petitioner would be clearly entitled to be given the benefit of Clause-17 of the UGC notification issued on 24121998.
13.
In view of the above, the writ petition is allowed.
The respondents are directed to re-determine the pension and the gratuity payable to the petitioner by giving benefit of the entire service rendered by the petitioner in different institutions as enumerated in the writ petition i.e. the petitioner is entitled to the benefit of service as follows :-  (i) Lecturer in Physics, Regional Engg.
College, Kurukshetra, from 1121965 to 9101967.
(ii) Lecturer in Physics, Punjab University, Post Graduate Regional Centre, Rohtak from 571970 to 30.9.1973.
(iii) Assistant Director (Class-I Gazetted Officer) Haryana Police Forensic Science Laboratory, Madhuban (Karnal) from 1.10.1973 to 2071977.
(iv) Lecturer in Physics, Kurukshetra University, Kurukshetra from 21.7.1977 to 18.10.1977.
The aforesaid period is total 9 years and 2 months in addition to the benefit of service which has already been granted to the petitioner from 19101977 to 3112000.
The petitioner shall also be entitled to the benefit of three years service for possessing the qualification of PhD The respondents are directed to re-determine the retiral benefits of the petitioner and release the same within a period of three months from the date of receipt of a certified copy of this order.
The writ petition is allowed in the terms as indicated above.
No costs.
Petition allowed.
