RL Anand, J - By this judgment, I dispose of three civil writ petitions-C.W.P.
No. 14053 of 1993 (The Punjab State Cooperative Supply and Marketing Federation Ltd v. Rajinder Pal Sharma and another), 2002(2) SCT 263 (P&H), C.W.P.
No. 8136 of 1989 (Rajinder Pal Sharma v. The Punjab State Cooperative Supply and Marketing Federation Limited and another) and C.W.P.
No. 2003 (Rajinder Pal Sharma v. The Punjab State Cooperative Supply and Marketing Federation Limited), as in my opinion, all the three writ petitions can be disposed of by one judgment.
2.
In C.W.P.
No. 14053 of 1993 the Federation has prayed that a writ in the nature of Certiorari be issued and order dated 1971993 (Annexure P-3) be quashed, whereas in other C.W.P.
No. 8136 of 1989 Shri Rajinder Pal Sharma has sought a direction of this Court for the quashment of the order dated 1851989 (Annexure P-3) and suspension letter dated 2881984 (Annexure P-2) issued by the Managing Director; in C.W.P.
No. 2003 of 2001 the petitioner Shri Rajinder Pal Sharma has further made a prayer that he be released salary in the pay scale of Rs. 1200-2100 w.e.f.
111986 and the action of the respondent authorities in putting him at the bottom of the pay scale of Rs. 1200-2100 is wrong.
It may be mentioned here that the fate of the two writ petitions filed by Shri Rajinder Pal Sharma will depend upon the fate of the writ petition filed by the Marketing Federation which will be treated as the primary writ.
For the sake of facts I am taking it from C.W.P.
No. 14053 of 1993 filed by the Federation.
3.

4.
Respondent No. 1 gave reply which was not found satisfactory.
Ultimately, an Enquiry Officer was appointed.
Both the parties led evidence.
After receipt of the report of the Enquiry Officer, the appointing authority issued order of show cause notice to the delinquent official and after considering the reply to the show cause notice, the punishing authority awarded the punishment to respondent No. 1 and he was inflicted a penalty of reduction of his pay fixing the same at the minimum of the scale and the recovery of the loss was divided amongst both respondent No. 1 and Shri Gurmukh Singh vide order dated 1771991.
Respondent No. 1 filed an appeal which was considered by the Board of Directors on 2621992 and the appeal was dismissed.
Thereafter, respondent No. 1 filed a revision petition under rule 217(e) of the Punjab State Cooperative Supply and Marketing Cooperative Service (Common Cadre) Rules, 1967.
The revision was accepted by respondent No. 2 and the order dated 2621992 was set aside.
According to the petitioner, the said order is illegal for the reasons given in para No. 8 of the writ petition.
It will be worth-mentioning to reproduce para No. 8 sub-para (V) on which the main reliance was placed by the learned counsel for the petitioner during the course of arguments :  "Because the revisional authority has observed that the charge sheet and the punishment orders should have been served upon the petitioner by the competent authority.
In this respect, it is submitted that the charge sheet and punishment orders at the relevant time were issued by the competent authority having the powers and the decision of the revisional authority to this extent is not correct.
Shri Rajinder Pal was served with a charge sheet on 25.4.1985 by the Managing Director as at that time the powers of punishment/appointment of the employees in the scale upto Rs. 570-1080 were with the Cheif Executive Officer.
These powers were delegated by the Administrative Committee in its meeting held on 2111983 to the Chief Executive Officer, who was at that time the Chairman.
But subsequently from January 1984, Managing Director became the Chief Executive Officer and the delegations made earlier on 2111983 were continued to be exercised by the Managing Director.
Under these powers as the Managing Director was competent in 1985, the charge (sheet) was validly issued.
At the time of delegation on 2111983, the Chief Executive was the Chairman in pursuance of the amendment made in Rule 80-C of the Punjab Cooperative Societies Rules, 1963.
In normal course under these rules only the Chairman appointed and working whole time in such capacity can exercise the powers of the Principal Executive Officer.
There was no provision for appointment of whole time Chairman of non-official members prior to December, 1982 but with the amendment in this rule by inserting to as public official, the then Chairman who was the M.L.A.
became the Principal Executive Officer.
Where there is no whole time Chairman under the ibid Rule, Rule 80-B is normally attracted which is reproduced for facility sake : 80-B "The Managing Director of Cooperative Societies shall be its Principal Executive Officer and the employees Cooperative Societies shall exercise their powers and perform their duties under his superintendence and control.
The Managing Director shall exercise his powers and perform his duties under the over all supervision and control of the committee."
On January 4, 1984, the words inserted vide Notification dated 13.12.1982 were omitted and Managing Director became the Principal Executive Officer in place of the Chairman.
Apart from the above, Administrative Committee in its meeting held on 31101986 passed the following resolution :-  "Resolved that the powers earlier delegated by the Committee in its meeting dated 19.1.1983 to the Chairman as Principal Executive Officer of the Federation be deemed to have been delegated to and vested in the Chief Executive Officer of the Federation i.e. the Managing Director with effect from 411984 and all actions of the Managing Direct and orders passed by him in exercise of such powers to and are hereby approved and ratified.
Thus, when the charge-sheet was issued to Shri Rajinder Pal on 25.4.1984, the Managing Director was competent.
It may also be added that later on under the punishment and appeal rules, which was approved by the Registrar Cooperative Societies on 871991, A.M.D.
(G) was competent to award the punishment to C- Group employees and thus the A.M.D.
(G) was fully competent to award the punishment which was awarded to him."
5.
Notice of the writ petition was given to the respondent who filed the written statement and denied the allegations.
According to the stand of the respondent, the charge-sheet was issued by the Managing Director who was not competent to issue the same and, therefore, the entire departmental proceedings are bad.
This plea of the respondent finds support with the revisional authority who gave the following findings :  "After hearing arguments from both the sides, I am inclined to agree with the arguments of the learned counsel for the petitioner that charge-sheet should have been served upon the petitioner by the competent authority and if any punishment was to be imposed, the competent authority should have done so.
The surcharge proceedings started against the petitioner have been dropped and the various issues taken up by the petitioner in this revision petition have not been properly considered and weighed by the punishing authority and appellant authority.
I set aside the orders of both punishing authority and appellate authority and remand this case to Markfed-respondent with the directions that the appropriate authority may consider all these facts and take a fresh decision.
The punishing authority may issue notice to the petitioner and hear him before taking a final decision."
6.
I have heard Shri Amar Vivek, Advocate on behalf of the petitioner and Shri Amarjit Markan, Advocate on behalf of Shri Rajinder Pal Sharma, respondent No. 1 in the main writ petition and the petitioner in the two subsequent writ petitions and with their assistance, have gone through the record of this case.
7.
The revisional authority had set aside the orders of the Federation mainly on the ground that the charge-sheet which was served upon Shri Rajinder Pal Sharma has not been issued by the competent authority which, in the opinion of the authority, was the competent authority.
Therefore, the orders of the punishing authority as well as the appellate authority were set aside and the revisional authority also gave directions to consider all these facts and take a fresh decision.
This order was passed on 1971993 by Shri GS Aggarwal, IAS, Additional Registrar (Admn.
), Cooperative Societies, Punjab, Chandigarh.
8.
The sole point which survives for determination is as to whether the charge-sheet which was issued in this case on 25th April, 1985 under the signatures of the Managing Director has been issued by a competent authority or not.
In order to appreciate this contention it has become necessary for me to examine the provisions of the Punjab State Supply and Marketing Co- operatives Services (Common Cadre) Rules, 1967.
Rule 215 of the said Common Cadre Rules will give a glance about the competent authority to impose penalty.
The reading of the same would show that Administrative Committee is the competent authority which could issue the charge-sheet to Shri Rajinder Pal Sharma.
The Administrative Committee has been again defined under Rule 15 of the said Rules, which means that the Committee constituted by the Board of Directors in pursuance of clause 16 of these rules.
Rule 16 (d) lays down that "The Administrative Committee shall be competent to delegate any of its powers to the Chief Executive Officer or any other Officer of the Federation not below the rank of Additional Managing Director".
As the charge sheet in this case was issued to Shri Rajinder Pal Sharma on 25th April, 1985 under the signatures of the Managing Director, therefore, it has to be seen whether this charge-sheet has been issued to him by a competent authority or not.
As I stated above, it was the Administrative Committee which is competent to delegate any of its powers to the Chief Executive Officer or any other officer of the Federation not below the rank of Additional Managing Director.
The reading of the finding of this case would show that the Administrative Committee could have delegated its powers to the Managing Committee on 25th April, 1985.
For the first time, the powers were granted on 31101986 when it was resolved that "the powers earlier delegated by the Committee in its meeting dated 19.1.1983 to the Chairman as Principal Executive Officer of the Federation be deemed to have been delegated to and vested in the Chief Executive Officer of the Federation i.e. the Managing Director with effect from 411984 and all actions of the Managing Director and orders passed by him in exercise of such powers to and are hereby approved an ratified."
Thus, the point for determination would be as to whether there can be delegation of powers with retrospective effect i.e. with effect from 411984 when the resolution itself was passed by the Administrative Committee on 31101986 and the answer of this Court is in the negative.
9.
Shri Amar Vivek, the learned counsel for the petitioner has not been able to convince me that the powers of the Administrative Committee could be delegated retrospectively upon the Executive Committee w.e.f 411984.
He tried to convince me by relying upon the averments of this writ petition as contained in paragraph 8(V) which I have reproduced above with the aid of Rule 80B, but I am not convinced with his submission because he cannot be deemed to have been conferred of the powers with retrospective effect.
The powers can be delegated prospectively.
As the Managing Director did not enjoy the powers of the Administrative Committee in a rightful manner on 25th April, 1985, therefore, the action of the petitioner-Federation in issuing charge-sheet and appointing the Enquiry Officer for going into the charges was without jurisdiction and any order passed by the punishing authority was rightly set aside by the revisional authority which gave discretion to the Federation to pass appropriate orders according to law.
10.
Resultantly, Civil Writ Petition No. 14053 filed by the petitioner- Federation is hereby dismissed, while the two Civil Writ Petition Nos. 8136 of 1989 and 2003 of 2001 filed by Shri Rajinder Pal Sharma are hereby allowed and directions are given to the Federation to release salary to the petitioner in the grade of Rs. 1200-2100 w.e.f 111986 and I further set aside the orders of the Federation vide which Shri Rajinder Pal Sharma was placed at the bottom of the domestic scale.
No order as to costs.
Petition dismissed.
