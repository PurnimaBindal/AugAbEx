Susanta Chatterji, J - The petitioner-Prafulla Kumar Dash has asked for the following reliefs :-  ".......... it is humbly prayed that the writ application be allowed; And (a) a writ of mandamus be issued quashing the impugned resolution of the Managing Committee under Annexure-6 and the subsequent order of the Secretary under Annexure-7 and the order of the Inspector of Schools under Annexure-3; And (b) a writ of mandamus be issued declaring the petitioner as senior to the opposite party No. 3; And (c) any other order/orders, writ/writs, direction/directions be issued so as to give complete relief to the petitioner."
2.
The petitioner challenges the decision of the Managing Committee dated 2781993 vide Annexure-6 to the writ application, declaring opposite party No. 6 as senior to him in the Trained Graduate cadre alleging the said decision to have been taken at the behest of the Inspector of Schools and the then Minister of Pension and Public Grievances.
3.
It is stated that the petitioner was appointed as an Assistant Teacher against a Trained Graduate post on 281971.
The appointment was approved with effect from 1-6-1973 as per the order of the Inspector of Schools, Puri Circle, Puri dated 4-11-1977 vide Annexure-2 to the writ application.
It is submitted that prior to 1989, the School in question was under the jurisdiction of Puri Education Circle and after bifurcation, it has come under the jurisdiction of Khurde Education Circle.
While continuing in service, the petitioner passed BEd.
examination on 561982.
Opposite party No. 5 had been appointed in the school as an Assistant Teacher on ad hoc basis with effect from 381972.
His appointment had been approved by the Inspector of Schools with effect from 161975 as per order dated 4.11.1977 Annexure-2 and his service was regularised by virtue of the Validation Act of 1978 in the year 1978.
The petitioner claims to be senior to opposite party No. 5 in view of his length of service, approval of appointment and regularisation of his service.
The Managing Committee also passed a resolution on 1741975 as per Annexure-12 declaring the petitioner as seniormost Assistant Teacher of the school.
Besides, the Managing Committee also by a comparative assessment of performance of both the petitioner and opposite party No. 5 had come to a conclusion that the petitioner was senior to opposite party No. 5 which decision was also communicated to the Inspector of Schools.
4.
It is alleged that the opposite party No. 5 had been throughout accepted as the 2nd Assistant Teacher of the School whereas the petitioner had been accepted as the first Assistant Teacher by the Managing Committee as well as by the educational authorities.
Opposite party No. 5, however, in the year 1993 made a representation to the then Minister of Pension and Public Grievances claiming seniority over the petitioner in the Trained Graduate cadre and at the behest of the Minister, the Inspector of Schools vide his letter dated 1211993 as per Annexure-8, directed the Secretary of the Managing Committee to re-open the matter after eighteen years and refix the seniority of the petitioner vis-a-vis opposite party No. 5, treating the opposite party No. 5 as senior to the petitioner.
The petitioner challenges the said order stating all the facts in greater details.
5.
Mr. Swain appearing for the petitioner has placed the pleadings of the parties and has drawn attention of this Court to the decisions reported in AIR 1989 SC 377 : 1989 Lab IC 1029, Union of India v. Ansuekhar Gain and AIR 1993 SC 2573 : 1993 Lab IC 2366 : 1993(4) SCT 318 (SC), Bhey Ram Sharma v. Haryana State Electricity Board.
He has argued that since the petitioner was appointed earlier and opposite party No. 5 came much later, in absence of any statutory provision for determining inter se seniority, the State Government have issued executive instructions for determining the seniority of Trained Graduate Teachers.
The first of its kind is the executive instruction dated 4121982, which inter alia provides that Untrained Graduate Teachers appointed prior to commencement of the Orissa Education (Recruitment and Conditions of Service of Teachers and Members of the Staff of Aided Educational Institutions) Rules of 1974 would be considered senior to the Trained Graduate Teachers appointed subsequently.
The word `subsequently' has been clarified by a later circular to mean after 141975.
It is submitted that the aforesaid circular was issued by the State Government because of the reason that prior to commencement of the aforesaid 1974 Rules which came into force with effect from 141975, an Untrained Graduate Teacher was holding Trained Graduate post substantively as per the provisions laid down in the Board's Regulation and the Orissa Education Code.
But when the 1974 Rules came into force with effect from 141975, under Rule 16(2) of the said rules it was provided that a teacher who had not undergone training prescribed for the post would not hold the post substantively.
Therefore in view of this and in order to protect the seniority of the teachers appointed prior to 141975, the State Government brought out the aforesaid circular laying down the principle that prior to 141975, length of service would only form the basis for determination of inter se seniority.
It is submitted that unfortunately the Inspector of Schools by misconstruing the aforesaid circular has stated that both the petitioner as also opposite party No. 5 were appointed prior to 141975 and their inter se seniority would be determined basing upon the date of their acquisition of BEd.
qualification.
6.
Attention of this Court is also drawn to several other decisions, namely, 1980 Lab IC 1155 (SC) Baleswar Das v. State of UP, 1993 AIR SCW 3559 : V Kameswari v Union of India and 1994 AIR SCW 4868 : 1995(1) SCT 353 (SC) V. Sreenivas Reddy v. Government of A.P.
It is submitted that applying the ratio of the aforesaid decisions the petitioner having been substantively appointed since 281971 and the opposite party No. 5 having initially been appointed on ad hoc basis and his service having been regularised in the year 1978, the latter cannot take edge over the petitioner.
7.
Regarding delay and laches, Mr. Swain has relied on the decisions reported in 1986(1) Serv LJ 52 (Kerala) TV Shelapani Warrior v. State of Kerala and 1983(3) Serv LJ (SC) 1 SC Gupta v. NK Pandey.
8.
Mr. Swain has further submitted that in the cadre list also the petitioner is shown senior to opposite party No. 6 as would be evident from Annexure-9.
Therein the petitioner's name finds place at serial No. 173 whereas the name of opposite party No. 5 is at serial No. 223.
Such seniority of the petitioner having not been altered any time, it will be highly illegal on the part of the Managing Committee to declare opposite party No. 5 as senior to the petitioner.
9.
Mr. Swain has also distinguished the decisions cited on behalf of opposite party No. 5 at the time of hearing, i.e. 1990(32) OJD 60 Ananda Sahoo v. State and in 1990(2) OLR 422 : 1991 Lab IC NOC 24 Nilamani Behera v. State of Orissa.
10.
Mr. Swain has concluded that judging from all angles, the petitioner should be held as senior to opposite party No. 5.
11.
Mr. JK Rath, learned counsel appearing for opposite party No. 5, has also made a very lengthy argument.
He has submitted that the petitioner had been appointed as an Assistant Teacher on 281971 in the scale of Rs. 115- 180.
Opposite party No. 5 had been appointed as an Assistant Teacher on 381972 in the scale of Rs. 820-500.
The School became an aided one in 1976.
The petitioner was receiving his salary in the scale of pay of Rs. 115-180 and opposite party No. 5 was receiving his pay in the scale of Rs. 320-500.
On 2961976, opposite party No. 5 was relieved from the school to undergo BEd.
training in the Radhanath Training College, Cuttack and after his training he joined the school on 1361972.
The result of BEd.
examination of opposite party No. 5 was published declaring him to have passed on 981977.
The Inspector of Schools approved the Trained Graduate scale of pay in favour of the opposite party No. 5 i.e. Rs. 400-620 with effect from the date of publication of BEd.
result of opposite party No. 5, i.e. 981977.
It is submitted that while opposite party No. 5 was in receipt of Trained Graduate scale of pay, the petitioner was in the scale of pay of Rs. 320-500.
The petitioner passed BEd.
examination on 561982 and was granted Trained Graduate scale of pay with effect from 561982, i.e. the date of publication of his BEd.
result by order of the Inspector of Schools dated 1161984.
It is also submitted that opposite party No. 5 got Trained Graduate scale of pay five years prior to receipt of the said scale by the petitioner.
Therefore, opposite party No. 5 was not only a Trained Graduate earlier, but was also paid Trained Graduate scale of pay earlier to the petitioner and he was all through receiving higher scale of pay in comparison to the petitioner.
12.
Mr. Rath has submitted that the Managing Committee of the school fixed inter se seniority of the petitioner vis-a-vis opposite party No. 5 on 9111981 and the Headmaster-cum-Secretary requested the Inspector of Schools to fix inter se seniority of the petitioner vis-a-vis opposite party No. 5 basing on the earlier letters sent by the Managing Committee on 2171992.
On 1911992 the Inspector of Schools intimated the Secretary of the Managing Committee that opposite party No. 5 was senior to the petitioner since both of them had been appointed prior to 141975 and opposite party No. 5 acquired training qualification earlier.
13.
Mr. Rath has also submitted that this nature of dispute has been set at rest by this Court earlier in a decision reported in 1990(32) OJD 60 holding inter alia that seniority of untrained teachers appointed prior to 141975 when the recruitment rules came into force was being determined taking the date of passing the BEd.
examination and the date of receipt of Trained Graduate scale of pay and it was decided that appointment of a teacher who was not a Trained Graduate does not make the appointment into the said cadre.
Since the petitioner in that case was not a Trained Graduate and had not received Trained Graduate scale of pay prior to opposite party No. 5 who had been appointed as a Trained Graduate Teacher and receiving Trained Graduate scale of pay prior to that of the petitioner, was declared senior to the petitioner.
14.
It is also placed on record that the aforesaid view of this Court was followed in another decision reported in 1990(2) OLR 422 : 1991 Lab IC NOC 24 Nilamani Mishra v. State of Orissa.
15.
It is argued that in the instant case, admittedly the petitioner had been appointed as an Assistant Teacher on 281971 and opposite party No. 5 had been appointed as an Assistant Teacher on 381972.
The State Government also in their letter No. 16841 dated 2651996 in modification of their previous instruction intimated to all concerned that Untrained Graduate Teachers appointed against trained graduate posts prior to 141975 and acquiring training qualification after 141975 would rank, according to date of passing next to trained graduate teacher.
This instruction being the latest in the field of determination of seniority and in conformity with the principles laid down by this Court in the decision mentioned above, seniority of opposite party No. 5 over the petitioner is to be obtained.
16.
Patently we have heard Mr. Swain and Mr. Rath, learned counsel for the contesting parties.
Learned Addl Govt. Advocate for the State supports the Government stand to declare opposite party No. 5 as senior to the petitioner.
Undisputedly both the petitioner and opposite party No. 5 had been appointed prior to 141975, the petitioner being appointed on 281971 0and opposite party No. 5 on 281972.
The school in question was not an aided one prior to 1976.
If both the petitioner and opposite party No. 5 were appointed against sanctioned posts of Trained Graduate Teachers, they cannot claim that their appointment was regular and their inter se seniority is to be determined on that score.
If by the Validation Act the appointments irregularly made were validated, the spirit of validation has to be reckoned in proper perspective.
The posts were of Trained Graduate Teachers and admittedly opposite party No. 5 acquired BEd.
training qualification prior to the date of acquisition of such training qualification by the petitioner, opposite party No. 5 having obtained BEd.
training on 981977 and the petitioner on 561982.
If the relevant circular of the State Government says that seniority of the teachers working against Trained Graduate posts without training qualification would be determined on the basis of their dates of acquisition of training qualification, the petitioner cannot make any grievance.
In this respect we find two reported decisions of this Court as referred to by Mr. Rath which are all the same relevant.
There is nothing wrong in the latest circular to reckon seniority.
If both the petitioner and opposite party No. 5 were initially appointed against Trained Graduate posts being untrained, opposite party No. 5 having obtained BEd.
training qualification earlier to the petitioner, there was nothing wrong in determining his seniority over the petitioner.
Mere length of service or names appearing in the cadre list, etc. does not matter at all.
17.
For the foregoing reasons, we find better merit in the case of opposite party No. 5.
We accordingly dismiss the writ application.
No costs.
Petition dismissed.
