MM Kumar, J - Whether petitioners who are higher in order of merit in the Select List for H.C.S.
(Executive Branch) as determined by the Haryana Public Service Commission (for brevity, `HPSC') be denied appointment after persons lower in order of merit have been appointed is the pristine question raised in this petition filed under Article 226 of the Constitution of India.
The petitioners have prayed for issuance of a writ in the nature of mandamus directing the respondents to consider and appoint the petitioners to H.C.S.
(Executive Branch) in accordance with their merit position secured by them in the select list of 1993 conducted by respondent No. 2 i.e. H.P.S.C.
A further prayer has also been made that the posts be filled up in accordance with the vacancies available on the date of declaration of result.
2.
Brief facts of the case are that HPSC advertised 12 vacancies of H.C.S.
(Executive Branch) in 1992.
Nine vacancies were allocated to General category and three were reserved.
In addition a large number of vacancies in Allied Services were also advertised.
The petitioner duly appeared in the examination conducted in October, 1993.
The result of the written examination was scrapped on account of internal wranglings of the Commission.
The order of scrapping the examination was challenged before this Court in Civil Writ Petition No. 8584 of 1994 and in other connected matters.
A Division Bench of this Court upheld the written examination and quashed the order of cancellation of examination.
It was further directed that the interview be held by the Punjab Public Service Commission.
On appeal to the Supreme Court the last direction was modified by the Supreme Court by holding that the result should be finalised by the HPSC.
Eventually, the result of the written examination was declared on 21.1.1996 and both the petitioners were called for interview.
The final result after the interview was declared on 15.3.1996.
It is pertinent to mention that in the meanwhile, a revised/additional requisition had been sent to HPSC by the Government in November, 1994, wherein the number of posts of Class `A' Tehsildars was raised from 9 to 13 and Block Development and Panchayat Officers from 10 to 17 and one additional post of District Food and Supplies Controller was also added raising the number from 62 to 74 (Annexure P-8).
On 24.5.1996 these additional posts from general category were again advertised.
3.
Sarvshri Sandeep Singh and Lalit Kumar filed Civil Writ Petition No. 6987 of 1996 claiming that they are entitled to be considered and appointed to H.C.S.
(Executive Branch) on anticipated vacancies which may have arisen in addition to the advertised vacancies despite the fact that in order of merit they were beyond serial No. 9.
A Division Bench of this court dismissed their petition on 3.11.1998 and they filed Special Leave Petition No. 10298 of 1999.
The special leave petition was granted and it became Civil Appeal No. 7422 of 1999.
Both the petitioners filed IA Nos. 2 and 3 in the aforementioned Civil Appeal, titled `Sandeep Singh and another v. State of Haryana' for being impleaded as parties.
The prayer of the petitioners emanated from the fact that Sandeep Singh and Lalit Kumar were at serial Nos. 12 and 17 in the order of merit respectively whereas the petitioners were at serial Nos. 13 and 14 in order of merit in the select list of 1993.
Both the applications of the petitioners were dismissed on 1042000 and 452000 by relegating them to first approach the High Court under Article 226 of the Constitution.
The orders passed by the Supreme Court are Annexures P-12 and P-13 with the writ petition.
It is, however, appropriate to mention that Sarvshri Varinder Dahiya and Varinder Lather who are at serial Nos. 10 and 19 respectively were allowed to be impleaded as party by the Supreme Court in Civil Appeal No. 7422 of 1999.
4.
After dismissal of their applications by the Supreme Court, the petitioners filed the instant petition which came up for motion hearing before a Division Bench of this Court on 162000.
The petition was adjourned sine die so as to await the decision of the Supreme Court in Civil appeal No. 7422 of 1999 filed by Sandeep Singh and another and liberty was granted to the petitioners to file an appropriate application at the relevant time.
On the filing of application registered as CM Nos. 27768-69 of 2000, this Court issued notice of motion on 22112000.
The principal claim made by the petitioners is that once a person lower in merit to the petitioners has succeeded, then there is no reason for denying the same relief to the petitioners.
The operative part of the judgment of the Supreme Court in Civil Appeal No. 7422 of 1999, decided on 9112000 reads as under :-  "......
In the aforesaid premises, we set aside the impugned judgments of the Punjab and Haryana High Court and allow these appeals and writ petitions and direct that these 4 persons, who had appeared at the competitive test in the year 1993 pursuant to the advertisement dated 24.12.1992 should be considered for being appointed to the Haryana Civil Service (Executive Branch).
We make it clear that if any other persons, who had appeared at the said examination and who had not approached the court till today, will not be entitled to file any such application for getting this relief in question so far as the examination of the year 1993 is concerned.
It is also made clear that the appellants will get the benefit of this order prospectively i.e. from the date of appointment.
The Government may decide the matter within three months."
(Emphasis added) 5.
The petitioners have also filed CM No. 25826 of 2003 and have brought additional facts on record.
In the application, it has, inter alia, been pointed out that one Ashok Kumar Garg belonging to the select list of 1991 has approached this Court by filing Civil Writ Petition No. 2157 of 2000 and a Division Bench of this Court has allowed the writ petition on 722002 (Annexure A-3).
The Supreme Court also dismissed the special leave petition preferred by the respondent-State on 1682002 (Annexure A-4).
He has also been issued appointment letter Annexure A-6 subject to the final order/judgment of the Supreme Court in Writ Petition No. 355 of 2003 and similar other writ petitions wherein the constitutional validity of the Haryana Civil Service (Executive Branch) and Allied Services & other Services Common/Combined Examination Act, 2002 (for brevity, `the Act') has been challenged.
6.
In the written statement, the respondent-State has taken the stand that by virtue of the Act which has come into force w.e.f.
29.8.1989, the advertised vacancies alone could be filled up and there is a statutory bar for filling up any vacancy beyond the advertised number.
The Act was enacted obviously to over-come the judgment of the Supreme Court in the case Varinder Singh Hooda v. State of Haryana, 1999(2) SCT 652 (SC) : 1999(3) SCC 696.
According to Section 4 of the Act, no appointment is to be made to a post or service beyond the number of vacancies advertised in the year 1993 for H.C.S.
(Executive Branch) were 12 and only 9 vacancies were advertised for the candidates belonging to general category.
Therefore, it has been urged that the candidates beyond merit list No. 9 could not now be permitted to be appointed.
It has been pleaded that the bar created by the Act is applicable to the petitioners but it was not applicable to those who were petitioners in Civil Appeal No. 7422 of 1999 as their appeal stood decided by the Supreme Court on 9112000.
The case of Ashok Kumar Garg belonging to select list of 1991 is also distinguished on the ground that this case was decided by the Division Bench of this Court on 722002.
7.
Mr. PS Patwalia, learned counsel for the petitioners has argued that a Division Bench of this Court in the case of Ashok Kumar Garg has allowed the petition on 722002 and the special leave petition preferred by the State was dismissed on 1682002.
According to the learned counsel, the Act had already come in operation as it was notified on 2732002.
Therefore, the plea with regard to the bar created by the Act could not now be raised against the petitioners.
The learned counsel has further pointed out that the vires of the Act have already been challenged by those candidates who have been appointed over and above the number of vacancies advertised in pursuance to the judgment of the Supreme Court in Sandeep Singh's case (supra) and Varinder Singh Hooda's case (supra) in Writ Petition No. 355 of 2003 and other connected matters are pending before the Supreme Court.
The learned counsel has referred to Annexure A-6 with CM No. 25826 of 2003 that Ashok Kumar Garg has been given appointment subject to the result of the writ petition pending before the Supreme Court.
8.
The learned counsel has also argued that once Sarvshri Sandeep Singh, Lalit Kumar, Varinder Dahiya and Varinder Lather who were at Serial Nos. 12, 17, 10 and 19 respectively have been given appointments to H.C.S.
(Executive Branch), the petitioners could not be denied appointments as they have been placed higher in order of merit by HPSC being at serial Nos. 13 and 14 of the select list.
Obviously, the petitioners who are at serial Nos. 13 and 14 are more meritorious than persons like Lalit Kumar at Serial No. 17 and Varinder Lather who is at serial No. 19.
The learned counsel has maintained that the Supreme Court has specifically permitted while deciding Civil Appeal No. 7422 of 1999 that those who had already approached the Court on or before 9112000 (i.e. the date of the judgment) would alone be entitled to pursue their remedy and no other person shall be entitled to seek any relief.
The learned counsel has argued that the petitioners have approached the Supreme Court by filing interlocutory applications i.e. IA Nos. 2 and 3 in Civil Appeal No. 7422 of 1999 and those applications were dismissed on 1042000 and 452000 directing the petitioners to approach this Court (Annexures P-12 and P-13) and they have accordingly approached this Court by filing the instant petition in May/June, 2000 itself.
The learned counsel has also made a reference to executive instructions dated 22.3.1986 to show that the vacancies which are to arise within six months from the recommendation of the names by the Commission were to be filled up from the wait-list candidates (Annexures P-5 and P-6).
On the basis of aforementioned instructions, this Court has allowed the writ petition in the case of `Surjan Singh and others v. State of Haryana' (Civil Writ Petition No. 10397 of 1999, decided on 3012001) by taking the view that anticipated vacancies should be filled up from wait-list.
9.
Mr. SS Brar, learned State counsel has argued that once there is a statutory bar created by the Act prohibiting to fill up vacancies more than the advertised number, then no direction could be issued by this Court.
Learned State counsel has pointed out that under Section 4 of the Act, there is a complete bar and the judgment of the Supreme Court in Varinder Singh Hooda's case (supra) would not be applicable.
The learned counsel has further submitted that the Act has been applied retrospectively w.e.f.
29.8.1989 and would extend to the case of the petitioners which belonged to 1993 batch.
The learned counsel has maintained that the Act could not have been pleaded before the Supreme Court when the judgment in Varinder Singh Hooda's case (supra) was pronounced in the year 1999 not it could be brought to the notice of the Supreme Court when the judgment of Civil Appeal No. 7422 of 1999 was pronounced on 9112000 as the Act itself has been published in the official Gazette on 2722002.
The learned counsel has also argued that the instructions dated 2231957 and 2651972 (Annexure P-5 and P-6) could not be made applicable once the Act has come into force.
10.
After hearing learned counsel for the parties, I am of the considered view that this petition deserves to be allowed because the right of the petitioners to approach this Court has been protected by the Supreme Court while dismissing their IA Nos. 2 and 3 of 2000 on 1042000 and 452000 respectively.
The orders passed by the Supreme Court on those applications are Annexures P-12 and P-13.
Moreover, when Civil Appeal No. 7422 of 1999 was decided on 9112000, the rights of the petitioners were not affected because it was observed that no candidate other than those who are already before the Courts could file a fresh petition after 9112000.
The petitioners have already approached the Supreme Court in April/May, 2000 and in pursuance to the orders passed by the Supreme Court, they have approached this Court in May/June, 2000 by filing the instant petition.
11.
I am further of the view that once the petitioners are higher in order of merit as determined hy HPSC than Lalit Kumar and Varinder Lather who are admittedly at serial Nos. 17 and 19, then they have fundamental right of equality granted by Articles 14 and 16(1) of the Constitution and both the petitioners deserve to be considered for appointment.
Apart from the fact that the Supreme Court has accorded protection to the rights of the petitioners inasmuch as the petitioners are entitled to take the benefit of the order passed in Civil Appeal No. 7422 of 1999 in the case of Sandeep Singh case (supra).
It is trite to observe that Government has to make appointment strictly in order of merit as determined by the HPSC.
The aforementioned principle of order of merit is so well entrenched in the service jurisprudence that any violation of the same results into violating fundamental rights under Articles 14 and 16(1) of the Constitution.
If any person lower in merit is allowed to steal a march over the person who is higher in merit, then in the absence of reasons concerning antecedents, the appointment has to be given to a person who is higher in merit.
In Jatinder Kumar v. State of Punjab, 1985(1) SCC 122, their Lordships of the Supreme Court made it clear beyond any doubt and the observations in this respect read as under :-  ".....
If the vacancy is to be filled up, the Government has to make appointment strictly adhering to the order of merit as recommended by the Public Service Commission.
It cannot disturb the order of merit according to its own sweet will except for other good reasons, viz., bad conduct or character.
The Government also cannot appoint a person whose name does not appear in the list.
But it is open to the Government to decide how many appointments will be made.
The process for selection and selection for the purpose of recruitment against anticipated vacancies does not create a right to be appointed to the post which can be enforced by mandamus.
"(Emphasis added) Similar observations have been made by the Supreme Court in the case of J&K Public Service Commission v. Narinder Mohan (Dr.), 1994(2) SCC 630 and the same read as under :-  ".....
Though it is settled law that consultation is not mandatory but the establishment of an independent body like PSC, is to ensure selection of best available persons for appointment to a post to avoid arbitrariness and nepotism in the matter of appointment.
Commission is constituted by persons of high ability, varied experience and of undisputed integrity and further assisted by experts on the subject.
Whenever the Government is required to make an appointment to a high public office, it is required to consult the PSC.
The selection has to be made by the PSC and the Government has to fill up posts by appointing those selected and recommended by the Commission, adhering to the order of merit in the list of candidates sent by the PSC.
The selection by the Commission, however, is only a recommendation of the Commission and the final authority for appointment is the Government.
Government cannot appoint a person whose name does not appear in the list...." (Emphasis added) It is thus obvious that once candidates lower in order of merit as determined by HPSC are appointed, then the petitioners who are higher in merit, have to be given appointment because otherwise the right of petitioners under Articles 14 and 16(1) of the Constitution would stand violated.
12.
The argument of learned State counsel that no direction could be issued in the face of Section 4 of the Act which has imposed a complete bar for granting appointment over and above the advertised posts is not acceptable because firstly the direction is being issued in the instant case on account of the orders passed by the Supreme Court on interlocutory applications i.e. IA Nos. 2 and 3 of 2000 on 1042000 and 452000 read with the directions issued by the Supreme Court in Civil Appeal No. 7422 of 1999, decided on 9112000, titled as `Sandeep Singh and another v. State of Haryana'.
If the prayer made by the petitioners is denied, then their fundamental right of equality granted by Articles 14 and 16(1) of the Constitution would be violated as candidates much lower in merit like Lalit Kumar who is at serial No. 17 and Varinder Lather at Serial No. 19 have already been given appointments in pursuance to the orders passed by the Supreme Court in Civil Appeal No. 7422 of 1999 in Sandeep Singh's case (supra).
I am further of the view that the direction issued by a Division Bench of this Court in Ashok Kumar Garg's case (supra) was challenged before the Supreme Court in a Special Leave Petition.
On 1682002 when the Special Leave Petition came up for hearing, the Act has already been notified in the official Gazette on 2732002.
Thereafter, it cannot be concluded that the provisions of the Act could not be referred to or brought to the notice of the Supreme Court.
The appointment letter issued to Ashok Kumar Garg makes his appointment subject to the result of writ petitions pending before the Supreme Court.
Similar condition could easily be imposed in the case of the petitioners.
13.
There is another aspect.
Once the petitioners are held entitled to be considered for appointment to H.C.S.
(Executive Branch) on account of their rights under Articles 14 and 16(1) of the Constitution, no statute or Act could be applied which would result into violation of their fundamental rights.
It is well settled that if the retrospective application of a statute results into violation of fundamental rights of a citizen, then such an Act or Statute could not be applied.
Therefore, the argument raised by the learned State counsel is liable to be rejected.
For the reasons stated above, the instant petition is allowed.
The respondents are directed to consider the case of the petitioners for appointment to H.C.S.
(Executive Branch) within a period of two months from today in terms of the directions issued by the Supreme Court in Sandeep Singh's case (supra).
The respondents shall be at liberty to impose the conditions which have been incorporated in the letter of appointment of Ashok Kumar Garg (Annexure A-6).
Petition allowed.
