JL Gupta, J (Oral) - The petitioner is working as an Inspector in the department of Food and Supplies.
He has filed this writ petition with a prayer for the issue of a writ quashing the seniority list dated August 31, 1976.
The petitioner alleges that this list was circulated on August 30, 1991 and his representation was rejected vide order dated October 15, 1996.
A few facts may be noticed.
2.
The petitioner was appointed as an Inspector on purely ad hoc basis on June 13, 1967.
The posts of Inspectors including that held by the petitioner were advertised.
The petitioner competed.
He was placed at Sr.No.
54 in the merit list.
Since a number of posts had been reserved, the petitioner could not be appointed to the post of Inspector.
However, he was offered the post of a Sub Inspector.
The petitioner accepted the offer and joined as a Sub Inspector.
He alleges that on April 20, 1970 he was promoted to the post of Inspector.
He joined as Inspector on April 30, 1970.
3.
On August 31, 1976 the department issued a seniority list.
The petitioner's name was included in the list of Inspectors.
He was placed at Sr. No. 362.
Mr. Rajpal Singh who according to the petitioner had competed with him was placed at Sr. No. 299.
The petitioner filed the representation after almost five years to challenge this list on February 3, 1981.
He was resting on his oars till 1987 and then he sent a reminder followed by another representation in the year 1988.
In the year 1995 the petitioner approached the Court with the grievance that his representations have not been decided.
This writ petition was disposed of by a Division Bench of this Court vide order dated April 11, 1996.
The respondents were directed to decide the representations submitted by the petitioner.
On October 15, 1996 the Director, Department of Food & Supplies had by a detailed order rejected the petitioner's representations.
Aggrieved by the order the petitioner has filed the present writ petition.
4.
A perusal of the order dated October 15, 1996 shows that Mr. Rajpal Singh had been appointed as an Inspector on November 19, 1969.
The petitioner was appointed as such on April 28, 1971.
Under Rule 11 of the Punjab Food and Supplies Department (State Service Class-III) Rules, 1968 the seniority inter se of the members of service is determined on the basis of the date of continuous appointment.
Since Rajpal Singh had joined in November 1969 and the petitioner had joined much later, it has been found that he has been rightly shown as junior to him.
5.
Mr. Kathuria, learned counsel for the petitioner submits that while determining the seniority the petitioner had to be given the benefit of service rendered by him from June 13, 1967.
Since the respondents had not given the benefit of the service, he had been wrongly treated as junior to respondent Nos. 3 to 63.
The contention is misconceived.
The petitioner had been appointed on purely ad hoc basis.
Thereafter regular selection had taken place.
The petitioner did not come in the merit list so as to be appointed immediately to the post of Inspector.
On the contrary, he was absorbed as a Sub Inspector and was later on appointed as an Inspector.
In this situation it is clear that the petitioner's appointment as Inspector in June 1967 was not continuous till his promotion in the year 1971.
Still further, it has been found as a fact by the Director that the petitioner had been promoted as an Inspector in April 1971.
No specific averment has been made by the petitioner to suggest that it was not so.
It has also not been shown that any of the persons who had started continuously officiating as Inspectors after his appointment have been treated as senior to him.
In this situation it is clear that the action of the respondents including the order dated October 15, 1996 is in strict conformity with the provisions of Rule 11.
6.
There is another aspect of the matter.
Admittedly the seniority list had been prepared by the department in August 1976.
The petitioner had submitted his representation after a lapse of 5 years.
He submitted another representation after 6 years on August 19, 1987.
Thereafter he had approached this Court in the year 1995.
The representations submitted by the petitioner were highly belated.
The claim made by the petitioner clearly surfers from the laches.
Any interference at this belated stage would result in unsettling the settled position.
No other point has been raised.
In view of the above, the present writ petition has no merit and consequently, it is dismissed.
