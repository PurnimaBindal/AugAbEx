NK Sodhi, J - Petitioner was appointed Assistant House Keeper by the Chandigarh Industrial and Tourism Development Corporation Ltd (for short the Corporation) on 1381987 in the scale of Rs. 570-1080 on temporary basis.
She was thereafter given a regular appointment on 581998.
By an order dated 6.1.1990 she was promoted to the post of House Keeper in the scale of Rs. 1400-2300 and posted in hotel Shivalikview, Chandigarh.
In pursuance of the approval accorded by the Board of Directors of the corporation and by an order dated 2241992 the pay and allowances of different categories of employees of the corporation were revised with effect from 111986 in terms of the provisions contained in Rule 11.1(a) of CITCO Employees Service Rules, 1977.
The arrears as a result of revision of pay scales were, however, to be paid with effect from 141991 onwards.
It may be mentioned that the unrevised scales of Assistant House Keeper and House Keeper were Rs. 570-1080 and Rs. 600-1120 respectively but after revision of grades both these posts carried the scale of Rs. 1500-2640.
Since after the revision an Assistant House Keeper was also given the same scale of pay as that of the petitioner, the latter made a representation to the Managing Director pointing out that the post of an Assistant House Keeper was a lower post from where she had been promoted and that it was quite anomalous that both these posts were carrying the same pay scale.
It appears that with the revision of pay scales there were some anomalies in the pay scales of different posts and, therefore, an anomalies committee was constituted with a view to remove those anomalies.
This committee considered the pay scales of different posts including that of the Assistant House Keeper and House Keeper and recommended to the Corporation that the post of a House Keeper should carry the pay scale of Rs. 1800-3200 whereas that of the Assistant House Keeper should be Rs. 1500-2700.
The recommendations of this committee were accepted by the corporation in regard to all the posts in relation to which such recommendations were made and the anomalies pointed out were ordered to be removed from the dates when the grades were revised or from dates prior to the date of the recommendation but in the case of House Keepers the recommendation was accepted with immediate effect.
In other words in the case of House Keepers the higher scale of Rs. 1800-3200 was given with effect from 1591995 on which date the recommendations of the anomalies committee were accepted.
It is against this action of the corporation that the present petition has been filed under Article 226 of the Constitution for a mandamus to direct the corporation to grant the scale of Rs. 1800-3200 to the petitioner with effect from the date when she was promoted as House Keeper i.e. 811990.
2.
In the written statement filed by the corporation the facts as stated by the petitioner have not been seriously controverted.
It is averred that the pay scales of both the posts of Assistant House Keeper and House Keeper were revised from Rs. 1500-2640 and then with a view to remove the anomaly the scale of a House Keeper was considerably enhanced to Rs. 1800-3200.
It is further averred that the post of House Keeper was upgraded in the sense that its pay scale was considerably improved from Rs. 1500-2640 to Rs. 1800-3200.
3.
The argument of Shri PS Patwalia Advocate for the petitioner is that since the pay scale of the post of House Keeper was revised to Rs. 1800-3200 with a view to remove the anomaly it should be given to her from the date when the grades were revised and since the petitioner was promoted on 811990 i.e. after the date of revision she is entitled to revised grade with effect from that date.
Shri KK Jagia, learned counsel for the corporation, on the other hand, contended that the petitioner is not entitled to the grade of Rs. 1800- 3200 with effect from the date of her promotion because the post of House Keeper was upgraded in September, 1995 when it was decided to give this grade to a House Keeper.
He also contended that the matter regarding grant of pay scale to the employees is a matter of policy which the employer alone can decide and that the court should not interfere in the matter of fixation of pay scales.
It was also contended that when the corporation decided to upgrade the post of House Keeper with effect from 1591995 it was perfectly valid for it to give higher grade of Rs. 1800-3200 to a House Keeper with effect from that date.
He referred to the judgments in Gyan Prakash v. Union of India and others, JT 1997(8) SC 400 : 1997(4) SCT.
758, Union of India and others v. The Secretary, Madras Civil Audit & Accounts Association and another, 1992(1) S.L.R.
667 : 1992(1) SCT 419 (SC), SK Bishnoi and others v. State of Haryana, 1993(6) S.L.R.
217 : 1993(4) SCT 127 (P&H) and Fertilizer Corporation of India Ltd v. Sarat Chandra Rath, 1996(4) SCT.
290 (SC) in support of his contentions.
4.
I have heard counsel for the parties and, in my opinion, the writ petition deserves to succeed.
The pay scales of all the categories of employees of the corporation were revised in April, 1992.
The pay scale of Assistant House Keeper and that of the House Keeper was fixed at Rs. 1500-2640 and it is not in dispute that prior to revision the grades of these two posts were different - the post of House Keeper then carrying higher scale of pay.
After the revision both the posts carried same scales and this anomaly was pointed out by the petitioner.
Similarly there were several other anomalies which were pointed out in regard to other posts and the corporation constituted an anomalies committee to look into those anomalies.
With a view to remove this anomaly this committee recommended that the post of House Keeper should carry higher scale of pay of Rs. 1800-3200 and that of the Assistant House Keeper should have the scale of Rs. 1500-2700.
This recommendation was accepted by the corporation.
In the case of Assistant House Keeper this revised grade was given with effect from the date when the grades were revised but in the case of House Keeper it was given with effect from the date it took the decision i.e. 1591995.
It is true that it is always open to an employer to revise the salaries/pay scales of its employees and also specify a date from which the revision of pay scales shall take effect but where an anomaly if pointed out in the revision of pay scales of any post and that anomaly is sought to be removed then it cannot be allowed to be removed from the date when the employer decides to remove it.
In the very nature of things it must relate back to the date when it existed.
There would be no meaning in removing an anomaly from a date subsequent to the date when the grades were revised.
In other words, if new grades had to be given by way of removing an anomaly such grades should take effect from the date when the grades were originally revised.
5.
The argument of Shri Jagia that the post of House Keeper was upgraded with effect from 1591995 cannot be accepted because the posts of Assistant House Keeper and that of House Keeper were both continuing to exist before and after the revision.
I have carefully gone through the judgments relied upon by Shri Jagia and find that they are not relevant for deciding the point in issue since those cases do not relate to the removal of any anomaly pointed out in the revision/implementation of the pay scales.
The view that I have taken finds support from a Division Bench judgment of this Court in Baghirath Ram and others v. State of Punjab and another, Civil Writ Petition 6778 of 1993 decided on 2671994 and also in Om Parkash Malik and others v. The State of Haryana, 1991(4) S.L.R.
751 : 1991(23) SCT 630 (P&H).
6.
In the result, the writ petition is allowed and the corporation directed to release the higher grade of Rs. 1800-3200 to the petitioner with effect from the date she was promoted to the post of House Keeper.
The arrears to which the petitioner has become entitled shall be paid within three months from the date of receipt of a copy of this order.
There is no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
