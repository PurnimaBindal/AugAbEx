RL Anand, J - Shri Jai Bhagwan has filed the present writ petition under Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India and it has been prayed by the petitioner that a writ in the nature of certiorari be issued against the respondents and the order dated 2381991 Annexure P-3 be quashed and a declaration be given that the petitioner be deemed to be in service throughout and is entitled to the consequential benefits of service.
2.
The case set up by the petitioner is that he joined the 3rd Battalion in Haryana Armed Police on 2271991.
He was permanently absorbed in the 3rd Battalion and was allotted Constable No. 3/776.
When he was serving in the 3rd Battalion, he was falsely involved in a case FIR No. 303 dated 3171991 under Section 324 IPC registered at Police-Station Sadar Karnal and he was placed under suspension vide order dated 281991 by the Commandant 2nd Battalion.
The grievance of the petitioner is that since he was not under the command and control of the 2nd Battalion, but was under the command and control of the 3rd Battalion, therefore, the Commandant of the 2nd Battalion could not pass the order of suspension nor could he pass the order of his discharge on the instructions of the D.I.G., Haryana Armed Police Madhuban who had given the directions to the Commandant 2nd Battalion on 781991.
It was also pleaded by the petitioner that since he has been acquitted subsequently by the Criminal Court, therefore, the order of discharge cannot stand in the eyes of law.
Moreover, the order of discharge is penal in nature.
It is stigmatic and it should be set aside with all consequential benefits.
3.
Notice of the writ petition was given to the respondents.
Reply was filed and it was pleaded that the order of discharge was passed by the Commandant, 3rd Battalion.
The petitioner was recruited as a constable in the strength of the Commandant, 3rd Battalion.
The writ petition is bad for non-joinder of necessary parties as the 3rd Battalion has not been impleaded as a party on merits, the stand of the respondents is that the service record of the petitioner was not found satisfactory during his probation period as he remained absent on several occasions and thereafter, he was awarded several minor punishments.
The petitioner remained absent from 1.1.1990 to 211990.
He.
also remained absent on 11.2.1990.
Again he remained absent from 30.4.1990 to 151990 and then on 24.4.1991 and then on 771991 and finally from 1771991 to 2271991.
It was also pleaded by the respondents that petitioner was appointed as a recruit constable on 7111989 in the 3rd Battalion HAP, Hisar.
Thereafter, he was transferred to 2nd Battalion on 471990.
The petitioner then was re-transferred to 3rd Battalion on 381991 and thereafter, he was put under the command and control of the Commandant of 3rd Battalion from 7111989 to 471990 and thereafter, from 381991 to 2381991.
The petitioner remained under the command and control of the Commandant, 2nd Battalion from 471990 to 281991.
Since the petitioner was under the control and command of the 2nd Battalion on 281991 i.e.
The date on which he was placed under suspension due to his involvement in criminal case under Section 324 of the Indian Penal Code, therefore, the Commandant 2nd Battalion had the jurisdiction to suspend the petitioner.
It was admitted by the respondents that DIG vide letter dated 781991 had directed the Commandant, 2nd Battalion, HAP, Madhuban, to take action against the petitioner in accordance with Punjab Police Rule 1221 but the said directions were not complied with as the petitioner was re-transferred to 3rd Battalion Hisar with effect from 381991.
The Commandant 2nd Battalion did not pass order dated 2381991.
This order is a order of discharge and not order of dismissal.
The total record of the service of the petitioner show that he was in the habit of absenting himself from duty as police constable.
Irrespective of the fact that the petitioner has been acquitted, it does not confer upon him any right of reinstatement in the police service.
With this defence, the petitioner has prayed for the dismissal of the writ petition.
4.
I have heard Shri I.S.
Balhara, learned counsel appearing on behalf of the petitioner and Shri Vijay Dahiya, learned counsel appearing on behalf of the examinations and with their assistance have gone through the record of the case.
5.
The case set up by the petitioners is of three fold; firstly that he was appointed in the 3rd Battalion on 2271991 and in these circumstances, he could not be placed under suspension by the Commandant, 2nd Battalion on 281991 as on that day he was not under the command and control of the 2nd Battalion.
Secondly, the order of discharge has been passed on the directions of the DIG, by the Commandant, 2nd Battalion who has not independently applied his mind to the case in hand and thirdly that the order is stigmatic in nature and it is not a simple order of discharge.
In support of his contention, the learned counsel for the petitioner relied upon 1995(1) SLR 509 : 1995(1) SCT 360 (P&H)(DB), Brahma Nand v. The State of Haryana and others, 1987(4) SLR 576 Hardeep Singh v. State of Haryana and others, 1984(1) SLR 426 Anoop Jaiswal v. Government of India and another and 1986(3) SLR 78 Rajinder Kaur v. Punjab State and another.
6.
I have considered all these cited judgments and these are helpful to the facts in hand.
Rajinder Kaur's case (supra) was a case where the services of a lady constable were terminated on the allegations of misconduct because she spent two nights with a constable.
In the light of that conduct of the lady constable an Inquiry was conducted and thereafter, the order of discharge was passed.
With this background it was observed that the order was virtually punitive in nature.
It is stigmatic one and, therefore, such order cannot stand in the eyes of law.
The judgment of Anoop Jaiswal's case (supra) is not again applicable because it was a case of indiscipline of a probationer.
Similarly, in the case of Hardeep Singh (supra) the order was again stigmatic when Shri Hardeep Singh was on probation.
There was specific allegation against him that he took part in a non-taking of food compaign.
It was held by the Hon'ble Supreme Court that in fact the order was by way of punishment.
Similarly, the judgment given in Brahma Nand's case (supra) is not applicable to the facts in hand.
The absence from duty or registration of a case may be a remote motive against the petitioner but it was never the foundation of the order of discharge.
Rule 1221 of the Punjab Police Rules as applicable to the State of Haryana lays down that a constable who is found unlikely to prove an efficient police officer may be discharged by the Superintendent at any time within three years of enrolment.
There shall be no appeal against an order of discharge under this rule.
7.
The reading of the above Rule would show that if a constable who is found unlikely to prove an efficient police officer, the punishing authority has the power to discharge such constable from service at any time within three years of enrolment.
The petitioner was taken into service on 2271991.
The impugned order of discharge was passed on 2381991 just after about one month.
During this period of one month there were several instances of absence of duty besides the registration of the FIR.
In these circumstances, this might have persuaded the punishing authority to discharge the petitioner from service but the order is not stigmatic.
It is true that there cannot be smoke without a fire but the smoke or fire has not been made the foundation of the discharge order.
The petitioner was a member of the disciplined force and if he could not confine himself to the discipline in such a situation the punishing authority always had the right to say good bye to a constable discharging him from service by resorting to the provisions of order 1221 of the Punjab Police Rules as applicable to the State of Haryana.
8.
The further argument of the learned counsel for the petitioner is that the petitioner was enrolled in the police department on 7111989 and he was allotted a constabulary number and was detailed in the 2nd Battalion and thereafter, he was transferred to 3rd Battalion on 2271991 and thereafter he was permanently absorbed in the 3rd Battalion and was allotted a new constable number.
In these circumstances, he could only be placed under suspension or discharged from service by the Commandant 3rd Battalion whereas the impugned discharge and suspension order have been passed by the Commandant 2nd Battalion.
Resultantly, these orders cannot sustain in the eye of law.
9.
On the contrary, the case of the respondents is that petitioner was appointed on 7111989 in the 3rd Battalion.
He was transferred to 2nd Battalion on 471990.
He was again re-transferred to 3rd Battalion on 381991 and in these circumstances, he was under the command and control of the 3rd Battalion, from 7111989 to 471990 and thereafter from 381991 to 2381991.
The petitioner was under the control and command of the 2nd Battalion from 471990 to 281991.
The order of discharge and order of suspension have been passed in accordance with law.
10.
I have considered the rival contentions of the parties and have also gone through the office file which shows that the petitioner along with two others was transferred from 2nd Battalion to 3rd Battalion vide order dated 2271991 and they were relieved on 381991.
Meaning thereby that for this period the petitioner remained under the control and command of 2nd Battalion but for the rest of the period he remained under the control and command of the Commandant of 3rd Battalion.
The order of suspension was passed on 281991 and on that day he was under the control and command of 2nd Battalion.
In these circumstances, Superintendent of Police of the 2nd Battalion was competent to pass the order.
The suspension order says that since the petitioner was arrested in FIR No. 303 dated 3171991 under Section 324 IPC, therefore, he is placed under suspension with effect from 181991.
The order dated 2381991 has been passed by the Commandant 3rd Battalion which runs as follows :-  "Discharge.
Constable Shri Jai Bhagwan No. 3/776 is hereby discharged under PPR 1221. with immediate effect as he is unlikely to prove an efficient police officer Sd/-  Commandant 3rd Battalion, HAP, Hisar".
11.
Copy of this order was also sent to DIG with reference to his letter No. 20875/A-7/16 dated 781991, which was addressed to the Commandant 2nd Battalion.
It will be wrong to say that the Commandant 3rd Battalion had passed the order dated 2381991 in a mechanical way.
If the arrest of the constable has been reported to the DIG, it is his duty to say to the subordinate to proceed according to law.
Thus, on 2381991 the petitioner was under the control of 3rd Battalion and the Commandant of the 3rd Battalion will be considered his appointing authority, therefore, the order of discharge which is not stigmatic in nature has been rightly passed by the punishing authority.
12.
In this view of the matter, I do not see any merit in this writ petition and the same is hereby dismissed with no order as to costs.
Petition dismissed.
