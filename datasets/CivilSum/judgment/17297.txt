THB Chalapathi, J - The petitioners are seeking issuance of a writ of certiorari to quash the order dated 1061997 (Annexure P-9) or in the alternative to direct the 2nd respondent to grant them those benefits which have been extended to the 3rd respondent.
2.
According to the petitioners, they joined the service in Kalsia State which was merged in Pepsu and the petitioners had worked as Clerks and promoted as Assistants and retired.
According to them, some employees namely Paramjit Singh, Faqir Chand and Gian Chand Singla who is respondent No. 3 herein filed the suit in the year 1986 seeking a declaration that they were seniors and that suit was decreed and those employees were juniors to the petitioners.
Therefore, the petitioners are claiming the same reliefs which have been granted to those persons including the 3rd respondent in the said civil suit.
According to the petitioners, they could have been treated to be promoted as Assistants from the deemed date of promotion when the plaintiffs were promoted in pursuance of the decree in the civil suit obtained by those persons.

When the writ petition was filed, all the petitioners have retired from service as indicated above.
While they were in service, they never challenged the seniority list.
The petitioners filed the writ petition in 1998 seeking the reliefs which some other employees got by filing a civil suit in the year 1986.
The Ist petitioner retired from service in the year 1982, the 2nd, petitioner retired from service in 1989, the 3rd petitioner retired from service in 1987, 4th petitioner retired in 1986, 5th petitioner retired in 1995 while the petitioners 6 and 7 retired in 1995 and in 1986 respectively.
The writ petition was filed in the year 1998.
The writ petition is liable to be dismissed on the sole ground of delay and laches on the part of the petitioners.
They never challenged the seniority list when they were in service.
Simply because some other employees challenged the seniority list and obtained a decree in the civil suit is no ground for granting the same reliefs to the petitioners which were granted to those employees by the Civil Court.
The petitioners accepted the seniority list which was circulated to them when they were in service and they never raised any objection.
In the civil suit they never got themselves impleaded as parties nor initiated any proceedings challenging the seniority list.
Simply because some other employees got certain benefits in the civil suit which was filed in the year 1986, the petitioners cannot be given the same benefits.
The writ petition is liable to be dismissed on the sole ground of delay and laches.
The claim of the petitioners has become stale.
Therefore, I am not in a position to grant any relief to the petitioners.
5.
In view of what has been stated above, I do not find any merit in the writ petition and the same is accordingly, dismissed.
No order as to costs.
Petition dismissed.
