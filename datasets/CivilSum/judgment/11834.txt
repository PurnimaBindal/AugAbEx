Jawahar Lal Gupta, J. (Oral) - This order will dispose of CWP Nos. 11307, 11317, 14436, 16126, 16332 and 16356 of 1989, 885 of 1990, 6414 and 12267 of 1992.
2.
The petitioners who are working as Inspectors of Cooperative Societies complain that the action of the State Govt. in not giving them the same scale of pay as has been given to the Inspectors working in the Department of Agriculture is discriminatory and, thus, violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
The petitioners place reliance on the letter dated February 6, 1958, issued by the Registrar, Cooperative Societies to the Secretary of their Association to contend that the posts of Inspectors in the two Departments had been equated and that they are entitled to the parity in the matter of pay scales.
3.
The respondents controvert the claim of the petitioners and maintain that the action is in conformity with law.
4.
It is the admitted position that prior to February 6, 1958, the posts of Inspectors of Cooperative Societies were in the scale of Rs. 90-5-140/6-200/7-235/8-275.
At that time the pay scale of the posts of Inspectors in the Department of Agriculture was Rs. 100-10-200/10-300.
It was only with effect from May 1, 1957, that Inspectors of the Cooperative Societies were placed in the scale of Rs. 100-300 so as to "bring them into line with Agriculture Inspectors."
However, the categorical case pleaded by the respondent-State is that the pay scales of two posts were never equated.
In fact the Inspectors in the Agriculture Department were in the scale of Rs. 130-320 with a provision for grant of selection grade of Rs. 320-400 (limited to 10% of the cadre strength).
Even, thereafter, the disparity in pay scales has continued.
The pay scales of the two categories were periodically revised and fixed as under :-  Date Scale for Inspectors in the Cooperative Department Scale for Inspectors in the Agriculture Department 121968 200-400 250-450 111978 570-1080with selection grade of 700-1200 limited to 20% of the posts 700-1200 1.1.1986 1500-2640 1640-2925 Laster on, the pay scales of both the posts were revised.
The Inspectors of the Cooperative Department were placed in the scale of Rs. 1640-2925 with effect from January 1, 1986.
The Inspectors in the Agriculture Department were placed in the scale of Rs. 2000-3500.
It is also the admitted position that the post of Agriculture Inspector has now been redesignated as District Agriculture Development Officer.
5.
On behalf of the petitioners, the two-fold contention is - (1) by order dated February 6, 1958 the Govt. had taken a conscious decision to equate the two posts.
That being so, the petitioners are entitled to parity in pay scales with the Inspectors working in the Department of Agriculture, (2) the action of the respondents is arbitrary and, thus, violative of the provisions of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
6.
The claim made on behalf of the petitioners has been controverted by the learned counsel for the respondents.
7.
The two questions that arise for consideration are :-  (1) Did the Govt. equate the post of Inspector of Cooperative Societies with that of Inspector in the Department of Agriculture ?
(2) Is the action of the Govt. in not giving the same scale of pay to the petitioners as had been granted to the Inspectors in the Department of Agriculture violative of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution ?
Reg.
(1):  The petitioners rely on the letter dated February 6, 1958 to contend that they had been equated with the Inspectors in the Department of Agriculture.
It is useful to notice the contents of this letters.
It has many mistakes.
However, the document as furnished to us reads as under :-  "Copy of letter No. EI-1572/VI/12-RCS dated 6.2.1958 from Shri SS Puri, IAS, Registrar, Cooperative Societies Punjab to the Secretary, the Punjab State Cooperative Inspectors Association, Jullundur.
Subject : Resolution No. 4 of the General meeting of the Punjab Cooperative Inspectors Association held at Jullundur on 21.7.1957.
Reference : Your endorsement No. 359-J, dated 791957.
MEMORANDUM Government has observed that the grades of Inspectors Cooperative Societies has been revised from Rs. 90-40-140/6-200/8-275/Rs.
100-7-170/8-250 to that of Rs. 100-10-200/10-200/10-300 with effect from 1st May, 1957 to bring them into line with Agriculture Inspectors.
Therefore, the question of further revision of grade of Inspectors can be taken up and as and when grade of Agriculture Inspectors in revised.
Government also do consider that the demand to bring the grade of Inspectors Cooperative Societies at par with Veterinary Surgeons had d a specialised education of grades should be given retrospective benefit is not appropriate.
Sd/- Puran Chand  Supdt."
A perusal of the above letter shows that it was a communication sent by the Registrar to the Society of the Association.
It was not a decision of the Govt. or even a communication thereof by the Registrar.
It is not a decision of the Govt. by which it may have been held that the posts of Inspectors of Cooperative Societies are equivalent to those of Inspectors in the Department of Agriculture.
Even if the Registrar had taken the view that the pay scale had been revised by the Govt. from Rs. 90-5-140/6-200/7-235/8-275 to Rs. 100- 10/200-10-300 "to bring them into line with Agriculture Inspectors" the letter cannot be treated as a decision of the Govt. to equate the two posts.
This was at best the view taken by the Registrar on account of the revision of pay scale of the posts of Inspectors of Cooperative Societies.
8.
Admittedly, the conditions of service governing the petitioners are laid down in the 1978 rules.
Rule 13 of those rules provides that "members of the service shall be entitled to such scale of pay including special pay, if any, as may be authorised by Government from time to time."
The authority to section pay scales vests only in the Govt.
Equally, even the power to declare the two posts in different departments as equivalent to each other is that of the State Government.
No order passed by the Govt. has been produced to show that the posts of Inspectors in the two departments had ever been declared as equivalent.
In such a situation, the plea that the posts had been equated cannot be sustained on the basis of the letter dated February 6, 1958.
9.
Faced with this situation, Mr. Rajiv Atma Ram, learned counsel for the petitioners submitted that the Registrar had merely conveyed the order.
In fact, the Govt. had taken the decision.
Admittedly, the matter under consideration was the representation submitted by the petitioners.
It has not been shown that the representation was submitted to the Govt.
Even a copy of the representation has not been produced.
In this situation, one has to decide the matter on the basis of the contents of the letter dated February 6, 1958.
On a persual of the letter, it appears clear to me that the Registrar had rejected the representation of the petitioners and declined the relief claimed by them.
The letter does not embody any decision of the Govt. to the effect that the two categories of the posts had been equated.
10.
In view of the above, the first question is answered in the negative.
It is held that the letter dated February 6, 1958, does not embody the decision of the Government.
It does not declare the posts of Inspectors in the Cooperative Department and those in the Agriculture Department as equal to each other.
The first contention, is consequently, rejected.
Reg.
(2):  Learned counsel then submitted that the action of the respondents in denying the scale of pay which had been sanctioned for the posts of Agriculture Inspectors to the petitioners is violative of the provisions of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
Learned counsel pointed out that even those categories of employees who were initially placed in lower scales of pay have now been given higher scale of pay while the petitioners who were in fact holding posts equivalent to those of Inspectors in the Agriculture Department have been treated in a discriminatory manner.
11.
In order to establish the charge of discrimination, it is incumbent on the petitioners to show that equals have been treated unequally.
The short question that arises is - Are the petitioners equal to the Inspectors in the Agriculture Department ?
12.
Admittedly, prior to 1958, they were in a scale of pay lower than that of Inspectors in the Agriculture Department.
Still further, in the year 1956, vide notification dated February 6, 1956, the Inspectors in the Agriculture Department were placed in the scale of Rs. 100-300.
The Inspectors of Cooperative Societies continued in the lower scale of pay.
It was given to them only with effect from May 1, 1957.
In the meantime, the pay scale of the post of Inspectors in the Agriculture Department had been revised and raised to Rs. 130-10-200/10-320.
Thereafter, as noticed above, on every occasion the pay scale of the Inspectors in the Agriculture Department was higher than that of the Inspector of Cooperative Societies.
To illustrate, while the petitioners were placed in the scale of Rs. 200-400 with effect from February 1, 1968, the Inspectors in the Agriculture Department were in the scale of Rs. 250-450.
At the time of next revision of scales, the petitioners were granted the scale of Rs. 570-1080.
Only 20% of the posts were placed in the selection grade of Rs. 700-1200.
As against this, the time scale granted to the Inspectors in the Agriculture Department was Rs. 700-1200.
Similarly, even at the subsequent revision of pay scale which was made effective from January 1, 1986, the disparity continued.
While the petitioners were given the scale of Rs. 1500-2040, the Inspectors in the Agriculture Department were in the scale of Rs. 1640-2925.
While revising the scales further, the petitioners have been granted the scale of Rs. 1640-2400 with effect from January 1, 1986.
However, their counterparts with whom they claimed parity were placed in the scale of Rs. 2000-3500.
Mr. Dhillon, appearing for the State further pointed out that the posts of Inspectors in the Agriculture Department were redesignated as Agriculture Development Officers and have been placed in the scale of Rs. 2200-4000 with effect from January 1, 1991.
13.
Thus, it is clear that the petitioners have never been at par with the Inspectors in the Agriculture Department and the two posts have never been equated.
Therefore, the charge of discrimination cannot be sustained.
14.
It also deserves notice that the two categories of employees are members of the different services, governed by different rules and are performing different functions.
The qualifications for the two posts are different.
While the petitioners have to be graduates with IInd division at the time of their direct recruitment to the posts of Inspectors of Cooperative Societies, the Inspectors in the Agriculture Department have to possess a minimum qualification of B.Sc.
(Agriculture).
Counsel for the petitioners were at pains to point out that they have to undergo training and qualify a diploma course for being eligible for the post of Inspector.
On behalf of the respondents it has been pointed out that this training/diploma is required to be undergone after selection and forms a part of the training which a direct recruit to the post of Inspector in the Agriculture Department has to undergo.
Still further, in the very nature of things, the duties of the two posts are different.
15.
If the members of different services performing different duties, governed by separate sets of rules are granted different scales of pay, it cannot be said that there is violation of the provisions of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution or that the principle of equal pay for equal work has been infringed.
Still further, the equivalence of pay scales is a matter for the authorities to decide.
The courts can interfere only when it is established that the two persons who are holding equivalent posts and performing similar duties have been treated dissimilarly.
There is nothing on the record to show that the functions performed by the petitioners are the same as those performed by the Inspectors in the Agriculture Department.
In fact, a disparity between the two categories of employees has remained since the hoary past.
The two have always been distinct and separate.
That being so, it cannot be said that the respondents have acted arbitrarily in declining the petitioners' request for the grant of higher scale of pay as has been sanctioned in the case of Inspectors of the Agriculture Department.
16.
Learned counsel for the parties have also referred to the fact that certain other categories of employees who were at one or other point of time in a lower scale of pay have now been placed in a higher scale of pay.
These are matters which require evidence.
At present, there is nothing to show that two equals have been treated unequally.
In such situation, the charge of discrimination cannot be sustained.
The second question is answered accordingly.
17.
Counsel for the parties have also referred to certain decisions.
The first decision that requires to be noticed is the pronouncement of their Lordships of the Supreme Court in The Employees of Tannery and Footwear Corporation of India Ltd and another v. Union of India and others, 1991(2) SCT 339 (SC) : 1991(2) SLR 131.
This was a case where the two categories of employees were placed in an identical scale of pay.
However, the benefit to one category was given with effect from an earlier date while to other, it was given with effect from a later date.
It was in this context that their Lordships of the Supreme Court had held that once the two categories of employees were declared equivalent, the State could not deny the benefit of revised pay scales and allowances to one set of employees while it had been granted to the other.
On this basis, the respondents were directed to so revise the pay scales and allowances of the four categories of employees in the said cadres that "they are at par with the pay scales and allowances of such staff employed with Cotton Corporation of India of August 1, 1983."
However, in the present case, the two categories of employees were never at par.
Consequently, the ratio of this decision is not attracted.
Mr. Atma Ram also referred to the decision of this Court in Dr. Sukhdev Singh and others v. The State of Punjab and another, 1991(2) SCT 209 (P&H) : 1991(2) SLR 336.
This was a case where the Govt. had taken a conscious decision on the file that Veterinary Assistant Surgeons were to be placed in the scale of Rs. 400-1100 which had been sanctioned for the members of Punjab Civil Medical Service (Class II).
Once the decision had been taken by the Govt. the Court had held that the benefit of as given above by the Govt. could not be denied to the petitioners.
Similarly, a number of other decisions have been cited.
These are Hersaran Singh v. The State of Punjab and others, 1984(2) SLR 384, Kirpal Jeet v. The State of Punjab and another, 1987(4) SLR 594, Shri Sat Pal and others v. State of Haryana and another, 1991(3) RSJ 368 and Haryana State Biologists Association v. The State of Haryana, 1994(3) SCT 198 (P&H) : 1994(4) RSJ 444.
All these decisions are clearly distinguishable on facts and do not call for a detailed examination.
18.
It also deserves notice that the position regarding the grant of equal scales of pay has been considered by their Lordships of the Supreme Court in State of Madhya Pradesh and another v. Pramod Bhartiya and others, 1993(1) SCT 138 (SC) : AIR 1993 SC 286.
Their Lordships of the Supreme Court held that "the material above mentioned goes to show that (a) the qualifications prescribed for the lecturers in the Higher Secondary Schools and the non-technical lecturers in Technical School, are same; (b) service conditions of both the categories of lecturers are same; and (c) that the status of the schools is also the same.
There is, however, a conspicuous absence of any clear allegation and/or material suggesting that functions and responsibilities of both the categories of lecturers is similar.
Much less is there any allegation or proof that qualitatively speaking, they perform similar functions.
It is not enough to say that the qualifications are same nor is it enough to say that the schools are of the same status.
It is also not sufficient to say that the service conditions are similar.
What is more important and crucial is whether they discharge similar duties, functions and responsibilities.
On this score there is a noticeable absence of material.
Whether we look at the averments in, and the material produced along with, the Original Petition or to the averments in the counter-affidavit or even to the averments in the counter- affidavit filed by the Government in MP 2277/85 (upon which the counsel for the respondents has placed strong reliance), we do not find any clear material to show that the duties, functions and responsibilities of both the categories of lecturers are identical or similar".
Similar is the position in the present case.
19.
Consequently, even the second question is also answered against the petitioners.
There is no violation of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.
20.
No other point has been urged.
21.
In view of the above, the claim made by the petitioners for grant of parity in the matter of pay scales with the Inspectors in the Agriculture Department cannot be sustained.
There is no merit in these writ petitions.
These are, consequently, dismissed.
However, there will be no order as to costs.
