Viney Mittal, J - The present petition under Article 226 of the Constitution of India has been filed the petitioner Daya Wati who is a retired Head Teacher (Primary) for the issuance of a writ of mandamus to the respondents to refix her pay in the pay scale of Rs. 120-175 as had been fixed by the Punjab Government with effect from July 16, 1960 and to pay her arrears along with interest.
2.
Certain facts may be noticed :  The petitioner states that she joined service as Primary Teacher on July 16, 1959 in the erstwhile State of Punjab and upon the reorganisation of the State her services were allocated to the State of Haryana with effect from December 6, 1968.
She continued in the service of the State of Haryana and got her due promotions from time to time till her retirement on attaining the age of superannuation.
Even prior to the petitioner entering into the Government service, erstwhile State of Punjab had issued a circular No. 5056-FR-II- 57/5600 dated July 23, 1957 and had taken a decision to revise the pay scales of low paid Government servants.
The low paid Government employees in the Education Department were also to be included in the said circular.
The teachers were classified in two broad categories on the basis of their qualifications namely category `A' and category `B'.
So far as category `B' is concerned, the teachers who were against JBT posts were granted three pay scales as under : i) Lower 60-4-80/5-120 ii) Middle 120-5-175 iii) Upper 140-10-320 The aforesaid communication further provided as follows :  "With a view to providing incentives, it has been decided that posts falling in these groups should be in the following : Lower Scale 85% Middle scale 15% 15% teachers in this group should straight away be promoted to the Middle Scale by selection, based on seniority and merit, while the rest should be given the lower scale.
Group III: Existing incumbents in this group should be allowed to retain their present scale of pay :- Rs. 80-5-100/8-190/10-250 Group III: Lower Scale 50 per cent Middle Scale 35 per cent Upper Scale 15 per cent" A copy of the aforesaid communication dated July 23, 1957 has been appended as Annexure P/1 with the petition.
3.
The case of the petitioner is that since the aforesaid instructions contained in the communication Annexure P/1 could not be implemented in its letter and spirit, for want of its proper interpretation, certain aggrieved persons approached this court through various writ petitions and while deciding one of the writ petitions No. 3218 of 1978, titled Ram Nath Chandok and others v. State of Haryana and others and the LPA No. 135 of 1986 decided on January 15, 1990, this Court directed the respondents to grant selection grade to the extent of 15 percent on the basis of seniority-cum-merit in the grade of Rs. 120-175.
However, since a similar relief was not granted to the petitioner, therefore, she along with some other persons, similarly situated, approached this court through another C.W.P.
No. 3430 of 1992, titled Dewan Chand and others v. State of Punjab and others for placing her in selection grade of Rs. 120-175 in view of the communication dated July 23, 1957 and on the basis of the judgment of this court in C.W.P.
No. 3218 of 1978 and LPA No. 135/86.
The aforesaid Civil Writ Petition filed by the petitioner and other persons was disposed of by this court vide order dated November 25, 1992 with the direction that the petitioners, if so advised, could file a representation to the respondents and if any such representation was filed by them, then the same would be disposed of by passing a speaking order.
4.
The petitioner further states that in compliance with the aforesaid directions given by this court, she filed a representation to the Secretary Education (Primary), Punjab, Chandigarh and Director Public Instructions (P), Punjab.
The aforesaid authorities placed the petitioner in the selection grade of Rs. 120-175 with effect from July 16, 1960 and refixed the pay of the petitioner with the observations that the claim for arrears would be restricted for a period of 38 months preceding the date of presentation of the writ petition along with interest at the rate of 18 percent per annum, as per the orders of the High Court.
A copy of the aforesaid order awarding the selection grade to the petitioner has been appended as Annexure P/4 with the petition.
Since upon re-organisation of the State, the service of the petitioner stood finally allocated to the State of Haryana, therefore, the Principal, Government Girls High School, Guru Harsahai where the petitioner had lastly worked before her selection to the State of Haryana wrote a communication dated July 7, 1995 to Headmistress, Government High School, Mandi Dabwali, District Sirsa, Haryana along with the aforesaid order dated March 11, 1995 (Annexure P/4).
A copy of the aforesaid communication dated July 26, 1995 is appended as Annexure P/5 with the petition.
5.
According to the petitioner, although the authorities in the State of Haryana were bound to implement the order Annexure P/4 passed by the authorities in the State of Punjab but the claim made by the petitioner was denied and the authorities in the State of Haryana declined to refix the pay of the petitioner primarily on the ground that since they were not arrayed as respondents in the petition, therefore, they were not bound to fix the pay of the petitioner as per the order Annexure P/4 passed by the Director Public Instructions (P), Punjab.
A communication in this regard was issued to the petitioner on August 30, 1977 written by the District Education Officer, Sirsa to the Headmistress, Government Girls High Schools, Mandi Dabwali and has been appended as Annexure P/6 with the petition.
The petitioner, thereafter, served a legal notice upon the respondents for implementing the order dated March 11, 1995 (Annexure P/4).
A copy of the aforesaid legal notice has been appended as Annexure P/7 with the petition.
Since the aforesaid legal notice bore no fruit, now the petitioner has approached this court through the present writ petition.
6.
The precise claim made by the petitioner in the present petition is that she is entitled to get her pay refixed in the pay scale of Rs. 120-175 with effect from July 16, 1960 by virtue of the orders Annexure P/4 and the respondents are bound to pay her arrears at least for 38 months prior to the filing of the writ petition No. 3430 of 1992 along with interest at the rate of 18 percent per annum with effect from the date the aforesaid arrears become due till its final payment.
7.
Upon notice issued of the writ petition, the respondents have put in their appearance.
A written statement has been filed on behalf of the respondents No. 1 to 3 contesting the claim made by the petitioner.
8.
A specific stand has been taken by the respondents that the petitioner was posted at Govt.
Girls High School, Mandi Dabwali under the control of State of Haryana, when she had filed the earlier writ petition No. 3430 of 1992 and in that writ petition she had stated her place of posting at Govt.
Girls High School, Guru Harsahai (Punjab) without disclosing the factum of her posting in the State of Haryana.
It is maintained by the respondents that since the petitioner concealed the factum of her posting in the State of Haryana and obtained financial benefits concealing her place of posting, therefore, she was not entitled to any benefit of the orders Annexure P/4.
It is further maintained by the aforesaid respondents that since while disposing of the earlier writ petition, no direction was issued by this court to the State of Haryana for granting the selection grade to the petitioner, therefore, the petitioner could not seek the implementation of the orders passed by the State of Punjab now against the respondents.
9.
We have heard Shri Harinder Sharma, Advocate, learned counsel for the petitioner and Shri Amol Rattan, learned Assistant Advocate General, appearing for the State of Haryana-respondents.
10.
Shri Harinder Sharma, learned counsel for the petitioner has submitted that the petitioner was originally serving in the erstwhile State of Punjab and the relief of refixation of pay sought by her was in fact based upon the communication dated July 23, 1957 issued by the erstwhile State of Punjab.
It wsa only on the basis of the aforesaid communication/instructions that the similar relief had been granted to the persons who were similarly situated in the earlier writ petition No. 3218 of 1978 (Ram Nath Chandok and others v. State of Haryana and others) and LPA No. 135 of 1986 decided on January 15, 1990.
Shri Sharma submits that since the similar relief was not being given to the petitioner, therefore, she approached this court through a Civil Writ Petition bearing No. 3430 of 1992 which was disposed of by the court vide order dated November 25, 1992 and the petitioner was relegated to the remedy of filing a representation in this regard.
Shri Sharma maintains that, accordingly, on aforesaid representation filed by the petitioner, necessary relief was granted to her by the Education Department of State of Punjab vide order dated March 11, 1995, when she was granted the selection grade of Rs. 120-175 with effect from July 16, 1960.
Shri Sharma submits that since the selection grade was to be granted with effect from the year 1960 i.e. much prior to the reorganisation of the erstwhile State of Punjab, therefore it was only the State of Punjab which was competent authority for granting the said relief and had rightly granted the relief while passing the order dated March 11, 195, appended as Annexure P/4 with the petition.
11.
On the other hand Shri Amol Rattan, learned Assistant Advocate General, haryana submits that the order annexure P/4 had been passed by the authorities in the Education Department in the State of Punjab on a direction issued by this Court in C.W.P.
3430 of 1992 on November 25, 1992.
Shri Amol Rattan submits that because of the fact that the petitioner had herself described her place of posting as Guru Harsahai (Punjab) when actually she was posted in Mandi Dabwali (Sirsa) and had intentionally not arrayed the authorities in the State of Haryana as respondents in the aforesaid writ petition, she was not entitled to any relief in the present writ petition.
Shri Amol Rattan further submits that the orders dated March 11, 1995 passed by the Education Department, Punjab were not binding upon the authorities on the State of Haryana.
12.
We have given our thoughtful consideration to the entire matter and find that the present writ petition deserves to succeed.
As a matter of fact, the petitioner had merely sought the relief which had been granted to her by this court in CWP No. 3218 of 1978 and LPA No. 135 of 1986 to similarly situated persons.
The refixation of the pay of the petitioner and all other persons similarly situated could only be done by the authorities in the State of Punjab.
While passing the orders dated November 25, 1992 in CWP 3430 of 1992, no relief actually has been granted to the petitioner but merely a direction had been given to the competent authority to decide the representation, if any made by the petitioner, in this regard.
It was only in the event of the petitioner found entitled to the relief that the same was to be released to her with interest at the rate of 18 percent per annum.
In these circumstances, the non-impleading of the authorities of the Education Department of the State of Haryana in the earlier writ petition was wholly of no consequence.
13.
We have noticed that while passing the order Annexure P/4 dated March 11, 1995, the petitioner was actually found entitled to the refixation of her pay in the selection grade of Rs. 120-175 with effect from July 16, 1960.
The aforesaid refixation carried out by the competent authorities of State of Punjab had to be implemented by the authorities in the State of Haryana, since the petitioner stood allocated in the State of Haryana on the reorganisation of the State.
Thus, we find that there is absolutely no justification for declining the aforesaid relief by the respondents while passing the orders dated August 30, 1997 (Annexure P/6).
No adjudication, whatsoever, was required by the authorities in the State of Haryana for finding the entitlement of the petitioner.
The authorities in the State of Haryana were merely required to carry out the implementation of the order Annexure P/4, passed by the authorities in the State of Punjab.
14.
Thus, the order Annexure P/4 cannot be legally sustained and is set aside.
The respondents are directed to refix the pay of the petitioner in the pay scale of Rs. 120-175 with effect from July 16, 1960, in accordance with the order dated March 11, 1995 (Annexure P/4).
The petitioner would be entitled to the payment of her arrears which are restricted to 38 months prior to the filing of writ petition No. 3430 of 1992 i.e. with effect from March 11, 1992 along with interest at the rate of 18 percent per annum as was directed by this Court vide order dated November 25, 1992, while disposing of the Civil Writ Petition No. 3430 of 1992.
The respondents are directed to carry out and implement these directions within a period of four months and pay the arrears as aforesaid to the petitioner within the aforesaid period.
This writ petition stands allowed with the above observations.
However, there shall be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
