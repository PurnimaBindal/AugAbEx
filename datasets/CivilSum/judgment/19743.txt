Jawahar Lal Gupta, J (Oral)- The petitioner is aggrieved by the order dated December 13, 1991 by which he was ordered to be retired when he had completed almost 57 years of age.
A few facts relevant for the decision of the case may be noticed.
2.
The petitioner joined service as an Inspector in the Department of Cooperative Societies in the year 1958.
He was promoted to the rank of Assistant Registrar on September 11, 1970.
In 1982, he was permitted to cross efficiency bar w.e.f.
September 1, 1977.
He was still further promoted to the post of Deputy Registrar vide order dated September 8, 1988.
He is due to superannuate in December, 1992.
This tenure of service is sought to be cut short by the impugned order.
This order has been challenged as being totally arbitrary and unfair.
3.
In the written statement filed on behalf of the respondent, the impugned order is sought to be justified on the basis of the petitioner's record of service.
Mr. Jaswant Singh, learned counsel for the respondent, has produced before me the file containing the Confidential reports of the petitioner.
A perusal thereof shows that the confidential Reports upto the year 1988-89 are available.
On the instructions from Mr. Jai Singh, dealing hand Mr. Jaswant Singh states that the reports for the subsequent period are not available.
4.
On a perusal of the reports from the years 1976-77 to 1988-89, I find that the petitioner's performance has been assessed as 'good' to 'very good'.
It maybe mentioned that the assessment for the years 1976-77, 1979-80, 1980-81, 1982-83, 1983-84, 1985-86 to 1987-88 is 'good'.
The assessment for the years 1977-78, 1978-79, 1981-82 and 1984-85 is 'very good'.
There is only one report for the year 1988-89, wherein the petitioner has been assessed as 'average'.
With regard to this report, Mr. Jaswant Singh states that actually certain adverse remarks have been recorded but these have not been communicated to the petitioner so far.
On a perusal of the original file and as per the comments recorded the Commissioner and Secretary to Government, it is apparent that the matter is yet under in investigation It appears that without completing the enquiry or conveying any adverse remarks to the petitioner, he was ordered to be retired.
It has also been mentioned that a charge-sheet under Rule 7 was pending against the petitioner, which was dropped after the decision to retire him had been taken.
5.
A perusal of the factual position, as noticed above, shows that the petitioner's performance has been invariably assessed as 'good' to 'very good'.
The remarks for the year 1988-89 have yet to be finalised.
At least nothing adverse has been communicated to the petitioner so far.
Nothing adverse regarding petitioner's performance has been pointed out otherwise.
Even the charges levelled in the charge-sheet have not been substantiated so far.
In this situation, it cannot be said that the petitioner was a `dead- wood' which needed to be chopped off.
Even otherwise, the petitioner's performance has been consistently good.
He was promoted in the year 1988.
It is no doubt correct that some charge-sheet has been issued to the petitioner with regard to the work done by him while working as Managing Director infed.
Yet, none of those charges was established.
In fact, even the enquiry has not been held into these charges.
If the Government wanted to punish the petitioner, it could have proceeded further with the enquiry.
Premature retirement cannot be used as a lever to punish an officer without establishing the charges.
6.
Accordingly, the writ petition is allowed.
The order dated December 13/24,1991 (Annexure P-1) is set aside.
It is declared that the petitioner shall be deemed to have continued into service and is entitled to all the consequential reliefs in the nature of salary etc.
In the circumstances of the case the parties are left to bear their own costs.
