SB Sinha, CJ.
- Having heard the learned counsel for the petitioner, we are of the opinion that Rule 22 of the AP State and Subordinate Services Rules (the Rules, for brevity) is only an enabling provision.
By reason of such an enabling provision, the State is not obliged to make any reservation for ex- servicemen.
If no notification has been issued directing reservation for ex- servicemen, the petitioner who had applied for appointment to the post of Assistant Public Prosecutor and having not been found suitable for such an appointment, cannot now turn round and contend that the State should have made reservation of ex-servicemen for the post of Assistant, Public Prosecutor.
2.
It may be that by reason of the AP State Prosecution Service Rules, Rule 22 of the Rules, would be applicable, by the same has to be read in the context thereof, i.e., having regard to the purpose for which such a rule has been made.
It is now well settled principle of law that the Court in exercise of its power under Article 226 of the Constitution of India neither can direct to give effect to an enabling provision nor the petitioner can be said to have acquired any legal right for issuance of a writ of mandamus in the absence of any notification fixing any quota for ex-servicemen.
It is also well settled principle of law that a writ of mandamus can be issued by the High Court if the petitioner has pleaded and proved the existence of a legal right of himself and corresponding legal duty upon the respondents.
3.
For the aforesaid reasons, there is no merit in the writ petition and it is accordingly dismissed.
No costs.
Petition dismissed.
