Harjit Singh Bedi, J. (Oral) - The petitioner's son, Girish, was detected as patient of Leukemia during the summer vacation of 1994.
On the inability of the petitioner to admit him to the All India Institute of Medical Sciences (in short the AIIMS) on account of excessive rush, he was admitted to the Sir Ganga Ram Hospital, New Delhi, as an Emergency case on 27th May, 1994, as per certificate Annexure P-1.
Girish remained as a Semi Paying Patient from 2751994 to 12.7.1994 but was shifted to the general ward as a paying patient on 12th June, 1994.
He was discharged from the hospital on 2nd September, 1994 but was advised to report for a check up every month.
He, however, suffered a relapse of his disease on 17th April, 1995 and was admitted to the said hospital once again, but ultimately passed away died on 23rd July, 1995.
The petitioner accordingly submitted three bills to the respondents for reimbursement as detailed below :-  (i) Bill for Rs. 52,304.55 for indoor treatment from 2751994 to 12.7.1994.
(ii) Bill for Rs. 24,440/- for indoor treatment for the period 2771994 to 291994.
(iii) Bill for Rs. 25,34175 for indoor treatment during the period from 1741995 to 2371995.
It is the conceded position that the petitioner has received full reimbursement for the bill mentioned at Srl.
No. (ii) and the dispute remains only with respect to the two bills mentioned at Srl.
Nos. (i) and (iii).
Mr. NS Bawa, the learned counsel for the petitioner has, however, pointed out that only a sum of Rs. 30,660/- has been sanctioned (though nothing has so far been paid) out of the first bill and that the deduction that had been made therein was based on a mis-conception that some part of the treatment had been taken as an outdoor patient whereas the entire treatment for the period in question had been taken by Girish as an indoor patient.
It has also been pointed out by Mr. Bawa that as far as the bill at Srl.
No. (iii) is concerned, no action has so far been taken by the respondents for its reimbursement.
2.
Mr. Jain, the learned State Counsel appearing for the respondents has, however, pointed out that as some of the expenses had been incurred on the treatment of Girish while he was an outdoor patient, the said amounts were not reimbursable though the respondents themselves were re-considering the matter with regard to the reimbursement of the said Bills and as was evident from Annexure R-III/T dated 19121997 and a decision was likely within a short time.
3.
After hearing the learned counsel for the parties, I am of the opinion that the petitioner would be entitled to reimbursement of the medical expenses incurred by her on the treatment given to Girish as an indoor patient.
It is evident from Annexure P-2 and P-5 dated 18th July, 1994 and 5th March, 1997 that Girish had received treatment as an indoor patient from 27th May, 1994 to 12th July, 1994.
The respondents opinion with regard to the nature of this treatment must therefore be held to be wrong in the light of these two annexures.
The objection of the respondents with regard to the bill at No. (iii) is also unsustainable for the reasons set out in the judgment of this Court in Satpal Kaur v. State of Punjab, 1999(3) S.C.T.
707, C.W.P.
No. 16622 of 1997 decided on 1951999, wherein it has been held that outdoor treatment which was a follow up and an integral part of the indoor treatment, must also be reimbursed as indoor treatment.
It is the undeniable position that there was a close connection between the indoor and outdoor treatment given to Girish for his disease and it could not be possible to separate the two.
4.
It is accordingly directed that notwithstanding the fact that the State Government is itself reconsidering the petitioner's claim for reimbursement, the payment will be made to the petitioner forthwith.
No costs.
Petition allowed.
