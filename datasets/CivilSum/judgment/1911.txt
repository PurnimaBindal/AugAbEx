RL Anand, J - I have heard the learned counsel for the parties and with their assistance have gone through the record of this case.
2.
Some facts can be noticed in the following manner :-  A dispute arose between the parties with regard to the execution of work and the matter was referred to the arbitrator, who gave the award dated 30.1.1993 clearly stipulating that the decree-holder will get interest @ 12% per annum on the awarded amount of Rs. 46,554.80 from the date of the passing of the award till the date of actual payment or till the date when a decree is drawn on the basis of the award.
The decree in this case was drawn by the trial Court on 2121996 when the objections of the parties under Section 30 of the Arbitration Act were disposed of and the award was made rule of the Court.
The Union of India was not satisfied with the decision dated 2121996.
It filed an appeal before the Addl.
District Judge, Ambala, who dismissed the appeal on 20111997.
Thereafter, the Union of India also filed a civil revision before the High Court which was also dismissed on 7121998 as having been withdrawn.
3.
The sole point for determination in this case whether the Union of India/judgment-debtor is liable to pay interest upto 2121996 or upto 7121998 when the revision of the Union of India was dismised as withdrawn in the High Court.
The learned counsel appearing on behalf of the petitioner submits that the executing Court cannot go behind the decree which was drawn on 2121996.
The first Appellate court and the Hon'ble High Court only approved the decree which was prepared for the first time by the trial Court which disposed of the objections and made the award rule of the Court on 2121996.
Therefore, the decree-holder can claim interest upto 2121996.
On the contrary, the learned counsel for the respondent submits that the decree dated 2121996 first merged in the order dated 20111997 passed by the first Appellate Court and that order again merged in the order dated 7121998 passed by the High Court vide which the revision of the Union of India was dismissed as withdrawn and in these circumstances the respondent/decree-holder is entitled to interest upto 7121998 on the awarded amount and this revision has no force.
4.
I do not subscribe to the arguemnt raised by the learned counsel for the respondent who relied upon the doctrine of merger.
This doctrine is not applicable to the facts in hand for the reason that the decree was drawn by the trial Court on 2121996 and that very decree was approved by the first Appellate Court and High Court.
No fresh decree of reversal was drawn by these courts.
In these circumstances, the judgment-debtor is liable to pay interest upto 2121996 and not thereafter.
Resultantly, the present revision is allowed and the impugned order dated 25.9.1999 is hereby set aside with the observations and directions that the judgment-debtor shall pay interest @ 12% per annum on the amount awarded by the arbitrator upto 2121996.
No order as to costs.
Revision allowed.
