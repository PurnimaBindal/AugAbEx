Harjit Singh Bedi, J - This appeal is directed against the judgment of the learned Single Judge dated 3012001 whereby the writ petition filed by the present appellant has been dismissed.
2.
The facts of the case are as under :-  The petitioner-appellant (hereinafter called the appellant) joined the Punjab Mandi Board (hereinafter called `the Board') on 181973.
He was allowed a proficiency step up on completion of eight years of service vide order, Annexure P-1 dated 30111988 and was also allowed to cross the efficiency bar on 121986 vide order, Annexure P-2, dated 15.2.1990.
He was, however, compulsorily retired on 18.6.1993 vide order, Annexure P-3, on the ground that his continuance in service was not in public interest.
This order was impugned by the appellant by way of filing Civil Writ Petition No 10024 of 1993.
3.
The Board filed its reply to the writ petition in which it took the plea that the work of the appellant had not been found to be satisfactory and he had not even been attending to his office regularly.
Reference was made to several documents including Annexures R-1 and R-2 dated 23101992 and 6.11.1992 respectively whereby inquiries had been made from one officer or the other as to whether the appellants had been attending to his duties.
It was further pointed out that the petitioner had been graded below average in his ACRs from 1987 to 1992 and the material on record did not justify his continuance in service.
4.
The learned Single Judge held that the order of compulsorily retirement was not to be construed as a punishment and that the assessment was to be made on the basis of the record of an employee with more emphasis on the latest record rather than the earlier one and it was clear from the order, Annexure P-3, that the record of the appellant had been perused and it had been found that his continuance in service was not in public interest and as such he had been rightly retired.
5.
The Board has during the pendency of this appeal filed CM No. 680 of 2004 putting the ACR's of the appellant from 141983 to 3091992 on record as Annexures A-1 to A-7.
We have carefully perused these documents.
We find that from 1987-1988 onwards, the overall assessment of the appellant's work and conduct does him no credit.
We reproduce here the gist of the entries made in his ACRs :-  Period Grading 1987-88 Below Average 1488 to 11111988 Average 1111988 to 3131989 Average 141989 to 31.3.1990 Average 1.4.1990 to 3131991 He had hardly shown his work as such no comments are offered.
141991 to 3131992 He had hardly shown his work as such no comments are offered.
141992 to 3091992 Remains absent from duty below average.
6.
It will be seen from the perusal of the aforesaid record that the appellant's record is either average or below average and in some years, it has been recorded that he had hardly shown any interest in his work and had often remained absent from duty.
This is also borne out from Annexures R-1 to R-3 filed alongwith the reply of the Board, which clearly reveal that the appellant had shown very little interest in his duties.
It is also significant that the marked decline in the appellant's performance had occurred after he had been allowed to cross the Efficiency Bar vide order dated 15.2.1990.
7.
The appellant has, however, relied upon a certificate dated 1551992 given to him by Sh. TC Gupta, the then Secretary of the Board.
As this document is the mainstay of the appellant's arguments, we have thought it fit to reproduce the same in extenso :-  "May 15, 1992 Dear S. Hara Sahib As I hand over office today, I am anxious to convey may sense of deep gratitude for the love and affection that you gave me in such ample measure.
It has been my shortest stay in any post but, I must admit, this has been very satisfying and rewarding one.
And if I was able to achieve some modicum of success, this was largely on account of the unstinted cooperation that I received and the hard work put in by you to take the `Board' forward.
I am also acutely aware of the `unfinished' tasks but I have no doubt that under the able stewardship of my successor you will go from strength to strength.
I was guided, in every action that I took by the sole consideration of welfare of the staff and larger interest of the Board but if along the way I have made mistakes, I hope you will extend your indulgence to me.
In the days to come I will continue to lean on you for support and strength.
With warm regards, Yours sincerely, Sd/- (TC Gupta) Sh. SPS Hara, Executive Engineer, Punjab Mandi Board, Chandigarh."
The Board has clearly denied the existence of any such letter on record.
A bare perusal of this letter would nonetheless show that the over laudatory references to the appellant's contribution to the success of the Board is clearly belied by the fact that he was merely an Executive Engineer and, therefore, a petty employee of the Board and clearly unable to direct or influence the working of the Board in the manner suggested.
These plaintiffs are also clearly outside the scope of an official record which casts a suspicion on their accuracy.
We, therefore, find that this certificate cannot be construed as over-ruling the wisdom of the many officers, who had recorded average or below average reports qua the appellant.
7.

It was this conduct amongst other issues, which led to average or below average ACR's being recorded against the appellant.
We are, therefore, of the opinion that the respondents were justified in making an order of compulsory retirement against the appellant.
We, thus, find no merit in the appeal.
It is accordingly dismissed.
Appeal dismissed.
