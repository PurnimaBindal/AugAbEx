RS Mongia, J - Civil Misc.
Application No. 2173 of 1993 was filed by the petitioner in the present writ petition, for directing the respondents to release the retiral benefits to the petitioner during the pendency of the writ petition.
It has been averred in the Misc. application that because of the pendency of the writ petition, the claim regarding the retiral benefits admissible to the petitioner was not being decided.
With the consent of the learned counsel for the parties, I heard arguments in the main case and I proceed to dispose of the same.
2.
Briefly the facts of the case are as under :  The petitioner was appointed as a Social Study Master in the J.A.V.
High School, Mandaura, District Rohtak, on 4th October, 1957.
Apart from the normal time scale of pay for the Masters, there was a Selection Grade of Rs. 250-10- 300 for 15% of the posts in the cadre.
The petitioner was given the selection grade of Rs. 250-10-300 with effect from 1st October, 1966.
On the recommendation of the Kothari Commission, the Selection grade was revised to Rs. 400-20-500 with effect from 1st December, 1967.
3.
The school, where the petitioner was working, was taken over by the Government on 26th February, 1969.
After the takeover, the school came to be known as Government High School, Mandaura.
According to the formal deed, dated 28th March, 1969 (Annexure P-1) executed between the School Authorities and the Government for taking over the school, the school was to be considered to have been taken over by the Government with effect from 26th February, 1969.
On that date the petitioner was drawing basic pay of Rs. 420/- in the selection grade of Rs. 400-20-500.
According to the terms of the deed of takeover of the school, as mentioned above, the petitioner was initially given the running time scale of Rs. 220-8-400.
However, by a letter dated 23/28th June, 1971 (Annexure P-2) from the Director of Public Instructions, Haryana the petitioner after being approved by the Haryana Public Service Commission (in short ('Commission'), was absorbed on regular basis in the Government service with effect from 26th February,1969.
4.
It was mentioned in Annexure P-2 that after the take over of the school, the pay of the teacher absorbed in the Government service after being approved by the Commission, was to be fixed in accordance with the Government instructions contained in Memo.
dated 11th April, 1965 a copy of which has been attached as Annexure P-3 to the writ petition.
The relevant extract of the instructions may be reproduced hereinunder -  "In supersession of Punjab Government memo No. 1663/ED/IV-(36/5) 4511 dated the 19th March, 1965 sanction of the Government of Punjab is accorded to the adoption of the following principles for the fixation of pay of teaching personnel of the privately managed schools taken over by Government.
(i) A teacher whose pay is less than the minimum of the Govt. scale should be given the minimum of the Govt. scale.
(ii) In other cases where the pay is more than the minimum of the Govt. scale he should continue to draw the same pay, subject to the condition that it may not exceed the maximum of the Grade.
The amount of this pay which he may be getting excess of the minimum of the Govt. pay scale being treated as personal pay and adjusted in his future increment."
It is the case of the petitioner that since on 26th February, 1969 (the date of the takeover and the absorption of the petitioner in the Government service), the petitioner was drawing the basic pay of Rs. 420/- in the selection grade of Rs. 400-20-500, his pay was fixed by the respondents in the scale of Rs. 400-20-500 (Selection grade) at Rs. 400/- and was given a personal pay of Rs. 20/- in accordance with principle (ii) of the instructions dated 11th April, 1965 (Copy Annexure P-3), the relevant portion of which has already been reproduced above.
5.
In the year 1980, the Selection grade of Rs. 400-500 was revised to Rs. 700-1150 and with effect from 1st January 1986, the same was further revised to Rs. 1400-2600/-.
On 1st January, 1988 the petitioner was drawing the maximum of the scale, i.e. Rs. 2600/- in the Selection grade of Rs. 1400-2600.
6.
The petitioner was to retire on 31st October, 1989.
A letter was issued by the Section Officer Audit of the office of the District Education Officer, Rohtak, on 14th March, 1989 (copy Annexure P-5) that according to the Government instructions in case where the pay of a teacher of a taken over school in more than the minimum of Government scale, he should draw the same pay but should not exceed the minimum of the grade, the pay in excess of minimum of the grade being treated as personal pay to be adjusted in future increments.
It is mentioned in the order that the petitioner's pay should have been fixed as under :  "2621969, 220+180 PP  26.2.70, 228+172 PP  26.2.71, 236+164 PP  26272, 244+156 PP  26.2.73, 252+148 PP  26274, 260+140 PP etc. etc.,  Excess payment may be got recovered and check that no other modification in this respect has been received from the deptartment."
Accordingly, a letter was issued by the District Education Officer, on 10489/7489 (Annexure P-6) re-fixing the pay of the petitioner.
The petitioner challenged the orders, Annexures P-5 and P-6.
The Motion Bench, while admitting the writ petition on June 1, 1989 stayed the operation of Annexures P-5 and P-6.
7.
The learned counsel for the petitioner submitted the following points for consideration :  (i) The respondents are not properly interpreting the Government instructions dated 1141965 (Annexure P-3) now.
In Tact, by correctly interpreting the instructions, the petitioner's pay was rightly fixed by the respondents at Rs. 400/- with Rs. 20/- as personal pay with effect from 2621969, under para (ii) of the instructions dated 1141965, already quoted above.
(ii) The action of the respondents is discriminatory, inasmuch as there were many other Masters who were fixed in the Selection grade on the takeover of the school as prior to the takeover whose Masters were in the Selection grade.
(iii) The respondents are estopped from refixing the pay of the petitioner and revise the same after almost 20 years of the grant of a particular pay in a particular pay scale.
(iv) No opportunity was granted to the petitioner before the action of re- fixing of the pay and reducing he same was taken by the respondents.
8.
In support of the first point, learned counsel for the petitioner submitted that by interpreting the Instructions dated 1141965 in a correct manner, the petitioner's pay, on absorption, was fixed at Rs. 400/- per mensam, Rs. 20/- as personal pay w.e.f.
2621969 i.e. the date when the petitioner was absorbed in the Government service.
On the date of absorption, there were two scales for the teachers like the petitioner i.e. time scale of Rs. 220-400 and Selection grade of Rs. 400-20-500.
According to principle (ii) of the Instructions dated 1141965 (relevant extract already reproduced above), the petitioner's pay had to be fixed in pay scale which was available and if there were two pay scales available, the pay of the incumbent had to be fixed in the pay scale which was nearest to the pay scale in which one was working prior to absorption.
The principle now adopted by the respondents to fix the pay of the petitioner would ultimately cause loss of atleast Rs. 20/- per month to the petitioner right from the date of absorption.
The petitioner was getting Rs. 420/- on 2621969 and by the impugned order, his pay now sought to be fixed in the pay scale of Rs. 220-400/- is Rs. 220+180/- as personal pay, meaning thereby that his total emoluments would be reduced by Rs. 20/- pm w.e.f.
2621969.
By no stretch of imagination an incumbent's pay can be reduced that what he is getting on the date of his absorption.
9.
On the other hand, learned counsel for the respondents submitted that pay of an incumbent on absorption, whether he was in the time scale or in the Selection grade, had to be fixed in the time scale and not in the Selection grade.
According to the counsel, the petitioner's pay was wrongly fixed in the selecting grade w.e.f.
2621969 and in fact his pay should have been fixed as sought to be done by the impugned order.
According to the learned counsel if a person was in the Selection grade, prior to the absorption, need necessarily be entitled to the Selection grade on absorption.
10.
After hearing counsel for the parties on this point, I am of the view that there is force in the contention of learned counsel for the petitioner.
According to the Instructions dated 1141965, the pay of an incumbent on absorption had to be fixed at the minimum of the scale if the pay was less than the minimum of the pay scale.
If it was more, then the pay was to be fixed at the minimum of the pay scale and the excess pay was to be considered as his personal pay.
Selection grade was one of the pay scales i.e. Rs. 400-20-500.
The petitioner was in this pay scale and was getting Rs. 420/- on 2621969.
Therefore, under principle (ii) of the Instructions dated 1141965 the pay was rightly fixed at Rs. 400/- in the pay scale of Rs. 400-20-500/- and Rs. 20/- was fixed as his personal pay.
If the interpretation put by the respondents is to be accepted, then the pay of an incumbent would stand reduced on absorption as is sought to be done by the respondents in case of the petitioner.
The impugned order is liable to be quashed on this short score alone.
11.
Points 2, 3 and 4 are inter-connected and are being dealt with together.
Assuming that the interpretation that was put by the respondents on the Instructions dated 1141965 (Annexure P-3) while fixing the pay in the pay scale of Rs. 400-20-500 was incorrect and the one that is being put now is correct, the point for consideration that arises is whether the Government can be permitted to reopen the matter almost 20 years the petitioner was granted a particular pay by interpreting the Instructions differently.
To my mind, the Government is estopped from reopening the matter at such a belated stage specially at the fag end of the career of an individual.
As observed above, it was only 2-3 months prior to the retirement of the petitioner that the pay was sought to be refixed which was fixed earlier almost 20 years back.
In a particular factual position, as it comes into existence by the order of the Government, an incumbent plans his future and starts acting accordingly.
It will be wholly arbitrary and inequitable if after such a long lapse of time the Government is permitted to reopen the matter and start making deductions from the salary, which would naturally affect the pension also.
Coupled with the above, is the fact that no opportunity was given to the petitioner before passing the impugned order reducing the pay.
Such an order would be a nullity being violative of principles of natural justice.
12.
Further, it has been averred in para 12 of the writ petition that one Baldev Singh Shastri, who was a Sanskrit teacher in the same scale as the petitioner, was also absorbed in the Selection grade of Rs. 400-500.
He retired in the year 1981 and was getting pension accordingly.
It was mentioned in the written statement that this factual position was correct but in September, 1990 ie after the filing of the present writ petition, an action was initiated against Baldev Singh Shastri for recovering the excess payment.
Learned counsel for the respondents was not in a position to apprise this Court as to what has happened till date in the case of said Shri Baldev Singh Shastri.
13.
Learned counsel for the petitioner has by way of additional affidavit also given another instance of one Ram Chander, a Drawing teacher, who was also absorbed in the Selection grade.
Learned counsel also cited a judgment rendered in Civil Writ Petition No. 2754 of 1983 decided on 8.12.1989 to buttress his argument that if there is no explanation coming forth as to why no action has been taken against the order similarly situated persons, then the only possible conclusion is that the State Government is not serious about taking any action against the similarly situated persons, and the notice issued to such person i.e. Shri Baldev Singh Shastri is just to contest the present writ petition.
No doubt, two wrongs cannot make a right but if the Government does not take interest to finalise the matter within reasonable time against the similarly situated persons, then that can be taken a circumstance against the State Government.
14.
For the aforesaid reasons, this writ petition is allowed and the impugned Annexures P/5 & P/6 are quashed.
Respondents are directed to release the retiral benefits of the petitioner, as if his pay had been rightly fixed initially in the pay scale of Rs. 400-20-500/-.
The retiral benefit be released within three months of the receipt of the copy of this order.
There will be no order as to costs.
Petition allowed.
