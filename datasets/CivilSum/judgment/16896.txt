GS Singhvi, J (Oral) - This is a petition to quash the order Annexure P10 passed by respondent No. 2 rejecting the petitioner's claim for compassionate appointment on a post carrying pay-scale of Rs. 950-1500/-.
2.
The averments made in the writ petition show that Shri Jagdish Chander, father of the petitioner, died on 1431991 while he was holding the post of Multi-Purpose Health Worker (Male).
At that time, the prescribed pay-scale of that post was Rs. 950-1500/-.
On 10.3.1992, the petitioner's mother applied for his compassionate appointment on the post of Clerk.
After some correspondence the respondent No. 2 ordered the appointment of the petitioner as a Class-IV servant in the pay-scale of Rs. 750-940.
In compliance of the direction given by the respondent No. 2, the respondent No. 5 issued order dated 18.7.1996 appointing the petitioner as a Class-IV employee in the General Hospital, Gurgaon.
The petitioner did not feel satisfied with the compassionate appointment offered to him and, therefore, he represented to the respondent No. 3 that he should be appointed on a post carrying the pay scale of Rs. 950-1500/- because the pay-scale of the post held by his father had been revised with effect from 111994.
The respondent No. 4 rejected the petitioner's request on the ground that at the time of his demise, Shri Jagdish Chander was holding a post in the pay-scale of Rs. 950-1500/-.
3.
Shri Amrit Paul argued that in terms of the instructions issued by the Government of Haryana vide letters dated 851995 and 13.1.1995, the petitioner is entitled to be appointed on a post carrying one pay-scale lower than the pay-scale held by his father and as the pay pay-scale of the post of Multi-Purpose Health Worker (Male) had been revised to Rs. 1200-2040/- with effect from 111994, the petitioner ought to have been appointed on a post carrying pay-scale of Rs. 950-1500/-.
The Learned counsel submits that even though the petitioner's father had died almost 3 years prior to the revision of the pay-scales, the Appointing Authority was duty-bound to take into consideration the revised pay-scale which was obtaining on the date of issuance of order of ex-gratia employment.
4.
We have considered the submissions of Shri Amrit Paul, but are unable to agree with him.
A look at the contents of circular letter dated 3181995 shows that the Government interpreted the expression `one step lower' used in the circular letter dated 851995 in the following manner :-  "6.
`One step lower' employment is interpreted as `One Pay-scale below' that of the deceased employee.
It is further clarified that appointment on compassionate grounds under ex-gratia scheme can be offered only on a post in a pay scale lower than that of the deceased employee."
(emphasis added) 5.
A bare reading of the above extract of the circular dated 3181995 shows that the dependent of the deceased Government servant is entitled to be considered for ex-gratia employment only on a post in a pay-scale lower than that of the deceased.
This means that the pay-scale of the post held by the deceased is determinative of the pay-scale of the post on which the dependent is to be appointed.
The crucial date is the date of death of the deceased and not the date of order of appointment.
Therefore, the mere fact that the pay- scale of the post held by late Shri Jagdish Chander was revised after almost 3 years of his demise, cannot confer a right upon the petitioner to be appointed on a post carrying the pay-scale of Rs. 950-1500/-.
For the reasons mentioned, we hold that the order Annexure P10 does not suffer from any legal error requiring interference of the Court.
Hence, the writ petition is dismissed.
Petition dismissed.
