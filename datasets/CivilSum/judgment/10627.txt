RL Anand, J - Sarvshri Jai Narain and Panna Ram petitioner Nos. 1 and 2, respectively, have filed the present writ petition under Articles 226 and 227 of the Constitution of India against respondent Nos. 1 to 5 and they have made a prayer for the quashment of the order Annexure P-12 vide which the claim of the petitioners for promotion to the rank of Inspectors has been rejected.
According to the petitioners this order has been passed in arbitrary and discriminatory manner without assigning any reason.
It has also been prayed by the petitioners that the letters Annexures P-5 and P-6 vide which the representations of other petitioners have been rejected be also set aside.
2.
The case set up by the petitioners is as follows :  3.
According to petitioner No. 1 Shri Jai Narain, he was enlisted as Constable in the Haryana Armed Police on 3011970 and petitioner No. 2 Shri Panna Ram was enlisted as Constable on 1121970.
The Government of Haryana in the year 1972 decided to establish Finger Prints Bureau at Madhuban as there was no Finger Print Bureau for that purpose.
Such Bureaus were in existence in the other States, including the State of Punjab.
It was decided by the Government to train their own constables.
A circular was issued on 27.1.1972 to all the DIG/CID of the different ranges in the State and they were directed to recommend/forward the names of the suitable Constables who were willing to undergo the training of Finger Print Bureau at Phillaur.
It was decided by Government that the constables who will be deputed for training will undergo training for six months at Philaur and thereafter they would be promoted as Head Constables on the basis of the report after successful completion of their training.
In pursuance of the said circular dated 27.1.1972 issued by the Government, the petitioners volunteered themselves to undergo the said training at Finger Print Bureau Phillaur and they were deputed for training from 141972 to 3091972.
The petitioners underwent the training successfully and they reported back to the CID Headquarters on 10th October, 1972.
Resultantly, the petitioners were promoted on 2831973 in the CID Department, Haryana.
The Government thereafter set up its own Finger Print Bureau at Rohtak w.e.f.
1101976.
The petitioners were required to give their willingness to accept their postings in the Finger Print Bureau as their future career permanently to which the petitioners gave their consent in writing on 1101974 and thereafter, the petitioners were absorbed.
With a view to encourage the officials who gave their willingness to serve at the newly created Finger Print Bureau, the eligible officials including the petitioners were promoted as ASI on 1101974.
Thereafter petitioner No. 1 had passed the Finger Print Expert Certificate Examination in the year 1975 and petitioner No. 2 passed the same examination in the year 1979.
As a result of which both of them promoted on adhoc basis to the rank of Sub Inspector w.e.f.
6.8.1976 and 28.3.1979 respectively.
4.
Now the case set up by the petitioner is that since they had given their option for permanent absorption in the Finger Print Bureau set up in Haryana and they are continuing as such on those posts, therefore, they should be given the benefit of promotion when they became ASI as on 1101974 and so on.
The petitioners further allege that though they were promoted to the post of ASI w.e.f.
1101974 yet they were deputed for Lower School Course.
Petitioner No. 1 also underwent the said course from 141982 to 30.9.1982.
The petitioner No. 2 was also deputed for the training programme.
It was also alleged by the petitioners that petitioner No. 1 was promoted as Sub Inspector w.e.f.
6.8.1976 and petitioner No. 2 w.e.f.
28.3.1979 while working in the Finger Print Bureau, therefore, they were senior to all those sub-inspectors who were absorbed in the Finger Print Bureau.
The petitioners further allege that respondent No. 4 also qualified the Finger Print Expert Examination in the year 1975, but he has been promoted to the post of Inspector on 111983, though he was junior to them in the rank of Sub Inspector.
Yet another junior Shri Ghanshyam Dass was promoted to the post of Inspector w.e.f.
111983.
It was incumbent upon the part of the respondents to consider the petitioners for promotion to the post of the Inspectors from the date when their juniors were going to be promoted.
5.
The petitioners further allege that in order to streamline the functioning and to make a better co-ordination with various wings of the CID, the State Government decided to set up an independent State Crime Record Bureau at Madhuban in which four units of CID, namely, Finger Print Bureau, Computer, Modus Operandi and Research wings were merged to forum one single cadre of State Crime Record Bureau.
The petitioner serving in the Finger Print Bureau were also transferred and merged in the Common Cadre of the S.C.R.B.
(State Crime Record Bureau).
After creation of this Bureau, the petitioners have been ignored in the matter of promotion to the post of Inspectors; whereas respondent No. 4 and Ghanshyam Dass were promoted and they were juniors.
Moreover, respondent No. 5 Nasib Singh was also promoted along with one Shri Harbhajan Singh in the year 1990 ignoring the claim of the petitioners.
According to the petitioners, respondent No. 5 was promoted as Sub Inspector on 2121986 whereas petitioner No. 1 was promoted as Sub Inspector on 981976 and the petitioner No. 2 was promoted as Sub Inspector on 28.3.1979 and shri Harbhajan Singh was promoted as Inspector in the year 1992 and he was actually promoted as Sub Inspector on 27.9.1989 and in this manner a gross injustice has been done to the petitioners who made representations but their representations have been rejected illegally and arbitrarily.
6.
In short, the case set up by the petitioners is that since they are senors to respondent Nos. 4 and 5 as they became Sub Inspectors on 981976 and 28.3.1979 respectively whereas respondent Nos. 4 and 5 became Sub Inspectors subsequently, hence the order Annexure P-12 should be quashed along with the letters Annexures P-5 and P-6.
7.
Notice of the writ petition was given to the respondents.
A joint written statement was filed on behalf of the respondents.
A preliminary objection was raised that ad hoc promotions of respondent Nos. 4 and 5 to the rank of Inspectors which were made in the years 1983 and 1990 respectively could not be challenged after a lapse of 15 and 8 years respectively as the writ petition has been filed in the year 1997.
Explaining the service record of the petitioners and the private respondents, it was submitted by these respondents that petitioner No. 1 became a Constable on 3011970 while petitioner No. 2 on 1121970 and they were taken on deputation in the C.ID.
on 141972.
The Finger Print Bureau in Haryana was set up on 1101974 and the police personnel were taken on deputation from the various districts/units for Finger Print Bureau and no accelerated promotion scheme was associated.
The petitioners were deputed for the training in Finger Print Bureau, Phillaur and on completion of the same, they were promoted as officiating Head Constables in the CID w.e.f.
28.9.1973 (petitioner No. 1) and 2831973 (petitioner No. 2).
The petitioners voluntarily opted for the Finger Print Bureau on 3.10.1974.
They were never absorbed in this unit i.e. Finger Print Bureau.
They were taken on deputation in the CID The petitioners were promoted as ASI in the Finger Print Bureau on adhoc basis w.e.f 1st October, 1974 and their promotions were fortuitous.
Both the petitioners passed the Finger Print Expert Examination in December 1995 and December 1979 respectively, as a result of which, petitioner No. 1 was promoted as Sub Inspector on adhoc basis w.e.f.
981976, but he was revert to the rank of Constable on 29.3.1983 and was repatriated to his parent unit Ist Battalion HAP Ambala City whereas petitioner No. 2 was promoted as Sub Inspector on ad hoc basis on 28.3.1979 in the Finger Print Bureau (CID).
They were never absorbed in the Finger Print Bureau on the basis of their options exercised.
For that reason, petitioner No. 1 was reverted as Constable and this was further reason that he was deputed for Lower School Course w.e.f.
141982 to 30.9.1982 as he was deputed for the aforesaid course by his parent unit and thereafter he was taken on deputation in the Finger Print Bureau (CID) as Sub Inspector on 6.10.1982.
Petitioner No. 2 was not deputed for any promotion course in view of his option.
8.
So far as respondent No. 4 Shri Ram Dhan is concerned, he was promoted as Sub Inspector w.e.f 1101974 in the CID, whereas petitioner Nos. 1 and 2 were promoted as Sub Inspectors in the CID on 981976 and 28.3.1979 respectively.
Petitioner No. 1 was reverted and he was deputed to the Lower School Course and after completion of the course he was again taken on deputation in the rank of Sub Inspector in the Finger Print Bureau on 6.10.1982.
Petitioner No. 1 remained out of Finger Print Bureau from 931982 to 5101982 and in this manner, Shri Ram Dhan and Nasib Singh respondent Nos. 4 and 5 were not junior to the petitioners.
It is admitted that respondent No. 4 and Ghanshyam Dass were promoted as ad hoc Inspectors in the CID w.e.f.
111983.
The petitioners are challenging this promotion after a lapse of 15 years.
9.
Explaining the case of respondent No. 5 it is stated by the respondents that he, along with Harbhajan Singh, was approved for promotion as adhoc inspector by the Director General of Police Haryana vide order dated 16th February, 1990 and they were promoted as adhoc Inspectors w.e.f.
19th February, 1990.
The case of the petitioner to the rank of Inspectors along with others, was sent to the Director General of Police but respondent No. 5 was promoted w.e.f 19.2.1990 and now after a lapse of eight years, his promotion can also not be challenged by the petitioners.
Respondents further maintain that no injustice has been done to the petitioners as no juniors were promoted to the post of Inspectors.
Respondent No. 4 was promoted as Sub Inspector on 1101974 whereas petitioner Nos. 1 and 2 were promoted as Sub Inspectors on 6.10.1982 and 28.3.1979.
The petitioners submitted their representations in the month of September 1995 and those were rejected as their cases for promotion have already been rejected by the Director General of Police Haryana.
It was further maintained that the Haryana State Crime Record Bureau Service Rules, 1997 have been notified and the seniority of the police personnel of State Crime Record Bureau who gave their options for permanent adsorptions has been fixed at their present rank irrespective of the fact as to whether they were holding the said rank on regular or adhoc basis, their promotion to their present rank shall be deemed to be regular promotion for all intents and purposes in future.
Now the seniority of the petitioners has been fixed in accordance with the new rules of the State Crime Record Bureau in view of their option.
10.
The petitioner earlier filed a writ petition in the High Court and in pursuance of the directions of the High Court, the service record of the petitioners was considered.
In short the stand of the respondents is that Shri Jai Narain petitioner No. 1 was promoted as ASI on ad hoc basis w.e.f.
1101974 and Sub Inspector from 981976 whereas Shri Panna Ram petitioner No. 2 was promoted on adhoc basis as ASI w.e.f 1101974 and Sub Inspector w.e.f 28.3.1979 and that the petitioner No. 1 was reverted to the rank of Constable on 28th March, 1982 when he was again taken on deputation as SI in the CID w.e.f.
7th October, 1982, therefore, they cannot be ranked senior to respondent Nos. 4 and 5 as Shri Ram Dhan was promoted as Sub Inspector w.e.f.
1101974.
11.
In short, the case set up by the respondents is that Shri Ram Dhan (respondent No. 4) was promoted as Sub Inspector w.e.f.
1101974 in the CID whereas petitioner Nos. 1 and 2 were promoted as Sub Inspectors in the CID on 981976 and 28.3.1979 respectively, therefore, both were junior to Shri Ram Dhan (respondent No. 4).
Moreover, petitioner No. 1 was reverted and remained out of Finger Print Bureau from 2931982 to 5101982.
In the manner, Sarvshri Ram Dhan and Nasib Singh (respondent Nos. 4 and 5) were not junior to the petitioners.
Respondent No. 4 was promoted as Inspector on adhoc basis w.e.f 111983.
The petitioners are challenging the promotions of respondent Nos. 4 and 5 after a lapse of 15 years and 7 years (respondent No. 5 was promoted as Inspector on 19.2.1990), therefore, the writ petition suffers from both on merits as well as on the ground of laches.
12.
I have heard the learned counsel for the parties and with their assistance, have gone through the record of this case.
13.
The case set up by the petitioners is that petitioner No. 1 became ASI on 1101974, SI on 981976 and also the case set up by petitioner No. 2 is that he became ASI on 1101974 and SI on 28.3.1979, whereas respondent No. 4 became ASI on 141972, SI on 1101974 and similarly respondent No. 5 became SI in the year 1986 ie 21st February, 1986, therefore, they are junior to them.
There is no force in the contention raised by the learned counsel for the petitioners.
In fact, the petitioners were never absorbed in the Finger Print Bureau.
They were subsequently sent on deputation though they were given the nomenclature of the post as of ASI and SI.
They were having a fortuitous designation as ASI and SI.
They were totally ad hocees.
This is the reason that petitioner No.1 was reverted as Constable and he was sent for Lower School Course and then he was again assigned the designation only in October 1982.
Similarly, petitioner No. 2 was also totally adhocee.
On the contrary, respondent No. 4 had already been promoted in his parent unit as Head Constable on Ist September, 1963, of course, he was adhocee as ASI and Sub Inspector but as Head Constable he was much senior to petitioner No. 1.
Therefore, he had to be ranked senior to the petitioners because they became Head Constables on 28.9.1973 and 2831973 respectively, whereas respondents No. 4 and 5 became Head Constables in their parent unit on 191963 and 511973 respectively much earlier to petitioner Nos. 1 and 2.
Moreover, respondent No. 5 got regular promotion in his parent Unit as ASI and he further got regular promotion in his parent unit i.e. Computer Wing as SI.
in this view of the matter, both the respondents were always senior and should be treated as such against the petitioners who had never worked against any regular vacancy.
14.
Faced with this difficulty, the learned counsel for the petitioners invited my attention to the Circular Annexure P-8 dated 3101994 and submitted that as per this circular, the adhoc promotions received by various officials during their posting in the State Crime Record Bureau or in its parent Organisations viz. MOS, FPB and Computer Wing etc. shall be deemed to be regular promotions for all intents and purposes.
Even if this argument is accepted, still the petitioners have no case.
Respondent No. 4 became ASI on 141972 whereas both the petitioners became ASI in their parent unit on 1101974.
Thus, both the petitioners will rank junior to respondent No. 4.
The adhocee cannot compete with the regular appointment.
Respondent No. 5 had already been promoted as Head Constable on 511973 in his parent unit, therefore, the promotion of the petitioners as ASI w.e.f.
1101974 cannot dislodge the claim of respondent No. 5.
Even otherwise, the writ petition suffers from delay, laches and limitation.
Both the private respondents were promoted in the years 1983 and 1990 respectively as Inspectors.
The writ petition has been filed in the year 1997 after lapse of 15 years and 7 years respectively vis-a-vis respondent Nos. 4 and 5.
The cause of action arose to the petitioners, if any, in the years 1983 and 1990.
Resultantly, I do not see any merit in this writ petition and the same is hereby dismissed with no order as to costs.
Petition dismissed.
