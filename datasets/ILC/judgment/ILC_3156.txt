5 JUDGMENT The insurer and the claimants are in appeal challenging the judgment and award dated 18.2.2017 passed in MVC No.521 2013 by the II Addl.
Sr. Civil Judge & JMFC Chikkamagaluru & MACTseeking compensation for the death of the said Bharath Kumar in the motor vehicle accident.
The claimants contended that the said Bharat Kumar while proceeding in Maruthi Wagon R car belonging to the respondent No4 Sri.Jithendra from Chikkamagaluru to Bengaluru met with the road traffic accident on 1032013 at about 715 am to 730 am owing to the actionable negligence of the driver of the Tractor and Trailer bearing Reg.
No.KA 16 T 6787 and KA 18 TA 6 3492 14 SCC 663 no endorsement was required to drive the transport vehicle the offending vehicle in question being a light motor vehicle as per the definition clause of Section 2of the Act.
It was further argued that the charge sheet was filed against the driver of the offending vehicle.
No independent evidence was let in by the appellant insurer to establish the factum of negligence on the part of the driver of the Maruthi Wagon R car.
Acquittal of the driver in the criminal proceedings would have no relevance for determining the negligence aspect in the present proceedings.
15.
Learned counsel appearing for the insurer of the Maruthi Wagon R car submitted that the Tribunal has analysed the material evidence in a right 13 perspective and has rightly fixed the liability on the appellant insurer.
The same requires to be confirmed rejecting the appeal filed by the insurer.
16.
We have carefully considered the rival submissions of the learned counsel appearing for the parties and perused the original records.
17.
Arguments of the learned counsel for the appellant insurer regarding the issue of negligence and liability requires to be rejected for the reason that admittedly the laden weight of the offending vehicle is 4000 kgs as per Ex.R3.
Section 2(21) of the Act contemplates that “light motor vehicle” means a transport vehicle or omnibus the gross vehicle weight of either of which or motor car or tractor or road roller the unladen weight of any of which does not exceed 7500 18.
The Hon ble Apex Court in Mukund Dewangan supra has categorically held that non kgs.
14 possessing of an endorsement on the driving licence to drive the transport vehicle by the driver holding the valid and effective driving licence to drive the light motor vehicle would not be the breach of the policy conditions and the same would not disentitle the claimants in claiming vehicular accident compensation.
In the light of the said judgment coupled with Ex.R3 we have no hesitation to hold that no endorsement was required to drive the transport vehicle.
Hence the appellant insurer cannot be exonerated from the liability to indemnify the owner.
Acquittal of the driver of the tractor and trailer in criminal proceedings would certainly have no bearing to adjudicate the issue of negligence in the motor vehicle compensation cases.
It is trite that in the criminal proceedings the order of acquittal may be passed on benefit of doubt and such order would not be considered to be a relevant factor to absolve the insurer from its liability.
Moreover police records support the case of the claimants.
Charge 15 sheet has been filed against the driver of the offending vehicle.
No independent evidence is adduced by the appellant insurer to rebut the claim of the claimants.
19.
RW 1 has categorically admitted that tractor and trailer could be used to transport the sand for the purpose of construction of pump house in the land and for agricultural purposes.
As per the police records Smt.Gangamma had entrusted the tractor and trailer to Sri.Basavaraju to take the sand from her house to Karvikere Beeradevanahalli Haliyuru.
The appellant insurer neither in the written statement nor in the evidence has taken a defence that the tractor and trailer was used for commercial purpose.
Merely it was carrying sand would not mean that the tractor was being used for commercial purpose and consequently there was a breach of the condition of policy on the part of the insured.
On this ground appellant insurer cannot be exonerated liability.
16 20.
We have perused the income tax returns and the letter of jurisdictional income tax officer dated 02.02.2016 which clearly demonstrates that the income tax return was filed by the deceased on 2932012 relating to the assessment year 2011 12 declaring the annual income of Rs.1 48 530 .
Returns were filed on 1712014 relating to the assessment year 2012 13 declaring the income as Rs.1 64 120 .
Thus it is clear that the income tax returns for the assessment year 2011 12 was filed by the deceased much prior to the accident.
It is evident that the deceased was the owner of Paridhi Sarees and Dress Materials situated at Bengaluru.
Ex.P13 is the Registration Certificate issued under the provisions of the VAT Act.
Ex.P15 is the business licence issued by CMC Chikkamagaluru.
The material evidence would disclose that the deceased in addition to textile business was engaged in jewellery business.
17 21.
Having regard to the business carried on by the deceased the Tribunal has determined the monthly income at Rs.15 000 for the purpose of reckoning the loss of dependency which cannot be faulted with.
As per the post mortem report Ex.P8 the age of the deceased was 42 years.
As per the driving licence at Ex.P10 the age of the deceased at the time of accident was 43 years.
Considering the same the Tribunal has applied the multiplier of 14 deducting 1 4th of the income towards the personal and living expenses of the deceased to compute the loss of dependency which in our considered opinion just and reasonable.
However the Tribunal has failed to award compensation towards future prospects.
22.
Having regard to the age of the deceased and his self employment 40% of the income requires to be added towards future prospects.
Thus the total annual income of the deceased would be Rs.2 52 000 .
18 Applying the multiplier of 14 deducting ¼ the of the income towards personal expenses of the deceased loss of dependency would work out to Rs.26 46 000 Rs.2 52 000 x 14 x ¾).
In terms of the judgment of the Hon ble Apex Court in National Insurance Company Limited Vs. Pranay Sethi and others and New India Assurance Company Limited V s. Somwati and Others the claimants are entitled to compensation of Rs.1 90 000 under the conventional heads.
