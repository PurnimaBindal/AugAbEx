“The word ‘detention’ is ejusdem generis with enticement and concealment” [Case Brief] Alamgir &amp; Another vs The State of Bihar Case name: Alamgir &amp; Another vs The State of Bihar Case number: 1959 AIR 436 Court: Supreme Court of India Bench: Justice Gajendragadkar, Justice P.B.
Sarkar Decided on: November 14, 1958 Relevant Act/Sections: Section 498 of Code of Criminal Procedure,1898, section 439    Rahmatia, wife of the complainant Saklu Mian used to live in the village Mohania, she had disappeared from her husband’s house on October 21,1952, after somedays Shakoor mian informed the complainant that he had seen his wife in appellants house.
The complainant asked appellant no.1 Alamgir to let go his wife so he told that he had married his wife and appellant no.2 warned him to get away.Appellant 1 wanted to marry to Rahmatia, and said she came to his house on her own will, because the woman was tired of living with the complainant and she freely come to stay with them.The magistrate convicted appellant no.1 and appellant no.2 and sentenced them to imprisonment for two months each.
On appeal the Sessions Judge confirmed the conviction but reduced the sentence to a fine of Rs. 50/- each.
The appellants filed a revision before the High Court.
The High Court issued a notice of enhancement and after hearing the appellants dismissed the revision and enhanced the sentence to rigorous imprisonment for six months each.Hence, the SLP.
ISSUE BEFORE THE COURT: The construction of the word ” detains ” occurring in a.
498 of the Indian Penal Code?Whether the High Court was justified in enhancing the sentence?
RATIO OF THE COURT On behalf of the appellants, Mr. B K Saran said that woman willfully come to stay with the appellant and according to him the word ” details ” used in s. 498 must necessarily imply that the woman detained is unwilling to stay with the accused and has been compelled so to stay with him against her will, and desire.
It is difficult to imagine that, if a woman is willing to stay with a person, it can be said that the person has detained her.
That is not the plain grammatical meaning of the word “detains “.
It is this argument which calls for our consideration in the present appeal.The court said that according to sec 498 the Indian Penal Code which deals with offences relating to marriage.
It protects the right of the husband and not of wife under s. 498 appears to be the deprivation of the husband of his custody and his proper control over his wife with the object of having illicit intercourse with her.
Section 366 deals with cases where the woman kidnapped or abducted is an unwilling party and does not respond to the criminal intention of the accused.
Incidentally it may be pointed out that the offence under s. 498 is a minor offence as compared with the offence under s. 366.The court observed that,” Whoever takes or entices away any woman who is and whom he knows or has reason to believe to be the wife of any other man, from that man, or from any person having the care of her on behalf of that man, with intent that she may have illicit intercourse with any person, or conceals or detains with that intent any such woman, shall be punished with imprisonment, 2 years and fine or both.The Calcutta High Court appears to have put a similar construction on the word “detention”.
In Prithi Missir v. Harak Nath Singh (3) it has been held by the said High Court that “the word ‘detention’ is ejusdem generis with enticement and concealment.
It does not imply that the woman is being kept against her will but there must be evidence to show that the accused did something which had the effect of preventing the woman from returning to her husband “The High Court was not justified in enhancing the sentence to six months rigorous imprisonment, and it should have only restored the sentence passed by the trial Court.
The question of sentence is normally in the discretion of the trial Court and the High Court can enhance the sentence only if it is satisfied that the sentence imposed by the trial Court is unduly lenient, or, that in passing the order of sentence, the trial Court had manifestly failed to consider the relevant facts.
The sentence of two months simple imprisonment imposed by the trial Court was not so unduly or manifestly lenient as not to meet the ends of justice.The court observed that there are three ingredients of the section.
The offender must take or entice away or conceal or detain the wife of another person from such person or from any other person having the care of her on behalf of the said person.
He must know or has reason to believe that the woman is the wife of another person; and the taking, enticing, concealing or detaining of the woman must be with intent that she may have illicit intercourse with any person.
It is clear that if the intention of illicit intercourse is not proved the presence of the first two ingredients would not be enough to sustain the charge tinder s. 498.
It is only if the said intention is proved that it becomes necessary to consider whether the two other ingredients are proved or not.Detention in the context must mean keeping back a wife from her husband or any other person having the care of her on behalf of her husband with the requisite intention.
Such keeping back may be by force; but it need not be by force.
It can be the result of persuasion, allurement or blandishments which may either leave caused the willingness of the woman, or may have encouraged, or co-operated with, her initial inclination, to leave her husband.
It seems to us that if the willingness of the wife is immaterial and it cannot be a defence in cases falling tinder the first three categories mentioned in s. 498, it cannot be treated as material factor in dealing with the last category of case of detention mentioned in the said section.The court was satisfied that the High Court was not justified in directing appellant No.
I to suffer rigorous imprisonment for six months by way of enhancement of the sentence.
It is unnecessary to emphasise that the question of sentence is normally in the discretion of the trial judge.
It is for the trial judge to take into account all relevant circumstances and decide what sentence would meet the ends of justice in a given case.
The High Court undoubtedly has jurisdiction to enhance such sentence under s. 439 of the Code of Criminal Procedure; but this jurisdiction can be properly exercised only if the High Court is satisfied that the sentence imposed by the trial judge is unduly lenient, or, that, in passing the order of sentence, the trial judge had manifestly failed to consider the 477 relevant facts.
DECISION HELD BY COURT: Court held appellant guilty and charged under section 498 of IPC for detaining the wife of the complainant.Appellant 2 was discharged and his appeal was approved, while the appeal of appellant 1 was dismissed and reduced the simple imprisonment for 2 months.
