In order to maintain the integrity and independence of the institution of CBI, there ought to be a complete insulation of the office of the Director, CBI from all kinds of extraneous influences.
 [Case Brief] Alok Kumar Verma V/s Union Of India Case name: Alok Kumar Verma vs. Union of India    Case number: Writ Petition (Civil) number-1309 of 2018 Court: The Supreme Court of India Bench: Justice Ranjan Gogoi, Justice Sanjay Kishan Kaul, Justice K.M.
Joseph Decided on: JANUARY 08, 2019 Relevant Act/Sections: Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003 ( Sec. 4, 6 and 8), Delhi Special Police Establishment  Act,1946 ( Sec. 4(1),4 A and 4(B) )    On 23rd October, 2018, the Central Vigilance Commission (CVC) passed an order under Section 8(1) (a) and 8(1) (b) of the Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003 (the CVC Act) divesting the petitioner who is the Director, Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) of the powers, functions, duties, supervisory role, etc. vested in him as the Director of the CBIOn the same date an order was also passed by the Government of India, Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions, Department of Personnel &amp; Training divesting the petitioner from exercising his functions and powers as the Director, CBI and by another order passed by this department Nageshwar Rao who was the Joint Director, CBI was asked to look after the duties and functions of Director, CBI.The legality of these orders has been challenged in the present writ petitions, one of which is filed by Shri Alok Kumar Verma and the other is filed by Common Cause.Various interlocutory applications were also filed by some CBI officers challenging the corruption charges registered against them and prayers were made for constitution of a Special Investigating Team (‘SIT’) for carrying out the investigations.
ISSUE BEFORE THE COURT: Whether the CVC and the Central Government were competent to divest the petitioner of his powers as the CBI Director before the expiry of his tenure?
RATIO OF THE COURT: The Court opined that in order to understand the true legislative intent behind the acts in question the court cannot ignore the guidelines laid down by this court in Vineet Narain and Others Union of India and Another (1998) 1 SCC 226.
In that case it was held that in order to ensure that the agencies like CBI discharge their duties in a proper manner it is necessary that the important offices of this institution are permanently insulated from any extraneous influence.The guidelines laid by this court in Vineet Narain’s case have played an important role in giving the CBI and CVC a pivotal position in our legal system.
The guidelines laid down in that case as are apt in the instant case are as follows: The CVC was given a statutory status.The Central Vigilance Commissioner is to be appointed by the President on the basis of the recommendations of the Committee comprising of the Prime Minister, Home Minister and the Leader of the Opposition in the House of the People from a panel of outstanding civil servants and others with impeccable integrity (as incorporated in Section 4 of the CVC Act).The Director, CBI must be appointed on the basis of the recommendations of the committee that was to be headed by the CVC and has the Home Secretary and Secretary (Personnel) as the member and the views of the incumbent Director shall be also considered by the Committee.
The final selection shall be made by the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet (ACC) from the panel recommended by the Selection Committee.The Director, CBI shall have tenure of two years.The transfer of an incumbent Director, CBI was to be approved by the Selection Committee.
According to section 4A (1) of the DSPE Act the Director, CBI is to be appointed on the recommendations of a Committee consisting of the Prime Minister (as the chairperson), the Leader of Opposition in the House of People and the Chief Justice of India or a judge of the Supreme Court nominated by him.
According to Section 4B the Director, CBI shall continue to hold his office for a period not less than two years notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained in the rules relating to his conditions of service and he shall not be transferred except with the prior consent of the Committee mentioned in section 4A (1)It was observed that the legislative intent behind enacting the Acts in question was clear that in order to uphold the integrity and independence of the institution of the CBI as a whole it is inevitable that the office of the Director, CBI is completely insulated from all sorts of extraneous influences.The Director, CBI is to be appointed by the Central Government on the recommendation of a High Power Committee.
Section 6 of the CVC Act gives the President the power to remove the CVC on certain specified grounds and also empowers the President to pass a suspension order from the office during the pendency of inquiry when a reference is made to the Supreme Court but no provision with regard to interim suspension or removal of the Director, CBI is found in the DSPE Act, 1946.
The only provision in respect of the CBI Director as contained in section 4B (2) provides that Director shall not be transferred except with the previous consent of the committee constituted under section 4A (1)If the legislature wanted to curtail the functions of the Director by conferring on any state authority the power to pass interim orders against the Director then it would have expressly stated that in the statute or would have provided enabling provisions in that behalf.
Since this is not the case the word ‘transferred’ in section 4B (2) of the DSPE Act cannot be understood in its ordinary parlance as being limited to a change from one post to another.
The word ‘transferred’ as appearing in the section must include all the aspects that have a bearing on the office of the Director including his divestment.The institution of CBI must be kept away from all the external influences so that it can perform its role as the premier investigating and prosecution agency without any fear and favor and in the best public interest.
The head of the institution, namely, the Director, must act as a role model of independence and integrity and this can be achieved only when his office is free from all kinds of control and interference except to the extent that Parliament may have intended.
This institution must be free from the interference of any public authority and interference should only be made when the public interest at large is the necessity of the situation and the adequacy of this necessity can be examined only by the Committee constituted under Section 4A(1) of the DSPE Act.
DECISION HELD BY COURT: In this case the judgment was given by JUSTICE RANJAN GOGOI that the writ petitions were disposed of with the following directions: The orders dated 23rd October, 2018 divesting the petitioner of his functions, powers, duties and supervisory role as the Director, CBI were set aside.The order by which IPS Nageshwar Rao was asked to look after the duties and functions of Director, CBI was also set aside.It was held that the matter should now be considered by the Committee constituted under Section 4A (1) of the DSPE Act and the Committee shall give its decision within a week.The petitioner was to be reinstated as the Director, CBI but he should not make any major policy decisions and his task as the Director must be confined only to the ongoing routine tasks till the time the Committee gives its decision.Regarding the various IAs filed by various officers it was said that it is not necessary for this court to determine their correctness but the parties have the opportunity of challenging them before an appropriate forum.
