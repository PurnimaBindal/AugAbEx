Allegations does not directly translate into the truth until proven in the court of law.
Until proven guilty, the law believes that no one should be unjustly held in custody.
This was held in the judgment passed by a single bench judge comprising HON’BLE MR. JUSTICE RAJNESH OSWAL, JUDGE, in the matter Jameela Bibi and another V Union Territory of J&amp;K (B.A.
No. 158/2021), dealt with an issue where the petitioner filed for bail in FIR No. 165/2020 for commission of offences under Sections 376, 366 and 109 IPC.
The petitioner has been in custody for the last nearly 5 ½ months and has one son, aged four years and one daughter, who is six years old.
The petitioner states that she has been falsely implicated, so she deserves to be enlarged on bail.
The respondents stated that the petitioner was also involved in commission of offence, so she does not deserve to be enlarged on bail.
The counsel for the petitioner vehemently argued that initially a false and frivolous FIR was registered for commission of offences under Sections 366 and 109 IPC was registered and just to ensure that the petitioner along with her husband remain in jail, the prosecutrix levelled false and frivolous allegations of commission of rape against the husband of petitioner and the petitioner was also falsely implicated.
AAG has vehemently argued that the petitioner has abetted the commission of offences, so she does not deserve to be enlarged on bail and further that the Court can frame the charges for commission of offence under Section 376-D IPC, as the arguments are yet to be heard on charges/discharge.
From the allegations levelled against the petitioner, it is evident that the only allegation against her is that she along with her husband and other persons abducted the prosecutrix and on 16102020, the petitioner brought the food for all the persons.
There is no allegation that the petitioner was present when the rape was committed or she has any knowledge of the same.
